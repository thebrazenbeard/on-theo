## PROPOSAL

Construct a deliberately austere control Yeshua from the earliest defensible evidence: undisputed Paul, possible embedded pre-Pauline tradition, Mark, first-century context, and later external controls whose date/authenticity/dependence remain explicit.

The proposal claims that this yields a strong minimal floor around:
- historical existence;
- Jewish embeddedness;
- a real follower/family network;
- crucifixion;
- execution under Pilate;
- early burial tradition;
- very early resurrection/vindication conviction;

with weaker/probable claims around:
- Galilee/Nazareth;
- John the Baptist;
- Kingdom proclamation;
- healing/exorcism reputation;

and unresolved claims around:
- Temple causation;
- exact self-conception;
- Judas mechanics;
- inward/mystical centrality.

> **HOSTILE REVIEWER**
>
> The control is useful, but it still smuggles several conclusions through the word "baseline."
>
> First, source age is not event probability. Paul is early but non-biographical; Mark is later but narratively dense. If the project calls Mark-only reconstructions part of a "historical floor," it risks converting survival chronology into historical privilege.
>
> Second, the historicity claim is strong but the argument must not lean too heavily on "James the Lord's brother." A cultic or honorific reading of brother-language is a real lexical challenge even if it is not the best total explanation. Josephus helps only if Antiquities 20.200 is authentic, and recent 2026 scholarship still treats authenticity questions as discussable.
>
> Third, "Jewish figure" is safer than "Jewish religious program." Paul and Mark are themselves Jewish-scriptural interpreters writing after Jesus. Their frames can tell us a great deal about the earliest movement without automatically giving direct access to Jesus' own halakhic or sectarian identity.
>
> Fourth, the Galilee/Nazareth claim is carried almost entirely by Mark in this restricted source set. It is probably right, but the baseline must say "probable geographic association," not treat it as independently secured.
>
> Fifth, John is a classic place where consensus can outrun independence. Josephus independently proves a Baptist movement and John; he does not independently connect Jesus to John. Mark supplies the connection. "Probably baptized" is reasonable, but the claim must stay below the level of multiply attested fact.
>
> Sixth, the Kingdom claim is still too comfortable. Mark makes the Kingdom programmatic, but R1 has not yet tested independent sayings, Q hypotheses, special Matthew/Luke traditions, or source strata. Calling Kingdom proclamation "moderate-high" before R2 prejudges R2. Keep it probable but explicitly provisional.
>
> Seventh, the healer/exorcist claim is the weakest positive characterization in the proposed floor. Narrative frequency inside Mark is not independence. The safe conclusion is that by Mark's composition Jesus was remembered/presented as a healer and exorcist. A pre-Markan public reputation is plausible, not established.
>
> Eighth, "the Twelve" is early in 1 Corinthians 15, but the exact extent of the pre-Pauline formula is reconstructed. Even if Paul himself wrote "the Twelve," that proves an early group-label/tradition, not a recoverable roster or that Jesus historically constituted exactly the later canonical Twelve.
>
> Ninth, the Temple-action-to-execution chain is explanatory elegance with weak R1 independence. It should remain UNRESOLVED in this baseline, even if later R4 makes it probable.
>
> Tenth, Pilate is strong at the high level, but the baseline should not pretend Tacitus fixes the independence problem. Mark is the first admitted explicit Pilate source. Tacitus is later and may have obtained information through Christians or Pliny; Josephus 18 is textually difficult. The result survives because the convergence is substantial and no serious early rival execution setting exists, not because there are three clean independent witnesses.
>
> Eleventh, the burial claim is well phrased as tradition history. Do not silently convert "burial was in an early formula" into "Joseph of Arimathea placed Jesus in the canonical tomb."
>
> Twelfth, the resurrection claim must distinguish "reported appearances" from "appearances happened." Paul proves very early conviction and appearance claims. He does not, by himself, decide the ontology or perceptual mechanism.
>
> Thirteenth, the negative propositions should be treated as actual failed promotion attempts. Baseline A does **not** establish exact messianic/divine self-conception, detailed Judas-for-money/arrest-information mechanics, or an inward-mystical central teaching. Those propositions should be REJECTED as supported Baseline-A conclusions, while their logical negations remain unproved.
>
> Finally, the project should admit the uncomfortable global result: Baseline A mostly resembles mainstream critical historical-Jesus reconstruction. Its distinctive Mary/Judas/mystical/psychological/Roman hypotheses receive essentially no positive promotion from R1.

## RESEARCH RESPONSE

The objections are substantially correct.

The baseline will be revised in the following ways:

1. "historical floor" will be reserved for the strongest claims; Mark-only claims will be explicitly labeled probable/provisional rather than independently secured.
2. Historical existence will retain very-high confidence but its ceiling will state that neither Pauline kinship language nor Josephus is individually decisive.
3. Jewish embeddedness will be narrowed away from any claim that R1 reconstructs a specific sectarian or halakhic program.
4. Galilee/Nazareth remains probable with Mark carrying the admitted geographic burden.
5. John association/baptism remains probable but explicitly not independently attested by Josephus.
6. Kingdom confidence will be lowered to moderate/provisional pending R2.
7. Healing/exorcism will be narrowed to an early Markan reputation/presentation with pre-Markan historicity plausible.
8. The follower-network claim will separate an early "Twelve" group-label/tradition from a reconstructed historical roster.
9. Temple causation will become UNRESOLVED.
10. Pilate survives at high level while Tacitus/Josephus independence stays contested.
11. Burial remains a strong early-tradition claim, not a tomb-logistics claim.
12. Resurrection language will be revised to "reported appearances" and early conviction.
13. Exact self-conception, detailed Judas mechanics, and inward-mystical centrality will be REJECTED as Baseline-A-supported conclusions.
14. The packet will state directly that R1 gives no positive support to the project's distinctive suppression/conspiracy/mystical/psychological hypotheses.

The review state remains same-runtime. No claim becomes independently reviewed.

> **HOSTILE REJOINDER**
>
> The revisions fix the main semantic overreach, but two additional cautions are necessary.
>
> First, do not let "rejected by Baseline A" become "historically false." The source constraint is intentionally artificial. A later independent tradition can still alter Baseline B.
>
> Second, do not let the absence of distinctive-project support become evidence that the mainstream reconstruction is complete. R1 is deliberately underpowered for sayings, noncanonical independence, social memory, and later counter-tradition.
>
> With those guards, the baseline is doing what a control should do: establishing what the project has to beat rather than what it wants to believe.
