## Challenge 1 — Does "selection pressure" smuggle in conspiracy?

Risk:
Yes, if used to imply coordination or hidden intent.

Repair:
Use "selection pressure" only to describe differential material conditions:
- funding;
- property;
- legal recognition;
- copying;
- assembly rights;
- exile;
- confiscation;
- monumentalization.

No common secret plan is required.

Disposition:
`SURVIVES_WITH_DEFINITION`.

## Challenge 2 — Did Constantine create episcopal power?

No.

Bishops already possessed authority, property, charitable functions, and
interregional networks.

Claudia Rapp specifically warns against exaggerating every Constantinian legal
measure into creation of a new secular episcopal jurisdiction.

Repair:

`CONSTANTINE_CREATED_BISHOPS_AS_CIVIC_LEADERS = REJECTED`.

`CONSTANTINIAN_POLICY_AMPLIFIED/FORMALIZED_EXISTING_EPISCOPAL_PUBLIC ROLES = SURVIVES`.

## Challenge 3 — Did Constantine impose Nicene orthodoxy successfully?

No.

The post-325 reversal is decisive:
- Arius recalled;
- opponents of Arius sometimes sidelined/exiled;
- Constantius II later promotes non-Nicene creeds.

This weakens any theory of a single fixed Constantinian doctrinal blueprint.

Repair:
Treat the imperial machinery as faction-amplifying but politically reversible.

## Challenge 4 — Does anti-Arius book burning prove broad canon suppression?

No.

The preserved edict against Arius is evidence for targeted coercive textual
suppression.

It is not evidence that:
- all alternative gospels were burned;
- the 27-book canon was enforced by police;
- libraries were systematically purged of every noncanonical Christian text.

Repair:
`TARGETED_TEXT_SUPPRESSION = SURVIVES`.
`GENERAL_NONCANONICAL_PURGE = REJECTED`.

## Challenge 5 — Do the fifty Scripture copies prove a fixed canon?

No.

The commission proves state-funded scripture production.

Exact contents remain unresolved.
Scholarly arguments that the commission presupposes some working canon do not
establish the modern 27-book New Testament.

Repair:
retain `AMPLIFICATION`, not `CANON_SELECTION`.

## Challenge 6 — Does Eusebius' Canon Table technology result from Constantine?

Not demonstrated.

Eusebius' apparatus belongs to fourth-century Caesarean scholarship and has
massive later influence.

Its overlap with the Constantinian scripture commission is historically
important, but direct imperial causation is unproven.

Repair:
classify as:
`CONVERGENCE / NOT DIRECT CAUSATION`.

## Challenge 7 — Does Constantinian holy-site construction fabricate the sites?

Not necessarily.

Some locations may preserve genuine older memories.
The Holy Sepulchre location in particular requires independent archaeological
and topographical assessment.

Joan Taylor's strong thesis that Constantinian Palestine is foundational for
Christian holy-place culture should not be flattened into:
"Constantine invented every site."

Repair:
`IMPERIAL_MONUMENTALIZATION_SHAPES_AUTHORITY = SURVIVES`.
`SITE_FALSE_BECAUSE_CONSTANTINIAN = REJECTED`.

## Challenge 8 — Does Easter policy erase Jewish origins?

No.

Christian scripture remains saturated with Jewish texts, characters, festivals,
and theology.

But Constantine's letter explicitly pushes Christian Easter calculation away
from Jewish practice using hostile rhetoric, and scholarship identifies Nicaea
as an important threshold in calendar cohesion.

Repair:
Use:
`INSTITUTIONAL_DE_JUDAIZATION_PRESSURE`,
not:
`TOTAL_ERASURE_OF_JEWISHNESS`.

## Challenge 9 — Is "Roman-Christian Jesus" too strong?

It can be.

Constantine did not rewrite the Gospel narratives into an emperor.

But imperial:
- architecture;
- pilgrimage;
- victory symbolism;
- liturgical calendrics;
- political theology

changed how Jesus was publicly represented and encountered.

Repair:
Use:
`IMPERIALLY_MEDIATED_CHRISTIAN_MEMORY_OF_YESHUA`.

## Challenge 10 — Is Constantine's personal motive reducible to mass control?

No.

Mark Edwards and other scholars argue that Constantine's writings are compatible
with genuine Christian conviction.

His own letter links theological unity and world order.

Therefore the strongest model is:
`SINCERE_RELIGIOUS_CONVICTION + IMPERIAL_UNITY_INTEREST`,
with relative weighting unresolved.

"Cynical control" is not required.

## Challenge 11 — Does "the Great" indicate moral approval?

Not reliably.

Contemporary honorifics already use:
- `Maximus Augustus`;
- `Constantini Maximi Victoris`.

These belong to Roman political honor culture and victory ideology.

The later epithet persists because Constantine was exceptionally consequential,
not because historians unanimously judged him morally good.

His reign includes:
- civil-war victories;
- sole rule;
- foundation of Constantinople;
- institutional Christianization;
- executions of close family members including Crispus and Fausta in 326 under
  obscure circumstances;
- coercion against religious rivals.

Therefore:
`GREAT = SCALE/LEGACY/HONORIFIC`,
not:
`GREAT = ETHICALLY EXEMPLARY`.

## Challenge 12 — Could the entire pattern arise without imperial effects on memory?

No.

Direct primary evidence establishes imperial:
- money;
- laws;
- buildings;
- councils;
- scripture commissions;
- confiscations;
- exiles.

The remaining uncertainty concerns magnitude and causal downstream effects, not
whether an imperial selection environment existed.
