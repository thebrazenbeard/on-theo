## Review scope

The exact reviewed branch contains two overlapping artifact families:

- dedicated Judas lane under `research/yeshua/judas/`;
- Judas controls under `research/yeshua/people/`.

Both are in scope. The review therefore tests:
1. historical semantics;
2. source dependence;
3. project bias;
4. repository collision/drift risk.

## PROPOSAL

The source-bound branch proposes that:

- a historical Judas associated with the disciple group is probable;
- some insider handover role is plausible-to-probable;
- timing/location/access is a plausible economical function;
- money is already part of Mark's extant narrative but greed is not established;
- exact thirty-silver, demonic causation, death mechanism, and later moral profile are not secure historical facts;
- later extant portrayals increasingly diversify/intensify Judas;
- total invention, secret authorization, and collective-agent hypotheses are unestablished.

> **HOSTILE REVIEWER**
>
> The dossier is substantially better than either the harmonized church Judas or a rescue-Judas theory, but it still leans too hard on a "small historical core" because that model feels economical.
>
> First, **Mark is doing almost all the historical work**. Paul does not name Judas. Matthew and Luke are literarily dependent on Mark for much passion material. John is later and its relation to Synoptic tradition is debated. Therefore "Judas existed as disciple = PROBABLE" may be a reasonable historical judgment, but the packet must not pretend it derives from multiple independent early witnesses.
>
> Second, the dossier risks using **later variation as evidence for an earlier smaller core**. Matthew, Luke-Acts, John, Papias, and the Gospel of Judas undeniably vary. That proves reception and literary development. It does not itself prove Mark's Judas was historically simpler than Mark portrays him.
>
> Third, the proposed practical function—location/timing/access—is attractive because it solves the familiar puzzle "why did anyone need Judas?" But this is still a modern operational reconstruction. Mark supplies stealth/opportunity and a kiss; John supplies knowledge of the meeting place but also has Jesus self-identify. Combining them can manufacture a historically efficient Judas neither author actually narrates in that exact form.
>
> Fourth, the `paradidomi` control is necessary but especially vulnerable to motivated reasoning. The verb does not intrinsically mean "treacherously betray," but Mark's context plainly casts Judas negatively. Lexical breadth can prevent overtranslation; it cannot neutralize the narrative.
>
> Fifth, **money is not a late invention**. Mark already says the chief priests promise Judas money. The project cannot tell a story in which a neutral logistical handover only later becomes financial unless it first explains Mark. Matthew intensifies the transactional framing with Judas asking a price and the thirty-silver amount, but a money component is already early at the extant narrative level.
>
> Sixth, the Judas-first bias guard needs to cut both directions. Because the project wants a possible exoneration, it rightly attacks pro-Judas claims. But it must also resist the opposite overcorrection: "Mark says Judas did it, therefore probable historical event." Mark is a theological passion narrative composed decades later. A named villain can be narratively old and still not be event-level recoverable.
>
> Seventh, the "Twelve" tension in 1 Corinthians 15 is useful but weak. Corporate labels persist. It should remain a discriminator, not a major argument either for or against Judas.
>
> Eighth, the Matthew/Acts death divergence is strong evidence that **death memory was unstable**. It is not strong evidence that **handover memory was unstable**. Do not let contradiction in one component contaminate another.
>
> Ninth, the Gospel of Judas is being handled correctly historically, but the dossier should say more sharply: later rehabilitation/reconfiguration can arise precisely because a negative Judas tradition was already culturally powerful. Reversal is compatible with prior notoriety and does not imply access to an innocent original Judas.
>
> Tenth, the full invention/scapegoat hypothesis currently lacks the evidence it would need. If broader intentional betrayal was concentrated into Judas, where are the early rival accusations against Peter, James, Thomas, or the Twelve? Mark itself preserves non-Judas failure—flight and Peter's denial—so Mark had no need to make Judas absorb **all** disciple failure.
>
> Eleventh, "historical Judas" and "historical handover" should not share one confidence label. A remembered disciple named Judas could be historical even if the arrest role was literary; conversely, an anonymous insider-handover tradition could theoretically be historicized under a name.
>
> Finally, the repository itself now violates the anti-drift principle. There are two overlapping Judas source-development dossiers and matrices in two directories. If both remain promotion-capable, they will diverge. One lane must be authoritative for the Judas dossier and the other explicitly supplemental.

## RESEARCH RESPONSE

The objections materially narrow the proposal.

Revisions required:

1. Keep **historical Judas existence** at `PLAUSIBLE_TO_PROBABLE`, with an explicit single-early-explicit-source ceiling rather than "probable" without qualification.
2. Keep **some Judas handover core** at `PLAUSIBLE` pending arrest/source-tradition testing; do not inherit the existence confidence.
3. Treat later variation as evidence for later extant portrayals only, not proof that Mark's core was historically smaller.
4. Reclassify timing/location/access as `ECONOMICAL_HYPOTHESIS / UNRESOLVED_TO_PLAUSIBLE`, not the current preferred historical function.
5. Preserve the lexical `paradidomi` guard while stating explicitly that Mark's narrative context is hostile and permits "betray" as contextual English interpretation.
6. Preserve money as an **early Markan narrative component**; only greed motive, exact amount, and later theft/demonic psychology remain unestablished.
7. Keep 1 Corinthians 15 "the Twelve" as a weak discriminator only.
8. Confine Matthew/Acts contradiction to death-memory instability.
9. State that Gospel of Judas reversal is compatible with already entrenched negative Judas memory and is not evidence of original innocence.
10. Keep full invention and broader intentional-collaboration concentration unpromoted until positive rival evidence appears.
11. Separate confidence for Judas existence from confidence for Judas's arrest role.
12. Designate `research/yeshua/judas/` as the authoritative dedicated Judas dossier lane. Files under `research/yeshua/people/` remain supporting source-clock/philological/diachrony controls and may not independently promote Judas conclusions.

> **HOSTILE REJOINDER**
>
> That is better, but one issue remains: even `PLAUSIBLE_TO_PROBABLE` for Judas's historical existence is a synthetic judgment from Mark plus later retention. The project should say why it is not merely "possible."
>
> The strongest argument is not raw source count. It is that Mark embeds Judas specifically within a Twelve roster and passion sequence, while later diverse traditions inherit a named Judas rather than offering competing named handover agents. A literary invention remains possible, but it requires an additional explanation for why this particular named insider became so early and stable.
>
> That still does not make the arrest role equally secure.
>
> Therefore the cleanest split is:
>
> - historical disciple/figure Judas: `PLAUSIBLE_TO_PROBABLE`;
> - historical insider handover by Judas: `PLAUSIBLE`;
> - payment as part of Markan memory: `TEXTUALLY_SECURE`, historical event `UNRESOLVED`;
> - historical motive: `UNKNOWN`;
> - location/timing/access function: `PLAUSIBLE_MODEL_NOT_ESTABLISHED`;
> - exact kiss/identification need: `UNKNOWN`;
> - later demonization/death/theft/fulfillment: source-specific reception/theology unless independently established.
>
> The project should resist both "Judas was obviously the traitor we know" and "Judas was probably framed." Neither survives the current evidence at full strength.
