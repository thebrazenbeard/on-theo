## PROPOSAL

Use a shared source clock for John the Baptist, James, Cephas/Peter, Mary Magdalene, Judas Iscariot, Thomas, and the Twelve before allowing dedicated reconstructions to compare their authority, failure, betrayal, or understanding.

> **HOSTILE REVIEWER**
>
> The source-clock idea is necessary, but several formulations are still too historical for the evidence actually cited.
>
> First, "later development" is sometimes being inferred merely from later surviving texts. A later source portraying Judas more demonically or Mary more authoritatively is evidence for a later **extant portrayal**. It is not automatically evidence that the tradition historically developed in a smooth chronological line. Lost earlier sources could complicate the sequence.
>
> Second, Peter/Cephas is being treated as one identity without stating the control. That identification is overwhelmingly conventional and probably correct, but a source-critical registry should say that it is an identification rather than silently making the Greek/Aramaic naming problem disappear.
>
> Third, James is exceptionally early, but "brother" cannot simultaneously be used as a strong historicity anchor and then treated as almost semantically empty when kinship becomes inconvenient. The disciplined position is asymmetric but explicit: ordinary kinship is the strongest reading, while exact biological configuration remains open.
>
> Fourth, Mary is being called "important" from Mark. Mark strongly remembers her at the passion, burial and tomb and says she was among women who followed/served in Galilee. That supports significance in the surviving Markan memory. It does not tell us her rank inside Yeshua's movement before Jerusalem.
>
> Fifth, Judas requires an even harder guard. Mark is indeed the earliest extant explicit named betrayal source in the current clock. But "Paul does not name Judas" is not evidence against a pre-Markan Judas tradition. Paul's silence is weak because his letters are occasional. Likewise, the Twelve formula is a real tension/discriminator, but it cannot carry an innocence theory.
>
> Sixth, Thomas is especially vulnerable to retrospective personality construction. Mark's roster establishes a named disciple in the narrative tradition; John gives a literary character; Thomasine texts give revelatory prestige. Those three layers do not establish one continuous biographical personality.
>
> Seventh, the source clock cannot answer "who understood Yeshua best" because the evidence is structurally incommensurable. Paul gives social-network information, Mark gives narrative memory, John gives literary characterization, and later dialogues give authority claims. The correct state is not merely "unsupported"; it is **not currently rankable from the available evidence**.
>
> Eighth, the proposed Judas-first work order is defensible only if it is admitted as a bias-control choice, not because Judas is historically the most important follower. The project has unusually strong prior interest in exonerating or reframing Judas, so Judas should go first precisely because it is dangerous.
>
> Finally, do not let "source clock" become "truth clock." Earlier is an important variable, not a truth score.

## RESEARCH RESPONSE

The objections are accepted.

The source clock will be revised to:

1. replace unsupported "development" language with "later extant portrayal" unless a transmission/development argument exists;
2. add an explicit Cephas/Peter identity control;
3. state that ordinary kinship is the strongest current reading of James while exact biological configuration remains unresolved;
4. narrow Mary from movement rank to strong Markan passion/tomb prominence plus remembered Galilean following/service;
5. state explicitly that Pauline silence does not disprove a pre-Markan Judas tradition and that the Twelve formula is a discriminator, not exoneration;
6. separate Thomas's roster identity, Johannine literary characterization, and Thomasine authority trajectory;
7. change superior-understanding claims from a simple rejection to NOT_CURRENTLY_RANKABLE / NOT_SUPPORTED;
8. explain Judas-first priority as asymmetric skepticism against project bias;
9. add the guard EARLIER_SOURCE_NE_TRUTH_SCORE.

> **HOSTILE REJOINDER**
>
> Those revisions are sufficient if they are actually written into the durable artifacts.
>
> One additional condition: later authority texts may be historically useful even when they are not biographical transcripts. Do not overcorrect by treating second-century Mary/Thomas/Judas material as "mere fiction." Their strongest immediate evidence class is later Christian authority/reception discourse; their possible preservation of older tradition remains a separate claim.
>
> With that distinction, the source clock can serve as the common control for the person-by-person dossiers.
