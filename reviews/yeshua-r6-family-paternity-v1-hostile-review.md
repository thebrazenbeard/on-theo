## Review question

Does R6 distinguish:
- early kinship memory;
- Joseph as later social-father tradition;
- biological paternity;
- extraordinary-conception tradition;
- Mark's "son of Mary";
- Panthera countertradition;
without promoting theological or polemical narratives into biography?

## 1. Early kin network

PASS STRONGLY.

Mother, brothers and sisters belong to early family memory, and James is
especially well anchored through Paul plus later external linkage controls.

This does not solve paternity.

## 2. Mark 6:3 — "son of Mary"

PASS WITH NARROWING.

The textual datum is real and unusual.

The R6 packet correctly preserves multiple live interpretations:
- absent/deceased father;
- illegitimacy/stigma;
- informal/local matronymic;
- literary/gendered Markan matronymic;
- later theological interpretation.

Recent scholarship gives a serious literary matronymic model, strengthening the
case against treating illegitimacy as the default inference.

Required registry change:

`HISTORICAL_ILLEGITIMACY`
should be:

`UNRESOLVED_ONE_LIVE_INTERPRETATION`

rather than wording that may sound like the project assigns the stigma reading
positive probability beyond its rivals.

## 3. Joseph as late-first-century tradition

PASS STRONGLY AS TRADITION HISTORY.

Matthew and Luke both narrate Joseph.
John preserves "son of Joseph" social/public identification.

The project can securely say:
JOSEPH_NAME_AND_SOCIAL_FATHER_IDENTITY_EXIST_IN_LATE_FIRST_CENTURY_CHRISTIAN_MEMORY.

## 4. Historical Joseph

PASS NARROWED.

The revision to:

`PLAUSIBLE_NOT_SECURE`

is appropriate.

The phrase "social/legal father" should be narrowed to:

`SOCIAL/RECOGNIZED_FATHER`

unless a specific legal institution is being argued.

Reasons confidence cannot rise:
- Paul and Mark do not name Joseph;
- Mark foregrounds Mary;
- Matthew/Luke use Joseph in heavily theological/genealogical infancy narratives;
- genealogies disagree;
- John is later literary evidence.

R6 may use historical Joseph as a plausible reconstruction, not a secure datum.
