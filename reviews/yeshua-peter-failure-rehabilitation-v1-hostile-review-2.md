## Review question

After revision, does the dossier avoid converting Peter's early authority and
later failure/restoration narratives into either institutional apologetic or a
hidden-betrayal theory?

## Findings

### 1. Historical Cephas / early authority

PASS.

Paul's personal-contact evidence securely places Cephas among major
first-generation authorities before the canonical Gospels.

This is a strong chronological constraint on theories that Peter's prominence
was invented only by later Gospel/church writers.

### 2. Cephas/Peter identity

PASS WITH GUARD.

The one-person identification remains the strongest current reconstruction.

The file correctly leaves it explicit and revisable.

### 3. Antioch conflict

PASS.

Paul's participant report strongly establishes a real dispute.

Paul's characterization of motives and blame remains partisan evidence rather
than an independently neutral transcript.

### 4. Markan Peter

PASS.

Mark's literary Peter combines:
- prominence;
- insight;
- misunderstanding;
- failure;
- denial;
- reinclusion pressure.

This is incompatible with describing Mark as simple Petrine whitewashing.

### 5. Historical denial

PASS.

The revised ceiling is appropriate:

PLAUSIBLE_WITH_SINGLE_EARLY_EXPLICIT_NARRATIVE_SOURCE_GUARD.

Later canonical preservation is not counted as automatic independent multiple
attestation.

The exact threefold scene remains unestablished.

### 6. Denial models

PASS.

P0 no longer receives an unjustified "low-assumption" preference.

P1 is correctly strong as literary function while the historical core remains
open.

P2/P3/P4 remain appropriately unpromoted.

### 7. Rehabilitation decomposition

PASS.

The dossier now separates:
- Markan narrative reinclusion;
- later source-specific literary restoration;
- first-generation/institutional Petrine authority;
- conspiratorial rehabilitation.

This is the decisive methodological correction.

### 8. Chronological implication

PASS.

Because Cephas has authority in Paul before the Gospel narratives, later
restoration scenes cannot be the origin of his historical movement prominence.

They may:
- narrate;
- theologize;
- legitimate;
- reinterpret
an authority structure that already existed.

### 9. Hidden greater betrayal / Judas frame-up

PASS.

No admitted evidence currently supports:
- Peter as actual handover agent;
- Peter framing Judas;
- Roman collaboration;
- denial as a sanitized memory of treachery.

Those remain speculative.

### 10. Peter/Mary authority conflict

PASS.

Later conflict texts remain reception evidence unless an older transmission
chain is demonstrated.

### 11. Peter's understanding of Yeshua

PASS.

Historical understanding cannot be ranked from:
- office;
- prominence;
- failure;
- later literary favor.

The dossier correctly leaves the question open.

## Residual vulnerabilities

1. The historical denial remains dependent on an early Gospel tradition rather
   than first-generation Pauline attestation.
2. The relation of Markan denial material to pre-Markan passion tradition
   remains debated.
3. Mark 16:7 is a secure literary reinclusion signal but its historical
   implications remain limited.
4. Later Petrine sources are highly diverse and require independent
   composition/authorship controls.
5. A historical failure can coexist with later literary rehabilitation without
   yielding a conspiracy theory; the project must preserve that middle model.
