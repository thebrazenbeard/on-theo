# Jain Ascetic / Lay Vow Packet V1

Status: LIVED ETHICS / INSTITUTIONAL DIFFERENTIATION CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Bind Jain vow practice without collapsing:
- mendicant mahāvratas;
- lay aṇuvratas;
- ascetic ideals;
- household religious life;
- alms/support relations;
- fasting and embodied discipline.

Core rule:

MAHĀVRATA != AṆUVRATA_IN_PRACTICAL_SCOPE.

A second rule:

ASCETIC_IDEAL != DESCRIPTION_OF_EVERY_LAY_JAIN_LIFE.

## 1. Five-vow family

Jain ethical traditions organize major conduct around:
- ahiṃsā — non-harm/nonviolence;
- satya — truthfulness;
- asteya/adattādāna — not taking what is not given;
- brahmacarya — sexual restraint/celibacy;
- aparigraha — non-possession/non-attachment.

Current result:

FIVE_VOW_STRUCTURE =
BOUNDED_CROSS_SECT_ETHICAL_FAMILY.

But vow interpretation and application vary by:
- renouncer/lay status;
- sect;
- gender;
- historical period;
- local practice.

## 2. Mendicant mahāvratas

Renouncers undertake the vows in radicalized/complete form as part of
houseless ascetic life.

Current scholarly descriptions emphasize:
- rigorous avoidance of harm;
- celibacy;
- radical limits on possession;
- speech/conduct restraints;
- bodily discipline;
- ritual obligations.

Current axis:

RITUAL_ORDER_MODEL =
BOUNDED_MENDICANT_DISCIPLINARY_ASCETIC.

TRANSFORMATION_MODEL =
BOUNDED_ASCETIC_KARMIC_PURIFICATION.

## 3. Lay aṇuvratas

Lay vows share names and ethical orientation with the great vows but are
adapted to household life.

They can include:
- limited nonviolence;
- truthfulness;
- non-stealing;
- sexual restraint appropriate to lay status;
- limitation of possessions/attachments.

Current axis:

RITUAL_ORDER_MODEL =
BOUNDED_LAY_RESTRAINT_DISCIPLINE.

The lay path is not merely:
FAILED_MONASTICISM.

It is an institutionally recognized religious mode.

## 4. Shared structure / different implementation

James Laidlaw's ethnographic analysis explicitly distinguishes:
- aṇuvratas as "lesser restraints";
- mahāvratas as "great restraints";
- formally homologous vow names;
- substantially different practical application.

Current result:

SHARED_VOW_LABEL
!=
SHARED_BEHAVIORAL_SCOPE.

This is a useful general comparison control.

## 5. Ahiṃsā

Ahiṃsā is central across mendicant and lay Jain ethical systems.

But implementation varies because:
- household life necessarily entails forms of activity renouncers seek to avoid;
- food production/preparation;
- commerce;
- movement;
- possessions;
- family life
create different practical constraints.

Current result:

AHIṂSĀ =
GRADED_DISCIPLINARY_PRACTICE,
not
ONE_SIMPLE_RULE.

## 6. Aparigraha / possession

For renouncers:
- non-possession is radicalized toward houselessness and minimal material
  attachment.

For lay practitioners:
- possession is limited rather than wholly eliminated;
- attachment and acquisition become objects of restraint.

Current result:

APARIGRAHA =
ROLE_DIFFERENTIATED_NONATTACHMENT/NONPOSSESSION.

This prevents the false inference:
WEALTHY_JAIN = NON-JAIN_BY_DEFINITION.

Ethnography instead documents a lived tension between:
- prosperity;
- gifting;
- household obligation;
- renunciatory ideals.

## 7. Lay–renouncer interdependence

Jain society is not simply:
ASCETICS versus WORLDLY PEOPLE.

Renouncers depend on lay communities for:
- alms;
- shelter;
- material support;
- institutional patronage.

Lay communities depend on renouncers for:
- teaching;
- exemplarity;
- ritual/religious authority;
- merit-bearing relations in many contexts.

Current axis:

RELATIONAL_ORDER_MODEL =
BOUNDED_LAY_RENOUNCER_INTERDEPENDENCE.

INSTITUTIONALIZATION_MODEL =
BOUNDED_DUAL_LAY_RENOUNCER_RELIGIOUS_ORDER.

## 8. Embodied ascetic practice

Jain religious discipline can include:
- fasting;
- dietary restriction;
- silence;
- repentance/confession;
- restraint of movement/activity;
- ritual observance.

Laidlaw and Cort both emphasize that ascetic practice can operate through the
body as a means of moral/karmic transformation.

Current axis:

TRANSFORMATION_MODEL =
BOUNDED_EMBODIED_ASCETIC_SELF_TRANSFORMATION.

This complements the earlier karmic-material liberation model.

## 9. Fasting

Fasting can function as:
- austerity;
- restraint;
- karmic purification;
- festival observance;
- disciplined religious practice.

Current guard:

FASTING != SELF-HARM_CATEGORY_BY_DEFAULT.

But:
specific extreme practices require separate historical/ethical/legal analysis.

V1 does not generalize one motive to all fasting.

## 10. Sallekhanā / santhārā

The Jain practice of sallekhanā is historically and ethically important but
requires its own packet because it involves:
- end-of-life discipline;
- fasting;
- renunciation;
- legal controversy;
- doctrinal distinctions from suicide.

Current status:

SALLEKHANĀ =
HOLD_FOR_DEDICATED_PACKET.

It is not used in this V1 to characterize ordinary Jain asceticism.

## 11. Ethics and karmic causation

Vows are not merely social rules in classical Jain thought.

They participate in:
- limiting harmful action;
- reducing passions/attachments;
- stopping karmic influx;
- supporting karmic shedding;
- progress toward liberation.

Current result:

ETHICAL_DISCIPLINE
and
KARMIC_CAUSAL_MODEL
are structurally linked.

But:
MORAL_RULE != MECHANICAL_ONE_TO_ONE_KARMA_EVENT.
