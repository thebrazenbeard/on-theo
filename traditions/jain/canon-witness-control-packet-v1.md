# Jain Canon / Witness Control Packet V1

Status: SECTARIAN DOCUMENTARY-MEMORY / WITNESS CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Separate:
- Mahāvīra's teaching horizon;
- remembered oral transmission;
- Śvetāmbara canon/recitation traditions;
- Digambara canon-loss and substitute-authority traditions;
- cross-sect systematic texts;
- surviving medieval manuscript witnesses.

Core rule:

CANON_MEMORY != MANUSCRIPT_WITNESS.

A second rule:

SECTARIAN_CANON_CLAIM != NEUTRAL_PROJECT_FACT.

## 1. Four clocks

Every early Jain text requires at least four distinct clocks.

### TEACHING_HORIZON

The historical period in which teaching is attributed to:
- Mahāvīra;
- earlier teachers;
- early Jain communities.

This is an event/source-claim horizon.

### ORAL_TRANSMISSION

The period in which material is remembered, recited, compiled, modified or
transmitted before extant redaction/witnesses.

### REDACTION / CODIFICATION

The period when a textual corpus is:
- compiled;
- standardized;
- recited/codified;
- reorganized;
- committed to stable written form.

### SURVIVING_MANUSCRIPT_WITNESS

The date of a physical manuscript now available for study.

Therefore:

TEACHING_DATE
!=
ORAL_TRANSMISSION_DATE
!=
REDACTION_DATE
!=
MANUSCRIPT_DATE.

## 2. Śvetāmbara canonical memory

Śvetāmbara traditions preserve an āgama corpus in Prakrit.

Current scholarship reconstructs:
- multiple historical strata;
- extensive oral transmission;
- redaction and standardization;
- council/recitation traditions;
- final major Vallabhī codification tradition in late antiquity.

Balcerowicz places the third Vallabhī recitation/codification in roughly
450–480 CE in his working historical reconstruction.

But historiography of the Jain councils cautions that:
- traditional council narratives are themselves sources requiring criticism;
- exact council dating and sequence are not simply direct archival fact;
- the received canon contains later redaction/standardization over earlier
  material.

Current project classification:

ŚVETĀMBARA_CANONICAL_CORPUS =
LAYERED_RECEIVED_TEXTUAL_TRADITION.

Not:
VERBATIM_SIXTH/FIFTH_CENTURY_BCE_TRANSCRIPT.

## 3. Early layers can survive late redaction

Balcerowicz argues some passages/terminology in received Śvetāmbara canonical
texts may preserve much earlier material, while also showing extensive later
standardization.

Therefore:

LATE_REDACTION
does NOT imply
ALL_CONTENT_LATE.

But:

POSSIBLE_EARLY_LAYER
requires
TEXTUAL_STRATIGRAPHY.

The project does not assign early date merely because tradition attributes a
passage to Mahāvīra.

## 4. Digambara textual memory

Current Digambara tradition maintains that:
- the original early canon was progressively lost;
- the extant Śvetāmbara āgamas are not accepted as an authentic preserved
  original canon;
- authoritative later/partial textual traditions preserve portions or
  doctrinal continuities.

Balcerowicz's historical reconstruction treats early Digambara siddhānta
literature such as:
- Ṣaṭkhaṇḍāgama;
- Kaṣāyapāhuda;
- Mahābandha

as compilatory substitute/authoritative corpora with their own dates and layers.

Current project classification:

ORIGINAL_CANON_LOST =
DIGAMBARA_SECTARIAN_MEMORY_CLAIM.

EARLY_DIGAMBARA_SIDDHĀNTA =
REAL_TEXTUAL_WITNESS_TO_DIGAMBARA_DOCTRINAL_HISTORY.

These propositions are distinct.

## 5. Digambara attitudes are historically nuanced

Fujinaga shows that "Digambaras reject the Śvetāmbara canon" is historically
too simple.

Digambara authors could:
- know Śvetāmbara āgama categories/titles;
- discuss scripture classifications;
- vary historically in how Śvetāmbara textual authority was evaluated.

Current result:

SECTARIAN_CANON_BOUNDARY =
DIACHRONIC_NOT_ONE_TIMELESS_REJECTION_FORMULA.

This prevents modern sectarian summaries from being projected backward.

## 6. Pūrvas / lost-text memory

Both major Jain traditions preserve memory of earlier Pūrva material as lost or
no longer fully extant, though they disagree about later preservation history.

Current documentary-memory result:

LOST_SCRIPTURE_CAN_REMAIN_AN_ACTIVE_AUTHORITY_MEMORY.

This gives Jainism a distinctive comparison subtype:

LOST_CANON_AS_AUTHORITY_REFERENCE.

A lost text can structure:
- claims of authenticity;
- succession;
- sectarian authority;
- later textual legitimacy

without a surviving physical witness.

## 7. Tattvārthasūtra cross-sect control

The Tattvārthasūtra, associated with Umāsvāmin/Umāsvāti and dated by current
scholarship broadly around 150–400 CE or c. 350–400 in some reconstructions:
- is the oldest extant systematic Jain treatise in Sanskrit;
- organizes canonical themes into a systematic philosophical structure;
- has major authority across both Śvetāmbara and Digambara traditions;
- survives in differing recensions/commentarial traditions.

Current result:

CROSS_SECT_SYSTEMATIC_AUTHORITY
CAN_EXIST_WITHOUT
ONE_SHARED_CANONICAL_TRANSMISSION.

This is important positive data.

Tattvārthasūtra does not erase:
- sectarian canon disagreement;
- recension differences;
- commentarial disagreement.
