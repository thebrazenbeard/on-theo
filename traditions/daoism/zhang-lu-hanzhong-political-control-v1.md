# Daoism — Zhang Lu / Hanzhong Political Control V1

Status: PRIMARY-HISTORIOGRAPHIC LOCATOR + SCHOLARLY CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

This packet tests whether the current Daoist lane can support
`POLITICAL_SACRALIZATION_MODEL` without inflating religious-political overlap
into a claim of divine kingship.

The bounded question is:

> In Zhang Lu's Hanzhong regime, did religious office merely coexist with
> political authority, or did religious offices themselves perform territorial
> governance?

## 1. Primary historiographic locus

The `Sanguozhi` biography of Zhang Lu (Wei shu 8) reports that after taking
Hanzhong Zhang Lu:

- taught the population through his religious system and styled himself
  `shijun` / "Master-Lord";
- organized adherents under `jijiu` / libationers;
- had libationers lead groups and administer hostels and disciplinary rules;
- did not appoint ordinary local officials, but instead used libationers to
  govern;
- held power in Ba and Hanzhong for nearly thirty years.

## 2. Han recognition layer

The same biography reports that when the late Han court could not effectively
subdue Zhang Lu, it granted him military/civil titles including administrator
of Hanning and accepted tributary relations.

This adds a second political layer:

`DE_FACTO_RELIGIOUS_TERRITORIAL_GOVERNANCE`
+
`PRAGMATIC_EXTERNAL_STATE_RECOGNITION`.

It does not establish that the Han court endorsed Celestial Masters theology or
regarded Zhang Lu's religious authority as divinely valid.

## 3. Jade-seal / kingship episode

The biography also reports that a jade seal was found in the ground and that
Zhang Lu's subordinates wished to elevate him as King of Hanning. Yan Pu advised
against taking the royal title, and Zhang Lu accepted that advice.

This is political-symbolic evidence that kingship was actively contemplated.

But the surviving wording does not itself state that:
- the seal was sent by Heaven;
- Zhang Lu claimed a divine mandate from it;
- kingship was ritually conferred.

Therefore the project records:

`ROYAL_ACCLAMATION_CANDIDATE` = ATTESTED

but:

`DIVINE_MANDATE_FROM_JADE_SEAL` = NOT_ADMITTED.

## 4. Scholarly control

A scholarly review of Terry Kleeman's
*Celestial Masters: History and Ritual in Early Daoist Communities* identifies
the main external narrative sources for early Celestial Masters history as the
`Sanguozhi`, `Dianlue`, `Huayang guo zhi`, and `Shenxian zhuan`.
It summarizes Zhang Lu as a local official/warlord who controlled Hanzhong for
nearly thirty years and describes the Hanzhong community as administered by
libationers.

## 5. Political-sacralization disposition

The evidence is sufficient to admit a bounded Daoist political cell, but the
mechanism must be stated narrowly.

Supported mechanism:

`RELIGIOUS_OFFICE_AS_TERRITORIAL_GOVERNANCE`.

Also supported:

`RELIGIOUS_POLITICAL_INSTITUTIONAL_ENTANGLEMENT`.

Current axis status:

`POLITICAL_SACRALIZATION_MODEL = PARTIAL_INSTITUTIONAL_GOVERNANCE`.

Not supported at this depth:

- one universal Daoist theory of rulership;
- divine grant of Zhang Lu's political authority;
- sacral monarchy;
- a proved Celestial Masters coronation theology;
- all later Daoist-state relations.

## 6. Why this belongs on the axis

The neutral axis asks not only whether rulers claim divine descent, but how
political authority is granted, inherited, ritually maintained, cosmically
ordered, divinely authorized, or otherwise structured through sacred systems.

Here the evidence shows that religious ranks and institutions are not merely
adjacent to government. They perform governance functions inside a territorial
polity.

That is a valid but partial political-sacralization result.

It is materially different from:
- Sumerian divine authorization of kingship;
- Vedic royal ritual;
- genealogical legitimation.
