## Purpose

The classical Daoist packet deliberately stopped before organized religious

This packet opens that later lane while preserving the historical discontinuity
and transformation.

## 1. Traditional founding claim versus direct evidence

Daoist tradition dates a revelation from Lord Lao to Zhang Daoling to 142 CE,
establishing the Covenant with the Powers of Orthodox Unity and the line of
Celestial Masters.

This is a traditional founding chronology.

The oldest presently controlled direct evidence in this packet is later.

A Zhang Pu stele dated 173 CE announces transmission of revealed scriptures to
libationers charged with spreading a Celestial Master ritual system.

Modern scholarship treats the stele as the earliest direct evidence currently
available in this packet for a text-based Celestial Masters liturgical
organization in the relevant region.

Therefore:

`TRADITIONAL_FOUNDING_DATE_142_CE`
!=
`EARLIEST_DIRECT_SURVIVING_EVIDENCE_DATE`.

Both belong in the history, but they are different evidence classes.

## 2. Zhang Lu and Hanzhong institutionalization

Later historical reconstruction associates Zhang Lu, grandson/third Celestial
Master in the traditional lineage, with a politically and economically
autonomous community in Hanzhong.

Sources describe organization through 24 `zhi`, commonly translated as
administrations and sometimes compared to parishes.

That comparison must remain an analogy.

`ZHI`
!=
`EARLY_CHRISTIAN_PARISH`
by definition.

### Registers

Celestial Masters communities used `lu`, registers.

The controlled scholarly description distinguishes:
- household registers recording births, marriages, deaths;
- individual registers conferring social/celestial rank and listing spirits
  associated with a person's religious status.

### Comparison effect

This strongly supports:
- INSTITUTIONALIZATION_MODEL;
- AGENCY_AND_AUTHORITY_MODEL;
- KNOWLEDGE_OR_DISCLOSURE_MODEL.

The evidence is no longer just philosophical prose.

It concerns:
- membership;
- office;
- hierarchy;
- textual authorization;
- ritual capability;
- communal administration.

## 3. Healing, confession, moral fault, petitions

Early Celestial Masters healing rites are described as linking illness with
moral fault.

The ritual system includes:
- confession;
- repentance;
- petitions;
- requests for aid addressed through divine/bureaucratic structures.

The Officers of:
- Heaven;
- Earth;
- Water

appear as important divine officials in this ritual model.

### Comparison consequence

This differs sharply from the earlier Laozi source architecture where the Dao
can be described as generating/nurturing without sovereign possession or
continuous command.

The religious Daoist lane introduces:
- moral accounting;
- divine offices;
- petitions;
- ritual mediation;
- authorized specialists.

Therefore:

`DAOIST_HISTORY`
contains a real diachronic change in how agency and mediation are represented.

It is methodologically invalid to use one layer to erase the other.

## 4. Xiang'er commentary

The Xiang'er commentary is known from Dunhuang manuscript S.6825.

The surviving manuscript copy is generally dated around 500 CE.

The underlying commentary is commonly dated much earlier, around 200 CE,
although authorship and precise dating remain disputed.

The work is closely linked in scholarship to the Way of the Celestial Masters
and has been attributed variously to figures including Zhang Daoling or Zhang
Lu.

The surviving manuscript is incomplete.

It corresponds roughly from the middle of received Laozi chapter 3 through
chapter 37.

### Historical significance

The Xiang'er is not merely another neutral textual witness to the Laozi.

It is interpretation.

It reads the Laozi through:
- moral discipline;
- precepts;
- self-cultivation;
- religious community;
- salvific or blessing-oriented consequences;
- divine Laozi/Lord Lao assumptions.

It therefore belongs in:

`LATER_RELIGIOUS_INTERPRETATION`

not silently in:

`EARLY_LAOZI_PRIMARY_MEANING`.

## 5. Laozi as Lord Lao

The early religious Daoist trajectory includes increasing divine status for
Laozi.

A late-second-century work commonly called the Laozi bianhua jing presents Lord
Lao as:
- personification/manifestation of Dao;
- descending into the human world;
- bestowing teachings;
- guiding rulers and humanity through successive manifestations.

This is a major shift in agency architecture.

Compare:

### Classical source architecture
`DAO -> GENERATION / ORDER / ZIRAN / NON-DOMINATION`.

### Later religious architecture
`LORD_LAO -> DESCENT / REVELATION / TEACHING / DIVINE_MEDIATION`.

This is not evidence that one layer is "true" and the other false.

It is evidence of historical reinterpretation and religious development.

## 6. Knowledge / disclosure transformation

The repo's `KNOWLEDGE_OR_DISCLOSURE_MODEL` can now express a diachronic
transition within one tradition family.

Early textual layer:
- aphorism;
- paradox;
- cultivation;
- difficult-to-name Dao;
- perspectival/epistemic limits.

Celestial Masters / early religious layer:
- revealed scripture;
- Lord Lao as revealer;
- authorized offices;
- registers;
- ritual specialists;
- petition systems.

This is exactly why "revelation" should not be treated as present or absent for
all Daoism in one row.

## 7. Moral causality

The Celestial Masters material also adds a bounded
`MORAL_CAUSALITY_MODEL`.

Illness can be interpreted as connected to moral fault, with confession,
repentance, and divine-administrative petition as ritual responses.

This is not the same structure as:
- Buddhist kamma;
- Upaniṣadic karma;
- Abrahamic final judgment;
- modern psychosomatic medicine.

Similarity words are not mechanism identity.

## 8. Institution and metaphysics

A tradition can develop institutional machinery that is not entailed by its
earliest philosophical texts.

The Celestial Masters case demonstrates:

`TEXTUAL_PHILOSOPHY`
->
`RELIGIOUS_INTERPRETATION`
->
`REVELATION_LINEAGE`
->
`REGISTERED_COMMUNITY`
->
`RITUAL_OFFICE`.

This sequence is a historical reconstruction framework, not a claim of simple
linear evolution.

Multiple antecedent religious and political practices remain relevant.
