# Norse Religion — Material and Ritual Evidence Packet V1

Status: ARCHAEOLOGICAL / MATERIAL CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## 1. Why material evidence must stay independent

Medieval literary narratives do not by themselves establish Viking-Age ritual
practice.

Conversely, archaeological finds do not automatically identify one literary
mythic scene.

Core rule:

`LITERARY_MYTH`
!=
`MATERIAL_RITUAL_EVIDENCE`.

## 2. Tissø ritual landscape

The National Museum of Denmark describes Tissø in western Zealand as a major
ritual landscape associated with a magnate's residence.

Evidence includes:
- weapons and jewelry deposited in the lake;
- ritual activity around the magnate's hall;
- fenced structures interpreted cautiously as possible cult buildings;
- Thor-hammer and valkyrie-form amulets;
- an open hilltop offering area;
- animal bones;
- silver objects;
- coins;
- tools and jewelry.

The museum describes several ritual loci operating in the same landscape.

### Comparison consequence

This supports:
- RITUAL_ORDER_MODEL;
- SACRAL_PRESENCE_MODEL;
- INSTITUTIONALIZATION_MODEL;
- OFFERING_EXCHANGE_MODEL as a candidate axis.

### Critical guard

`TISSO_OBJECT_DEPOSIT`
does not automatically prove:
- the exact recipient deity;
- the exact prayer;
- the exact myth invoked;
- the exact theology of participants.

Even the interpretation of some buildings and offerings remains probabilistic.

## 3. Blót

National Museum synthesis uses `blót` for sacrificial/ritual offerings and
describes ritual feasts and offerings at elite and landscape sites.

The current packet admits a bounded source proposition:

`OFFERING_AND_FEASTING_CAN_FORM_PART_OF_RITUAL_RELATIONSHIP`.

This is not yet one universal Norse blót liturgy.

### Candidate neutral axis

`OFFERING_EXCHANGE_MODEL`.

Question:

> What is offered, to whom or what, through what mediator, for what requested or
> commemorative outcome, and how reciprocal is the relationship represented?

This can compare sacrifice without assuming:
- payment;
- appeasement;
- Eucharist;
- karma;
- magical mechanics.

## 4. Thor's-hammer amulets

Thor-hammer amulets are material evidence of symbolic use associated with Thor.

They become especially visible in late Viking-Age contexts in which Christian
symbols are also present.

Their existence supports:
- devotional/protective identity signaling;
- divine-symbol material culture.

It does not establish every literary Thor narrative.

## 5. Conversion-period mixed symbols

The National Museum highlights a soapstone mould from Trend capable of producing:
- two crosses;
- one Thor's hammer.

Other material includes Thor-hammer objects with cross ornamentation.

This is powerful evidence that Christianization was not always an instantaneous
binary replacement.

### Comparison consequence

Strengthens:
- CONTACT_AND_REINTERPRETATION_MODEL;
- SACRAL_PRESENCE_MODEL;
- INSTITUTIONALIZATION_MODEL.

### Guard

`CROSS_PLUS_HAMMER`
does not by itself tell us whether an individual:
- believed both systems;
- sold to multiple clientele;
- used one symbol polemically;
- understood the symbols in exactly the same way.

Material coexistence is secure; inner belief requires separate evidence.

## 6. Human-sacrifice evidence caution

Late Christian textual accounts of human sacrifice are not eyewitness records.

Archaeological finds have caused scholars and museums to take human-sacrifice
claims more seriously, but identifying a death as ritual killing requires
contextual argument.

Therefore:

`UNUSUAL_BURIAL_OR_BODY_DEPOSIT`
!=
`HUMAN_SACRIFICE`
without independent controls.

This packet does not use human sacrifice as a broad-comparison proposition yet.

## 7. Regionality

Tissø is Danish/Zealand evidence.

It cannot be silently universalized to:
- Iceland;
- Norway;
- Sweden;
- the North Atlantic;
- every Viking-Age community.

Regional evidence remains regional until comparison establishes broader
distribution.
