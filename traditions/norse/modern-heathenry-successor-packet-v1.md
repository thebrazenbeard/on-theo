## 1. Scholarly historical frame

Current scholarship treats modern Paganisms, including Ásatrú/Heathenry, as
modern revival/reconstruction movements drawing on extinct or interrupted
pre-Christian religious traditions.

Research on Norwegian Ásatrú emphasizes:
- sparse historical evidence;
- no recognizable uninterrupted continuity of practice;
- active use of history, text, symbol and ritual in modern reconstruction.

Current classification:

REVIVAL_RECONSTRUCTION_MODEL =
BOUNDED_MODERN.

## 2. Ásatrúarfélagið

Current official evidence:
- founded in Iceland in 1972;
- legally recognized in 1973;
- current public values include tolerance, honesty, integrity, respect for
  cultural heritage and nature, and individual responsibility;
- goðar serve as current religious officials;
- four principal annual blóts are institutionally specified;
- current law/rules permit individual practice within legal and organizational
  boundaries.

Evidence class:
CURRENT ORGANIZATIONAL SELF-DESCRIPTION /
DOCUMENTARY PRACTICE.

### Siðfesta as explicit innovation

Ásatrúarfélagið explicitly states:
- this confirmation-like rite is not known to have existed in this form among
  ancient Nordic pagans;
- the first such ceremony was held in 1973;
- it developed as a modern alternative to Christian confirmation;
- current ceremonies vary and do not require a creed.

Current result:

ACKNOWLEDGED_RITUAL_INNOVATION =
DIRECT_PRIMARY_CONTROL.

This is unusually strong evidence for:

REVIVAL_WITH_INNOVATION.

## 5. The Troth

Current official materials define Heathenry as a family of modern religions and
spiritual traditions restoring/reconstructing Germanic religious relationships
among:
- gods;
- ancestors;
- spirits;
- human communities.

The Troth explicitly states:
- modern communities reconstruct, revive and renew ancient traditions;
- no one strict theology or practice is required for membership;
- multiple modern expressions coexist;
- current organization includes board, officers, clergy/education and service
  functions.

Current result:

REVIVAL_RECONSTRUCTION_MODEL =
BOUNDED_PLURAL_MODERN_RESTORATION.

INSTITUTIONALIZATION_MODEL =
BOUNDED_DIVERSE_SERVICE_EDUCATION_ORGANIZATION.

AGENCY_AND_AUTHORITY_MODEL =
BOUNDED_ORGANIZATION_PLUS_PERSONAL_CONSCIENCE.

## 6. Polytheism and other-than-human relations

The Troth currently self-describes as polytheistic and names:
- gods;
- ancestors;
- land/water/home spirits;
- other beings.

Ásatrúarfélagið current rules permit honoring:
- Æsir;
- other gods;
- landvættir;
- other powerful beings.

Forn Siðr centers divine powers and old Nordic religion.

Current axis:

SACRAL_PRESENCE_MODEL =
BOUNDED_CURRENT_MULTI_BEING_POLYTHEISTIC_LAYER.

This is evidence for modern organizations.

It is not retroactive proof of one ancient ontology.

## 8. Reconstruction and selection

Modern Heathen communities:
- choose which sources to prioritize;
- interpret gaps;
- reconstruct ritual;
- innovate rites;
- adapt institutional roles;
- respond to modern legal/social settings.

Therefore:

RECONSTRUCTION_IS_AN_ACTIVE_RELIGIOUS_PROCESS.

It is not merely an academic recovery exercise.

## 9. Cross-organizational variation

Important differences already visible:
- Ásatrúarfélagið: Icelandic legally recognized institutional form with goðar,
  annual principal blóts and explicit modern innovations;
- Forn Siðr: Danish recognized community, broad old/new ritual sources, multiple
  magical/ritual categories;
- The Troth: international/U.S.-based umbrella organization emphasizing
  polytheistic plurality, education, clergy/service and diverse modern
  traditions.

Therefore:

MODERN_HEATHENRY != ONE_ORGANIZATION_MODEL.

## 10. Ancient-modern comparison rule

Allowed:
- compare which ancient sources modern groups use;
- compare which rites are reconstructed versus acknowledged innovation;
- compare modern institutional forms;
- compare explicit continuity claims.

Not allowed:
- use modern rite to fill ancient evidence gap;
- infer Viking-age values from modern bylaws;
- treat modern office title as unchanged medieval office.
