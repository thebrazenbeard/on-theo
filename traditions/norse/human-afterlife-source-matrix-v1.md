# Norse Religion — Human Afterlife Source Matrix V1

Status: MEDIEVAL LITERARY SOURCE CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

The previous Norse packet was stronger on world-renewal than on ordinary human afterlife.

This packet adds a source-controlled human-afterlife lane without turning medieval Icelandic literature into a transparent Viking-Age creed.

Core rule:

`VALHOLL`
!=
`ALL_NORSE_AFTERLIFE`.

And:

`MEDIEVAL_TEXTUAL_DESTINATION`
!=
`UNIVERSAL_PRECHRISTIAN_BELIEF`.

## 1. Witness control

The Codex Regius of the Poetic Edda, GKS 2365 4to, is a late-thirteenth-century manuscript.

The 2024 electronic edition by the Árni Magnússon and Arnamagnæan institutes supplies:
- manuscript images;
- diplomatic transcription;
- normalized text;
- morphological analysis.

Source:
https://eae.ku.dk/q?p=eae%2Fvols%2Ftext%2F1

### Guard

`CODEX_REGIUS_DATE`
!=
`POEM_COMPOSITION_DATE`.

The manuscript is evidence for its medieval witness state.

Claims about older poetic strata require separate argument.

## 2. Grímnismál and the battle-slain

### Valhöll

Grímnismál stanza 8 represents Óðinn as selecting battle-slain men for Valhöll.

This supports a bounded proposition:

`SOME_BATTLE_SLAIN -> ODINIC_HALL_DESTINATION`.

### Fólkvangr

Grímnismál stanza 14 gives Freyja a separate destination:
- Fólkvangr;
- she chooses half of the slain;
- Óðinn receives the other half.

This immediately blocks the popular simplification:

`ALL_HONORED_BATTLE_DEAD -> VALHOLL`.

### Comparison consequence

The source itself preserves postmortem plurality among the battle-slain.

Therefore:

`POSTMORTEM_PATH_MODEL = PLURAL_AT_SELECTED_LOCUS`.

## 3. Helgakviða Hundingsbana II

Helgakviða Hundingsbana II is textually composite.

Its afterlife sequence is nevertheless important because it combines several motifs in one literary unit:
- Helgi is dead;
- he is represented in Valhöll;
- he rides with dead companions;
- he returns temporarily to his burial mound;
- Sigrún meets him there;
- the dead depart again.

Public-domain English translations differ in details, but the sequence is stable enough for a bounded literary proposition.

Sources:
- Codex Regius witness base via the Árni Magnússon electronic edition.
- Bellows translation:
  https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Poetic_Edda_%28tr._Bellows%29/Helgakvitha_Hundingsbana_II

### Comparison consequence

This is strong evidence against forcing the textual afterlife into one exclusive place model.

In the heroic poem:

`VALHOLL_ASSOCIATION`
and
`BURIAL_MOUND_RETURN`

can coexist.

### Guard

This does not prove:
- all dead inhabit their mounds;
- all Valhöll dead return;
- a universal doctrine of detachable souls;
- historical performance of the narrative.

## 4. Snorri's Hel systematization

Gylfaginning gives Hel authority over those assigned to her and specifically mentions people dying of sickness or old age.

Source:
https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Prose_Edda_%281916_translation_by_Arthur_Gilchrist_Brodeur%29/Gylfaginning

This is a later thirteenth-century learned prose systematization in Christian Iceland.

Therefore:

`SNORRI_HEL_RULE`
is evidence for
`MEDIEVAL_MYTHOGRAPHIC_SYSTEMATIZATION`.

It is not automatically:
`PAN_SCANDINAVIAN_VIKING_AGE_RULE`.

## 5. Literary plurality

The controlled literary record currently admits at least:
- Óðinn/Valhöll;
- Freyja/Fólkvangr;
- Hel in Snorri's systematization;
- mound-return/revenant-like continuity in heroic poetry.

These are not yet one coherent map.

The correct project model is:

`MULTIPLE_LITERARY_POSTMORTEM_LOCATIONS_AND_MODES`.

## 6. Personhood consequence

These texts imply that a dead person can retain:
- name;
- social identity;
- relationships;
- rank/enmity;
- agency in narrative;
- recognizable embodiment or presence.

But they do not yield one explicit philosophical definition of the self.

Therefore:

`PERSON_OR_SELF_MODEL = PARTIAL_STRENGTHENED`.

## 7. Continuity consequence

For selected human literary figures, death does not equal narrative nonexistence.

Helgi remains:
- socially identifiable;
- relationally connected;
- mobile in the narrative;
- associated with both Valhöll and the burial mound.

Thus:

`CONTINUITY_MODEL = HUMAN_LITERARY_CONTINUITY_BOUNDED`.

This must remain distinct from:
- biological continuity;
- resurrection in a Christian doctrinal sense;
- reincarnation;
- universal ghost doctrine.

## 8. Postmortem-path consequence

The strongest improvement from this packet is:

`POSTMORTEM_PATH_MODEL = BOUNDED_PLURALITY`.

The current evidence supports plurality more strongly than a single sorting rule.

## 9. Evidence ceiling

Not established:
- one universal Norse afterlife map;
- one rule based only on manner of death;
- Valhöll as destination of all worthy dead;
- Snorri's rules as unchanged pagan doctrine;
- mound-dwelling as universal;
- one stable soul concept across the corpus.
