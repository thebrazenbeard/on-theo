## 1. Exact evidence class

Modern Ásatrú is evidence for:
- contemporary religious practice;
- contemporary self-description;
- revival and reconstruction;
- institutional innovation.

It is not direct evidence for Viking-Age belief.

## 2. Ásatrúarfélagið

Ásatrúarfélagið's official site states:
- founded in 1972;
- legally recognized in Iceland in 1973.

Its current self-description emphasizes:
- tolerance;
- honesty;
- integrity;
- respect for ancient cultural heritage;
- nature;
- individual responsibility.

These are current institutional propositions.

They are not automatically ancient Norse propositions.

## 3. Siðfesta as documented innovation

Ásatrúarfélagið's official description of `siðfesta` is methodologically useful.

The organization states that rites of this form are not known to have existed
among ancient Nordic pagans.

Its first such ceremony was held in 1973.

The rite developed as a modern alternative to Christian confirmation.

## 4. Reconstruction model

The National Museum of Denmark explicitly describes modern Nordic paganism as a
revival/reinterpretation rather than a direct continuation of Viking-Age
religion.

Modern practitioners draw on:
- Eddic texts;
- later folklore;
- archaeological sites;
- cultural heritage;
- contemporary ethical/community needs.

This is a distinct historical layer.

## 5. Comparison consequence

The project needs to preserve:

## 6. Guard against reverse projection

Current Ásatrú values or rites must not be used to fill gaps in Viking-Age
religion unless supported by independent historical evidence.

Likewise, historical reconstruction must not be used to deny a modern
community's own current self-definition.

The two questions are different.
