# Norse Religion — Christianization and Source Boundary V1

Status: CONTACT / TRANSMISSION / SOURCE-CRITICISM CONTROL

## 1. Medieval textual problem

Much detailed Norse mythology survives in manuscripts written after the
Christianization of Scandinavia.

The National Museum of Denmark describes the source record as a patchwork:
- Eddic poetry;
- Snorri;
- skaldic poetry;
- runestones;
- foreign accounts;
- archaeology.

Cambridge scholarship similarly distinguishes:
- contemporary or near-contemporary evidence;
- later medieval evidence.

Neither evidence class is sufficient alone.

### Rule

`MEDIEVAL_CHRISTIAN_PRESERVATION`
!=
`FABRICATION`.

But:

`MEDIEVAL_CHRISTIAN_PRESERVATION`
!=
`TRANSPARENT_PRECHRISTIAN_REPORT`.

## 2. Gradual Christianization

Material evidence in Denmark indicates long overlap.

The Jelling monument records Harald Bluetooth's Christianization claim around
965, but archaeological and textual evidence shows Christianity present before
that and older practices continuing afterward.

The transition cannot be modeled as one switch event.

## 3. Trend mould

The Trend mould's capacity to cast Christian crosses and a Thor hammer is a
particularly clean material example of contact-period coexistence.

Project abstraction:

`RELIGIOUS_CONTACT_STATE`
can contain:
- competition;
- supplementation;
- commercial accommodation;
- personal mixture;
- political conversion;
- delayed ritual change.

No one interpretation is inferred from the mould alone.

## 4. Contact and reinterpretation

The National Museum notes mixed and reinterpreted symbols in the conversion
period.

Later Christian literary transmission also places older narratives inside new
intellectual frames.

Therefore the project's CONTACT_AND_REINTERPRETATION_MODEL must distinguish:

1. Viking-Age contact;
2. political conversion;
3. coexistence;
4. medieval preservation;
5. medieval reinterpretation/systematization;
6. modern revival.

These are not one event.

## 5. Snorri and euhemerization/systematization

Snorri's Edda was composed in Christian Iceland and serves poetic and learned
purposes.

Its organized mythic exposition is enormously valuable, but the organization
itself can be an authorial/editorial intellectual product.

### Guard

`SYSTEMATIC_SNORRI_COSMOLOGY`
!=
`PROVEN_SYSTEMATIC_VIKING_AGE_CREED`.

## 6. Comparison consequence

Norse evidence strengthens the existing:

`CONTACT_AND_REINTERPRETATION_MODEL`

and adds a project-wide source rule:

`PRESERVER_WORLDVIEW`
must be represented separately from
`PRESERVED_TRADITION_CLAIM`.

This rule can later be applied to:
- Arabic preservation of Greek astrology;
- Persian/Arabic Dorotheus;
- Christian preservation of Norse myth;
- Buddhist-Shinto combination;
- medieval transmission generally.
