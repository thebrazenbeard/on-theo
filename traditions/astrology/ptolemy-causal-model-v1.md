## Astronomy and prognostication are different epistemic classes

Ptolemy begins by distinguishing:
1. knowledge of celestial motions and configurations;
2. prognostication of terrestrial changes associated with those configurations.

He treats the first as more exact and the second as materially uncertain.

This is important because:

PTOLEMY_ASTROLOGY != CLAIM_OF_EQUAL_CERTAINTY_WITH_ASTRONOMY.

## Claimed physical pathway

Ptolemy argues that a power from the heavens affects the mutable sublunar world.

His examples include:
- solar seasonal heating;
- lunar moisture and tides;
- planetary and stellar relations associated with weather;
- combined celestial configurations affecting the ambient environment.

He assigns active qualities such as:
- heat;
- cold;
- dryness;
- moisture.

This is an ancient natural-philosophical causal model.

Guard:

PTOLEMAIC_ACTIVE_QUALITIES != NEWTONIAN_GRAVITATION.

## Birth and individual temperament

Ptolemy extends the ambient model to birth.

His reasoning is approximately:

celestial configuration
-> ambient physical quality
-> bodily/temperamental disposition
-> probabilistic life consequences.

He does not reduce the individual to celestial state alone.

He explicitly adds other causes such as:
- seed/heredity or generative type;
- country/place;
- rearing;
- customs;
- other terrestrial circumstances.

Project abstraction:

Outcome = F(celestial_ambient, inherited_structure, geography, upbringing, other_causes)

This is much closer to a multicausal susceptibility model than to a one-variable Sun-sign model.

## Error model

Ptolemy gives multiple reasons prognostication may fail:
- incompetent practitioners;
- fraudulent overclaiming;
- material complexity;
- non-identical recurrence of celestial configurations;
- failure to account for terrestrial co-causes;
- limited human knowledge.

This is historically important because the Tetrabiblos contains its own theory of model error.

## Fate is not uniformly irresistible

Book I.3 rejects the idea that every personal event is fixed by an irrevocable celestial decree.

Ptolemy distinguishes:
- stronger/general causes that can overwhelm local causes;
- weaker causes that can be resisted or modified;
- outcomes that follow only when no opposing factor intervenes.

He uses medicine as an analogy:
prediction can have value precisely because foreknowledge may allow mitigation.

Therefore:

PTOLEMY != SIMPLE_HARD_FATALISM.

## Medical astrology interface

Ptolemy explicitly connects astronomical prognostication with medicine.

The architecture is:

astronomy/prognostication -> expected bodily disposition or risk
medicine -> countermeasure or treatment.

Historical claim only.

This file does not endorse medical astrology as modern healthcare.

## Technical plurality inside Ptolemy

Tetrabiblos I.20-21 records competing term/bound systems:
- Egyptian;
- Chaldaean.

Ptolemy criticizes internal inconsistencies in received systems and attempts rational comparison.

This is direct evidence that ancient astrology was already internally plural.

Guard:

ANCIENT_TECHNICAL_TABLE != UNIVERSAL_ASTROLOGICAL_CONSENSUS.

## Relation to the on-theo celestial-conditioning hypothesis

Ptolemy's model has structural resemblance to runtime conditioning:

celestial_state
-> ambient_state
-> organism_disposition
-> probabilistic_behavior/outcome.

But the resemblance must not be back-projected.

Ptolemy is arguing from ancient natural qualities, not hidden computation.

Classification of the comparison:
PROJECT_ANALOGY.

## Falsifiable modern translation

If a modern researcher wanted to preserve the causal architecture but replace the ancient physics, separate these hypotheses:

P0:
No measurable celestial contribution after terrestrial/environmental controls.

P1:
Known physical variables such as illumination, seasonality or gravity mediate the effect.

P2:
An unknown physical variable mediates the effect.

P3:
Astrological symbolic variables predict beyond physical variables.

P4:
A hidden common cause conditions both celestial configuration and agent state.

The primary text supports none of P1-P4 as modern scientific conclusions.

It only supplies a historically interesting causal architecture.
