# Astrology Source Map V1

Status: FOUNDATION / SOURCE-CONTROLLED

This file is a starting bibliography and provenance map. It is not a claim that every source listed has been exhaustively ingested.

## Source-use rules

- Primary texts establish what a historical source says, not whether its cosmology is true.
- Scholarly histories reconstruct chronology, transmission, institutions, and technical development.
- Modern empirical studies test narrowly specified claims; a result about Sun signs is not automatically a result about every astrological technique.
- Practitioner material may document living practice but does not establish historical priority or empirical validity.
- Later translations and summaries must not be cited as though they were the ancient witness.

## Mesopotamia / Babylon

### Francesca Rochberg — The Heavenly Writing

Francesca Rochberg, The Heavenly Writing: Divination, Horoscopy, and Astronomy in Mesopotamian Culture, Cambridge University Press, 2004.

https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511617409

Use for:
- celestial divination in Mesopotamian scribal culture;
- relationship among omen texts, astronomy, and horoscopy;
- transition toward individual birth horoscopes;
- methodological caution against imposing modern “science versus superstition” categories.

Key bounded finding used in this branch:
- celestial divination is well attested long before personal horoscopy;
- by the late fifth century BCE, cuneiform horoscopes tied celestial configurations to individual births.

### British Museum tablet 77824

https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1885-0430-15

Late Babylonian tablet, about 500 BCE, listing months and associated zodiacal signs.

Use for:
- material witness to zodiacal organization;
- not by itself a complete history of the zodiac or horoscopy.

### Metropolitan Museum — The World between Empires

https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/The_World_between_Empires_Art_and_Identity_in_the_Ancient_Middle_East.pdf

Use for:
- institutional summary of Babylonian omen theory;
- zodiac and horoscope development;
- distinction between older state/royal omen traditions and later private horoscopy.

### Yavanajātaka — dating/transmission dispute

David Pingree’s 1978 critical edition remains foundational but later manuscript evidence challenges key elements of his dating reconstruction.

Bill M. Mak, “The Date and Nature of Sphujidhvaja’s Yavanajātaka Reconsidered in the Light of Some Newly Discovered Materials,” History of Science in South Asia 1 (2013).

https://doi.org/10.18732/H2RP4T
https://journals.library.ualberta.ca/hssa/index.php/hssa/article/view/7

Use for:
- direct example of why source dating cannot be copied uncritically from older secondary literature;
- reassessment of Pingree’s reconstructed dates and readings.

## Modern empirical evaluation

### Shawn Carlson — “A double-blind test of astrology”

Nature 318, 419–425 (1985).

https://doi.org/10.1038/318419a0
https://www.nature.com/articles/318419a0

Use for:
- a prominent double-blind natal-chart/personality matching test;
- historical role in modern empirical debate.

Guard:
One experiment does not establish every possible astrological proposition, and later methodological/statistical critiques should be tracked separately.

### Hartmann, Reuter, Nyborg — date of birth, personality, and intelligence

Personality and Individual Differences 40 (2006), 1349–1362.

https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2005.11.017

Use for:
- two large samples totaling more than 15,000 participants;
- no detected relationship between date of birth and tested personality/intelligence measures;
- no support for tested popular Sun-sign associations.

### Kelly et al. — twin replication/reanalysis

Personality and Individual Differences 21 (1996), 449–454.

https://doi.org/10.1016/0191-8869(96)00112-2

Use for:
- replication/reanalysis of a prior positive twin-astrology report;
- example of response-set, stereotype, and data-quality concerns.

### Dickson and Kelly — Barnum effect review

Psychological Reports 57 (1985), 367–382.

https://doi.org/10.2466/pr0.1985.57.2.367

Use for:
- psychological mechanisms by which vague/generic feedback can feel personally accurate;
- not as proof that every specific astrological judgment is Barnum feedback.
