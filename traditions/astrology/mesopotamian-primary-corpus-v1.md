# Mesopotamian Astral Divination and Horoscopy — Primary Corpus V1

Status: RESEARCHED FOUNDATION / PRIMARY-SOURCE MAP

## Scope

This file separates three related but non-identical Mesopotamian evidence streams:

1. celestial omen scholarship;
2. mathematical/observational astronomy used to predict celestial states;
3. personal horoscopy tied to an individual's birth.

The existence of one does not imply the full technical form of another.

## Enūma Anu Enlil

Enūma Anu Enlil is the major Mesopotamian series of celestial and atmospheric omens.

Reference orientation:
- Anne-Caroline Rendu Loisel, “Enuma Anu Enlil,” Encyclopedia of Ancient History:
  https://doi.org/10.1002/9781444338386.wbeah21126
- Cuneiform Commentaries Project, Yale:
  https://ccp.yale.edu/
- ORACC corpora:
  https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/

The series covers multiple categories of signs, including:
- lunar visibility;
- lunar eclipses;
- solar phenomena and eclipses;
- weather/Adad omens;
- stellar and planetary phenomena.

A surviving commentary on an eclipse section glosses an omen in which an early lunar eclipse is interpreted for the king of Akkad. This illustrates the older political/royal orientation of much omen scholarship rather than individual natal character analysis.

Primary/commentarial witness:
https://ccp.yale.edu/P393836

Control:

ECLIPSE_OMEN_FOR_KING_OR_STATE != BIRTH_CHART.

## Omen logic

The core omen form is conditional:

IF celestial/atmospheric phenomenon X
THEN terrestrial consequence Y.

The relationship may be read as signification rather than modern mechanical causation.

The branch must not rewrite an omen relation as:
- gravitational mechanism;
- electromagnetic mechanism;
- statistical correlation;
- simulation signal.

Those are later mechanism hypotheses.

## Babylonian astronomical prediction

Personal horoscopy required more than naked-eye observation at the moment of birth.

Francesca Rochberg notes that cuneiform horoscopes depend on recorded and computational planetary/lunar data because:
- a birth can occur during daylight;
- not all planets are above the horizon;
- celestial positions therefore may need to be derived from astronomical texts.

Source:
Francesca Rochberg, The Heavenly Writing, chapter 4:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/heavenly-writing/sources-for-horoscopes-in-astronomical-texts/22B1BE8899693B56239893CF3CBCB1FA

This is important to on-theo because astrology is already an information-processing system here: a horoscope may represent computed system state, not merely direct visual omen reading.

That is a historical observation about technique, not evidence for the truth of the interpretation.

## Zodiacal organization

British Museum tablet 77824, dated about 500 BCE, lists months and associated zodiacal signs.

Source:
https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1885-0430-15

Use:
- material evidence for late Babylonian zodiacal organization;
- chronology anchor.

Do not use:
- as proof that the twelve-sign zodiac originated on that exact date;
- as proof that full natal astrology existed in the same form;
- as proof of later Greek or Indian doctrine.

## Personal horoscopes

Rochberg's Babylonian Horoscopes (1998) is the core corpus publication.

Digital access:
https://sites.dlib.nyu.edu/viewer/books/isaw_aphs000011/3

Bibliographic:
Francesca Rochberg, Babylonian Horoscopes, Transactions of the American Philosophical Society 88.1 (1998).

The surviving horoscope corpus establishes that by the late fifth century BCE, celestial configurations were being attached to individual birth dates.

The corpus is technically significant because horoscopes can include combinations of:
- date/time information;
- planetary positions;
- lunar data;
- zodiacal positions;
- computed astronomical information;
- in some cases interpretive statements.

Guard:

EARLIEST_SURVIVING_HOROSCOPE != FIRST_HOROSCOPE_EVER_CREATED.

## Transition question

One of the branch's central historical problems is not “when did astrology begin?”

It is:

When and how did Mesopotamian astral knowledge change from predominantly state/royal omen interpretation into individualized birth-based prognostication?

That question requires tracing:
- scribal institutions;
- zodiac standardization;
- mathematical astronomy;
- horoscope tablet chronology;
- transmission into Hellenistic cultures.
