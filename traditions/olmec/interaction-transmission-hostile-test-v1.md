# Olmec Interaction / Transmission Hostile Test V1

Status: ADVERSARIAL CAUSAL-TRANSMISSION TEST / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Test the strongest version of the historical claim that Olmec religion/culture
was the "mother culture" from which later Mesoamerican religious systems
derived.

Core rule:

WIDESPREAD_OLMEC_STYLE != ONE_WAY_CULTURAL_TRANSMISSION.

A second rule:

CONTACT_AND_EXCHANGE != SEMANTIC_BORROWING_BY_DEFAULT.

## 1. Historical hypothesis under test

Strong mother-culture model:

OLMEC_HEARTLAND
->
DIRECT_ONE_WAY_TRANSMISSION
->
CORE_MESOAMERICAN_RELIGIOUS_AND_CIVILIZATIONAL_SYSTEMS.

This hypothesis predicts more than resemblance.

It requires:
- chronological priority;
- demonstrable contact;
- directional transfer;
- intermediary evidence;
- continuity of specific forms or meanings;
- exclusion of independent/local development where relevant.

## 2. What current scholarship securely supports

The 2025 Oxford Handbook confirms:
- Olmec-related materials and styles circulated widely;
- Gulf Coast centers participated in substantial exchange networks;
- Early Formative San Lorenzo exported significant pottery;
- obsidian and ceramic sourcing demonstrate long-distance interaction;
- Olmec-style visual/material forms occur outside the heartland.

Therefore:

OLMEC_INTERREGIONAL_INTERACTION =
SECURE.

This is not controversial at the project level.

## 3. Directionality can sometimes be measured

Archaeometric ceramic research summarized in the Oxford Handbook indicates:
- Early Formative San Lorenzo exported substantial pottery;
- some earlier debates about ceramic directionality have been clarified;
- later Middle Formative exchange became more regionalized.

This supports:

SOME_GULF_COAST_TO_OTHER_REGION_MATERIAL_TRANSFER =
DEMONSTRATED.

It does not establish:

EVERY_RELIGIOUS_MOTIF_MOVED_WITH_THE_POT.

Material direction and semantic direction are separate claims.

## 4. Central Mexico hostile control

Current Oxford synthesis for central Mexico reports:
- Early Formative populations adopted multiple foreign styles, including
  Olmec-style material and West Mexican styles;
- direct interaction/trade and possibly migration occurred;
- by the Middle Formative, some innovations originated in the Basin of Mexico
  and spread outward;
- regional populations later reduced foreign-style appropriation.

This directly defeats a simple permanent one-way model.

Result:

INTERACTION_NETWORK_CAN_CHANGE_DIRECTION.

Therefore:

OLMEC_PRIORITY_IN_ONE_PHASE != PERMANENT_CAUSAL_CENTER.

## 5. Morelos hostile control

Current scholarship on the "Olmec phenomenon" in Morelos emphasizes:
- multiethnic social settings;
- local chronological stages;
- heterogeneous settlement histories;
- locally produced representational codes and ideology.

Result:

OLMEC_STYLE_OUTSIDE_OLMAN_CAN_BE_LOCAL_SOCIAL_PRODUCTION.

Therefore:

OLMEC_STYLE != HEARTLAND_OLMEC_POPULATION_BY_DEFAULT.

## 6. Guerrero hostile control

Oxtotitlan and other Guerrero evidence shows early and Middle Formative
Olmec-style material outside the Gulf Coast.

Recent dating pushes some interaction earlier than previously assumed.

But:

EARLY_DATE + OLMEC_STYLE

still does not independently identify:
- migrants;
- missionaries;
- conquest;
- direct religious teaching;
- exact transmission route.

Regional interaction mechanism remains an empirical question.

## 7. Maya interaction control

Current scholarship supports substantial Early/Middle Preclassic interaction
between:
- Gulf Coast Olmec/Isthmian spheres;
- early Maya communities.

Evidence includes:
- ceramics;
- jade/greenstone;
- public architecture;
- ritual artifacts;
- behavioral patterns.

But current synthesis also notes:
- Maya ceremonial village life already existed before or alongside major
  La Venta development;
- the interaction sphere was not a blank-recipient model;
- early Maya and Olmec developments overlapped.

Therefore:

OLMEC_MAYA_INTERACTION =
SUPPORTED.

OLMEC_CREATED_MAYA_RELIGION =
NOT_SUPPORTED.

## 8. Shared religious themes

Olmec and later Mesoamerican traditions share or resemble themes involving:
- maize;
- rain;
- mountain/cave;
- sacred landscape;
- greenstone;
- rulers;
- calendar/time;
- composite supernatural beings.

Possible causal explanations include:
- Olmec-to-later transmission;
- earlier common regional inheritance;
- repeated local convergence;
- network interaction;
- selective borrowing;
- resemanticization;
- combinations of mechanisms.

Similarity does not select among them.

## 9. Direct historical approach remains allowed, with bridges

Current religion scholarship uses later Mesoamerican evidence to interpret
earlier Olmec imagery.

ON_THEO permits this as:

HISTORICAL_RECONSTRUCTION.

Required:
- temporal bridges;
- intermediate evidence;
- controlled motif/form comparison;
- explicit semantic uncertainty.

Not allowed:
- later name projected directly backward;
- later narrative assumed as lost Olmec narrative;
- continuity asserted merely because form resembles later form.

## 10. Legacy versus transmission

The project separates:

LEGACY:
later developments occur in a Mesoamerican world partly shaped by earlier
Formative interactions.

TRANSMISSION:
a specific form/meaning/practice moves through demonstrable contact.

BORROWING:
a receiving community adopts/reworks a specific source element.

GENEALOGICAL_CONTINUITY:
a practice/category persists through an identifiable historical chain.

These are not synonyms.

"Olmec legacy" may be defensible at a broad historical level while a specific
religious transmission claim remains unproven.

## 11. Mother versus sister culture test

The latest field synthesis explicitly treats the old mother/sister opposition as
insufficient.

Current research instead examines:
- regional settlement;
- interaction;
- exchange;
- political economies;
- local innovation;
- heterogeneous adoption;
- multiple directions of influence.

Hostile-test result:

ONE_WAY_MOTHER_CULTURE_MODEL =
NOT_SUPPORTED_AS_DEFAULT_EXPLANATION.

This does not mean Olmec influence was trivial.

It means influence must be demonstrated proposition by proposition.

## 12. Religious-transmission admission test

For a future specific Olmec -> later religion claim, require:

1. OLMEC_SOURCE_FORM
2. SECURE_DATE
3. LATER_SOURCE_FORM
4. INTERMEDIATE_BRIDGES
5. CONTACT_ROUTE
6. DIRECTIONAL_EVIDENCE
7. SEMANTIC_CONTINUITY_OR_RECODING_ARGUMENT
8. ALTERNATIVE_CAUSAL_MODELS_TESTED

Without these:

TRANSMISSION = HOLD.
