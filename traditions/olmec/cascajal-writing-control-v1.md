# Cascajal Writing Control V1

Status: POSSIBLE / LIKELY WRITING WITHOUT DECIPHERED SEMANTICS / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Control the Cascajal Block so that evidence for early Olmec writing does not
become evidence for readable Olmec theology.

Core rule:

WRITING_EXISTENCE != DECIPHERED_CONTENT.

A second rule:

AUTHENTIC_OBJECT != RECOVERED_SEMANTICS.

## 1. Object and discovery

The Cascajal Block is a polished/incised serpentinite object discovered near
Lomas de Tacamichapa in southeastern Veracruz, less than twenty kilometers from
San Lorenzo.

It was not recovered through a controlled archaeological excavation.

Published research places it broadly in the transition from Early to Middle
Formative Olmec contexts, around 1000-800 BCE, with some analyses using
approximately 1000-900 BCE.

Evidence class:
MATERIAL EVIDENCE + DISCOVERY-CONTEXT RECONSTRUCTION.

The uncontrolled discovery context remains a permanent source-critical
limitation.

## 2. Inscription

The block preserves:
- 62 incised signs;
- repeated sign forms;
- patterned distribution;
- an arrangement interpreted by multiple specialists as writing or text-like
  notation.

Current project classification:

CASCAJAL_SIGN_SYSTEM =
LIKELY_WRITING / SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION_STRENGTHENED.

It is not:

DECIPHERED_SCRIPT.

## 3. Authenticity and Olmec affiliation

A 2020 Ancient Mesoamerica archaeometric study applied:
- polynomial texture mapping;
- X-ray fluorescence;
- scanning electron microscopy;
- manufacturing-trace comparison.

Its results found that:
- the stone/material is consistent with serpentinite;
- patination is coherent;
- incising/manufacturing traces correspond to Formative-period Olmec lapidary
  technology;
- material/manufacture are consistent with securely provenienced Olmec objects.

Current status:

OBJECT_AUTHENTICITY_AND_OLMEC_CULTURAL_AFFILIATION =
STRONGLY_SUPPORTED_SCHOLARLY_RECONSTRUCTION.

This does not remove the original provenience problem.

## 4. Writing-system evidence

Mora-Marín's 2020 distributional analysis argues that:
- sign co-occurrence patterns are compatible with a linguistically motivated
  logosyllabic script;
- repeated pairings and distributional regularities can support writing-system
  analysis;
- reading order and formatting remain under investigation.

Stephen Houston's 2025 Oxford Handbook chapter argues affirmatively that
Cascajal is an Olmec text and likely evidence for true writing.

Current project ceiling:

WRITING_SYSTEM_EXISTENCE =
STRONG_SCHOLARLY_SUPPORT.

## 5. Decipherment status

Despite stronger support for authenticity and writing:

THE_SCRIPT_REMAINS_UNDECIPHERED.

No current consensus reading yields:
- sentences;
- deity names;
- ritual instructions;
- creation account;
- royal titulary;
- prayers;
- cosmological propositions;
- theological doctrine.

Therefore:

CASCAJAL_CONTENT =
UNKNOWN.

Not:
ABSENT.

UNKNOWN means the evidence does not currently permit semantic recovery.

## 6. Proposed semantic controls remain hypotheses

Scholarly work has proposed:
- sign inventories;
- possible sign pairings;
- possible diphrastic kennings;
- reading-direction models;
- analogies with later Mesoamerican scripts.

These are useful for decipherment research.

They remain:
SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION.

A proposed sign pairing cannot be promoted into a religious proposition unless
the decipherment becomes independently controlled.

## 7. Individual Olmec signs outside Cascajal

Houston notes that isolated graphs/signs occur more widely and some appear to
continue as part of later Mesoamerican writing traditions.

This can support:

OLMEC_SIGN_TRADITION_HAS_BROADER_WRITING_HISTORY_RELEVANCE.

It does not support:

LATER_SCRIPT_SEMANTICS = CASCAJAL_SEMANTICS.

Graph persistence does not guarantee:
- same sound;
- same word;
- same meaning;
- same theological use.

## 8. Knowledge/disclosure axis

Because the script cannot be read:

KNOWLEDGE_OR_DISCLOSURE_MODEL =
NOT_ADMITTED_FROM_CASCAJAL.

The existence of writing demonstrates:
- symbolic recording;
- formal sign use;
- probably linguistic representation at current scholarly ceiling.

It does not tell us what religious knowledge was recorded.

## 9. Institutionalization implication

If Cascajal is writing, it weakly-to-moderately supports a formalized symbolic
recording practice within Olmec society.

Current axis:

INSTITUTIONALIZATION_MODEL =
PARTIAL_WRITING_PRACTICE_CANDIDATE.

Limitation:
- one rare text;
- uncertain production context;
- no identified scribal institution;
- no archive;
- no readable administrative or ritual corpus.

## 10. Textual-theology ceiling

Current project result:

PRIMARY_TEXT_THEOLOGICAL_PROPOSITION =
NOT_AVAILABLE.

This is an evidence-class ceiling, not a claim that Olmec people had no theology
or no textual religious knowledge.

Required guard:

ABSENCE_OF_DECIPHERMENT != ABSENCE_OF_RELIGIOUS_CONTENT.
