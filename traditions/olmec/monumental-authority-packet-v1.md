# Olmec Monumental Authority Packet V1

Status: MATERIAL / ICONOGRAPHIC AUTHORITY CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Test how far Olmec monumental sculpture supports claims about political and
sacral authority without converting elite portraiture into divine kingship.

Core rule:

ELITE_MONUMENTAL_REPRESENTATION != DIVINE_KINGSHIP.

A second rule:

MONUMENT_TYPE != ONE_FIXED_FUNCTION_ACROSS_SITES_AND_TIME.

## 1. San Lorenzo as first major political center

Current INAH and Oxford Handbook synthesis identify San Lorenzo as:
- the earliest/first major Olmec regional center;
- extremely large for its period;
- deliberately reshaped at landscape scale;
- supplied with monumental basalt from the Tuxtla Mountains;
- home to ten colossal heads, four thrones, and many other stone monuments;
- center of a complex settlement hierarchy.

Evidence class:
MATERIAL EVIDENCE / SETTLEMENT ARCHAEOLOGY / SCHOLARLY SYNTHESIS.

Secure proposition:

SAN_LORENZO_SUPPORTS_HIGHLY_CONCENTRATED_ELITE_AUTHORITY_AND_LARGE_SCALE_LABOR_MOBILIZATION.

The religious character of that authority requires separate evidence.

## 2. Colossal heads

Colossal heads are:
- individualized human faces;
- helmet/headgear-bearing;
- extremely labor-intensive;
- carved from transported basalt;
- found at major centers including San Lorenzo and La Venta.

Current institutional/scholarly interpretation strongly favors:

COLOSSAL_HEAD =
ELITE_OR_RULER_PORTRAIT.

This is a SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION grounded in material/contextual evidence.

It is not a native caption.

Secure implication:

MONUMENTAL_HUMAN_PORTRAITURE_PUBLICLY_ENCODES_SOCIAL_DISTINCTION_AND_AUTHORITY.

Not established:
- deceased ruler;
- living ruler;
- ancestor;
- deity;
- priest;
- warrior office

for every head without object-specific evidence.

## 3. Thrones / altar-thrones

Large carved stone monuments historically called "altars" are often interpreted
in current scholarship as thrones or ruler-associated monuments.

At San Lorenzo and other sites they form part of an elite monumental repertoire.

Current project label:

THRONE_OR_ALTAR_THRONE =
ELITE_AUTHORITY_MONUMENT_WITH_FUNCTION_REQUIRING_OBJECT_SPECIFIC_CONTROL.

Guard:

ALTAR_OLD_LABEL != SACRIFICIAL_ALTAR_FUNCTION_PROVED.

A historical museum label is not functional evidence.

## 4. Monumental sculpture is heterogeneous

The 2025 Oxford Handbook chapter on monumental sculpture explicitly warns that
Olmec monument studies have often been narrowed to:
- colossal heads;
- altar-thrones;
- stelae.

Current research instead emphasizes varied:
- subjects;
- materials;
- audiences;
- functions;
- regions;
- recarving/mutilation histories.

Therefore:

OLMEC_MONUMENTAL_SCULPTURE != ONE_PROPAGANDA_SYSTEM.

Any authority model must remain:
- site-specific;
- chronological;
- monument-specific.

## 5. Recurrence and public communication

La Venta INAH interpretation characterizes monumental sculpture as an official
visual communication system and notes structured placement in relation to
architecture.

Combined with San Lorenzo's labor-intensive monumentality, this supports:

ELITE_AUTHORITY_WAS_PUBLICLY_MATERIALIZED_THROUGH_MONUMENTAL_VISUAL_FORMS.

This is stronger than:

ALL_MONUMENTS_PORTRAY_KINGS.

## 6. Political economy

The 2025 Oxford Handbook political-economy synthesis notes that San Lorenzo,
La Venta, and Tres Zapotes show extraordinary labor investment, public
architecture, special goods, specialized production, and imported materials.

Current axis:

INSTITUTIONALIZATION_MODEL =
BOUNDED_LARGE_SCALE_LABOR_AND_PRESTIGE_MATERIAL_MOBILIZATION.

POLITICAL_SACRALIZATION_MODEL =
PARTIAL_MONUMENTAL_AUTHORITY.

Why only PARTIAL:
political authority is secure; its precise sacral legitimation is not readable.

## 7. Religious/sacral authority cannot be assumed from monumentality

Olmec major centers also contain:
- ritual offerings;
- ceremonial precincts;
- sacred-landscape patterning;
- composite supernatural imagery.

Those contexts make political/sacral entanglement plausible and in some cases
strongly suggestive.

But:

ELITE_MONUMENT
+
RITUAL_SITE

does not automatically yield:

DIVINE_RULER.

Required stronger evidence would include:
- repeated object-specific ruler/supernatural conjunction;
- clear ritual context;
- diachronic pattern;
- reliable iconographic semantics;
- ideally readable textual evidence, which is currently absent.

## 8. San Lorenzo versus La Venta

San Lorenzo:
- earlier;
- large landscape engineering;
- numerous heads/thrones;
- strong regional-center authority.

La Venta:
- later;
- ceremonial precinct;
- complex offering system;
- monumental visual communication;
- elite burial evidence.

Therefore:

AUTHORITY_MODEL_MAY_CHANGE_DIACHRONICALLY.

The project does not assume one unchanged Olmec kingship institution from
San Lorenzo through La Venta.

## 9. Monument mutilation/recarving

Current scholarship treats mutilation and recarving as significant parts of the
monument biography.

Possible meanings include:
- reuse;
- political transition;
- memory work;
- destruction/deactivation;
- material recycling.

No single interpretation is default.

Current consequence:

MONUMENT_BIOGRAPHY_IS_PART_OF_AUTHORITY_EVIDENCE.

The final visible state of a monument need not equal its original function.

## 10. Current axis admission

POLITICAL_SACRALIZATION_MODEL:
PARTIAL_COMPARISON_CANDIDATE.

Secure:
- elite/ruler authority;
- monumental public representation;
- labor/material mobilization;
- co-location with ceremonial systems.

Not secure:
- divine kingship;
- ruler-deity identity;
- one hereditary theological doctrine.

INSTITUTIONALIZATION_MODEL:
BOUNDED_ELITE_LABOR_MATERIAL_AND_MONUMENTAL_LAYER.

AGENCY_AND_AUTHORITY_MODEL:
PARTIAL_ELITE_POLITICAL_AUTHORITY.

## 11. Strongest result

OLMEC_MONUMENTAL_SCULPTURE_SECURELY_SUPPORTS_PUBLIC_ELITE_AUTHORITY_SOCIAL_DIFFERENTIATION_AND_LARGE_SCALE_LABOR_MOBILIZATION_BUT_THE_CURRENT_EVIDENCE_DOES_NOT_JUSTIFY_PROMOTING_RULERS_INTO_DIVINE_KINGS_OR_TREATING_ALL_MONUMENT_TYPES_AS_ONE_FIXED_POLITICAL_THEOLOGY.
