# Olmec Iconographic Taxonomy Hostile Test V1

Status: ADVERSARIAL ICONOGRAPHIC METHOD TEST / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Stress-test modern scholarly labels for recurrent Olmec beings and motifs so
that useful iconographic classification does not become a fictional native
pantheon.

Tested labels include:
- "Olmec Maize God";
- "were-jaguar";
- "Olmec Dragon";
- "Earth Monster";
- "Rain God";
- "supernatural infant."

Core rule:

RECURRENT_ICONOGRAPHIC_CLUSTER != NATIVE_NAMED_DEITY.

A second rule:

LATER_MESOAMERICAN_SEMANTIC_PARALLEL != OLMEC_SEMANTICS_PROVED.

## 1. Why taxonomies are still useful

Olmec art recurrently depicts:
- cleft heads;
- maize/vegetal elements;
- feline traits;
- saurian/crocodilian traits;
- avian traits;
- infant-like beings;
- composite human/animal figures;
- mountain/cave/earth-related imagery.

Classification helps identify:
- repetition;
- variation;
- chronology;
- regional distribution;
- associations among motifs.

Therefore:

ICONOGRAPHIC_TAXONOMY = LEGITIMATE_ANALYTIC_TOOL.

The hostile test concerns claim ceiling, not prohibition.

## 2. "Olmec Maize God"

Current scholarship, including recent religion synthesis, identifies a recurrent
maize-centered religious/iconographic complex and argues that agriculture,
especially maize, was a major religious theme.

Selected greenstone celts and cleft-headed youthful figures have been
interpreted as maize sprouts or maize supernatural figures.

Supportive evidence:
- recurrent vegetal/maize imagery;
- greenstone deposit contexts;
- Formative agricultural importance;
- later Mesoamerican maize-deity iconographic bridges.

Hostile controls:
- "Maize God" is a modern analytic name;
- exact Olmec word/name is unknown;
- gender/personhood/deity status may vary among images;
- later Maya Maize God semantics cannot simply be transferred backward;
- some objects are unprovenanced or museum-collected outside secure context.

Status:

OLMEC_MAIZE_SUPERNATURAL_ICONOGRAPHIC_COMPLEX =
BOUNDED_SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION.

"OLMEC MAIZE GOD" AS NATIVE NAMED DEITY =
NOT_ADMITTED.

## 3. "Were-jaguar"

The "were-jaguar" label has historically grouped infant-like or human-feline
figures with traits such as:
- cleft head;
- downturned mouth;
- almond/slanted eyes;
- feline features;
- sometimes fangs.

The visual cluster is real.

But the label itself can imply:
- human-to-jaguar transformation;
- hybrid species;
- one named supernatural being;
- one mythic narrative.

Those implications are not established merely by morphology.

Current status:

HUMAN_FELINE_INFANT_ICONOGRAPHIC_CLUSTER =
BOUNDED_VISUAL_CLASSIFICATION.

"WERE-JAGUAR" AS LITERAL TRANSFORMATION ONTOLOGY =
NOT_ADMITTED.

TRANSFORMATION_MODEL =
NOT_ADMITTED_FROM_THIS_LABEL.

## 4. "Supernatural infant"

Infant-like figures occur in:
- portable sculpture;
- Las Limas;
- seated adult/infant compositions;
- other Olmec visual contexts.

The Met and other scholarship use "supernatural baby/infant" for selected
figures.

Secure:
INFANT_LIKE_OTHERWORLDLY_OR_NONORDINARY_VISUAL_TYPE.

Not secure:
- one deity;
- one myth;
- sacrificial victim;
- dead child;
- maize being in every case.

Status:

SUPERNATURAL_INFANT =
USEFUL_SCHOLARLY_CLASS_WITH_OBJECT_SPECIFIC_REBINDING_REQUIRED.

## 5. Las Limas hostile control

The Lord of Las Limas sculpture securely preserves:
- seated human-like main figure;
- limp/reclining infant-like figure;
- incised supernatural/composite motifs on the principal figure;
- greenstone monumental/portable religious art.

Interpretations include:
- priest;
- ruler;
- ritual specialist;
- maize/supernatural infant;
- cosmological tableau.

No readable caption chooses among them.

Therefore:

LAS_LIMAS =
HIGH_VALUE_ICONOGRAPHIC_ASSEMBLAGE.

Not:

KNOWN_PRIEST_HOLDING_KNOWN_MAIZE_GOD.

## 6. "Olmec Dragon" / "Earth Monster"

Modern taxonomies group repeated composite saurian/feline/avian/earth-related
motifs under labels such as:
- Olmec Dragon;
- Earth Monster;
- crocodilian earth being.

Current religion scholarship uses direct-historical comparison with later
Mesoamerican:
- mountain;
- earth;
- crocodilian;
- cave;
- world-direction

systems.

This can generate bounded historical hypotheses.

Hostile controls:
- no native Olmec name survives;
- not every saurian composite belongs to one being;
- later Maya witz/earth-monster semantics cannot be assumed identical;
- "dragon" is an English scholarly label with non-Olmec associations.

Status:

SAURIAN_EARTH_MOUNTAIN_COMPOSITE_COMPLEX =
PARTIAL_SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION.

"OLMEC DRAGON" AS ONE NATIVE GOD =
NOT_ADMITTED.

## 7. "Rain God"

Water/rain/fertility themes are supported by:
- cave contexts such as Oxtotitlan;
- agricultural interpretation;
- later Mesoamerican continuities;
- iconographic scholarship.

But no readable Olmec text names a Tlaloc/Chaac-equivalent deity.

Therefore:

RAIN_WATER_SUPERNATURAL_COMPLEX =
PARTIAL_SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION.

LATER_TLALOC_OR_CHAAC_IDENTITY =
NOT_ADMITTED.

## 8. Direct historical approach test

The 2025 Oxford Handbook religion synthesis explicitly uses the direct
historical approach:
- begin with later, better-understood Mesoamerican systems;
- bridge backward through intermediate evidence;
- avoid leaping across long temporal gaps without bridges.

ON_THEO accepts the method only with this chain:

LATER_SEMANTIC_MODEL
-> INTERMEDIATE_TEMPORAL_BRIDGE
-> OLMEC_ICONOGRAPHIC_CLUSTER
-> BOUNDED_HYPOTHESIS.

Prohibited:

LATER_GOD_NAME
-> OLMEC_IMAGE
-> SAME_GOD.

## 9. Temporal bridges

Potential bridge evidence can include:
- Late Preclassic Maya murals;
- Middle/Late Formative iconography;
- repeated regional motifs;
- later readable scripts;
- persistent material conventions.

A bridge strengthens continuity hypotheses.

It does not guarantee:
- unchanged meaning;
- direct descent;
- one ethnic transmission route.

## 10. Pantheon test

Question:
Can current evidence recover an Olmec pantheon?

Result:

ONE_NAMED_SYSTEMATIC_OLMEC_PANTHEON =
NOT_ADMITTED.

What can be recovered:
- recurrent visual complexes;
- probable supernatural/otherworldly figures;
- agricultural/rain/earth/mountain themes at scholarly ceiling;
- ritual contexts;
- human-spirit relations in portable art scholarship.

What cannot currently be recovered securely:
- native names;
- exact genealogies;
- universal functions;
- hierarchy among beings;
- canonical list;
- one theology.

## 11. Sacral-presence axis

Iconography can support that supernatural/sacral relations were:
- represented visually;
- embodied in portable/monumental forms;
- embedded in ritual deposits and sacred landscapes.

But:

SACRAL_PRESENCE_MODEL =
PARTIAL_ICONOGRAPHIC_MATERIAL.

No one localization ontology is recoverable.

OLMEC_ICONOGRAPHY_SUPPORTS_RECURRENT_SUPERNATURAL_AGRICULTURAL_EARTH_WATER_ANIMAL_AND_HUMAN_SPIRIT_VISUAL_COMPLEXES_BUT_MODERN_LABELS_FOR_THOSE_COMPLEXES_MUST_NOT_BE_PROMOTED_INTO_NATIVE_DEITY_NAMES_TRANSFORMATION_DOCTRINES_OR_A_SYSTEMATIC_PANTHEON.
