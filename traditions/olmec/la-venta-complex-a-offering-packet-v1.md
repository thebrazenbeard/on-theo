# La Venta Complex A Offering and Mortuary Packet V1

Status: MATERIAL RITUAL-DEPOSITION CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Deepen the strongest direct Olmec ritual-practice evidence: La Venta Complex A.

This packet distinguishes:
- ordinary offering deposits;
- massive buried offerings;
- funerary deposits;
- staged figurine assemblage;
- later iconographic/theological interpretation.

Core rule:

RITUAL_DEPOSITION != ONE_OFFERING_MECHANISM.

## 1. Complex A as ceremonial precinct

INAH identifies Complex A as La Venta's ceremonial enclosure.

Original plan:
- nine structures;
- symmetrically arranged around two courtyards;
- northern courtyard bounded by basalt columns.

Evidence class:
MATERIAL EVIDENCE / ARCHITECTURAL CONTEXT.

Current axis:

RITUAL_ORDER_MODEL =
BOUNDED_CEREMONIAL_PRECINCT.

## 2. Approximately fifty offerings

INAH reports approximately fifty offerings in Complex A containing combinations
of:
- ceramic vessels;
- figurines;
- jade ear ornaments;
- necklaces/beads;
- votive celts/chisels;
- other greenstone objects;
- hematite/magnetite mirrors;
- additional artifacts.

INAH museum interpretation reports thousands of jade objects deposited in the
ceremonial precinct, especially around 800-600 BCE.

Secure proposition:

LA_VENTA_COMPLEX_A_CONTAINS_REPEATED_HIGH_VALUE_STRUCTURED_DEPOSITION.

This establishes repeated ritual action.

It does not establish one recipient or one reason for all deposits.

## 3. Massive offerings

Five massive offerings are reported.

They contain:
- tons, in some cases extraordinary quantities, of imported serpentine blocks;
- deep prepared cavities;
- deliberate covering/fill;
- in selected cases abstract mosaic arrangements;
- votive greenstone objects placed in associated fill.

Evidence class:
MATERIAL EVIDENCE / STRATIGRAPHIC ARCHAEOLOGICAL CONTEXT.

Current classification:

MASSIVE_OFFERING =
LARGE_SCALE_SUBTERRANEAN_RITUAL_CONSTRUCTION.

This is not merely "valuable objects dropped in a pit."

It required:
- material procurement;
- long-distance transport;
- labor;
- planning;
- burial/concealment.

## 4. "Mother Earth" interpretation

INAH public interpretation states that the most widely accepted explanation for
selected massive offerings is an offering to Mother Earth.

Because no readable Olmec text names the recipient:

MOTHER_EARTH_RECIPIENT =
SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION.

Not:
PRIMARY_TEXTUAL_DEITY_IDENTIFICATION.

ON_THEO may compare this interpretation with:
- subterranean placement;
- greenstone symbolism;
- later Mesoamerican earth concepts

but later parallels cannot convert the interpretation into direct Olmec textual
fact.

## 5. Offering 4

Offering 4 includes:
- sixteen anthropomorphic greenstone figures;
- six celt/axe-like objects arranged as upright elements;
- deliberate staged/spatial arrangement;
- placement associated with Massive Offering 3;
- working date broadly around 800-600 BCE in current INAH public synthesis.

INAH public interpretation proposes possibilities including:
- procession;
- political ceremony;
- religious ceremony;
- mortuary/underworld relation.

The arrangement is secure.

The narrative meaning is not.

Current classification:

OFFERING_4 =
STAGED_FIGURAL_RITUAL_ASSEMBLAGE_WITH_CONTESTED_SCENIC_MEANING.

Guard:

FIGURAL_SCENE != HISTORICAL_EVENT_IDENTIFIED_BY_DEFAULT.

## 6. Elite burial evidence

INAH reports that Building A-2 included:
- a double tomb;
- remains of two dignitaries;
- rich jade offerings.

Other La Venta greenstone objects also occur as funerary goods in tomb/cist
contexts.

Current axis:

MORTUARY_RELATIONSHIP_MODEL =
PARTIAL_ELITE_FUNERARY_DEPOSITION.

Secure:
- elite burial;
- high-value material relation to dead.

Not established:
- ancestor worship;
- postmortem destination;
- resurrection;
- soul ontology;
- precise offering recipient.

## 7. Long-distance ritual material mobilization

Material provenance reported by INAH includes:
- jade from Guatemala;
- serpentine from Oaxaca;
- volcanic stone from Los Tuxtlas.

Current proposition:

RITUAL_DEPOSITION_DRAWS_ON_LONG_DISTANCE_EXCHANGE_NETWORKS.

This supports:
- political economy;
- elite coordination;
- ritual value of nonlocal materials.

It does not prove:
- materials were valuable only because distant;
- all trade was religious;
- political control over source regions.

## 8. Greenstone meaning

INAH and museum scholarship commonly interpret greenstone through:
- sacred value;
- water/fertility;
- maize/growth;
- permanence;
- preciousness.

Met scholarship on votive celts also develops maize/growth interpretations.

Current project status:

GREENSTONE_SYMBOLISM =
SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETIVE_FIELD.

Secure material fact:
greenstone was preferentially selected and repeatedly deposited in ritual
contexts.

Do not reduce its meaning to one symbolic term.
