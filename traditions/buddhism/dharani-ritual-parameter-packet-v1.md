# Buddhism — Dhāraṇī Ritual-Parameter Packet V1

Status: MEDIEVAL MAHĀYĀNA / ESOTERIC-ADJACENT RITUAL TEXT + MATERIAL EVIDENCE / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Test a high-information Buddhist ritual family against the current
`RITUAL_PARAMETER_SENSITIVITY` research question.

Do medieval Buddhist dhāraṇī traditions preserve procedures whose represented
efficacy depends on combinations of exact verbal form, repetition, written
inscription, spatial configuration, offerings, timing, bodily carriage,
visualization, maṇḍala structure, or ritual sequence?

Yes, in selected source families.

Does that establish anomalous causal efficacy or an external information
channel?

No.

## 1. This is not the early Vinaya layer

The current Buddhist parent branch is strongest for:

- early discourse;
- parallel textual transmission;
- Aśokan institutional evidence;
- selected Vinaya legal/ritual procedure.

Dhāraṇī literature belongs to later and internally diverse Buddhist developments.

Required guard:

`VINAYA_RITUAL_ORDER != MEDIEVAL_DHARANI_RITUAL`.

And:

`MEDIEVAL_DHARANI != ALL_BUDDHISM`.

## 2. The term dhāraṇī is polysemic

Paul Copp, “Notes on the term ‘Dhāraṇī’ in medieval Chinese Buddhist thought,”
*Bulletin of the School of Oriental and African Studies* 71.3 (2008),
DOI 10.1017/S0041977X08000852, argues that the term cannot be reduced simply to
“memory” or “spell.” Its practical and doctrinal range includes ideas of
grasping/holding as well as incantatory usage.

Ronald Davidson, “Studies in Dhāraṇī Literature I: Revisiting the Meaning of the
Term Dhāraṇī,” *Journal of Indian Philosophy* 37 (2009),
DOI 10.1007/S10781-008-9054-8, likewise emphasizes the term's polysemy and
context dependence.

Therefore:

`DHARANI != ONE_RITUAL_TECHNOLOGY`.

Each source must be classified locally.

## 3. Do not retroject mature Tantra backward

Richard McBride, “Dhāraṇī and Spells in Medieval Sinitic Buddhism”
(*Journal of the International Association of Buddhist Studies*, 2005),
criticizes teleological approaches that treat widespread medieval Chinese
dhāraṇī/spell material simply as proto-Tantric evidence.

This is directly relevant to the current project.

Required guard:

`SPELL_OR_DHARANI_PRESENT != MATURE_TANTRIC_SYSTEM`.

Later maṇḍala/initiation/visualization structures must be dated rather than
projected backward.

## 4. Atikūṭa's Collected Dhāraṇī Scriptures — T. 901

Koichi Shinohara's study of Atikūṭa's *Collected Dhāraṇī Scriptures*,
Taishō 901, translated in 654 CE, is one of the strongest diachronic sources for
ritual development.

Shinohara reconstructs a broad historical movement from relatively simple
incantation-recitation practices in earlier Chinese collections toward more
complex rites involving:

- images;
- maṇḍalas;
- initiation;
- visualization;
- increasingly elaborate ceremonial sequence.

The key result is not “Buddhist ritual got more powerful.”

It is:

`PARAMETER_COMPLEXITY_INCREASES_DIACHRONICALLY`.

That creates a major ordinary historical explanation for complexity:

`RITUAL_ELABORATION_CAN_EVOLVE`.

Therefore a later complex procedure must not be treated as an unchanged ancient
technical protocol.

## 5. Uṣṇīṣavijayā dhāraṇī family

The Uṣṇīṣavijayā tradition is especially valuable because:

- multiple Chinese translations survive;
- Tibetan canonical versions survive;
- ritual manuals survive;
- Dunhuang manuscript evidence survives;
- monumental/material use survives in dhāraṇī pillars.

Relevant textual identifiers include Chinese Taishō 967–974, 978, 979 and
Tibetan Toh 594–598 in related textual/ritual families.

Modern 84000 translations of Toh 594 and Toh 598 provide accessible translation
of Tibetan canonical witnesses while remaining modern translations, not ancient
witnesses themselves.

Selected ritual manuals prescribe combinations of:

- recitation;
- high repetition counts;
- offerings;
- maṇḍala construction;
- specific spatial placement;
- specific ritual timing in some rites;
- written installation or physical emplacement;
- claimed benefits such as purification, protection, longevity, or improved
  postmortem destiny.

Source-level abstraction:

`Dharani_Form + Repetition + Material/Spatial_Configuration + Ritual_Context ->
SOURCE_CLAIMED_EFFECT`.

The right-hand side is a historical claim, not empirical verification.

## 6. Mahāpratisarā — text worn on the body

Paul Copp's *The Body Incantatory: Spells and the Ritual Imagination in Medieval
Chinese Buddhism* (Columbia University Press, 2014) analyzes textual and
archaeological/material evidence for dhāraṇī practices, including
Mahāpratisarā amulets.

The material corpus includes surviving written/printed amulets intended to be
worn or carried.

This is a major result for the interface hypothesis because it falsifies a
sound-only interpretation of the source tradition.

The operative form can be:

- recited;
- written;
- carried;
- installed;
- materially associated with a body or place.

Therefore:

`ACOUSTIC_FORM != ONLY_RELEVANT_HISTORICAL_FORM`.

A broader source-level category is needed:

`FORM_BEARING_MEDIUM`.

## 7. Uṣṇīṣavijayā pillars — material/epigraphic embodiment

Copp also analyzes dhāraṇī pillars bearing the Uṣṇīṣavijayā dhāraṇī.

These provide MATERIAL / EPIGRAPHIC evidence that the written dhāraṇī was not
merely a literary prescription.

It could be installed into landscapes, tomb/funerary settings, and built
religious environments.

This supports:

`WRITTEN_FORM_AS_RITUAL_OBJECT`.

It does not prove the text produced the claimed religious effect.

This distinction is crucial:

`MATERIAL_USE_ESTABLISHED`
does not imply
`CLAIMED_EFFECT_ESTABLISHED`.

## 8. Transliteration, translation, and semantic dependence

Dhāraṇī transmission raises a high-value question:

What exactly is being preserved when a formula crosses languages?

In Chinese and Tibetan transmission, dhāraṇī/mantra material can be preserved
through transliteration rather than ordinary semantic translation.

Tibetan canonical presentations can likewise leave dhāraṇī formulae
untranslated while translating surrounding ritual instructions.

This creates a genuine historical category:

`FORM_PRESERVATION_WITH_PARTIAL_OR_NONORDINARY_SEMANTIC_ACCESS`.

But the project must not overstate it.

Possible reasons include:

- sacred prestige of source-language sound/form;
- inability or unwillingness to translate technical/name strings;
- ritual conservatism;
- mnemonic identity;
- perceived efficacy;
- translational convention.

Therefore:

`TRANSLITERATION != PROOF_SOUND_ITSELF_IS_CAUSAL`.

## 9. Semantic opacity is high-interest but not decisive

Some dhāraṇī formulae contain strings whose lexical semantics are incomplete,
unclear, composite, or highly technical.

This makes the tradition relevant to the “low-semantic parameter” question.

But:

`SEMANTIC_OPACITY != MEANINGLESSNESS`.

And:

`MEANINGLESS_TO_ANALYST != MEANINGLESS_TO_TRADITION`.

A formula may carry:
- names;
- grammatical remnants;
- ritual indexicality;
- conventional sacred identity;
- mnemonic structure;
- translational residue.

## 10. Repetition counts

Selected dhāraṇī manuals prescribe exact or bounded repetition counts, sometimes
in the hundreds, thousands, or much larger ritual totals.

This is genuine procedural parameterization.

Possible ordinary explanations include:

- mnemonic reinforcement;
- ascetic discipline;
- time-on-task;
- attentional entrainment;
- institutional convention;
- symbolic numerology;
- cumulative merit logic;
- ritual intensification.

Therefore:

`EXACT_COUNT_PRESENT != COUNT_CAUSES_EXTERNAL_EFFECT`.

For the current hypothesis, exact counts become high-information only where:
- source explicitly contrasts correct versus incorrect count;
- semantically equivalent alternatives are disallowed;
- an independently measurable outcome is specified;
- the count can be safely varied prospectively.

## 11. Maṇḍala, orientation, and sequence

Later ritual manuals can combine:

- central and directional placement;
- ordered deity arrangement;
- geometric maṇḍala construction;
- offerings;
- recitation;
- visualization;
- ritual sequence.

This is one of the strongest Buddhist examples of multimodal parameter
composition.

But Shinohara's diachronic reconstruction supplies a powerful null:

complexity can be historically assembled.

Therefore:

`MULTIMODAL_RITUAL_COMPLEXITY != PRIMORDIAL_TECHNICAL_PROTOCOL`.

## 12. Written form versus spoken form is experimentally useful

The dhāraṇī tradition gives a rare historically grounded way to separate media.

If one tradition represents efficacy in both:
- recited form;
- written/carried form;

then a modern structural experiment can ask whether any effect tracks:

- acoustic realization;
- orthographic/visual form;
- participant belief;
- physical carrying/placement;
- ritual context.

That is more discriminating than treating “the mantra” as one indivisible
variable.

No such experiment has established an anomalous external effect.

## 13. Material evidence improves historical confidence, not metaphysical confidence

The Mahāpratisarā amulet and Uṣṇīṣavijayā pillar evidence matters because it
shows that these practices were materially enacted.

This raises confidence in:

`PEOPLE_ACTUALLY_USED_WRITTEN_DHARANI_AS_RITUAL_OBJECTS`.

It does not raise confidence equivalently in:

`THE_CLAIMED_PROTECTION_OR_POSTMORTEM_EFFECT_OCCURRED`.

Preserve the evidence-class separation.

## 14. Claimed effect domains

Selected dhāraṇī texts/manuals represent effects including:

- protection;
- purification;
- removal of karmic obstacles;
- health/longevity;
- postmortem benefit;
- merit;
- ritual empowerment;
- successful initiation;
- memory/retention or religious attainment in other dhāraṇī contexts.

These are heterogeneous.

Do not create one variable called:

`DHARANI_EFFECT`.

## 15. Strongest ordinary rivals

### H0 Cultural formalization
Highly structured ritual can emerge through institutional tradition.

### H1 Mnemonic stability
Repetition and formal preservation improve transmission.

### H2 Institutional authority
Specialist ritual knowledge differentiates trained practitioners.

### H4 Psychophysiology
Repetition, breath, vocalization, visualization and attention can change
participant state.

### H5 Acoustic neurophysiology
Recitation can affect physiology and attention without external agency.

### H7 Expectancy/suggestion
Sacred context can alter perception and reported outcome.

### H10 Editorialization/ritual elaboration
Later ritual manuals can systematize and expand earlier incantation practice.

### H11 Contact/transmission
Indian, Central Asian, Chinese and Tibetan networks transmit and transform
ritual forms.

These ordinary models are all strongly live.

## 16. Strongest high-information residue

The most interesting surviving historical feature is not “Buddhists used
magic words.”

It is:

`FORM_CAN_BE_TREATED_AS_EFFECTIVE_ACROSS_MULTIPLE_MEDIA`.

Selected dhāraṇī traditions preserve a ritual object's identity through:

- sound;
- script;
- inscription;
- bodily carrying;
- monumental installation;
- ritual sequence;
- maṇḍala placement.

This is a broader and more source-secure parameter family than a pure acoustic
model.

Its mechanism remains unresolved historically and unverified empirically.

## 19. Strongest result

This lane materially changes the broader interface hypothesis.

A sound-only model is too narrow.

The source-secure historical pattern is better described as:

`FORM + MEDIUM + REPETITION + MATERIAL/SPATIAL_CONTEXT + SEQUENCE ->
SOURCE_CLAIMED_EFFECT`.

The same dhāraṇī identity can be carried by voice, script, body-worn object, or
monumental inscription.

That is genuinely interesting.

It remains historical ritual evidence, not evidence that reality parses the
form as an external command.
