# Buddhism — Early Parallel and Witness Map V1

Status: CROSS-TRANSMISSION SOURCE CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

The previous packet bound several neutral comparison axes to named Pāli
discourses. This packet asks a stricter question:

> Which propositions are also represented in other early Buddhist transmission
> lines, and where do the witnesses diverge?

A parallel designation is evidence of textual relationship or comparable
discourse material. It is not automatic proof of:
- identical wording;
- identical chronology;
- identical school affiliation;
- one original archetype;
- semantic identity at every clause.

## 1. Not-self discourse family — SN 22.59

Pāli:
- SN 22.59, Anattalakkhaṇa / Pañcavaggiya.

Chinese and Vinaya parallels catalogued by SuttaCentral include:
- SĀ 34 / T 99.34;
- independent translation T 102;
- Mahīśāsaka Vinaya parallel;
- Dharmaguptaka Vinaya parallel;
- SĀ 33 as a closely related witness.

A Gāndhārī version is preserved as:
- Robert Senior Scroll 22;
- text 2;
- reconstructed title Aṇatvalakṣaṇa Sutra;
- Gāndhārī language;
- Kharoṣṭhī script;
- birch bark;
- approximately 140 CE;
- present location: University of Washington, Seattle;
- findspot uncertain, probably Haḍḍa, Afghanistan.

The Gandhāran Buddhist Texts edition explicitly identifies parallels with:
- SN 22.59;
- Pāli Vinaya;
- Sanskrit witnesses;
- SĀ 34 and SĀ 33;
- T 102;
- Chinese Vinaya witnesses;
- Tibetan Vinaya versions.

### Proposition-level convergence

Across SN 22.59, SĀ 34, and the Gāndhārī witness, the preserved discourse family
includes the same central architecture:

1. form, feeling, perception, formations, consciousness;
2. not-self characterization;
3. counterfactual control argument: if an aggregate were self, it would be
   controllable and not lead to affliction in the same way;
4. impermanence / suffering or affliction / change;
5. rejection of self-identification with the aggregates;
6. liberation as the downstream result.

This substantially strengthens the claim that the five-aggregate not-self
analysis is not merely a peculiarity of one surviving Pāli wording.

### Ceiling

This does not establish:
- one verbatim Ur-text;
- identical wording among all witnesses;
- a universal Buddhist metaphysical doctrine outside this discourse family.

## 2. Kaccānagotta discourse family — SN 12.15

Pāli:
- SN 12.15.

Chinese:
- SĀ 301 / T 99.301.
- SĀ 262 contains a partial/cited parallel in another discourse context.

Sanskrit:
- SF 168, Kātyāyanaḥ, from the Nidānasaṃyukta manuscript tradition.

SuttaCentral's parallel inventory directly aligns SN 12.15 with SĀ 301 and
SF 168.

### Proposition-level convergence

The parallel family supports a bounded comparison around:
- rejection of the exhaustive binary of existence and nonexistence;
- conditioned arising/cessation as the middle explanatory route;
- non-clinging to a self formulation.

### Witness control

The surviving Sanskrit manuscript witness is not treated as the composition
date of the discourse.

## 3. Volition / consciousness / renewed-existence family — SN 12.38

Pāli:
- SN 12.38, Cetanā.

Chinese:
- SĀ 359 / T 99.359.

SuttaCentral's parallel inventory directly aligns the pair.

Comparative scholarship reports the corresponding SĀ 359 material as preserving
the same core sequence:
- willing/intending or related activity;
- a basis/object for consciousness;
- consciousness supported/established;
- future renewed existence / future life;
- birth, aging, death, and suffering;
- cessation when the supporting condition is absent.

### Comparison effect

The source-controlled proposition:

`CONDITIONED_CONTINUITY_WITHOUT_ASSERTED_PERMANENT_SELF`

is therefore supported in more than one transmission line.

Exact term-by-term alignment remains a separate task.

## 4. Kamma/intention family — AN 6.63

Pāli:
- AN 6.63, Nibbedhika.

Chinese parallels catalogued by SuttaCentral:
- MĀ 111 / T 26.111;
- T 57.

The Pāli discourse identifies intention as kamma and treats bodily, verbal, and
mental action as performed after intending.

### Important divergence

The Chinese MĀ 111 parallel is reported with a formulation distinguishing:
- intention;
- action performed after intending.

It also differs in how some karmic-result classifications are enumerated.

This is not noise.

It demonstrates the exact ON_THEO rule:

`PARALLEL_DISCOURSE_FAMILY`
does not imply
`IDENTICAL_PROPOSITION_AT_EVERY_SUBCLAUSE`.

### Comparison effect

The cross-tradition evidence supports the broader axis:

`INTENTION_IS_CENTRAL_TO_KARMIC_ACTION`

more strongly than it supports any one complete harmonized mechanics of karma.

## 5. Dependent-origination formula — SN 12.1–2 / 12.2

SuttaCentral's parallel index for the opening dependent-origination material
lists multiple non-Pāli parallels, including:
- SĀ 298 for the paired SN 12.1–2 material;
- T 124;
- EĀ 49.5;
- Sanskrit fragment witnesses.

For SN 12.2 specifically, EĀ 49.5 is listed as a parallel.

### Ceiling

This packet does not yet claim complete identity of every twelve-link definition
across those witnesses.

It does support the broader methodological point that conditioned-origination
formulae circulate across multiple early Buddhist textual lineages.

## 6. Physical-witness significance

The Gāndhārī Aṇatvalakṣaṇa witness is particularly valuable because it changes
the evidence type.

We are no longer comparing only later canonical editions.

The Robert Senior collection is an early-second-century Gāndhārī manuscript
collection. The project dating page argues for approximately 140 CE using:
- the associated pot inscription and Kaniṣka-era dating;
- radiocarbon results on collection fragments with a 130–250 CE two-sigma range;
- paleographic features;
- common scribal production of the collection.

Therefore:

`EARLY_NOT_SELF_DISCOURSE_FAMILY`

has both:
- cross-canonical textual parallels;
- an early physical manuscript witness.

This does not date the composition of the discourse to 140 CE.

!=
COMPOSITION_DATE.

## 7. Synthesis consequence

The neutral comparison axes now have different support levels.

### Stronger cross-line support

- PERSON_OR_SELF_MODEL:
  SN 22.59 + SĀ 34 + Gāndhārī Aṇatvalakṣaṇa family.

- CAUSAL_ORDER_TYPE:
  SN 12.15 + SĀ 301 + Sanskrit parallel family.

- CONTINUITY_MODEL:
  SN 12.38 + SĀ 359.

- MORAL_CAUSALITY_MODEL:
  AN 6.63 + MĀ 111/T 57 parallel family, with explicit divergence retained.

### Still not licensed

The cross-parallel evidence does not license:
- one timeless Buddhism;
- creator/no-creator claims for all Buddhist strata;
- one complete theory of rebirth;
- one complete theory of nirvāṇa;
- a simulation interpretation;
- later-school metaphysics projected backward.

## 8. Method gain

The strongest result of this pass is not that "Buddhism agrees with itself."

It is more precise:

> Several high-value proposition families survive across Pāli, Chinese, Sanskrit,
> and in one case Gāndhārī transmission, while meaningful local differences
> remain visible.

That is exactly the kind of evidence ON_THEO can safely use for comparative
ontology.
