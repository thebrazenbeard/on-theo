# Buddhism — Aśokan Inscription and Material Packet V1

Status: MATERIAL / EPIGRAPHIC HISTORY CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

This packet adds an evidence class independent from transmitted discourse
collections.

Question:

> What can third-century-BCE inscriptions and archaeology establish about
> Buddhist institutions, places, texts, and patronage without importing later
> Aśoka legends?

Core rule:

`ASOKAN_INSCRIPTION`
!=
`LATER_ASOKA_LEGEND`.

A second rule is equally important:

`ASOKA_DHAMMA_POLICY`
!=
`EVERY_ASOKAN_INSCRIPTION_IS_SECTARIAN_BUDDHIST_TEACHING`.

## 1. Bhabra / Bairat inscription

This inscription is explicitly Buddhist.

A widely used translation has the king of Magadha address the Saṅgha and state
his reverence/faith in:
- Buddha;
- Dhamma;
- Saṅgha.

It then recommends a set of named Dhamma teachings for repeated hearing and
reflection by:
- monks;
- nuns;
- laymen;
- laywomen.

### What this establishes

At minimum, the inscription provides third-century-BCE epigraphic evidence for:
- a king self-positioning in relation to Buddha, Dhamma, and Saṅgha;
- differentiated monastic and lay Buddhist audiences;
- the idea that identified teachings could be recommended for repeated study;
- concern for continuity/endurance of Dhamma.

### What it does not establish

It does not establish:
- that the named textual items were identical in wording to every later Pāli
  canonical identification;
- that one complete Buddhist canon had already reached its later fixed form;
- that the king authored Buddhist doctrine;
- that every imperial dhamma inscription is merely a Buddhist missionary tract.

Identification of some recommended text titles with later canonical loci remains
a scholarly reconstruction and should be tracked title by title.

## 2. Rummindei / Lumbini pillar inscription

The Lumbini pillar is a physical inscription associated with Aśoka's personal
visit.

The inscription states, in translation, that the king came and paid reverence
because Buddha Śākyamuni was born there, and records a fiscal privilege for the
village.

UNESCO describes:
- the sandstone Aśokan pillar;
- its Brāhmī inscription;
- archaeological remains including monasteries and stūpas;
- evidence for Lumbini as a Buddhist pilgrimage centre from at least the third
  century BCE.

### What this establishes

- third-century-BCE royal identification of Lumbini with Buddha Śākyamuni's
  birthplace;
- royal cultic/pilgrimage attention to the site;
- a material anchor independent of later Buddha biographies.

### Ceiling

The inscription is evidence that the place was identified as the Buddha's
birthplace by Aśoka's period.

It is not, by itself, a contemporary witness to the Buddha's birth event
centuries earlier.

`THIRD_CENTURY_BCE_BIRTHPLACE_IDENTIFICATION`
!=
`CONTEMPORARY_BIRTH_RECORD`.

## 3. Nigali Sagar pillar inscription

The Nigali Sagar inscription refers to the stūpa of Buddha Konākamana
(Koṇāgamana/Kanakamuni in later forms).

Translations report that Aśoka:
- enlarged the stūpa;
- later came in person and paid reverence;
- erected a stone pillar.

### What this establishes

The inscription materially attests royal engagement with:
- Buddhist stūpa practice;
- a Buddha other than Śākyamuni;
- a remembered multiple-Buddha framework by the Aśokan period.

### Ceiling

It does not establish the historical biography of Konākamana.

It establishes the cultic/memorial proposition represented by the inscription.

## 4. Minor Rock Edict I

Minor Rock Edict I is often cited for Aśoka's personal Buddhist commitment.

Older and modern translations differ over important wording, including how
closely the king says he approached or joined the Saṅgha.

Therefore:

`ASOKA_BUDDHIST_ASSOCIATION`
is strongly relevant,

but:

`ASOKA_FORMALLY_BECAME_A_MONK`

must not be treated as secure solely from one English rendering.

This is a translation/interpretation control, not a denial of his Buddhist
affiliation.

## 5. The so-called Schism Edict

Versions occur at sites including:
- Sarnath;
- Sanchi;
- Kauśāmbī/Allahabad.

The inscription has often been described as evidence of royal intervention
against schism in "the Buddhist Church."

Scholarly criticism warns that this can project later centralized institutional
assumptions backward.

A more careful current ceiling is:
- royal concern with division in saṅgha communities;
- administrative intervention affecting monastic unity;
- exact institutional scope and some philological details remain debated.

### Guard

`LOCAL_OR_PLURAL_SANGHA_CONTROL`
!=
`ONE_CENTRALIZED_IMPERIAL_BUDDHIST_CHURCH`.

## 6. General imperial dhamma versus explicitly Buddhist inscriptions

The major rock and pillar edicts often promote:
- nonviolence or restraint;
- respect;
- generosity;
- moral conduct;
- inter-sect tolerance;
- administrative welfare.

They cannot all be treated as if every occurrence of `dhamma` were a technical
synonym for a narrow Buddhist doctrinal proposition.

The explicitly Buddhist inscriptional subset must therefore be tracked
separately.

### Proposed evidence classes

`ASOKA_EXPLICIT_BUDDHIST_EPIGRAPHY`
- Bhabra/Bairat;
- Lumbini;
- Nigali Sagar;
- saṅgha-related minor pillar material;
- relevant minor rock inscriptions with explicit Buddhist self-reference.

`ASOKA_GENERAL_DHAMMA_GOVERNANCE`
- broader moral/administrative edicts.

Overlap is possible.

Identity is not assumed.

## 7. Why this matters for ON_THEO

The discourse branch reconstructs Buddhist propositions.

The inscriptional branch establishes different historical facts:
- communities exist;
- monastic/lay categories are publicly recognized;
- Buddhist places are visited and patronized;
- stūpas are enlarged;
- named teachings are recommended;
- royal authorities intervene in institutional disputes.

This matters because:

`DOCTRINAL_TEXT_EXISTENCE`
does not automatically prove
`SOCIAL_INSTITUTIONAL_PRACTICE`.

Aśokan epigraphy gives an independent line of evidence that Buddhism had
material, institutional, and political presence by the third century BCE.

## 8. Comparison consequence

The neutral comparison framework should eventually contain a separate axis:

`INSTITUTIONALIZATION_MODEL`

Questions include:
- Is there an identifiable specialist/religious community?
- How is authority organized?
- What roles do rulers play?
- What evidence is textual versus inscriptional versus archaeological?
- How are teachings stabilized, selected, copied, or publicly sponsored?

This axis should not be collapsed into metaphysical truth claims.
