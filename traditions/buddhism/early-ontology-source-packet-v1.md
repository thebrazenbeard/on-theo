# Buddhism — Early Ontology Source Packet V1

Status: PRIMARY-TEXT-BOUND RESEARCH PACKET / PALI WITNESS LAYER

## Scope

This packet strengthens a narrow set of comparison questions using named
discourses from the Pāli Nikāyas.

It does not claim:
- that every item is the earliest recoverable formulation;
- that the Pāli wording is the sole early witness;
- that all Buddhist schools interpret these passages identically;
- that later Abhidharma, Madhyamaka, Yogācāra, Buddha-nature, Tibetan, or East
  Asian systems can be projected backward into these passages.

Where cross-canonical parallels have not been independently aligned in this
packet, the evidence claim is limited to the named Pāli witness.

## 1. SN 22.59 — Anattalakkhaṇa / Characteristic of Not-Self

Source:
- Saṃyutta Nikāya 22.59
- https://suttacentral.net/sn22.59/en/sujato
- Pāli/translation cross-display:
  https://plaindharma.com/not-self/source

Primary proposition:

The discourse applies `anattā` / not-self to:
- form;
- feeling;
- perception;
- volitional formations/choices;
- consciousness.

Its argument is not merely a dictionary equation.

For each aggregate the discourse uses control and affliction:
if the aggregate were self, it would be subject to command in the desired way;
instead it is afflictive and not under such control.

It then combines:
- impermanence;
- unsatisfactoriness/affliction;
- changeability;
- the instruction not to identify the aggregate as
  "mine / I / my self."

### Comparison ceiling

SUPPORTED:

`FIVE_AGGREGATES_ARE_NOT_TO_BE_IDENTIFIED_AS_SELF`.

NOT established by this passage alone:

`NO_PERSON_EXISTS`.

NOT established:

`A_HIDDEN_PERMANENT_SELF_EXISTS_OUTSIDE_THE_AGGREGATES`.

The passage is therefore evidence against using either a simple persistent-soul
model or a simple annihilation slogan as a neutral project translation.

## 2. SN 44.10 — Vacchagotta and the binary self question

Source:
- Saṃyutta Nikāya 44.10
- https://suttacentral.net/sn44.10/en/bodhi

Vacchagotta asks:
- whether there is a self;
- whether there is no self.

The Buddha does not affirm either answer.

The subsequent explanation to Ānanda states that:
- affirming a self would align with eternalism;
- denying a self in that form would align with annihilationism;
- affirming self would conflict with the knowledge that phenomena are not-self;
- a simple negative answer would further confuse Vacchagotta.

### Comparison consequence

The repository must distinguish:

`NOT_SELF_ANALYSIS`

from

`METAPHYSICAL_PROPOSITION_NO_ENTITY_OF_ANY_KIND_EXISTS`.

This also blocks the opposite move of smuggling a permanent entity back in merely
because the simple negative answer is refused.

## 3. SN 12.2 — dependent origination as a conditioned sequence

Source:
- Saṃyutta Nikāya 12.2
- https://suttacentral.net/sn12.2/en/sujato

The discourse gives the familiar conditioned sequence:

ignorance
-> formations/choices
-> consciousness
-> name-and-form
-> six sense fields
-> contact
-> feeling
-> craving
-> grasping
-> continued existence/becoming
-> birth
-> aging-and-death and suffering.

It also supplies definitions for the factors.

### Comparison consequence

This is direct evidence that a major explanatory structure in the discourse
corpus is relational/conditional.

Project abstraction:

`WHEN_CONDITION_X -> CONDITIONED_Y`

is safer than forcing the sequence into:

`ROOT_AGENT_CALLS_Y`.

This is a comparison abstraction, not a claim that dependent origination is one
complete Buddhist metaphysics.

## 4. SN 12.15 — existence/nonexistence as an inadequate binary

Source:
- Saṃyutta Nikāya 12.15, Kaccānagotta
- https://suttacentral.net/sn12.15/en/bodhi
- Pāli witness:
  https://dhammatalks.net/suttacentral/sc2016/sc/pi/sn12.15.html

The discourse characterizes:
- "all exists";
- "all does not exist"

as two extremes and teaches dependent origination as a middle account.

It also associates correct seeing with the arising and cessation of suffering
rather than attachment to "my self."

### Comparison consequence

Do not encode Buddhist ontology as a forced choice:

`SUBSTANCE_EXISTS`
versus
`NOTHING_EXISTS`.

The source itself rejects a closely related binary framing.

## 5. SN 12.38 — conditioned continuity and future renewed existence

Source:
- Saṃyutta Nikāya 12.38
- https://suttacentral.net/sn12.38/en/bodhi

The discourse says that:
- intention;
- planning;
- latent tendency / dwelling on something

provide a basis for the maintenance or establishment of consciousness.

With consciousness established and growing, it describes production of future
renewed existence, followed by future birth, aging, death, and suffering.

When the basis is absent, the chain is described as ceasing.

### Comparison consequence

This passage is important because it supplies a continuity mechanism without
stating a permanent self-substance.

The neutral comparison axis should therefore allow:

`CAUSAL_CONTINUITY_WITHOUT_ASSERTED_PERMANENT_ENTITY`.

It would be methodologically wrong to translate the passage as:
- soul migration;
- software-object copying;
- pure discontinuity.

Those would be added models, not the wording of the source.

## 6. AN 6.63 — kamma as intention and consequence

Source:
- Aṅguttara Nikāya 6.63
- https://suttacentral.net/an6.63/en/sujato

The discourse explicitly identifies intention/choice as `kamma` and says action
is performed through body, speech, and mind.

It distinguishes results:
- in the present life;
- on a following rebirth;
- later.

It also describes a path leading to cessation of kamma.

### Comparison consequence

This is evidence for a moral/behavioral causal structure that is not expressed
here as sentencing by a personal judge.

Therefore the neutral project schema must keep separate:

`MORAL_CAUSAL_CONSEQUENCE`

and

`PERSONAL_JUDGMENT_BY_AUTHORITY`.

This source alone does not define every Buddhist theory of karmic mechanics.

## 7. MN 72 — post-mortem predication and the fire simile

Source:
- Majjhima Nikāya 72, Aggivacchagotta
- SuttaCentral access:
  https://suttacentral.net/mn72/en/sujato
- accessible cross-display:
  https://www.ruyimeditation.org/discourses/majjhima-nikaya/mn-072.html

Vacchagotta asks a set of metaphysical questions including whether a liberated
Tathāgata:
- exists after death;
- does not exist after death;
- both exists and does not;
- neither exists nor does not.

The Buddha does not adopt those alternatives.

The discourse uses the image of a fire no longer sustained by fuel to challenge
the applicability of the ordinary directional/existence question.

### Comparison consequence

The repository must not convert liberation into a simple location model such as:

`PERSON_OBJECT_MOVES_TO_EXTERNAL_REALM`.

Nor does this discourse by itself establish annihilation.

The safest neutral axis is:

`POSTMORTEM_PREDICATION_MODEL`

with the possibility that ordinary existence/nonexistence categories are
explicitly treated as inapplicable.

## 8. Udāna 8.3 — conditioned and unconditioned

Sources:
- Pāli:
  https://dhammatalks.net/suttacentral/sc2016/sc/pi/ud8.3.html
- English translation:
  https://www.dhammatalks.net/suttacentral/sc2016/sc/en/ud8.3.html

The passage contrasts:
- born / become / made / conditioned

with:
- unborn / unbecome / unmade / unconditioned,

and says escape from the conditioned is possible because there is the
unconditioned.

### Comparison consequence

This is strong source evidence for a conditioned/unconditioned distinction.

It is not source evidence that the unconditioned is:
- a creator;
- a programmer;
- a physical place outside the cosmos;
- a persistent individual soul;
- a computer substrate.

Those remain unsupported systems or metaphysical mappings.

## 9. Combined source-level model

The bounded source packet supports the following project representation:

`
aggregates/processes not-self
+
conditioned causal sequence
+
intention-linked future renewed existence
+
moral/behavioral consequence
+
liberation not reducible to ordinary postmortem existence categories
+
conditioned/unconditioned distinction
`.

This is not a complete definition of Buddhism.

It is enough to show that a comparison framework centered by default on:
- creator;
- persistent agent object;
- personal judgment;
- afterlife destination

would distort these source propositions.

## 10. Comparison admission

This packet raises confidence for the following neutral axes:

- PERSON_OR_SELF_MODEL
- CAUSAL_ORDER_TYPE
- CONTINUITY_MODEL
- MORAL_CAUSALITY_MODEL
- LIBERATION_OR_FINAL_TRANSFORMATION_MODEL

It does not yet authorize broad claims for:
- creator theology;
- divine/nondivine hierarchy;
- revelation;
- miracles;
- complete cosmology;
- every account of rebirth;
- later Buddhist metaphysics.
