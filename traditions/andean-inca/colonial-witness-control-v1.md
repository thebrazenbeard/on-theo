# Andean / Inca Colonial Witness Control V1

Status: COLONIAL WITNESS DIFFERENTIATION / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Prevent the phrase "the Spanish chronicles say" from collapsing distinct
sixteenth- and seventeenth-century witnesses into one synthetic Inca voice.

Core rule:

COLONIAL_CHRONICLES != ONE_SOURCE.

A second rule:

CHRONICLER_ACCESS != UNMEDIATED_PRECONTACT_TRANSCRIPT.

## 1. Felipe Guaman Poma de Ayala

Witness:
El primer nueva corónica y buen gobierno.

Current physical control:
- autograph manuscript;
- approximately 1,200 pages;
- roughly 400 full-page drawings;
- Spanish with substantial Quechua passages;
- completed ca. 1615/1616;
- now held by the Royal Danish Library.

Guaman Poma writes as an Indigenous Andean colonial author addressing Philip III
and advocating reform of colonial government.

His work preserves:
- Andean historical memory;
- images;
- Quechua terminology;
- Inca/colonial institutions;
- Indigenous critique.

It also operates within:
- Christianity;
- colonial administration;
- universal-history frameworks;
- authorial reform agenda.

Evidence class:
COLONIAL INDIGENOUS MANUSCRIPT / IMAGE PROGRAM / COMMENTARY.

Current rule:

GUAMAN_POMA =
INDIGENOUS_COLONIAL_INTERPRETER_AND_WITNESS.

Not:

UNMEDIATED_PRECONTACT_INCA_TEXT.

## 2. Inca Garcilaso de la Vega

Comentarios reales de los Incas was published in 1609.

Garcilaso explicitly grounds his authority in:
- Quechua competence;
- family/oral memory;
- correspondence;
- reinterpretation/correction of Spanish historians.

Modern scholarship emphasizes:
- philology;
- translation;
- humanist exegesis;
- literary structure;
- classical/Christian intellectual context.

Archaeological/ethnohistorical criticism also warns against accepting his
coherent early-imperial model uncritically.

Evidence class:
COLONIAL MESTIZO HISTORIOGRAPHY / FAMILY MEMORY / COMMENTARY.

Current rule:

GARCILASO =
MAJOR_QUECHUA_INFORMED_RETROSPECTIVE_INTERPRETER.

Not:

DEFAULT_CANONICAL_INCA_HISTORY.

## 3. Pedro de Cieza de León

Cieza:
- Spanish soldier/traveler/chronicler;
- moved widely through the Andes during early colonial decades;
- wrote Crónica del Perú;
- first part published 1553;
- gathered historical/geographical information comparatively early after
  conquest.

Strengths:
- early chronology;
- broad regional observation;
- descriptions of roads, settlements, provincial practices;
- accounts gathered from local people.

Limitations:
- Spanish colonial observer;
- participant in conquest-era institutions;
- translated/interpreted Indigenous information;
- not an Indigenous-language transcript.

Evidence class:
EARLY COLONIAL SPANISH HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION.

## 4. Juan de Betanzos

Betanzos:
- Spanish colonial author;
- learned Quechua to a significant degree;
- served as interpreter;
- wrote Suma y narración de los Incas;
- had unusually close access to Cusco elite Indigenous information.

His witness is especially valuable for:
- ruler narratives;
- Cusco tradition;
- political institutions;
- ritual/history remembered by elite informants.

But:

BETANZOS_SOURCE_NETWORK =
ELITE_CUSCO_CENTERED_AND_COLONIAL.

It cannot stand for:
- all provinces;
- all ethnic groups;
- all Andean traditions.

Evidence class:
EARLY COLONIAL QUECHUA-MEDIATED HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION.

## 5. Cristóbal de Molina

Molina "of Cusco" wrote Relación de las fábulas y ritos de los Incas in the
sixteenth century.

The text is especially valuable for:
- public/imperial ritual;
- creation/origin narratives;
- calendrical rites;
- prayers;
- Cusco ceremonial material.

It is also:
- clerical;
- missionary-era;
- produced under a Christian colonial institutional setting;
- dependent on collected Indigenous testimony and interpretation.

Evidence class:
COLONIAL CLERICAL RITUAL DOCUMENTATION /
HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION.

Current rule:

MOLINA_RITUAL_DETAIL
requires
MISSIONARY_CONTEXT_AND_SOURCE_MEDIATION_VISIBLE.

## 6. Bernabé Cobo

Cobo's Historia del Nuevo Mundo was completed in 1653.

He is substantially later than Cieza, Betanzos, and Molina.

His synthesis is exceptionally important for:
- Cusco huacas;
- ceque system;
- Inca customs/religion;
- historical and natural-history information.

But:

COBO =
SEVENTEENTH_CENTURY_SYNTHESIS_USING_EARLIER_AND_CONTEMPORARY_MATERIAL.

Not:

EARLY_CONQUEST_EYEWITNESS.

The ceque system is strongest when Cobo's descriptions are independently tested
through archaeological and comparative documentary evidence.

Evidence class:
LATER COLONIAL COMMENTARY / HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION.

## 7. Chronicle agreement

When two chroniclers agree, possible explanations include:
- independent preservation of a widespread tradition;
- shared informants/social milieu;
- shared earlier written source;
- copying/intertextual dependence;
- common colonial interpretive categories.

Therefore:

CHRONICLER_AGREEMENT != INDEPENDENT_CONFIRMATION_BY_DEFAULT.

Independence must be argued.

## 8. Chronicle disagreement

Disagreement is not automatically error.

It can preserve:
- regional variants;
- lineage perspectives;
- chronological change;
- political contest;
- different informants;
- translation choices;
- Christian reframing;
- authorial agendas.

Current rule:

VARIANT_COLONIAL_ACCOUNT =
SOURCE_DATA.

Do not harmonize before source criticism.

## 9. Extirpation records

Documents produced during campaigns against Indigenous religious practice may
preserve highly specific evidence for:
- huacas;
- offerings;
- specialists;
- ritual;
- local sacred landscapes.

But their institutional context includes:
- prosecution;
- interrogation;
- suppression;
- Christian classification of "idolatry."

Evidence class:
COLONIAL SUPPRESSION DOCUMENT / HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION.

Therefore:

EXTIRPATION_TESTIMONY != VOLUNTARY_ETHNOGRAPHIC_INTERVIEW.

## 10. Contact/reinterpretation model

The colonial witness corpus itself demonstrates multiple contact mechanisms:
- translation;
- Christian reinterpretation;
- Indigenous critique;
- mestizo philology;
- missionary documentation;
- suppression/interrogation;
- colonial legal/government reform.

Current axis:

CONTACT_AND_REINTERPRETATION_MODEL =
BOUNDED_COLONIAL_MULTI_WITNESS_LAYER.

## 11. Evidence ladder

For an Inca proposition preserved in colonial sources:

1. identify exact chronicler/manuscript;
2. date witness;
3. identify author/social position;
4. identify Indigenous source access where known;
5. separate Quechua/native-language term from Spanish translation;
6. identify Christian/classical/legal framing;
7. test intertextual dependence;
8. compare independent archaeology/material evidence;
9. preserve variants;
10. assign claim ceiling.
