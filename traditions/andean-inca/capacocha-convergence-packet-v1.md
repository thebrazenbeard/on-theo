# Inca Capacocha Material-Text Convergence Packet V1

Status: IMPERIAL SACRIFICE / MATERIAL-TEXT CONVERGENCE CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Bind capacocha/qhapaq hucha through independent archaeological and colonial
evidence while preventing one spectacular ritual from becoming a generic model
of Inca religion.

Core rule:

CAPACOCHA != ALL_INCA_SACRIFICE.

A second rule:

TEXT_MATERIAL_CONVERGENCE != PERFECT_RITUAL_RECONSTRUCTION.

## 1. Archaeological attestation

Capacocha-associated contexts are archaeologically documented at:
- high-altitude mountain shrines;
- selected lower-elevation sites such as Choquepukio;
- locations across multiple parts of the former empire.

Material evidence can include:
- child burials;
- miniature human/animal figures;
- gold/silver objects;
- Spondylus shell;
- Inca ceramics;
- textiles/adornment;
- constructed summit or shrine contexts.

Evidence class:
MATERIAL EVIDENCE / BIOARCHAEOLOGY.

## 2. Colonial descriptions

Multiple colonial sources describe imperial sacrifice involving:
- selected children;
- precious offerings;
- Cusco;
- provincial huacas;
- ruler/state events;
- catastrophes or calendrical/political occasions in selected accounts.

Evidence class:
COLONIAL HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION.

No single colonial account is treated as the canonical rite.

## 3. Choquepukio control

Bioarchaeological work at Choquepukio identified:
- seven child burials;
- high-status Inca objects;
- two individuals with non-local strontium signatures.

Current proposition:

SELECTED_CAPACOCHA_EVENTS_CAN_INVOLVE_NONLOCAL_CHILDREN_AND_IMPERIAL_MATERIALS.

This independently supports colonial claims about movement/selection across
imperial regions.

It does not establish:
- origin of every victim;
- identical route;
- one universal selection rule.

## 4. Mountain-shrine control

High-altitude sites at Misti, Ampato, Llullaillaco and others strongly support:
- mountain/huaca relation;
- imperial movement to sacred peaks;
- high-value offerings;
- selected child sacrifice.

Current axes:

SACRIFICE_AND_BODILY_OFFERING_MODEL =
BOUNDED_INCA_CAPACOCHA.

SACRED_LAND_PLACE_MODEL =
BOUNDED_MOUNTAIN_SHRINE_RELATION.

## 5. Statecraft and sacred order

Archaeological/ethnohistorical scholarship interprets capacocha as linking:
- Cusco;
- ruler/state;
- provincial communities;
- huacas;
- imperial objects;
- selected victims.

Current axis:

POLITICAL_SACRALIZATION_MODEL =
BOUNDED_IMPERIAL_CAPACOCHA_INTEGRATION.

This is not merely:
STATE_PROPAGANDA.

Nor automatically:
LOCAL_VOLUNTARY_DEVOTION.

The precise power relation varied and must remain historically situated.

## 6. Offering and bodily sacrifice must be separate dimensions

Capacocha can include:
- children;
- animals in some accounts/contexts;
- precious objects;
- textiles;
- food/drink;
- miniatures.

Therefore:

SACRIFICE_AND_BODILY_OFFERING_MODEL
and
OFFERING_EXCHANGE_MODEL

overlap but are not identical.

A child victim cannot be analytically flattened into the same class as a ceramic
vessel or shell miniature.

## 7. Mountain huaca hierarchy/integration

Current scholarship on provincial capacocha argues Inca rule could:
- support local huacas;
- rank them;
- connect them to imperial cult;
- adapt royal rites to important local sacred powers.

Current classification:

IMPERIAL_HUACA_INTEGRATION =
BOUNDED_HISTORICAL_RECONSTRUCTION.

Guard:

STATE_SUPPORT_NE_LOCAL_MEANING_ERASURE.

## 8. Pilgrimage/procession

Recent spatial research on high-altitude capacocha emphasizes:
- movement through Andean landscapes;
- approach routes;
- camps/tambos;
- staged access to high peaks.

Current axis:

RITUAL_ORDER_MODEL =
PARTIAL_PILGRIMAGE_PROCESSIONAL_LAYER.

This does not imply every capacocha followed one route architecture.

## 9. Mortuary relation

Sacrificed children are buried in highly structured contexts.

Current axis:

MORTUARY_RELATIONSHIP_MODEL =
PARTIAL_SACRIFICIAL_BURIAL_LAYER.

Not established as one general Inca mortuary model.

Do not infer:
- all child death rites;
- ancestor cult;
- ordinary afterlife destination.

## 10. Material-text convergence schema

Capacocha is a positive control for cross-evidence reconstruction because:
- colonial texts predict selected ritual features;
- archaeology independently recovers corresponding classes;
- isotopes/provenance test movement;
- mountain location tests sacred-place relation.

Current classification:

CAPACOCHA =
STRONG_MULTI_EVIDENCE_CONVERGENCE_CASE.

But each proposition still carries its evidence class.
