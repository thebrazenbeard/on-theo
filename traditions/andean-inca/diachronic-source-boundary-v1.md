# Andean / Inca Diachronic Source Boundary V1

Status: ACTIVE RESEARCH CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Open the Andean/Inca lane without collapsing:
- Tawantinsuyu / Inca imperial religion;
- pre-Inca Andean traditions;
- Quechua-speaking peoples;
- Aymara peoples;
- colonial Indigenous chronicles;
- Spanish missionary/administrative sources;
- living Andean communities.

Core rule:

INCA_IMPERIAL_RELIGION != ALL_ANDEAN_RELIGION.

A second rule:

QUECHUA != INCA_BY_DEFAULT.

## 1. Working labels

### Inca / Inka

Use for:
- the imperial polity of Tawantinsuyu;
- Cusco state institutions;
- imperial-period archaeological contexts;
- rulers, state rituals and state-sponsored sacred landscapes where evidence
  supports imperial attribution.

Do not use "Inca" for all Andean peoples.

### Andean

Use as a broad geographic/cultural comparison umbrella only when:
- multiple regional traditions are explicitly intended;
- source community/period remains visible.

"Andean religion" is not one evidence-bearing unit.

### Quechua

Use for:
- Quechua-language sources;
- living Quechua communities;
- historical communities identified as Quechua-speaking.

Quechua language/community identity is wider than the Inca imperial ruling
system.

### Aymara

Keep distinct from Quechua and Inca unless a source-specific interaction or
shared regional mechanism is being discussed.

## 2. Imperial chronology and historiography

Tawantinsuyu reached maximum imperial expansion shortly before Spanish invasion
in 1532.

Colonial chronicles provide major narratives of:
- rulers;
- conquests;
- rites;
- huacas;
- ceques;
- capacocha;
- political institutions.

But modern archaeology/radiocarbon work has shown that colonial chronological
lists can misorder imperial expansion.

Therefore:

COLONIAL_INCA_CHRONOLOGY != ARCHAEOLOGICAL_CHRONOLOGY_BY_DEFAULT.

## 3. Colonial chroniclers remain separate witnesses

### Guaman Poma

Felipe Guaman Poma de Ayala:
- Indigenous Andean author;
- autograph Nueva corónica y buen gobierno;
- completed ca. 1615/1616;
- written/drawn under colonial Christian conditions;
- critiques Spanish colonial government;
- also interprets Inca/Andean history through Christian universal-history
  frameworks.

Evidence:
COLONIAL INDIGENOUS TEXT / MANUSCRIPT WITNESS / IMAGE PROGRAM /
COMMENTARY.

Guard:

GUAMAN_POMA != UNMEDIATED_PRECONTACT_TRANSCRIPT.

### Inca Garcilaso de la Vega

Mestizo author:
- born 1539;
- published Comentarios reales in 1609;
- drew on family/oral memory and written chronicles;
- shaped Inca history through literary/classical/Christian intellectual forms.

Evidence:
LATER COLONIAL HISTORIOGRAPHY / COMMENTARY / INDIGENOUS-MESTIZO MEMORY.

Guard:

GARCILASO_COHERENT_SYSTEM != EARLY_INCA_FACT_BY_DEFAULT.

### Cieza de León

Early Spanish colonial chronicler:
- traveled widely;
- gathered local accounts;
- published Crónica del Perú in 1553.

Evidence:
EARLY COLONIAL SPANISH HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION.

### Betanzos

Early colonial Spanish chronicler with extensive Cusco/Quechua context and
elite Indigenous informant access.

Evidence:
EARLY COLONIAL HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION.

### Molina

Cristóbal de Molina "el Cuzqueño":
- sixteenth-century clerical account of Inca rites and traditions.

Evidence:
COLONIAL MISSIONARY/CLERICAL DOCUMENTATION.

### Cobo

Bernabé Cobo:
- seventeenth-century Jesuit synthesis;
- later than conquest;
- incorporates earlier written and oral material.

Evidence:
LATER COLONIAL COMMENTARY / HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION.

No chronicler is the canonical Inca voice.

## 4. Extirpation records

Colonial campaigns against "idolatry" generated documents about:
- huacas;
- offerings;
- specialists;
- local rites;
- sacred places.

These are valuable.

But:

EXTIRPATION_RECORD =
COLONIAL_SUPPRESSION_DOCUMENT.

Not:
NEUTRAL_ETHNOGRAPHIC_TRANSCRIPT.

Accusation/interrogation context must remain visible.

## 5. Archaeology

High-value independent evidence includes:
- Cusco ceremonial/urban archaeology;
- ceque/huaca spatial archaeology;
- mountain shrines;
- capacocha burials;
- isotopes;
- textiles/ceramics/miniatures;
- Qhapaq Ñan;
- provincial Inca centers;
- mummies/burials where ethically documented.

Evidence class:
MATERIAL EVIDENCE.

Archaeology can:
- confirm;
- constrain;
- contradict;
- refine
colonial texts.

It does not automatically name ritual meaning.

## 6. Khipu

Khipu are knotted-cord record technologies used extensively by the Inca state
and other Andean peoples.

Secure:
- numerical recording;
- administrative/accounting functions;
- specialized khipukamayuq record keepers.

Current scholarship also investigates:
- categorical coding;
- non-numerical information;
- possible narrative/semasiographic or logo-syllabic dimensions in selected
  corpora.

But:

KHIPU != FULLY_DECIPHERED_ALPHABETIC_WRITING.

No religious proposition is admitted from a khipu unless its semantic reading is
independently controlled.

## 7. Huaca / wak'a

Colonial and archaeological sources use/identify huaca/wak'a for diverse sacred
entities/places/objects.

Potential referents include:
- stones;
- springs;
- caves;
- mountains;
- buildings;
- ancestors/mummies;
- shrines;
- other sacred entities.

Therefore:

HUACA != ONE_OBJECT_TYPE.

And:

HUACA != GENERIC_ENGLISH_"GOD".

Lexical/source packet required.

## 8. Ceque system

Cusco sources describe more than 328 huacas organized along roughly forty-two
ceques/pathways radiating from the Temple of the Sun.

Archaeology has independently identified probable shrine locations/pathways for
parts of the system.

Current status:
HIGH-VALUE RITUAL-LANDSCAPE / SOCIAL-ORGANIZATION EVIDENCE.

Guard:

CUSCO_CEQUE_SYSTEM != ALL_ANDEAN_SACRED_GEOGRAPHY.

## 9. Capacocha / qhapaq hucha

Colonial sources and archaeology independently attest imperial sacrificial
practices involving:
- high-status goods;
- animals and/or children in selected events;
- Cusco;
- provincial/mountain shrines;
- imperial political-religious relations.

Capacocha is a strong material-text convergence lane.

It is not:
ALL_INCA_SACRIFICE.

## 10. Qhapaq Ñan

UNESCO documents:
- Inca imperial road integration;
- pre-Inca antecedents;
- political/economic/ceremonial centers;
- sacred/religious sites;
- current community custodianship and living cultural practices.

Guard:

QHAPAQ_NAN_CONTINUITY != UNCHANGED_INCA_RELIGION.

## 11. Living Andean sources

NMAI and UNESCO publicly document living:
- Quechua/Aymara communities;
- ayni/reciprocity;
- Pachamama and apu offerings in selected communities;
- communal road maintenance;
- land/community relation.

These are current traditions.

They are not automatic survivals of imperial state ritual.

## 12. Comparison hold

No Andean/Inca cell enters synthesis until:
- Inca versus broader Andean scope is explicit;
- source/chronicle is named;
- colonial mediation is explicit;
- archaeology is separated from text;
- living community evidence remains present-tense;
- exact neutral axis is source-admitted.

Current status:

ANDEAN_INCA_CROSS_TRADITION_COMPARISON = HOLD.
