## Purpose

This packet moves the Shinto lane from source-map scaffolding into proposition-level reconstruction while preserving a basic fact of the corpus:

`KOJIKI`
!=
`NIHON_SHOKI`
!=
`ONE_UNIFORM_MYTHIC_ARCHETYPE`.

Kojiki was compiled in 712.

Nihon shoki was compiled at court in 720.

Both are evidence for their own compilation environments and for traditions they preserve.

Neither is treated as an unfiltered transcript of prehistoric belief.

## 1. Why Nihon shoki variants matter

The Divine Age material in Nihon shoki explicitly preserves:
- a primary account;
- multiple alternative accounts.

Kokugakuin's source discussion treats this structure as the result of compilation from multiple traditions.

Therefore:

`ALTERNATIVE_WRITING`
is not
`ERROR_TO_HARMONIZE`.

The variants are positive evidence that more than one narrative form circulated or was available to the compilers.

## 2. Izanagi / Izanami and the birth of the major kami

### Kojiki pattern

Kojiki places:
- Izanami's death after birth of the fire kami;
- Izanagi's journey to Yomi;
- Izanagi's return;
- purification/misogi;
- birth of Amaterasu, Tsukuyomi, and Susanoo from that purification.

### Nihon shoki main-text pattern

The main Nihon shoki account instead has Izanagi and Izanami jointly produce major kami including:
- the sun kami;
- the moon kami;
- Susanoo.

### Nihon shoki alternate pattern

At least one alternate writing brings the sequence closer to Kojiki:
- Izanami dies;
- Izanagi returns from Yomi;
- purification produces the three major kami.

### Comparison consequence

The proposition:

`AMATERASU_WAS_BORN_FROM_IZANAGI'S_PURIFICATION`

is not a source-neutral proposition for all classical Shinto mythic witnesses.

It is:
- supported by Kojiki;
- supported by at least one Nihon shoki alternate account;
- not the only Nihon shoki account.

## 3. Tsukuyomi variants

Tsukuyomi is a useful variant-control case.

Across the classical accounts, Tsukuyomi may be:
- born through Izanagi's purification;
- produced jointly with the sun deity;
- produced through a mirror in an alternate Nihon shoki account;
- assigned different domains in different passages.

Kojiki gives little further narrative role after birth.

An alternate Nihon shoki account preserves the food-deity episode involving Ukemochi and the separation of sun and moon.

### Guard

`TSUKUYOMI_ONE_FIXED_BIOGRAPHY`
= false at current source depth.

## 4. Land-generation variants

Kojiki and Nihon shoki preserve differing island sequences in the Great-Eight-Island material.

Nihon shoki's alternative writings themselves vary in:
- island order;
- which islands are counted;
- handling of Onogoro;
- details of the first malformed or preliminary offspring.

Source:
https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9162

### Comparison consequence

The land-generation material should be modeled as:

`VARIANT_GENEALOGICAL_COSMOGRAPHY`

not one fixed canonical map.

## 5. Izanami and Yomi

Both classical traditions preserve:
- Izanami's death;
- Izanagi's encounter with the realm of death;
- taboo violation;
- pollution associated with death;
- separation between the pair.

But details differ across accounts.

Kojiki explicitly calls Izanami the great kami of Yomi after the episode.

Nihon shoki preserves different location and genealogy details in alternative writings.

### Source consequence

The Yomi cycle is eligible for:
- POSTMORTEM_PATH_MODEL;
- PURITY_POLLUTION_MODEL;
- TRANSFORMATION_MODEL;
- PERSON_OR_SELF_MODEL only with caution.

It is not yet a source for:
- a complete Shinto afterlife doctrine;
- a universal doctrine of souls;
- one fixed map of the dead.

## 6. Amaterasu's origin and authority

Amaterasu is a major example of source convergence plus variation.

Across accounts:
- solar rulership is central;
- celestial authority is central;
- relation to the imperial lineage is central in the compiled classical mythology.

But birth details differ:
- purification;
- joint birth by Izanagi and Izanami;
- mirror-related alternate account.

### Guard

`SHARED_ROLE`
does not entail
`SHARED_ORIGIN_NARRATIVE`.

## 7. Kami is not a translation-equivalent of one English category

The classical corpus uses `kami` for a wide range of beings/presences.

Later Shinto scholarship has repeatedly resisted defining kami through one simple etymology.

Motoori Norinaga's influential inductive definition included:
- classical heavenly and earthly kami;
- shrine spirits;
- humans;
- animals;
- plants;
- seas;
- mountains;
- things that inspire unusual awe through exceptional qualities.

This is later interpretation of the older corpus, not the direct meaning of every ancient use.

Source:
https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9958

## 8. Current classical-source model

The source-controlled result is not "Shinto says X."

It is:

- court-compiled classical mythic corpora preserve overlapping but non-identical narratives;
- Nihon shoki intentionally records variant traditions;
- divine genealogies, cosmic sequences, and origins differ across witnesses;
- ritual and imperial-political concerns are interwoven with mythic narration.

This makes Shinto a strong test of whether ON_THEO can compare traditions without pretending internal plurality is textual noise.
