## 1. Evidence layer

The ritual lane is not inferred from mythology alone.

A major textual corpus is the set of norito preserved in Book 8 of Engishiki.

Engishiki was compiled in 927.

The corpus contains twenty-seven norito associated with:
- court rites;
- seasonal rites;
- shrine rites;
- purification;
- harvest;
- palace protection;
- Ise;
- extraordinary circumstances.

Kokugakuin's source summary notes that individual norito have different compositional histories and some preserve material older than the 927 compilation.

Source:
https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=8632

## 2. Norito as ritual speech

Norito are ritual declarations/recitations used in ceremonies involving kami.

They are not merely doctrinal essays.

This creates a separate evidence mode:

`RITUAL_SPEECH`.

A statement in a norito may reveal:
- ritual roles;
- invoked kami;
- offerings;
- categories of impurity/transgression;
- desired outcomes;
- political or agricultural concerns.

It does not automatically reveal a complete metaphysical doctrine.

## 3. Great Purification

The Great Purification norito is associated with formal purification rites carried out at regular calendrical intervals.

Kokugakuin's harae and haraedo entries preserve distinctions among:
- `tsumi`;
- `kegare`;
- `harae`;
- `misogi`.

These terms overlap historically but are not exact synonyms.

### Kegare

Kegare is treated as a polluted/defiled condition.

Kokugakuin distinguishes it from human transgression in at least one common explanatory scheme:
- tsumi often involves action/transgression;
- kegare may arise through conditions such as death and other polluting circumstances.

Source:
https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=8664

### Harae

Harae is ritual purification/removal of impurity or transgression.

Source:
https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=8732

### Misogi

Misogi is ablution/purification involving washing.

The classical mythic precedent is Izanagi's purification after returning from Yomi.

Source:
https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=8723

## 4. Haraedo kami

The Great Purification norito names four purification kami:
- Seoritsuhime;
- Hayaakitsuhime;
- Ibukidonushi;
- Hayasasurahime.

Kokugakuin notes that these four names do not appear in Kojiki or Nihon shoki.

Later scholars proposed identifications with kami in the mythic corpora, but those are later interpretive harmonizations.

Source:
https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9449

## 7. Ritual efficacy and agency

Norito and purification also pressure the existing `INTERVENTION_MODEL`.

Some ritual systems do not center on:
- a sovereign deity choosing whether to intervene.

Instead, efficacy may be represented through:
- correct ritual performance;
- correct words;
- offerings;
- purified status;
- authorized officiants;
- appropriate time/place.

This justifies keeping:

`RITUAL_MEDIATION`

separate from:
