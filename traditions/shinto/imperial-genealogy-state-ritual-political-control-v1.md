`POLITICAL_SACRALIZATION_MODEL` from exact evidence while preserving the

1. court-compiled mythic genealogy;

## 1. Nihon shoki descent command

The `Nihon shoki`, compiled at court in 720 CE, preserves a descent narrative
in which Amaterasu gives Ninigi the three treasures, sends divine attendants,
and commands her descendant to proceed to the terrestrial realm and govern it.

In W. G. Aston's translation, the command explicitly connects:
- rule of the land;
- descent from Amaterasu;
- dynastic prosperity and continuity.

Primary-text access:
- `Nihongi`, Book II:
  https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Nihongi/Book_II
- page image / Aston translation:
  https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Nihongi_by_Aston.djvu/108

Evidence class:
- PRIMARY TEXT: court-compiled classical chronicle;
- MODERN/OLDER TRANSLATION ACCESS SURFACE: Aston.

### Witness / layer guard

`NIHON_SHOKI_720_COURT_COMPILATION`
!=
`UNFILTERED_PREHISTORIC_BELIEF`.

The passage directly supports the proposition that the court chronicle encodes
political authority through celestial genealogy. It does not prove that all
earlier communities held one identical doctrine.

## 2. Scholarly mythic control

Kokugakuin's Encyclopedia of Shinto identifies the Ninigi descent traditions
across the Kiki and other corpora and describes the Nihon shoki version as the
most politically developed form, including the divine treasures and commands.

Source:
https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=8746

A comparative study of Japanese descent myths likewise identifies Ninigi's
descent as a major element of the official early myth system emphasizing the
heavenly origin of the imperial family line.

Source:
https://asianethnology.org/downloads/ae/pdf/a491.pdf

Evidence class:
- SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION.

## 3. State ritual / administrative layer

The political-sacralization model is not limited to mythic ancestry.

Kokugakuin's historical synthesis of ancient Shinto describes:
- a developing principle of close relation between ritual and rule;
- establishment of the Department of Divinities / Jingikan;
- instructions to provincial governors concerning rites and shrine upkeep;
- institutionalization of national kami rites and court ritual;
- development of enthronement-related ritual in the late seventh and eighth
  centuries.

Source:
https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9112

Kokugakuin's institutional overview further describes the ancient shrine system
as closely connected to national and imperial rites and identifies structures
for imperial administration of major shrines.

Source:
https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=8966

Evidence class:
- DOCUMENTARY / INSTITUTIONAL HISTORY through scholarly synthesis;
- HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION.

## 4. Political-sacralization disposition

The current Shinto lane supports two distinct mechanisms:

### A. Genealogical-celestial legitimation

`AMATERASU_DESCENT_LINEAGE -> AUTHORITY_TO_GOVERN`

This is directly represented in the selected court-compiled mythic source.

### B. Court-administered cultic order

`POLITICAL_INSTITUTION -> KAMI_RITUAL_ADMINISTRATION`

This is historically supported for the ritsuryo / early Heian state layer.

These mechanisms are related but not identical.

Current axis status:

`POLITICAL_SACRALIZATION_MODEL = BOUNDED_COURT_MYTH_AND_STATE_RITUAL_LAYER`.

## 5. Non-admissions

This packet does not establish:
- one timeless Shinto political theology;
- that every local shrine cult derived from imperial genealogy;
- that the Nihon shoki narrative is a transparent prehistoric transcript;
- that mythic divine descent proves historical or biological descent;
- that state administration of cult means doctrinal uniformity;
- uninterrupted identity between ancient court institutions and any modern
  political order.

## 6. Comparison consequence

The Shinto lane is now eligible for bounded comparison with other political
sacralization mechanisms.

Its strongest neutral description is:

`GENEALOGICAL_CELESTIAL_LEGITIMATION`
+
`COURT_ADMINISTERED_CULTIC_ORDER`.

This differs from:
- Sumerian divine grant of kingship in controlled literary traditions;
- Vedic ritual legitimation/consolidation of sovereignty;
- Daoist religious office functioning as territorial governance.
