# Shinto — Buddhist Interaction Boundary V1

Status: DIAChRONIC CONTACT CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Buddhist-Shinto interaction is historically central.

It must not be used to:
- project medieval combinatory doctrine into the earliest classical mythic texts;
- pretend pre-Buddhist kami practice was culturally isolated from all continental influence;
- define "pure Shinto" as the default historical state.

## 1. Buddhist arrival and combination

Buddhism entered Japan in the sixth century.

Kokugakuin's historical summaries describe progressive combination of kami and Buddhist institutions beginning well before the medieval period.

By the medieval period, `honji suijaku` became a major explanatory model.

Sources:
- https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=8945
- https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=8529

## 2. Honji suijaku

In honji suijaku theory:
- Buddhist deities are the `honji`, original ground;
- kami appear as `suijaku`, manifested traces.

This is a historically specific doctrinal solution to kami-buddha relation.

It is not:
- the semantic meaning of kami in Kojiki;
- proof that early kami were originally understood as Buddhist beings;
- the only medieval combinatory model.

## 3. Material effects of combination

Combinatory practice affected:
- shrine-temple complexes;
- sutra recitation at shrines;
- priestly roles;
- imagery;
- divine identifications.

Wooden images of kami appear in a cultural environment shaped partly by Buddhist art.

Source:
https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9962

## 4. Meiji separation is also historical

The Meiji government's shinbutsu bunri policies formally promoted separation of kami and buddhas after centuries of combination.

Kokugakuin explicitly describes the prior system as deeply rooted and widespread.

Source:
https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=8851

### Guard

`MEIJI_SEPARATION`
!=
`RESTORATION_OF_A_SINGLE_UNCHANGED_PRIMORDIAL_SHINTO`.

Modern separation is itself a historical intervention.

## 5. Comparison consequence

The Shinto lane requires an explicit:

`CONTACT_AND_REINTERPRETATION_MODEL`.

Question:

> How does contact with another religious/intellectual tradition alter names, categories, institutions, iconography, ritual, and metaphysical explanation without necessarily replacing the earlier system?

This is broader than simple "syncretism."

It can represent:
- translation;
- identification;
- hierarchy;
- accommodation;
- polemic;
- institutional fusion;
- later separation.
