# Diné Sacred Land and Mountain Public Packet V1

Status: PUBLIC SACRED-GEOGRAPHY / CURRENT LAW CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Bind Diné sacred-land relations from public Navajo Nation sources while avoiding
sensitive site inventories, restricted ceremonial geography, and false
simplification of the four/six sacred mountain formulations.

Core rule:

PUBLIC_SACRED_GEOGRAPHY != PERMISSION_TO_MAP_SENSITIVE_SITES.

## 1. Four boundary mountains

Multiple current/public Navajo Nation sources describe Diné Bikéyah through four
principal sacred mountains associated with the cardinal directions:
- Sisnaajini / eastern boundary;
- Tsoodził / southern boundary;
- Dook'o'oosłííd / western boundary;
- Dibé Nitsaa / northern boundary.

Public contexts include:
- Navajo Nation agriculture;
- Navajo Nation flag/history;
- treaty/public history;
- Human Rights sacred-sites documentation.

Current bounded proposition:

FOUR_SACRED_MOUNTAINS =
PUBLIC_CARDINAL_BOUNDARY_FRAME_OF_DINE_BIKEYAH.

This is public institutional geography.

## 2. Six sacred mountains in Fundamental Law

Diné Natural Law explicitly names six sacred mountains:
- the four cardinal/boundary mountains above;
- Dził Na'oodiłii;
- Dził Ch'ool'í'í.

Navajo Nation public sacred-sites materials describe the latter two as
additional significant sacred mountains located within the boundaries of the
four cardinal mountains.

Therefore:

FOUR_BOUNDARY_MOUNTAINS
and
SIX_SACRED_MOUNTAINS

are not treated as mutually exclusive claims.

Current project model:

SIX_MOUNTAIN_PUBLIC_SACRED_GEOGRAPHY =
FOUR_CARDINAL_BOUNDARY_MOUNTAINS
+
TWO_ADDITIONAL_INTERNAL_SIGNIFICANT_MOUNTAINS.

This is a bounded synthesis of public Navajo Nation sources.

## 3. Sacred mountains as relations, not map markers

Fundamental Law treats the mountains as:
- deserving respect and protection;
- leaders/foundations of the Navajo Nation;
- embedded in duties toward creation.

Public Human Rights material describes sacred mountains as living beings and
connects continuing relation to land with Diné identity.

Current axis:

SACRED_LAND_PLACE_MODEL =
BOUNDED_RELATIONAL_SACRED_GEOGRAPHY.

Guard:

SACRED_MOUNTAIN != SYMBOLIC_BORDER_ONLY.

The public legal/traditional relation is ethical, spiritual, social, and
political.

## 4. Diné Bikéyah

Navajo Nation public sources use Diné Bikéyah for homeland/land relation.

Current propositions include:
- geography and identity are linked;
- sacred mountain boundaries are spiritually significant;
- sovereignty and land stewardship are connected;
- agriculture/livestock and contemporary livelihood remain part of living land
  relation.

Current classification:

DINE_BIKEYAH =
RELATIONAL_HOMELAND_CATEGORY_IN_CURRENT_PUBLIC_SOURCES.

Not:
PROPERTY_OWNERSHIP_ONLY.

## 5. Traditional Cultural Properties

Navajo Nation HHPD policy recognizes traditional cultural properties including
publicly described categories such as:
- sacred places;
- prayer-offering places;
- places associated with origin traditions;
- plant/mineral/material gathering places;
- graves;
- ceremonial locations;
- other culturally significant landscapes.

A place may be culturally significant even without visible archaeological
evidence.

This is an important method correction:

MATERIAL_TRACE_ABSENT != SACRED_PLACE_ABSENT.

## 6. Sensitive-location hold

HHPD also maintains highly sensitive cultural-resource and geographic data.

Therefore ON_THEO stores:

SENSITIVE_SITE_COORDINATES =
PROHIBITED_RESEARCH_TARGET.

The project will not:
- compile hidden sites;
- reconstruct private routes;
- publish sensitive GIS layers;
- infer confidential locations from indirect sources.

## 7. Sacred elements and land

Natural Law publicly connects:
- air;
- light/fire;
- water;
- earth/pollen;
- mountains;
- animals;
- plants;
- Mother Earth/Father Sky;
- Diné responsibility.

Current result:

SACRED_LAND_PLACE_MODEL
must include
RELATION_AMONG_PLACE_ELEMENTS_LIVING_BEINGS_AND_HUMAN_DUTY.

This is broader than "holy sites."

## 8. Respect, protocol, offering

Natural Law publicly states that use of:
- sacred elements;
- land;
- natural resources;
- sacred sites;
- living beings

must follow proper respect/protocol and offering.

Current axis:

OFFERING_EXCHANGE_MODEL =
PARTIAL_RELATIONAL_PROTOCOL.

What is secure:
- offering/protocol exists publicly as part of lawful respectful relation.

What is not:
- one item;
- one recipient;
- one requested result;
- one reciprocity formula.

## 9. Future generations

Public law repeatedly makes future generations part of land/resource duty.

Current proposition:

INTERGENERATIONAL_OBLIGATION_IS_PART_OF_DINE_PUBLIC_SACRED_LAND_ORDER.

This strengthens:

RELATIONAL_ORDER_MODEL.

It also distinguishes sacred land from a purely present-tense human-use model.

## 11. Origin narratives remain bounded

Public Nation sources mention Diné creation/origin narratives in relation to:
- sacred mountains;
- homeland;
- Holy People;
- movement/emergence.

But Navajo Nation policy also explicitly protects culturally sensitive
narratives and ceremonial knowledge.

Therefore:

HUMAN_ORIGIN_OR_EMERGENCE_MODEL =
HOLD.

WORLD_ORIGIN_MODEL =
HOLD.

The project does not need a detailed origin packet to compare sacred-land
relations.
