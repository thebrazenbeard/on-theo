# Diné Public-Source and Restricted-Knowledge Boundary V1

Status: ACTIVE COMMUNITY-AUTHORITY RESEARCH CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Establish the evidence and ethics boundary for ON_THEO research on Diné
religious, philosophical, legal, ceremonial, land, and knowledge traditions.

Core rule:

PUBLICLY_ACCESSIBLE != CULTURALLY_UNRESTRICTED.

A second rule:

DINE_PUBLIC_LAW_AND_EDUCATION != COMPLETE_DINE_CEREMONIAL_KNOWLEDGE.

## 1. Naming

Preferred project label:
DINÉ.

"Navajo" remains necessary when:
- quoting institutional/legal names such as Navajo Nation;
- identifying public agencies;
- tracking historical sources that use the exonym;
- describing external legal/documentary history.

The project does not treat "Navajo" and "Diné" as evidence-neutral stylistic
variants when source self-identification matters.

## 2. Highest-authority public source classes

### Navajo Nation law and courts

High-value for:
- publicly codified Fundamental Laws;
- current sovereignty/governance;
- k'é;
- natural law;
- public land/sacred-place obligations;
- current legal articulation of tradition.

Evidence class:
DOCUMENTARY PRACTICE / MODERN TRADITION / CURRENT INDIGENOUS LAW.

Critical guard:

CURRENT_DINE_FUNDAMENTAL_LAW != VERBATIM_PRECONTACT_CODE.

It is current sovereign legal articulation grounded in Diné traditional,
customary, natural, and common law.

### Diné College

High-value for:
- community-controlled educational philosophy;
- Sa'ah Naagháíí Bik'eh Hózhóón;
- current Diné language/culture teaching;
- institutional transmission;
- public values and worldview.

Evidence class:
COMMUNITY EDUCATIONAL SELF-DESCRIPTION / MODERN TRADITION.

### Navajo Nation Heritage and Historic Preservation Department

Highest authority for:
- cultural-resource handling;
- traditional cultural properties;
- sacred-place protection;
- confidentiality;
- ethnographic-research restrictions;
- culturally sensitive knowledge governance.

Evidence class:
DOCUMENTARY PRACTICE / INDIGENOUS RESEARCH-GOVERNANCE POLICY.

## 3. Nation-published restricted-knowledge rule

Navajo Nation cultural-resource policy publicly states that:
- sacred and ceremonial information is held in trust;
- some sacred stories, songs, sandpaintings, ceremonial knowledge, and sacred
  place information are not intended for unrestricted permanent recording;
- some kinds of information should never be collected or disseminated;
- some information is restricted by season, age, maturity, gender, or other
  cultural conditions;
- ethnographic collection requires consultation/permits and informed consent;
- confidential material requires controlled handling.

ON_THEO adopts the stricter rule:

IF_NAVAJO_NATION_POLICY_CLASSIFIES_INFORMATION_AS_SENSITIVE_RESTRICTED_OR_NOT_FOR_GENERAL_RECORDING
THEN
ON_THEO_DOES_NOT_SEEK_RECONSTRUCT_OR_REPUBLISH_IT.

This applies even if:
- an older ethnographer published it;
- a third-party website reposted it;
- a scanned book is publicly accessible.

## 4. Restricted knowledge is not a research gap

The project explicitly classifies:

CULTURALLY_RESTRICTED_BY_SOURCE_COMMUNITY

as a legitimate stopping condition.

It is not:
UNKNOWN_DUE_TO_INADEQUATE_RESEARCH.

Therefore:

RESTRICTED_KNOWLEDGE != MISSING_DATA_TO_BE_FILLED.

This is especially important for:
- ceremonial procedure;
- songs/prayers;
- sandpainting content;
- sacred bundles/paraphernalia;
- specialist knowledge;
- sensitive site locations;
- seasonal teachings.

## 5. Sacred-site location control

The Navajo Heritage and Historic Preservation Department maintains:
- traditional cultural property protections;
- culturally sensitive location data;
- archaeological/sacred-site controls.

ON_THEO may discuss:
- publicly named major sacred mountains;
- public legal principles concerning sacred sites;
- publicly authorized general geography.

ON_THEO will not:
- collect sensitive coordinates;
- reproduce restricted sacred-site inventories;
- infer hidden site locations from reports;
- expose confidential GIS/cultural-resource data.

## 6. Public ceremony category versus procedure

Public Navajo Nation sources may establish:
- that ceremonial systems exist;
- their role in health, balance, education, or community continuity;
- public practitioner/apprenticeship structures.

That does NOT authorize:
- procedural reconstruction;
- prayer/song transcription;
- sandpainting reproduction;
- sequencing of restricted ceremonial acts.

Public existence != public procedure.

## 7. Older ethnography

Older Navajo/Diné ethnography can preserve:
- terminology;
- historical context;
- participant accounts;
- colonial/contact documentation.

But it can also violate current Diné knowledge-governance norms.

Therefore:

OLDER_ETHNOGRAPHIC_PUBLICATION != CURRENT_PERMISSION_TO_REPUBLISH_CONTENT.

When needed, ON_THEO may record:
- that a source exists;
- that it made a claim;
- source history;
- methodological controversy

without reproducing restricted details.

## 8. Christian, Native American Church, and other contact layers

Diné religious history includes multiple contact traditions and institutions.

These require separate source lanes.

Do not flatten them into:
SYNCRETISM.

Potential mechanisms:
- conversion;
- coexistence;
- translation;
- selective participation;
- institutional contact;
- legal suppression/protection;
- reinterpretation.

## 9. Comparison hold

No Diné proposition enters cross-tradition synthesis until:
- source authority is explicit;
- public/restricted status is reviewed;
- modern legal/educational formulations are not reverse-projected unchanged;
- exact neutral axis is source-admitted.

Current status:

DINE_CROSS_TRADITION_COMPARISON = HOLD.
