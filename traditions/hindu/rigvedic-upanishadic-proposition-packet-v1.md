## 1. Ṛgveda 10.129 — Nāsadīya hymn

Source subject:
- Ṛgveda, Śākala Saṃhitā;
- maṇḍala 10;
- sūkta 129;
- seven verses.

Primary Sanskrit access:
- Vedic Heritage Portal:
  https://vedicheritage.gov.in/hi/samhitas/rigveda/shakala-samhita/rigveda-shakala-samhita-mandal-10-sukta-129/

### Source-level propositions

Verse 1 does not begin with a straightforward creator-agent story.

It says that at the relevant beginning there was neither `asat` nor `sat`
in the formulation used by the hymn, and asks about space, covering, location,
and deep water.

Verse 2 denies, for that state:
- death;
- immortality;
- the usual distinction of night and day.

It then describes "that one" in difficult language whose exact interpretation
requires philological control.

Verse 3 presents darkness hidden by darkness and an undifferentiated or
unmanifest condition, followed by emergence associated with `tapas`.

Verse 4 introduces `kāma` (desire) as an early/first seed associated with mind
and says the poets/sages, searching in their hearts, discerned a relation or
bond between `sat` and `asat`.

Verses 6–7 are epistemically decisive:
- the hymn asks who truly knows the origin of the emergence/creation;
- it says the gods are later than the relevant coming-forth;
- therefore the gods cannot simply function as prior eyewitnesses in the
  rhetorical argument;
- the final verse says the highest overseer may know — or may not know.

### Scholarly interpretation

Joel Brereton's 1999 study, "Edifying Puzzlement: Ṛgveda 10.129 and the Uses of
Enigma," argues that the unresolved final question is not an accidental defect.
On his reading, it calls into question the possibility of a final cosmogonic
narrative and makes the audience's reflective activity part of the hymn's
function.

Classification:
`SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION`.

It is not silently promoted to the only possible interpretation of the hymn.

### Comparison effect

This locus supports:
- `KNOWLEDGE_OR_DISCLOSURE_MODEL`: explicit limit/questioning about ultimate
  origin knowledge;
- `CAUSAL_ORDER_TYPE`: origin architecture remains unresolved/questioned at
  this locus;
- `WORLD_ORIGIN_MODEL`: useful future neutral axis candidate.

This locus does **not** support:
- one final Hindu creation doctrine;
- atheism;
- a creator God doctrine;
- simulation theory;
- later Brahman/ātman doctrine read backward into the hymn;
- the proposition that "the Vedas say nobody knows anything."

### Guards

`RV_10_129`
!=
`ALL_RIGVEDIC_COSMOGONY`.

`EPISTEMIC_UNCERTAINTY`
!=
`ATHEISM`.

`THAT_ONE`
!=
`PERSONAL_CREATOR_GOD`
without further argument.

`SAT_ASAT_LANGUAGE`
!=
`LATER_VEDANTA_ONTOLOGY`
without transmission/semantic reconstruction.

## 2. Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.4.5 — action, desire, resolve, becoming

Source subject:
- Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad;
- chapter 4;
- section 4;
- passage 5;
- recension control required because Bṛhadāraṇyaka survives in Mādhyaṃdina and
  Kāṇva recensions.

Institutional source context:
- Vedic Heritage Portal identifies both Mādhyaṃdina and Kāṇva recensions and
  places Bṛhadāraṇyaka in the Śukla Yajurveda / Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa textual
  complex.

### Source-level propositions

The passage connects conduct/action with what a person becomes.

Its sequence includes the proposition that:
- good action/conduct is associated with becoming good;
- harmful/bad action is associated with becoming bad;
- a person is described in relation to desire;
- desire conditions `kratu` (resolve/intention/determination in translation);
- `kratu` conditions action;
- action conditions what is attained/becomes.

A compact source abstraction is:

`KĀMA -> KRATU -> KARMA -> ABHISAMPATTI / OUTCOME`.

That is a project abstraction of the textual chain, not a claim that the source
uses modern causal-diagram theory.

### Comparison effect

This locus supports:
- `MORAL_CAUSALITY_MODEL`;
- `PERSON_OR_SELF_MODEL` in a limited action/becoming sense;
- `CONTINUITY_MODEL`, when read inside the death/rebirth discussion of the
  surrounding section.

It is significant that the immediate causal language is not merely a decree by
an external judge.

### Ceiling

This passage does not establish:
- one complete Hindu theory of karma;
- one complete theory of rebirth;
- that all Hindu schools interpret `ātman`, `kāma`, `kratu`, or `karma`
  identically;
- that moral causality is purely impersonal in every Hindu source;
- a deterministic modern state-transition function.

`BU_4_4_5`
!=
`ALL_HINDU_KARMA`.

`KRATU`
!=
`MODERN_WILLPOWER`
without lexical qualification.

`KARMA`

## 3. Chāndogya Upaniṣad 5.10.7 — conduct and differentiated rebirth

Source subject:
- Chāndogya Upaniṣad;
- 5.10.7.

The passage distinguishes people of:
- good/pleasant conduct;
- bad/foul conduct;

and associates them with different subsequent births.

The examples in the transmitted passage include:
- Brāhmaṇa;
- Kṣatriya;
- Vaiśya;

for favored births, and:
- dog;
- pig;
- Caṇḍāla/outcaste;

for disfavored births.

### Comparison effect

This locus supports a stronger source-specific connection among:
- conduct;
- moral valuation;
- post-mortem continuity;
- differentiated rebirth outcome.

Therefore it supports:
- `MORAL_CAUSALITY_MODEL`;
- `CONTINUITY_MODEL`;
- `SOCIAL_COSMOLOGY_MODEL` as a future axis candidate, because social hierarchy
  appears inside the stated rebirth outcomes.

### Critical historical guard

The social hierarchy encoded by the source is itself historical evidence.

The project must not:
- sanitize it out of the source;
- universalize it into every Hindu tradition;
- convert description of the text into project endorsement.

### Ceiling

`CHU_5_10_7`
does not by itself establish:
- every mechanism of karma;
- every route of rebirth;
- one timeless Hindu caste doctrine;
- one universal Hindu afterlife model.

## 4. Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.8.7 — subtle essence, self, and `tat tvam asi`

Source subject:
- Chāndogya Upaniṣad;
- 6.8.7;
- Uddālaka's instruction to Śvetaketu.

The surrounding chapter develops an argument from:
- `sat` / the existent;
- living things and their root;
- subtle or finest essence;
- `ātman` / self.

The recurrent refrain contains:

`tat tvam asi, śvetaketo`.

### Translation / interpretation dispute

A traditional rendering is:

`that thou art / you are that`.

Joel Brereton, followed in translation by Patrick Olivelle, argues that the
Vedic grammar is better rendered adverbially:

`that's how you are / in that way are you, Śvetaketu`.

On that reading, the refrain does not grammatically function as a simple
identity equation between "you" and "that."

Other scholars and traditional Vedānta interpretation defend or preserve the
identity reading or its philosophical force.

### Project rule

The repository must therefore store at least two layers:

1. `PRIMARY_TEXT_FORMULA`:
   `tat tvam asi`.

2. `TRANSLATION_INTERPRETATION`:
   competing grammatical/philosophical readings.

Do not silently replace the primary phrase with either later doctrinal outcome.

### Source-level propositions safe at current depth

The passage directly links:
- a subtle/finest essence;
- the self (`ātman`);
- truth/reality language;
- Śvetaketu through the disputed refrain.

The passage should not yet be reduced to the simple project proposition:

`INDIVIDUAL_SELF = UNIVERSAL_BRAHMAN`.

That is a major later/traditional Vedāntic reading, but proposition-level
historical work must distinguish:
- the Chāndogya passage;
- its grammar;
- later Vedānta exegesis.

### Comparison effect

This locus is eligible for:
- `PERSON_OR_SELF_MODEL`;
- `WORLD_STATUS_MODEL`;
- `CAUSAL_ORDER_TYPE`;

but only with a visible interpretation dispute.

It is not yet eligible as a single settled statement of:
- monism;
- Advaita;
- creator theology;
- simulation substrate.

### Guards

`TAT_TVAM_ASI`
!=
`ONE_UNDISPUTED_ENGLISH_PROPOSITION`.

`CHU_6_8_7`
!=
`MATURE_ADVAITA_VEDANTA`.

`ATMAN`
!=
`WESTERN_SOUL`

## 5. Diachronic contrast

These sources should not be concatenated into a synthetic "Hindu answer."

At minimum the current packet contains:

### Ṛgvedic hymn
An origin inquiry that culminates in radical epistemic uncertainty.

### Bṛhadāraṇyaka
A strong action/desire/resolve/becoming chain inside an early Upaniṣadic
discussion of self, death, and continuity.

### Chāndogya 5.10.7
A concrete conduct-to-rebirth hierarchy.

### Chāndogya 6.8.7
A self/essence formula with a consequential translation and reception dispute.

The diversity is itself source data.
