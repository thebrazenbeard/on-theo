# Hindu Traditions — Early Upaniṣadic Continuity Context V1

Status: CONTEXT EXPANSION / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

The first Hindu proposition packet intentionally used narrow loci.

This packet expands two of those loci into their immediate textual neighborhoods
so the project does not build a comparison model from isolated aphorisms.

Targets:
- Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.4.2–6;
- Chāndogya Upaniṣad 5.3–10, with immediate emphasis on 5.10.

## 1. Bṛhadāraṇyaka 4.4.2–6 — death, transfer, action, desire, return

### 4.4.2 — departure sequence

The passage describes the dying process in terms of the senses/functions
becoming gathered and the self departing.

The translation tradition used in the current access surface says that:
- vital force follows the departing self;
- the organs/functions follow;
- the self goes with a particular consciousness;
- knowledge, action/work, and prior experience accompany it.

### Source ceiling

This is a source model of continuity across death.

It is not automatically:
- a Cartesian soul;
- a modern information object;
- a software state vector;
- a universal Hindu doctrine.

### 4.4.3 — caterpillar analogy

The text compares the transition to a caterpillar:
- reaching the end of one support;
- taking hold of another;
- drawing itself across.

### Comparison consequence

The image supports continuity across embodiments more directly than BU 4.4.5
alone.

Neutral abstraction:

`OLD_EMBODIED_STATE -> TRANSITION -> NEW_EMBODIED_STATE`.

That abstraction is downstream and deliberately says nothing yet about the exact
metaphysical substance of the continuing self.

### 4.4.4 — goldsmith analogy

The next image compares the transition to a goldsmith reshaping material into a
newer form.

### Comparison consequence

The paired analogies suggest continuity with transformation, not merely:
- total numerical replacement;
- unchanged bodily persistence.

The source itself remains primary; a modern identity-theory solution is not
inserted.

### 4.4.5 — conduct, desire, resolve, action, becoming

The prior packet bound the chain:

`kāma -> kratu -> karma -> outcome/becoming`.

Placed after the transition analogies, this chain now has stronger textual
context for `CONTINUITY_MODEL`.

### 4.4.6 — attachment, action, return, desirelessness

The passage links:
- attachment;
- action/work;
- the object/state toward which mind is attached;
- reaching the end/result of action;
- return from that world to this world for further action.

It then contrasts the desiring/transmigrating condition with the person without
desire.

### Comparison effect

The current Bṛhadāraṇyaka unit supports a bounded composite proposition:

`
death_transition
+
continuity_into_new_form
+
knowledge/action/experience_carried
+
desire_and_action_condition_outcome
+
return_for_further_action
`.

This is substantially stronger than treating 4.4.5 as a detached ethical maxim.

### Critical guard

The current English access surfaces include later commentarial interpretation.

Therefore:
- exact Sanskrit/recension collation remains required;
- primary Upaniṣad text must be separated from Śaṅkara's later commentary;
- metaphysical conclusions attributed to Advaita must not be back-projected into
  every clause.

## 2. Chāndogya 5.3–10 — five fires and differentiated post-mortem paths

The unit beginning at 5.3 frames a teaching commonly described in scholarship as
the "five fires" doctrine.

By 5.10 the text distinguishes post-mortem routes.

### 5.10.1–2 — path associated with knowledge/forest practice

The text describes a route through:
- light;
- day;
- bright fortnight;
- the sun's northward months;
- year;
- sun;
- moon;
- lightning;

and an associated non-human person leading onward.

### 5.10.3 onward — contrasting route

The text gives another route beginning with:
- smoke;
- night;
- dark fortnight;
- the sun's southward months.

The immediate text associates this route with a different pattern of practice.

### 5.10.7 — conduct and rebirth

Already controlled in V1:
- favorable conduct -> favorable births;
- unfavorable conduct -> unfavorable births;
- social hierarchy appears directly in the examples.

### 5.10.8 — repeated birth/death outside the two routes

The passage describes a "third place" involving beings repeatedly:
- born;
- dying;
- returning again.

### Comparison consequence

ChU 5.10 cannot be represented as merely:

`good_deed -> better_next_body`.

The unit instead contains multiple post-mortem routes, differentiated practices,
return, and a repeated birth/death condition.

The stronger neutral axes are therefore:
- `CONTINUITY_MODEL`;
- `POSTMORTEM_PATH_MODEL`;
- `MORAL_CAUSALITY_MODEL`;
- `SOCIAL_COSMOLOGY_MODEL`;
- `KNOWLEDGE_OR_PRACTICE_DIFFERENTIATION_MODEL`.

## 3. Cross-text warning

Scholarship notes related "five fires / paths" material in other early
Upaniṣadic or Brāhmaṇa contexts, including Bṛhadāraṇyaka 6.2.

That observation is a research lead, not yet a transmission claim.

Before treating the parallels as one inherited unit, ON_THEO should bind:
- exact passages;
- recension;
- wording;
- sequence;
- distinctive additions;
- chronology;
- direction hypotheses.

## 4. Hindu continuity model at current depth

The Hindu lane can now support more than the generic label "reincarnation."

At least two text-specific structures are visible:

### Bṛhadāraṇyaka 4.4
A continuing self is described through death/transition analogies, with knowledge,
action, experience, desire, attachment, and outcome tied into the process.

### Chāndogya 5.3–10
Embodiment and post-mortem routing are embedded in a larger five-fire/path
scheme, with differentiated routes, return, conduct-linked birth, and repetitive
birth/death.

### Project conclusion

`REBIRTH`
is too lossy as a single comparison cell.

The neutral schema needs at least:
- what continues;
- how continuity is described;
- what conditions destination/path;
- whether return is expected;
- whether liberation/non-return is distinct;
- whether social status is encoded into outcome.
