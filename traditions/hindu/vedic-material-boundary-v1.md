# Hindu Traditions — Vedic Material-Evidence Boundary V1

Status: MATERIAL EVIDENCE CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Ritual texts are rich.

Ancient archaeological attribution is much harder.

This packet prevents ON_THEO from using any fire feature, altar-shaped feature, or modern Vedic ritual as automatic proof of ancient Vedic continuity.

## 1. Modern documented performance

Cornell University's architectural/landscape archive preserves documentary photographs of Vedic ritual in Vārāṇasī in 1972.

The archive identifies:
- ritual fires;
- a gārhapatya altar;
- an āhavanīya altar;
- priests;
- offerings;
- sacrificial utensils.

Sources:
https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/ss%3A3859101
https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/ss%3A3859053

This is strong evidence for:

`DOCUMENTED_20TH_CENTURY_VEDIC_RITUAL_PERFORMANCE`.

It is not direct evidence for:
- exact Bronze/Iron Age practice;
- uninterrupted line-by-line ritual continuity;
- the date of any individual ritual rule.

## 2. Textual continuity and performance continuity are different claims

A modern performance may reproduce:
- Sanskrit texts;
- altar terminology;
- priestly offices;
- fire layouts;
- offering forms.

Even when those correspond closely to older manuals, two questions remain distinct:

1. Is the text old?
2. Is this exact performance sequence historically continuous?

Therefore:

`OLD_TEXT + MODERN_PERFORMANCE`
!=
`PROVEN_UNBROKEN_PRACTICE`.

## 3. Archaeological fire features

South Asian archaeological sites contain many features interpreted as hearths, fire installations, pits, altars, or ritual structures.

The identification of some Indus-period examples as specifically Vedic fire altars has been contested in scholarship.

This packet therefore adopts a strict rule:

`FIRE_FEATURE`
!=
`VEDIC_ALTAR`

without:
- date compatibility;
- layout compatibility;
- contextual evidence;
- associated material;
- independent cultural attribution.

### Guard

Similarity in shape or the presence of ash does not establish Vedic ritual identity.

## 4. Material evidence needed for future admission

A future archaeological packet should record, for each candidate:
- site;
- stratigraphic phase;
- absolute/relative date;
- plan;
- number and geometry of fire installations;
- associated faunal/botanical remains;
- inscriptions, if any;
- excavation history;
- competing functional interpretations;
- relation to textual ritual layouts.

Until then:

`ANCIENT_VEDIC_RITUAL_ARCHAEOLOGY = PARTIAL / NOT_YET_ADMITTED`.

## 5. Comparison consequence

The Hindu lane can now support `RITUAL_ORDER_MODEL` from textual ritual evidence.

It still cannot claim:

`MATERIAL_EVIDENCE_CONFIRMS_THE_TEXTUAL_RITUAL_SYSTEM`.

That remains a separate evidentiary frontier.
