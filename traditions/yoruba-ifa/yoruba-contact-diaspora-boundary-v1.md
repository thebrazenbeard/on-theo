# Yorùbá Contact / Diaspora Boundary Packet V1

Status: CONTACT-HISTORY / DIASPORA-TRANSFORMATION CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Bind major Yorùbá religious contact and diaspora layers without treating Islam,
Christianity, Òrìṣà religion, modern Ìṣẹ̀ṣe revival, Lukumí/Santería,
Candomblé or other Atlantic traditions as one undifferentiated "syncretism."

Core rule:

CONTACT != ONE_SYNCRETISM_PROCESS.

A second rule:

DIASPORA_CONTINUITY != UNCHANGED_HOMELAND_REPLICATION.

## 1. Long-term religious plurality in Yorùbá history

J. D. Y. Peel's historical work treats Yorùbá religious history as deeply shaped
by the coexistence and interaction of:
- Òrìṣà religion;
- Islam;
- Christianity.

His work emphasizes:
- conversion;
- coexistence;
- competition;
- appropriation;
- cultural translation;
- historical change.

Current classification:

CONTACT_AND_REINTERPRETATION_MODEL =
BOUNDED_MULTI_RELIGIOUS_YORÙBÁ_FIELD.

No generic "replacement" model is sufficient.

## 2. Religious encounter and modern Yorùbá identity

Peel argues that nineteenth-century encounters involving:
- Christian missions;
- African mission agents;
- literacy;
- warfare/political reorganization;
- religious boundary making

contributed to the making of modern pan-Yorùbá identity.

Current result:

RELIGIOUS_CONTACT_CAN_PARTICIPATE_IN_ETHNO_RELIGIOUS_CATEGORY_FORMATION.

This is not:
"missionaries invented the Yorùbá."

It means the modern broad identity was historically shaped through encounter,
textualization and self-redefinition.

## 3. Islam is not a late external footnote

Yorùbá religious history includes long-standing Islamic presence and interaction.

Current research therefore rejects:
TRADITIONAL_RELIGION -> CHRISTIANITY
as the only contact narrative.

At minimum:

ÒRÌṢÀ_RELIGION
↔ ISLAM
↔ CHRISTIANITY

have interacted through historically variable institutions and communities.

The arrows do not imply equal chronology or symmetrical influence in every
period.

## 4. Modern Ìṣẹ̀ṣe / "Yorùbá religion" revival

Contemporary scholarship documents modern organizations that explicitly revive,
reformulate or institutionally promote Yorùbá indigenous religion/Ifá in
religiously plural urban settings.

Current classification:

REVIVAL_RECONSTRUCTION_MODEL =
PARTIAL_MODERN_YORÙBÁ_INDIGENOUS_REVIVAL.

This is not:
unchanged survival of one precolonial institutional form.

## 5. Atlantic forced migration

Historical scholarship documents the major role of captives from the Bights of
Benin/Biafra and Yorùbá-related populations in the formation of Atlantic
diasporic cultures.

Current historical result:

ATLANTIC_DIASPORA_CREATED_REAL_TRANSMISSION_PATHWAYS.

This is stronger than motif resemblance.

It establishes:
- human carriers;
- displacement;
- community formation;
- contact environments.

But each religious feature still requires source-specific transmission analysis.

## 6. Multiple diaspora traditions

Yorùbá-derived religious formations in the Americas include distinct traditions,
among them:
- Lukumí / Santería in Cuba and beyond;
- Brazilian Candomblé lineages;
- Trinidad and other Òrìṣà traditions;
- contemporary transnational Òrìṣà/Ifá communities.

Current rule:

DIASPORA_YORÙBÁ_DERIVED_RELIGIONS =
MULTIPLE_HISTORICAL_FORMATIONS.

Not:
ONE_NEW_WORLD_YORÙBÁ_RELIGION.

## 7. Construction / transformation in diaspora

Joseph Murphy explicitly describes Yorùbá traditions in the Americas as
historically constructed forms rooted in Yorùbá civilization.

Oxford diaspora scholarship similarly emphasizes that displaced Africans:
- recreated;
- retained;
- modified;
- reorganized
customs and traditions under New World conditions.

Current axis:

CONTINUITY_MODEL =
BOUNDED_DIASPORA_CONTINUITY_WITH_TRANSFORMATION.

## 8. Contact in the Americas

Diaspora traditions developed under conditions including:
- slavery;
- Catholic/Christian institutions;
- racial hierarchy;
- colonial and national law;
- interaction with other African traditions;
- local social organization.

Therefore:

AFRO_ATLANTIC_RELIGIOUS_FORM
cannot be reduced to
HOMELAND_EXPORT + CATHOLIC_OVERLAY.

Mechanisms can include:
- retention;
- selection;
- recombination;
- translation;
- concealment;
- institutional innovation;
- later de-syncretizing reform;
- transnational reconnection.

Each requires source evidence.

## 9. Òrìṣà continuity does not imply invariant identity

Names, attributes, ritual roles and cult relations can:
- persist;
- shift;
- merge;
- specialize;
- acquire new social meanings.

Current guard:

SAME_OR_COGNATE_ÒRÌṢÀ_NAME != IDENTICAL_RITUAL_SYSTEM.

This extends the homeland local-variation control into diaspora.

## 10. Ifá in diaspora

Ifá and related Yorùbá-derived divination systems are present in selected
diaspora traditions.

But:
- Ifá;
- sixteen-cowrie divination;
- Lukumí divination;
- Candomblé divinatory institutions

must not be collapsed.

William Bascom's work already demonstrates that Ifá and sixteen-cowrie systems
are distinct even when historically related and both occur across Atlantic
contexts.

Current rule:

DIVINATION_TRANSMISSION_REQUIRES_SYSTEM_LEVEL_IDENTIFICATION.

## 11. Diaspora can feed back into homeland

Modern religious globalization allows:
- travel;
- ordination/initiation networks;
- publications;
- digital communication;
- heritage politics;
- transatlantic religious authority claims.

Therefore:

TRANSMISSION_NE_ONE_WAY_ONLY.

V1 records the methodological requirement but does not yet map all return flows.

## 12. Diaspora identity and race

Recent scholarship on Lucumí shows contemporary practitioners actively negotiate
race, ethnicity and transnational identity within an Òrìṣà religion.

Current result:

DIASPORA_RELIGIOUS_IDENTITY_REMAINS_DYNAMIC.

This is modern evidence, not historical proof about slavery-era communities.

Current Yorùbá/Ifá work requires at least:
