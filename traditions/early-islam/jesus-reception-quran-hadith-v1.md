## Layer A — Qur'anic Jesus

Evidence class:
`PRIMARY TEXT` for Qur'anic discourse.

### Jesus as servant, prophet, Messiah, son of Mary

Q 19:30-36 presents Jesus as:
- servant of God;
- recipient of scripture;
- prophet;
- blessed;
- associated with prayer and almsgiving;
- son of Mary;
- explicitly not divine offspring.

Q 3 and Q 5 additionally call him Messiah and associate him with signs/miracles
by divine permission.

### QJ-V1-001

Proposition:
The Qur'an presents Jesus positively as Messiah, prophet/messenger, son of Mary,
and recipient/bearer of divine signs while rejecting divine-sonship readings.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_QURANIC_TEXT`

## Miracles and divine permission

Q 5:110 attributes to Jesus:
- speech in infancy and maturity;
- knowledge of scripture/wisdom/Torah/Gospel;
- clay-bird animation;
- healing blind/leper;
- raising the dead;

repeatedly under the qualifier "by My permission."

### QJ-V1-002

The Qur'an preserves a high miracle profile for Jesus while subordinating
miraculous agency explicitly to God.

`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_QURANIC_TEXT`

## Q 4:157-158 — killing, crucifixion, appearance, raising

Q 4:157 says that the boasting opponents:
- did not kill Jesus;
- did not crucify him;
- `shubbiha lahum` — commonly translated "it was made to appear so to them"
  or, in some translations, "another was made to resemble him to them";
- were in doubt and lacked knowledge;
- did not kill him with certainty.

Q 4:158 says instead that God raised Jesus to Himself.

### QJ-V1-003

Proposition:
The Qur'an explicitly denies that the addressed opponents successfully killed
or crucified Jesus and juxtaposes this denial with divine raising.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_QURANIC_TEXT`

### QJ-V1-004

Proposition:
Q 4:157 explicitly identifies the name and identity of another person who was
crucified in Jesus's place.

Status:
`NOT_ADMITTED`

Reason:
the verse does not name Judas, a disciple, or any substitute.

Translation note:
some English translations insert "another" to resolve the passive expression;
that is already interpretation of an ambiguous construction and must not be
mistaken for a named Qur'anic character.

Guard:

`SHUBBIHA_LAHUM != NAMED_SUBSTITUTE_BY_TEXT_ALONE`

## Q 3:55 / 5:117 — taking / raising language

Q 3:55 uses `mutawaffika` plus "raising you to Myself."
English translations vary among:
- take you;
- gather you;
- terminate your stay;
- other renderings.

Q 5:117 places on Jesus's lips a contrast between:
- the period when he was a witness among his people;
- the period after God "took" him.

### QJ-V1-005

Proposition:
The Qur'an contains "taking" and "raising" language about Jesus whose exact
relationship to death, removal, and later eschatology is interpretively

Status:

## Does the Qur'an itself teach future descent?

Later Islamic theology commonly connects Jesus with eschatological return.

The Qur'an contains passages used in that discussion, especially Q 4:159 and
Q 43:61 in later interpretation.

But the fully developed narrative of:
- Jesus descending;
- judging justly;
- breaking the cross;
- killing swine;
- abolishing jizya;
- joining end-time Muslim leadership

is not narrated as a single explicit Qur'anic story.

### QJ-V1-006

Proposition:
A detailed eschatological descent scenario should not be attributed to the
Qur'an alone without separating later hadith/exegesis.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_AS_SOURCE_CONTROL`

## Layer B — canonical Sunni hadith collections

Evidence class:
`PRIMARY TEXT` for the hadith traditions as collected in their extant
canonical literary form.

Historical layer:
major Sunni canonical hadith collections are ninth-century compilation nodes,
though individual reports may preserve substantially earlier transmission.

The present V1 does not independently authenticate each report back to the
seventh century.

### Sahih al-Bukhari 3448

The report attributed through Abu Hurayra says Jesus son of Mary will descend
and:
- judge justly;
- break the cross;
- kill swine;
- remove/abolish jizya;
- exist in a world of extraordinary abundance.

Abu Hurayra is then represented as connecting the report to Q 4:159.

### Sahih Muslim 155 cluster

Muslim preserves closely related reports with variants:
- descent as just judge;
- cross broken;
- swine killed;
- jizya removed;
- Jesus descending while a Muslim leader/imam is present;
- versions discussing whether/how Jesus leads within the community.

## Crucifixion interpretation — scholarly dispute

Most classical Muslim commentators came to read Q 4:157 together with a
substitution scenario in which another person was crucified while Jesus was
raised.

However, modern scholarship disputes whether the Qur'an itself requires that
reading.

Gabriel Said Reynolds, for example, argues that the Qur'anic passages can be
read differently and challenges the assumption that the text unambiguously
teaches that Jesus never died.

Whatever verdict one adopts, the project-level methodological result is secure:

### QJ-V1-007

Proposition:
The distinction between:
1. the wording of Q 4:157-158,
2. classical substitution interpretations,
3. modern historical-critical readings

must remain explicit.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_AS_METHOD_CONTROL`

Project status of exact Qur'anic death chronology:
`CONTESTED / UNKNOWN_AT_SYNTHESIS_LEVEL`

This does not erase the text's explicit denial that the addressed opponents
successfully killed/crucified him.

## Historical-Jesus control

The Qur'an is seventh-century evidence for Qur'anic/early Islamic reception of
Jesus.

Canonical hadith collections are primary evidence for later collected Islamic
tradition and possible evidence for earlier transmitted reports after source
criticism.

Neither source layer becomes independent first-century eyewitness biography
simply because it discusses Jesus.

### ISAJ-V1-005

Proposition:
Islamic Jesus traditions are historically important reception evidence without
automatically becoming first-century historical controls.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_AS_METHOD_CONTROL`

## Testament constraint

Book VI may safely say:
- the Qur'an honors Jesus as Messiah, prophet/messenger, son of Mary, miracle
  worker by God's permission, and explicitly rejects divine-sonship;
- Q 4:157-158 denies that the addressed opponents killed/crucified him and says
  God raised him;
- the verse itself does not identify Judas or another named substitute;
- later canonical hadith tradition gives Jesus a developed eschatological
  descent role;
- Bukhari/Muslim are later collection layers, not seventh-century books;
- later substitution and descent interpretation must remain historically
  layered.

Book VI should not say:
- "the Qur'an says Judas was crucified";
- "the Qur'an explicitly narrates Jesus returning to break the cross";
- canonical hadith wording is direct contemporary documentation from Muhammad's
  lifetime;
- later tafsir and hadith are interchangeable with the Qur'an;
- Islamic reception independently proves or disproves the first-century
  crucifixion event.
