`QURAN_4_157 != AL_TABARI_TAFSIR`

`AL_TABARI_COLLECTION_DATE != ATTRIBUTED_EARLIER_REPORT_DATE`

## Primary tafsir witness — al-Tabari, Jami al-bayan on Q 4:157

Evidence class:
`PRIMARY TEXT` for al-Tabari's early tenth-century Qur'anic commentary.

Authorial/historical layer:
Abu Ja'far Muhammad b. Jarir al-Tabari died in 310/923.

Al-Tabari's commentary is especially valuable because it:
- preserves multiple transmitted interpretations;
- gives chains/attributions for many reports;
- does not simply present one modernized harmonized story.

It remains a later compilation.

### TAB-Q4-V1-001

Proposition:
Al-Tabari interprets Q 4:157 through substitution/resemblance scenarios in which
Jesus himself is not the crucified victim.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_AL_TABARI`

## Variant A — all companions made to resemble Jesus

Al-Tabari transmits a report attributed through Wahb b. Munabbih in which:
- Jesus and companions are surrounded;
- all are made to look like Jesus;
- one companion goes out;
- that person is killed/crucified while believed to be Jesus;
- Jesus is raised.

### TAB-Q4-V1-002

Proposition:
Al-Tabari preserves a mass-resemblance variant in which the attackers cannot
distinguish Jesus from his companions.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_AL_TABARI_REPORT`

Attribution ceiling:
`REPORTED_WAHB_TRADITION / EARLIER_LAYER_NOT_INDEPENDENTLY_VERIFIED_IN_V1`

## Variant B — willing substitute / disciple

Al-Tabari also preserves reports in which Jesus asks who will accept his
likeness and death, and a companion volunteers.

Different transmitted forms give different narrative details.

### TAB-Q4-V1-003

Proposition:
A willing-substitute/companion interpretation is present in al-Tabari's
commentary tradition.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_AL_TABARI_REPORT`

Guard:
the volunteer is not one uniquely stable named character across all reports.

## Variant C — Sergius / named companion report

One narrative preserved by al-Tabari names a figure rendered in Arabic as
`Sarjis/Sergius`, who accepts Jesus's place and is crucified after Jesus is
raised.

### TAB-Q4-V1-004

Proposition:
A named non-Judas substitute appears in one of the classical tafsir narratives
preserved by al-Tabari.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_AL_TABARI_REPORT`

Guard:

`NAMED_SERGIUS_VARIANT != UNIVERSAL_ISLAMIC_SUBSTITUTION_TRADITION`

## Variant D — Mujahid / unnamed other

Al-Tabari preserves material attributed to Mujahid that simply says another
man was crucified while being taken for Jesus.

### TAB-Q4-V1-005

Proposition:
Some transmitted tafsir material preserves substitution without naming the
substitute.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_AL_TABARI_REPORT`

This is especially important because it shows that named identity is secondary
to the interpretive mechanism in at least some streams.

## Variant E — Judas as a reported Christian claim

Within one extended discussion al-Tabari reports that some Christians claimed
Judas was the one made to resemble Jesus and crucified.

This is not the same thing as:
- the Qur'an naming Judas;
- al-Tabari presenting Judas as the only Islamic interpretation;
- one universal Muslim doctrine.

### TAB-Q4-V1-006

Proposition:
A Judas-substitution claim is attested within the interpretive material
preserved by al-Tabari.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_AL_TABARI_TEXT`

Source-role limitation:
in the relevant passage it is presented as one claim among competing narratives,
including explicitly ascribed Christian opinion.

Guard:

`JUDAS_APPEARS_IN_TAFSIR != QURAN_NAMES_JUDAS`

## Al-Tabari's own handling of disagreement

Al-Tabari explicitly says interpreters differed over how the resemblance
occurred.

His discussion compares scenarios rather than erasing disagreement.

He appears to consider resemblance/substitution the governing explanation while
working through competing mechanisms.

### TAB-Q4-V1-007

Proposition:
Classical tafsir already contains internal disagreement over the mechanics and
identity of substitution.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_AL_TABARI`

## Earlier attributed authorities — chronology warning

Al-Tabari's chains attribute material to figures such as:
- Wahb b. Munabbih;
- Mujahid;
- Ibn Jurayj.

Those attributions are historically relevant.

But V1 does not silently convert:
`AL_TABARI_REPORTS_X_FROM_EARLIER_AUTHORITY`

into:
`WE_POSSESS_AN_EXTANT_FIRST/SECOND-CENTURY_ISLAMIC_DOCUMENT_FROM_X`

### TAB-Q4-V1-008

Proposition:
Al-Tabari's source chains are evidence for claimed transmission history and
possible earlier tradition layers, but require separate isnad/source criticism
before exact early dating is promoted.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_AS_METHOD_CONTROL`

## Muqatil boundary

Gabriel Said Reynolds's study cites Muqatil b. Sulayman's tafsir on Q 4:157 as
an earlier exegetical witness to substitutionary interpretation.

V1 has **not** completed a direct independent readback of the relevant Muqatil
edition.

Therefore:

`MUQATIL_SUBSTITUTION = SCHOLARLY_ATTESTED / DIRECT_READBACK_PENDING`

This avoids pretending a second-hand citation is a direct primary-text check.

## Hostile self-check

Potential overclaim:
"Islam says Judas was crucified."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

Potential overclaim:
"Substitution was invented by al-Tabari."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

Al-Tabari preserves attributed earlier traditions; direct early dating remains
a separate question.

Potential overclaim:
"Because al-Tabari preserves multiple reports, none of them mattered."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

The shared substitution mechanism is highly significant for classical
reception even when details vary.

Potential overclaim:
"One report names Sergius, therefore Sergius was a standard Muslim substitute."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

Potential overclaim:
"An isnad reaching an early authority proves the report in exactly that form
comes from that authority."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

## Testament constraint

Book VI may safely say:
- by the classical tafsir layer, substitutionary readings are firmly attested;
- al-Tabari preserves multiple competing scenarios;
- some versions use an unnamed or volunteer companion;
- one report names Sergius;
- Judas also appears in the interpretive dossier, but not as the Qur'an's named
  substitute;
- the diversity of stories is itself evidence for reception history.

Book VI should not say:
- "the Qur'an says Judas was crucified";
- "Muslims always agreed Judas was the substitute";
- "al-Tabari proves an early first-century event";
- "all classical commentators used the same mechanism/details."
