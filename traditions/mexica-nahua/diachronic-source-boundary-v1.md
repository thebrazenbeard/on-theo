# Mexica / Nahua Diachronic Source Boundary V1

Status: ACTIVE RESEARCH CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

This lane prevents three labels from collapsing into one another:

MEXICA
AZTEC
NAHUA.

Core rule:

MEXICA != ALL_NAHUA.

A second rule:

AZTEC_IS_A_MODERN_UMBRELLA_LABEL_REQUIRING_SOURCE_CONTROL.

## 1. Working terminology

### Mexica

Use for:
- the people/polity centered on Mexico-Tenochtitlan and closely related
  Tlatelolco contexts when the source actually concerns them;
- the imperial/court/ritual system represented by Templo Mayor and specific
  late Postclassic/early colonial sources.

Do not use "Mexica" for all Nahuatl-speaking peoples.

### Nahua

Use for:
- the wider Nahuatl-speaking cultural-linguistic field;
- colonial Nahuatl authors, elders, communities, and manuscripts where the
  source is broader than Mexica/Tenochca identity;
- living Nahua communities, which are geographically and culturally diverse.

INPI's current catalogue explicitly documents Nahua communities across many
Mexican states and emphasizes diversity produced through distinct historical
processes and regional traditions.

### Aztec

Use only as:
- a historiographic/modern umbrella label;
- a source's own cataloguing term where retained for discoverability;
- a label requiring clarification about whether the evidence is specifically
  Mexica, Triple Alliance, or broader Nahua.

Do not infer:

AZTEC = MEXICA = NAHUA.

## 2. Required evidence layers

### Late Postclassic Mexica archaeology

Primary strengths:
- Templo Mayor/Huey Teocalli;
- sacred precinct;
- offerings;
- monumental sculpture;
- architecture;
- burials/sacrificial deposits;
- city-political context.

Evidence class:
MATERIAL EVIDENCE / ARCHAEOLOGICAL CONTEXT.

### Indigenous pictorial manuscripts

Examples:
- Codex Borbonicus;
- Codex Mendoza;
- other early colonial or conquest-era pictorial manuscripts.

Each requires independent:
- date;
- provenance;
- language;
- patronage;
- annotation;
- colonial-contact controls.

One codex is not a Mexica canon.

### Florentine Codex

A sixteenth-century bilingual encyclopedic manuscript produced through
collaboration among:
- Bernardino de Sahagun;
- Nahua elders;
- Nahua authors/scholars;
- Nahua artists/scribes.

The Nahuatl and Spanish columns are distinct textual layers.

The manuscript is colonial and collaborative.

Therefore:

FLORENTINE_CODEX != UNMEDIATED_PRECONTACT_TRANSCRIPT.

And:

SAHAGUN != SOLE_AUTHOR_OF_INDIGENOUS_CONTENT.

### Living Nahua communities

Current INPI sources document major regional variation in:
- ritual;
- land relation;
- agriculture;
- funerary practice;
- Catholic contact;
- music;
- sacred sites;
- local political/social organization.

Living Nahua sources are present-tense evidence.

They are not automatic evidence for unchanged Mexica imperial religion.

## 3. Templo Mayor control

INAH identifies Templo Mayor as:
- the central religious and political complex of Mexica Tenochtitlan;
- dedicated prominently to Tlaloc and Huitzilopochtli;
- repeatedly enlarged;
- surrounded by other sacred precinct buildings;
- materially associated with offerings, monumental sculpture, sacrifice, and
  elite/religious institutions.

This is high-value direct material evidence.

But:

ARCHAEOLOGICAL_BUILDING_OR_OBJECT != COMPLETE_THEOLOGY_BY_ITSELF.

Textual/mythic identification must retain its route:
- inscription if available;
- early colonial text;
- iconographic interpretation;
- archaeological context;
- later scholarly synthesis.

## 4. Florentine Codex control

Getty's Digital Florentine Codex documents:
- 12 books;
- 2,446 pages;
- parallel Spanish and Nahuatl columns;
- nearly 2,500 images;
- completion in 1577;
- collaboration with named Nahua scholars, scribes, artists, and elders;
- content ranging from religion/calendar/divination to rhetoric, rulers,
  merchants, people, natural history, and conquest.

The manuscript itself contains multiple narrative/textual voices.

Current project rule:

NAHUATL_COLUMN
!=
SPANISH_COLUMN
!=
IMAGE_PROGRAM
!=
GETTY_MODERN_METADATA.

All can be evidence, but not the same evidence.

## 5. Codex Borbonicus control

Current institutional catalogues describe the Codex Borbonicus as:
- a pictorial Nahua/Mexican religious-divinatory calendar;
- a ritual/festival manuscript;
- screenfold format;
- carrying later Spanish annotations;
- dated around the conquest/early colonial boundary, with exact dating debated
  in scholarship/cataloguing.

Therefore:

BORBONICUS_DATE_AND_PRE_OR_POSTCONQUEST_STATUS =
SOURCE_CRITICAL_QUESTION.

Do not label it simply "precontact scripture."

## 6. Codex Mendoza control

Codex Mendoza is an early colonial pictorial manuscript, commonly dated around
1542.

Its major sections include:
- Mexica/Aztec imperial history/conquests;
- tribute;
- social/life-cycle material.

It is colonial and produced for a Spanish imperial audience.

Therefore:

CODEX_MENDOZA != PRECONTACT_IMPERIAL_ARCHIVE_UNMEDIATED.

Its religious value must be proposition-specific, not assumed from the codex's
general cultural scope.

## 7. Living Nahua diversity control

INPI currently documents Nahua communities across many regions and explicitly
notes diversity.

Examples in current public ethnographic summaries include:
- Huasteca ceremonial systems tied to maize, land, rain, music, offerings,
  Xantolo, and agricultural cycles;
- Morelos communities combining Catholic liturgical cycles, local sacred
  geography, agricultural rites, and mayordomia/cofradia structures;
- Milpa Alta sacred mountains/caves and Catholic mayordomias;
- other regional afterlife, land, ancestor, and ritual systems.

Therefore:

LIVING_NAHUA_RELIGION != ONE_SYSTEM.

And:

LIVING_NAHUA != MODERN_MEXICA_IMPERIAL_SURVIVAL.

## 8. Contact control

Colonial and living Nahua sources sit within long Christian-contact histories.

The project must distinguish:
- coercive missionization;
- Nahua authorship inside colonial institutions;
- translation;
- pictorial continuity;
- Catholic saint/festival incorporation;
- land/agricultural continuity;
- local reinterpretation;
- modern Indigenous self-definition.

Do not use "syncretism" as a substitute for mechanism analysis.

## 9. Comparison hold

No Mexica/Nahua proposition enters cross-tradition synthesis until:
- source community/polity is named;
- period is explicit;
- evidence mode is explicit;
- colonial mediation is explicit where applicable;
- exact neutral axis is source-admitted.

Current status:

MEXICA_NAHUA_CROSS_TRADITION_COMPARISON = HOLD.
