# Codex Borbonicus Ritual-Calendar Packet V1

Status: PICTORIAL MANUSCRIPT / CONQUEST-BOUNDARY SOURCE CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Bind Codex Borbonicus as an independent Nahua/Mexica ritual-calendar manuscript
without forcing its disputed date or treating it as a universal "Aztec
scripture."

Core rule:

BORBONICUS_DATE_IS_SOURCE_CRITICAL.

A second rule:

PICTORIAL_CALENDAR != ALPHABETIC_TEXTUAL_THEOLOGY.

## 1. Manuscript identity

Codex Borbonicus is held by the Bibliothèque de l'Assemblée nationale in Paris.

Current public custodial/catalogue evidence describes it as:
- a pictorial Mexican/Nahua manuscript;
- bark/amate screenfold;
- over fourteen meters long;
- approximately 36 folios/leaves in current cataloguing;
- religious/divinatory calendar;
- ritual/feast guide;
- carrying later Spanish annotations.

Evidence class:
MANUSCRIPT WITNESS / PICTORIAL RITUAL-CALENDAR SOURCE.

## 2. Dating disagreement is evidence

Assemblée nationale public cataloguing describes the manuscript as late
fifteenth century.

BnF authority cataloguing instead gives:
- work date uncertain;
- possible production approximately 1522-1540;
- Spanish annotations.

Modern scholarship continues to debate its precise preconquest/early colonial
position.

Therefore:

BORBONICUS_PRECONQUEST_STATUS = UNRESOLVED_SOURCE_CRITICAL_QUESTION.

The project will use:

CONQUEST_BOUNDARY_MANUSCRIPT

until a dedicated codicological/material dating packet resolves more.

This is not a weakness to hide.

It is part of the evidence.

calendar.

tonalpohualli:
- twenty trecenas;
- thirteen-day periods;
- day signs/numbers;
- deity/patron imagery;
- divinatory associations.

BOUNDED_BORBONICUS_PICTORIAL_LAYER.

## 4. Solar-year festival cycle

Current scholarship on Borbonicus identifies a major pictorial record of the
eighteen twenty-day festival periods / veintenas of the 365-day solar year.

This is independent of the Florentine Codex Book 2 textual festival archive.

Current proposition:

BORBONICUS_PRESERVES_A_PICTORIAL_MEXICA_FESTIVAL_CALENDAR.

Evidence class:
PICTORIAL MANUSCRIPT + SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION.

The exact year-specific/historical framing of scenes remains a scholarly
question.

## 5. Cyclical and linear time can intersect

Recent scholarship argues that Borbonicus veintena imagery may situate recurring
solar-year festivals within specific historical year reckoning, including the
year 2 Reed / 1507.

Current project status:

LINEAR_CYCLICAL_TIME_INTERSECTION =
SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION / PROMISING.

Not yet:
PRIMARY_MANUSCRIPT_PROPOSITION.

This distinction matters because sacred time need not be modeled as "cyclical"
versus "linear" as exclusive opposites.

## 6. Spanish annotations

The manuscript carries Spanish-language annotations added after its pictorial
production.

Therefore the witness contains at least:
- Indigenous pictorial layer;
- later alphabetic colonial annotation layer.

Current method rule:

SPANISH_GLOSS != ORIGINAL_PICTORIAL_SEMANTICS_BY_DEFAULT.

But Spanish annotations can preserve early colonial interpretation/reception.

Evidence class:
COLONIAL COMMENTARY LAYER.

## 7. Relation to Florentine Codex

Both Borbonicus and Florentine Codex preserve:
- ritual-calendar material;
- festival structure;
- divinatory/calendar categories.

But they differ:
- pictorial screenfold versus encyclopedic bilingual manuscript;
- source production;
- dating problem;
- colonial mediation;
- social/intellectual context.

Therefore:

BORBONICUS_AND_FLORENTINE_CONVERGENCE
may strengthen a proposition,

but:

BORBONICUS != ILLUSTRATED_VERSION_OF_FLORENTINE_CODEX.

Future exact festival comparison should bind the same named veintena across both
sources.
