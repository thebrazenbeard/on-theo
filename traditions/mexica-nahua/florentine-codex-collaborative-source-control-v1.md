# Florentine Codex Collaborative Source Control V1

Status: COLONIAL NAHUA / FRANCISCAN MANUSCRIPT CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Bind the Florentine Codex as a collaborative, multilingual, multi-modal
sixteenth-century manuscript before using it to reconstruct Mexica/Nahua
religion.

Core rule:

FLORENTINE_CODEX != SAHAGUN_ALONE.

A second core rule:

NAHUATL_TEXT != SPANISH_TEXT != IMAGE_PROGRAM.

The three layers can interact, overlap, diverge, translate, reinterpret, or
comment on one another.

## 1. Physical and documentary identity

The Florentine Codex / Historia general de las cosas de nueva España:
- consists of twelve illuminated books;
- totals 1,223 folios / 2,446 pages;
- is bound in three volumes;
- was completed in 1577;
- was created at the Colegio Imperial de la Santa Cruz in Tlatelolco;
- is held by the Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana in Florence;
- contains 2,472 images and decorative elements in Getty's current count.

Evidence class:
MANUSCRIPT WITNESS / COLONIAL DOCUMENTARY PRACTICE.

Modern access:
Digital Florentine Codex, Getty Research Institute.

## 2. Collaborative authorship

Getty's current research describes the codex as created by Bernardino de
Sahagun together with Nahua elders, authors, grammarians, scribes, and artists.

Named Nahua collaborators include:
- Antonio Valeriano, Azcapotzalco;
- Alonso Vegerano, Cuauhtitlan;
- Martin Jacobita, Tlatelolco;
- Pedro de San Buenaventura, Cuauhtitlan;
- Diego de Grado, Tlatelolco;
- Bonifacio Maximiliano, Tlatelolco;
- Mateo Severino, Xochimilco.

Sahagun also records consulting:
- ten or twelve leading elders at Tepepulco, 1559-1561;
- eight or ten leaders at Tlatelolco, 1561-1562.

Current classification:

FLORENTINE_CODEX_AUTHORSHIP =
COLLABORATIVE_NAHUA_FRANCISCAN_WORKSHOP.

This does not erase Sahagun's institutional/editorial role.

It also does not reduce Nahua collaborators to anonymous "informants."

## 3. Nahuatl textual layer

Getty describes the right-hand column as the primary Nahuatl text.

The Nahuatl layer preserves Indigenous-language descriptions of:
- deities;
- festivals;
- divination;
- omens;
- rhetoric;
- rulers;
- merchants;
- people;
- nature;
- conquest;
- many other domains.

Evidence class:
COLONIAL NAHUATL TEXT.

Guard:

COLONIAL_NAHUATL_TEXT != UNMEDIATED_PRECONQUEST_TRANSCRIPT.

The information was gathered, selected, organized, written, and recopied under
colonial conditions through a European encyclopedic project.

But:

COLONIAL_MEDIATION != INDIGENOUS_VOICE_ABSENT.

## 4. Spanish textual layer

Getty characterizes the left-hand Spanish column as Sahagun's interpretation of
the Nahuatl text.

Therefore:

SPANISH_COLUMN != AUTOMATIC_TRANSLATION_EQUIVALENT.

Future proposition-level work must ask whether the Spanish:
- translates;
- abbreviates;
- expands;
- moralizes;
- reclassifies;
- omits;
- reframes

the Nahuatl passage.

Evidence class:
COMMENTARY / COLONIAL SPANISH TEXT.

A Spanish theological term cannot silently replace the Nahuatl source category.

## 5. Image program as third narrative

Getty's current scholarship treats the codex's images as a third narrative,
not merely illustration.

The image program:
- was created by multiple Nahua artists/tlahcuilohqueh;
- contains 1,844 narrative images and 628 decorative elements in the current
  count;
- combines Indigenous and European visual conventions;
- can interact with, supplement, or diverge from the alphabetic columns.

Evidence class:
INDIGENOUS IMAGE PROGRAM / MATERIAL-VISUAL TEXT / COLONIAL ART PRACTICE.

Therefore:

IMAGE != DECORATION_BY_DEFAULT.

And:

IMAGE != NAHUATL_TEXT_IN_PICTURES_BY_DEFAULT.

## 6. Workshop and draft history

Getty's source history identifies earlier stages/drafts including:
- Tepepulco investigations;
- Tlatelolco investigations;
- Codices Matritenses / Madrid Codices;
- now-lost Manuscrito de 1569;
- final clean Florentine Codex.

Current method rule:

FINAL_1577_MANUSCRIPT != ENTIRE_SOURCE_HISTORY.

If an earlier draft differs, that is transmission/redaction evidence rather than
noise to harmonize.

## 7. European encyclopedic form

The codex's organizational model draws on Roman and medieval encyclopedic
traditions available through the Colegio de Santa Cruz, including works such as
Pliny's Natural History and Isidore's Etymologies.

Therefore:

BOOK_STRUCTURE_AND_CATEGORY_ORDER
can reflect
EUROPEAN_ENCYCLOPEDIC_FORM.

This does not make the Nahua content European invention.

It means source form and Indigenous content must be analytically separated.

## 8. Mexica / Nahua scope

Getty notes that the codex focuses strongly on Mexica life and culture and was
produced especially through descendants of Mexicas in Tlatelolco, while also
drawing on a wider central Mexican Nahua knowledge network.

Therefore:

FLORENTINE_CODEX =
MEXICA_HEAVY_COLONIAL_NAHUA_ARCHIVE.

Not:

ONE_COMPLETE_NAHUA_CULTURE.

Not:

ALL_NAHUA_COMMUNITIES.

## 9. Book 12 as perspective-specific conquest history

Book 12 preserves the longest major Nahuatl narrative of the conquest from a
Mexica/Tlatelolcan perspective.

It is one Indigenous perspective among several, not "the Indigenous version."

Current method rule:

BOOK12_TLATELOLCAN_PERSPECTIVE != ALL_INDIGENOUS_CONQUEST_MEMORY.

## 10. Religious books require layer-specific reading

Priority ON_THEO books:
- Book 1: gods/deities;
- Book 2: ceremonies/festivals/calendar;
- Book 3: origins of gods;
- Book 4: divinatory arts;
- Book 5: omens/prognostications;
- Book 6: rhetoric, moral philosophy, theology;
- Book 7: astral/natural philosophy;
- Book 8: kings and lords;
- Book 12: conquest/contact history.

Each future religious proposition must name:
- book;
- folio;
- Nahuatl text;
- Spanish text if used;
- image if used;
- translation/editor;
- evidence class;
- colonial-contact caveat.

## 11. Modern digital edition is another layer

The Digital Florentine Codex supplies:
- manuscript images;
- transcriptions;
- modern translations;
- tags/metadata;
- modern Nahuatl chapter summaries for selected material;
- modern scholarly essays.

These are invaluable research tools.

But:

DIGITAL_METADATA != SIXTEENTH_CENTURY_MANUSCRIPT_TEXT.

MODERN_TRANSLATION != ORIGINAL_NAHUATL.

MODERN_HUASTECA_NAHUATL_SUMMARY != SIXTEENTH_CENTURY_TLATELOLCAN_NAHUATL.
