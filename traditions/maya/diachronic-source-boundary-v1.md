# Maya Diachronic Source and Community Boundary V1

Status: ACTIVE RESEARCH CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

"Maya religion" is not treated as one timeless system.

The research lane must preserve:
- region;
- language/community;
- archaeological period;
- genre;
- physical witness;
- colonial mediation;
- living-community provenance.

Core rule:

MAYA != ONE_TIMELESS_RELIGION.

A second core rule:

CLASSIC_INSCRIPTION
!=
POSTCLASSIC_CODEX
!=
COLONIAL_KICHE_TEXT
!=
LIVING_MAYA_TRADITION.

## 1. Required chronological layers

### Preclassic

Material, architectural, iconographic, and early writing evidence predating the
Classic-period dynastic inscriptional florescence.

No later deity name, myth, or ritual system is projected backward without an
independent attribution route.

### Classic

Approximate working frame:
ca. 250-900 CE, with regional variation.

Primary strengths:
- hieroglyphic inscriptions;
- monuments;
- ceramics;
- architecture;
- burials;
- iconography;
- archaeological context.

Major guard:

CLASSIC_ELITE_EPIGRAPHY != ALL_MAYA_RELIGION.

Royal inscriptions strongly represent court history, ritual, dynastic authority,
warfare, calendrics, and elite religious action.

They are not transparent surveys of household religion or every Maya community.

### Postclassic

Approximate working frame:
after ca. 900 CE through early colonial disruption, with regional variation.

High-value sources include surviving screenfold codices.

The Dresden, Madrid, Paris, and Maya Codex of Mexico are separate manuscripts
with distinct dates, provenance, contents, and preservation histories.

Guard:

ONE_CODEX != PAN_MAYA_CANON.

### Colonial Indigenous textual layer

K'iche', Yucatec, Kaqchikel, and other Indigenous-language texts written or
transcribed under colonial conditions require separate witness histories.

Popol Wuj is specifically K'iche'.

Guard:

POPOL_WUJ != UNIVERSAL_MAYA_BIBLE.

### Living Maya traditions

There are more than thirty living Maya languages and many communities across
Mexico, Guatemala, Belize, Honduras, El Salvador, and diasporas.

Living Maya sources are not "survivals" only.

They are contemporary Indigenous traditions with:
- continuity;
- adaptation;
- local variation;
- Christian and other contact histories;
- modern institutions;
- community knowledge governance.

Guard:

LIVING_MAYA != UNCHANGED_CLASSIC_MAYA.

## 2. Evidence hierarchy by question

There is no single source hierarchy valid for every Maya research question.

### For Classic dynastic/religious propositions

Prefer:
1. inscription / monument itself;
2. archaeological context;
3. current epigraphic reading;
4. scholarly historical reconstruction.

### For Postclassic manuscript propositions

Prefer:
1. physical/digital codex witness;
2. codicological context;
3. glyphic reading;
4. historical reconstruction.

### For Popol Wuj propositions

Prefer:
1. Ximenez K'iche'/Spanish manuscript witness;
2. controlled transcription;
3. K'iche'-competent editions/translations;
4. source criticism;
5. later popular retellings.

The surviving Ximenez manuscript is colonial and mediated.

Claims about a lost earlier source remain historical reconstruction.

### For living Maya practice

Prefer:
1. Maya-authored/community-grounded public sources;
2. Maya-curated collaborative public projects;
3. public community institutions;
4. scholarship with named community provenance.

Do not use ancient texts to overrule living Maya self-description.

Do not use living practice to reconstruct Classic ritual unchanged.

## 3. Classic inscription control

Harvard Peabody's Corpus of Maya Hieroglyphic Inscriptions documents ancient
Maya inscriptions and associated art through photographs, drawings, site
records, and publication.

This is a primary research gateway for:
EPIGRAPHIC EVIDENCE + MATERIAL CONTEXT.

The corpus itself is a modern scholarly documentation project.

Therefore:

CMHI_DRAWING != ORIGINAL_MONUMENT

while remaining a high-value controlled representation of it.

Source:
https://peabody.harvard.edu/corpus-maya-hieroglyphic-inscriptions

## 4. Surviving codex control

Four original Maya manuscripts are currently recognized:
- Dresden Codex;
- Madrid Codex;
- Paris Codex;
- Maya Codex of Mexico.

Each must retain:
- manuscript identity;
- approximate date;
- provenance history;
- content profile;
- current custodian;
- interpretation history.

The Dresden manuscript is later Postclassic and preserves ritual/divinatory,
calendar, astronomical, and deity material.

The Maya Codex of Mexico is early Postclassic and independently authenticated by
a modern interdisciplinary INAH project.

Sources:
https://www.slub-dresden.de/en/explore/manuscripts/the-dresden-maya-codex
https://codicemayademexico.inah.gob.mx/

## 5. Popol Wuj witness control

The surviving Ximenez manuscript is:
- K'iche' and Spanish;
- colonial;
- transcribed by Dominican friar Francisco Ximenez;
- dated to the early eighteenth century;
- held by the Newberry Library;
- digitized through Newberry/Ohio State and Library of Congress pathways.

Library of Congress describes it as one of the earliest surviving copies and
notes scholarly hypotheses about an earlier sixteenth-century Indigenous
Latin-script source.

The earlier source is not physically extant in the current project.

Therefore:

SURVIVING_POPOL_WUJ_WITNESS = XIMENEZ_COLONIAL_MANUSCRIPT.

EARLIER_16C_KICHE_SOURCE = HISTORICAL_RECONSTRUCTION / PROBABLE_BUT_NOT_PHYSICAL_WITNESS.

PRECONTACT_GLYPHIC_POPOL_WUJ = NOT_ESTABLISHED_AS_EXTANT_SOURCE.

Sources:
https://www.loc.gov/item/2021668226/
https://library.osu.edu/projects/popolwuj/

## 6. Living Maya source control

Smithsonian NMAI's Living Maya Time project was developed with Maya curators and
many named K'iche', Kaqchikel, Q'eqchi', Tzeltal, Yucatec, Peten Itza, and
other Maya collaborators, including the Komon Tohil Association of Maya
Calendar Keepers.

This is a high-value collaborative public source for contemporary public
tradition.

It still requires speaker/community attribution.

A statement by a Tzeltal Maya cultural astronomer is not silently converted into
a K'iche' or Yucatec doctrine.

Sources:
https://maya.nmai.si.edu/
https://maya.nmai.si.edu/credits

## 7. Colonial/Christian contact control

Colonial Maya texts and contemporary ceremonies can contain:
- Indigenous continuity;
- Christian vocabulary;
- Catholic saints;
- cofradia institutions;
- reinterpretation;
- strategic preservation;
- colonial textual mediation.

Do not reduce all such material to:
- "pure precontact survival";
- "Christian contamination";
- generic syncretism.

Each mechanism must be source-specific.

## 8. Restricted/community knowledge rule

Public collaborative sources may establish:
- that a practice exists;
- public meanings;
- calendrical categories;
- public offerings;
- named community roles.

They do not create permission to reconstruct private, closed, or
community-restricted ceremonial knowledge.

PUBLICLY_ACCESSIBLE != ETHICALLY_UNRESTRICTED.

## 9. Comparison hold

No Maya proposition enters the existing cross-tradition matrix until:
- the relevant historical layer is explicit;
- source and witness are bound;
- language/community scope is explicit where applicable;
- colonial mediation is controlled;
- the exact neutral axis is source-admitted.
