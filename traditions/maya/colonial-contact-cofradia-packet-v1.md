# Maya Colonial Contact and Cofradia Packet V1

Status: K'ICHE' LIVING/COLONIAL-CONTACT CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Bind one concrete Maya-Christian contact structure without collapsing:
- continuity;
- Catholic institutional form;
- Maya ritual practice;
- calendar-keeper authority;
- colonial adaptation

into the single label "syncretism."

Primary case:
Santo Tomas Chichicastenango, K'iche' Guatemala.

Core rule:

CONTACT != REPLACEMENT.

A second rule:

CONTACT != PURE_UNCHANGED_SURVIVAL.

## 1. Current Chichicastenango source

NMAI Living Maya Time identifies Chichicastenango as:
- a K'iche' community;
- organized in part through fourteen cofradias;
- each cofradia associated with a Catholic patron saint;
- a setting where many customs and rituals are described as Maya in origin;
- a community where ajq'ijab' continue to give counsel through Chol Q'ij
  calendar knowledge.

Evidence class:
LIVING TRADITION / COMMUNITY-COLLABORATIVE PUBLIC SOURCE /
HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION.

This is present-tense evidence of a contact-shaped religious institution.

## 2. Cofradia is not one mechanism

The Spanish colonial cofradia form is historically Catholic.

In Chichicastenango's present public description it can carry:
- patron-saint organization;
- communal office;
- ritual responsibility;
- Maya-origin practices;
- local social/civil structure.

Therefore:

COFRADIA =
CONTACT_SHAPED_INSTITUTIONAL_CARRIER

at this source locus.

Not:

COFRADIA = PURE_CATHOLIC_REPLACEMENT.

Not:

COFRADIA = UNCHANGED_PRECONTACT_MAYA_INSTITUTION.

## 3. Ajq'ijab' coexist with cofradia structures

NMAI separately states that ajq'ijab':
- remain in Chichicastenango;
- provide counsel using Chol Q'ij knowledge.

This means current K'iche' religious authority is not exhausted by Catholic
office or cofradia organization.

Current proposition:

MULTIPLE_RELIGIOUS_AUTHORITY_STRUCTURES_CAN_COEXIST_WITHIN_ONE_KICHE_COMMUNITY.

Potential mechanisms:
- CALENDAR_COUNSEL_AUTHORITY;
- COFRADIA_COMMUNAL_OFFICE;
- CATHOLIC_SAINT_INSTITUTION;
- MAYA_RITUAL_CONTINUITY_OR_REINTERPRETATION.

## 4. Contact and institutionalization must remain distinct axes

The case supports both:

CONTACT_AND_REINTERPRETATION_MODEL

and:

INSTITUTIONALIZATION_MODEL.

But those answer different questions.

Contact:
how Maya and Catholic forms interact historically.

Institutionalization:
how offices, knowledge, ritual responsibility, and community organization are
stabilized now.

Therefore:

CONTACT_EFFECT != INSTITUTIONAL_FORM.

## 5. Popol Wuj supplies a different colonial contact mechanism

The Ximenez Popol Wuj witness also exists within colonial Catholic institutional
history.

But its mechanism differs from cofradia practice.

Popol Wuj:
INDIGENOUS_TEXT + DOMINICAN_TRANSCRIPTION_TRANSLATION.

Chichicastenango:
COMMUNITY_INSTITUTION + CATHOLIC_SAINT_STRUCTURE + MAYA_RITUAL_PRACTICE.

These should not be merged into one colonial-contact type.

## 6. Christian vocabulary requires source tagging

Colonial and living Maya sources can use:
- Catholic saint language;
- church space;
- feast-day calendars;
- Christian names;
- Indigenous calendrical/religious categories.

A Christian term in a Maya context does not automatically establish:
- total theological adoption;
- superficial disguise;
- direct equivalence with a precontact deity/category.

Required method:

TERM_OR_INSTITUTION
-> SOURCE_CONTEXT
-> COMMUNITY_USE
-> HISTORICAL_CONTACT_MECHANISM.

## 7. Ritual-calendar overlap

Some contemporary Maya celebrations intersect Catholic calendar dates, while
Maya sacred calendars continue to structure other ceremonies and counsel.

This creates multiple temporal systems in one living religious field.

Current implication:

SACRED_TIME_CALENDAR_MODEL
must allow
MULTIPLE_INTERACTING_CALENDRICAL_REGIMES.

This is stronger than a one-calendar model.

## 9. What is not admitted

Not admitted:
- all modern Maya religion is syncretic in one identical way;
- every cofradia practice is precontact;
- every cofradia practice is Spanish/Catholic replacement;
- ajq'ijab' authority is identical across Maya communities;
- Catholic saints are simply renamed Maya gods;
- all Maya communities share Chichicastenango's institution structure.
