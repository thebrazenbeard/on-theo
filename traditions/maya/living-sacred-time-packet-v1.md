# Living Maya Sacred-Time Packet V1

Status: LIVING COMMUNITY / CALENDAR-CONTINUITY CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Bind contemporary public Maya evidence for sacred time, calendar keeping,
agriculture, offerings, and community guidance without treating living practice
as an unchanged survival of Classic or Postclassic ritual.

Core rule:

LIVING_CONTINUITY != UNCHANGED_RITUAL_FORM.

A second rule:

ONE_MAYA_SPEAKER != ALL_MAYA.

## 1. Source provenance

Primary public source:
Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian, Living Maya Time.

The project credits explicitly name Maya curators and contributors, including:
- Alonso Mendez, Tzeltal Maya;
- Jose Huchim Herrera, Yucatec Maya;
- Maria Avila Vera, Yucatec Maya;
- Juana Batz Puac, K'iche' Maya;
- Roberto Poz Perez, K'iche' Maya;
- Ixkik Poz Salanic, K'iche' Maya;
- numerous Kaqchikel, Q'eqchi', Peten Itza, Lacandon, Yucatec and other Maya collaborators;
- Komon Tohil Association of Maya Calendar Keepers.

Evidence class:
LIVING TRADITION / COMMUNITY-COLLABORATIVE PUBLIC SOURCE.

This makes the project substantially stronger than anonymous ethnographic
summary.

It does not make every statement pan-Maya.

## 2. Maya plurality is explicit in the source

NMAI emphasizes:
- more than thirty living Maya languages;
- regional/community differences;
- continuing cultural change;
- shared historical roots without one uniform contemporary culture.

Current proposition:

LIVING_MAYA_RELIGIOUS_EVIDENCE_REQUIRES_SPEAKER_LANGUAGE_AND_COMMUNITY_ATTRIBUTION.

Guard:

MAYA = ONE_CONTEMPORARY_RELIGIOUS_COMMUNITY

is invalid.

## 3. Sacred 260-day calendar remains living

NMAI documents living use of:
- Tzolk'in in Yucatec terminology;
- Chol Q'ij in K'iche' terminology;
- 260-day sacred/ceremonial cycles.

In highland Guatemala, Ajq'ijab' / Day Keepers:
- maintain the Chol Q'ij;
- mark new calendar cycles;
- counsel communities;
- undergo/initiate calendar-keeper succession in public descriptions.

Current axis:

SACRED_TIME_CALENDAR_MODEL =
BOUNDED_LIVING_MULTI_COMMUNITY_WITH_ATTRIBUTION.

This is independent evidence that sacred calendar relation is not only a
surviving Postclassic manuscript feature.

## 4. Calendar keeping as religious/social office

NMAI defines Day Keepers as specially trained people in the highlands of
Guatemala who use the 260-day sacred calendar as a symbolic system of values to
guide communities.

K'iche' contributor Ixkik Poz Salanic describes Day Keeping and legal work as
parallel forms of helping restore balance to community.

Current propositions:

CALENDAR_KEEPING_CAN_FUNCTION_AS_SPECIALIZED_RELIGIOUS_SOCIAL_COUNSEL.

INSTITUTIONALIZATION_MODEL =
PARTIAL_LIVING_DAY_KEEPER_OFFICE.

Guard:

AJQ'IJ_ROLE != ONE_STANDARDIZED_PAN_MAYA_PRIESTHOOD.

## 5. Corn, land, sky, and time are relationally linked

NMAI's living Maya material connects:
- solar cycles;
- lunar cycles;
- human gestation;
- corn growth;
- sacred calendar;
- agricultural calendar;
- ceremonial timing.

Tzeltal Maya cultural astronomer Alonso Mendez describes the land as teaching
through the relationship between corn growth and ceremonial cycles.

Current internal result:

SACRED_TIME_IS_RELATIONAL_TO_LAND_SKY_AGRICULTURE_AND_HUMAN_LIFE_IN_SELECTED_LIVING_MAYA_SOURCES.

This supports:
- SACRED_TIME_CALENDAR_MODEL;
- RELATIONAL_ORDER_MODEL;
- SACRED_LAND_PLACE_MODEL.

## 6. Offerings and ceremonies remain calendrically embedded

NMAI documents:
- corn atole offerings every 260 days in highland Guatemala;
- Yucatec agricultural ceremonies during the corn cycle;
- K'iche' Wajxaqib' B'atz' at a new sacred-calendar cycle;
- Yucatec Sac Ha' and Pa Puul ceremonies in agricultural/rain contexts.

Current axis:

OFFERING_EXCHANGE_MODEL =
PARTIAL_LIVING_COMMUNITY_SPECIFIC.

Supported:
- offerings linked to calendar/agriculture/rain/gratitude in selected communities.

Not yet supported:
- one recipient class;
- one reciprocal economy;
- identical offering logic across communities.

## 7. Reciprocity language

NMAI explicitly describes contemporary offerings and ceremonies as rooted in a
reciprocal relationship between Maya people and land.

This is a modern collaborative public formulation.

Current proposition:

RELATIONAL_RECIPROCITY_WITH_LAND_IS_PUBLICLY_ATTESTED_IN_SELECTED_LIVING_MAYA_MATERIAL.

It does not automatically mean:
- identical reciprocity language existed in Classic glyphic texts;
- land is treated as one ontological category across all Maya languages;
- all offerings are quid-pro-quo transactions.

## 8. Day meanings and person/community balance

NMAI's public K'iche'-oriented sacred-calendar materials describe days as
bearing meanings relevant to:
- personal strengths/weaknesses;
- balance;
- continuity;
- social/natural relation.

This supports:

PERSON_OR_SELF_MODEL =
PARTIAL_LIVING_CALENDAR_RELATIONAL_LAYER.

But:

CALENDAR_DAY_MEANING != TOTAL_DETERMINISM.

The public source frames day meanings as guidance/orientation rather than a
complete denial of agency.

## 9. Continuity and change

NMAI presents current practices as living traditions with ancient roots.

The source also documents:
- community variation;
- Catholic-calendar intersections;
- modern professional identities;
- printed calendars;
- public museums/education.

Therefore:

CONTINUITY_MODEL =
BOUNDED_CULTURAL_PRACTICE_CONTINUITY_WITH_CHANGE.

Not:

UNBROKEN_IDENTICAL_RITUAL_FORM.

## 10. Sacred-time continuity with Dresden requires controlled comparison

The Dresden Codex and living Maya evidence both attest:
- 260-day sacred/ritual calendars;
- ritual timing;
- agricultural concerns;
- divinatory/interpretive use.

But they differ in:
- period;
- region;
- manuscript versus oral/community practice;
- language;
- institutional context.

Current allowed relation:

POSTCLASSIC_DRESDEN_AND_LIVING_MAYA_SHARE_CALENDAR_STRUCTURAL_CONTINUITY_SIGNALS.

Not yet:

DIRECT_UNCHANGED_RITUAL_CONTINUITY_PROVED.

A future transmission/continuity packet must bind specific calendar forms and
regional histories.
