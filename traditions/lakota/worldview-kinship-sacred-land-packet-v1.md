# Lakota Worldview, Kinship, Sacred Land, and Public Ceremony Packet V1

Status: PUBLIC-SOURCE INTERNAL RECONSTRUCTION / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Open the first dedicated Lakota research lane using living community and tribal
sources before comparative synthesis.

This packet is intentionally incomplete.

It asks only what current public sources can establish responsibly about:
- relational worldview and kinship;
- Wakan / Wakan Tanka language;
- sacred land/place;
- public ceremonial categories;
- sacred pipe public meaning;
- vision/knowledge at a high level;
- colonial suppression and living continuity.

## 1. Lakota religion is not treated here as a detachable belief list

Oglala Lakota College publicly describes Lakota leadership education as grounded
in Lakota language, spirituality, beliefs, values, thoughts, philosophies, and
worldview.

Sitting Bull College similarly places worldview, ceremonies, values, belief,
kinship, social systems, land base, and government inside one Native American
Studies curriculum.

Rosebud tribal sources frame Wolakota as lived conduct involving respect,
relations, elders, leadership, compassion, harmony, and community.

Current project proposition:

LAKOTA_RELIGIOUS_WORLDVIEW =
RELATIONAL_LIVED_SYSTEM
rather than
ISOLATED_CREED_ONLY.

Evidence:
DOCUMENTARY_PRACTICE / MODERN_TRADITION.

This does not mean every Lakota person practices one identical system.

## 2. Kinship is religiously and socially central

Standing Rock's current Children's Code defines tiospaye as the root of Lakota
social structure and incorporates traditional-law concepts into decisions about
children and family.

Rosebud's Wolakota material similarly treats social harmony as relational and
ethical rather than merely individual.

Sitting Bull College explicitly teaches kinship and social systems as part of
Lakota/Dakota worldview.

Bounded proposition:

KINSHIP_IS_NOT_ONLY_GENEALOGICAL;
IT_STRUCTURES_ETHICS_IDENTITY_AND_RELATIONAL_ORDER.

A broader "all relations" cosmological reading is supported in scholarship and
public contemporary usage, but exact formulation should remain source-tagged.

Guard:

MITAKUYE_OYASIN != GENERIC_NEW_AGE_UNIVERSALISM.

## 3. Wakan and Wakan Tanka require lexical restraint

Current public sources use multiple English renderings.

Standing Rock legal/public language can describe Wakan Tanka as:
- Great Spirit;
- Creator;
- supreme being/power in current legal English.

Other community and scholarly sources use:
- Great Mystery;
- sacred/mysterious;
- powerful;
- beyond ordinary comprehension.

Posthumus and related scholarship emphasize the polysemy and semantic breadth of
wakan/wakhan rather than treating one English theological category as
exhaustive.

Therefore:

WAKAN_TANKA = ONE_FIXED_CHRISTIAN_STYLE_MONOTHEISTIC_CATEGORY
is NOT ADMITTED.

And:

WAKAN_TANKA = IMPERSONAL_FORCE_ONLY
is also NOT ADMITTED.

Current ceiling:

WAKAN_TANKA_LANGUAGE_MARKS_ULTIMATE_OR_COMPREHENSIVE_SACRED_MYSTERY_POWER_IN_MULTIPLE_PUBLIC_FORMULATIONS.

The precise ontology remains source- and speaker-dependent.

## 4. Relational ontology and more-than-human community

Contemporary tribal education and scholarship repeatedly frame Lakota worldview
through relationship with creation, land, animals, and powers rather than a
strict sacred/secular split.

Oglala Lakota College's Lakota Thought & Philosophy course explicitly situates
Lakota persons in relation to the universe, the supernatural, and all creation.

Current bounded proposition:

HUMAN_PERSONHOOD_AND_ETHICS_ARE_EMBEDDED_IN_A_WIDER_RELATIONAL_WORLD.

This packet does not yet promote one formal animism/perspectivism ontology as
the sole Lakota model.

## 5. Sacred land is not scenery added to theology

Public tribal and public-site sources establish continuing sacred-land
relationships.

Examples include:
- Paha Sapa / Black Hills;
- Mato Paha / Bear Butte;
- Bear Lodge;
- Wind Cave and other Black Hills sacred sites.

National Park Service public interpretation records Wind Cave as an emergence
place in Lakota tradition and presents multiple Black Hills places as connected
to sacred narratives and practice.

Standing Rock's Cultural Resource Code explicitly links protection of cultural
and sacred resources with spiritual and community well-being.

Bounded proposition:

SACRED_PLACE_RELATION_IS_CONSTITUTIVE_OF_LIVING_RELIGIOUS_AND_COMMUNAL_LIFE.

Not:

EVERY_BLACK_HILLS_SITE_HAS_ONE_UNIFORM_LAKOTA_MEANING.

And not:

PUBLIC_SITE_INTERPRETATION = COMPLETE_CEREMONIAL_KNOWLEDGE.

## 6. The pipe is publicly central, but source layers differ

Black Elk's account to Joseph Epes Brown presents the sacred pipe as central to
the public account of seven Oglala rites.

Current tribal/legal and educational sources also retain public pipe language.

Oglala Lakota College's educational framework uses the canupa as a symbol of
prayer/communication with Wakan Tanka.

Current proposition:

THE_PIPE_CAN_FUNCTION_AS_A_CENTRAL_MEDIATOR_OF_PRAYER_RELATION_AND_RITUAL_ORDER_IN_PUBLIC_LAKOTA_SOURCES.

Guard:

BLACK_ELK_SEVEN_RITES_ACCOUNT != ONE_UNIVERSAL_TIMELESS_LAKOTA_RITUAL_SYSTEM.

No step-by-step ceremonial reconstruction is admitted from V1.

## 7. Public ceremonial categories exist; procedural reproduction is held

Standing Rock's current Children's Code publicly names seven ceremonies in its
traditional-law framework.

Black Elk/Brown also preserves a public Oglala seven-rite account.

The overlap supports historical and contemporary continuity of named ceremonial
categories.

It does not authorize ON_THEO to reproduce:
- full procedural instructions;
- private songs;
- private prayers;
- bundle knowledge;
- community-restricted details.

Current axis status:

RITUAL_ORDER_MODEL = PARTIAL_PUBLIC_CATEGORY_SUPPORT.

Detailed ceremonial mechanics:

HOLD.

## 8. Vision and disclosed knowledge

Public Lakota educational and historical sources recognize vision, prayer, and
spiritual guidance as important.

The project can currently admit:

KNOWLEDGE_OR_DISCLOSURE_MODEL =
PARTIAL_VISION_AND_PRAYER_SUPPORT.

Do not reduce this to:
- hallucination;
- prophecy in an Abrahamic technical sense;
- shamanism as a universal generic label;
- one standardized vision procedure.

## 9. Colonial suppression is part of religious history

Late-nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century federal policy targeted Indigenous
ceremonial practice, including Sun Dance.

Public archival summaries record periods of suppression and later continuation
or renewal in public/private forms.

The American Indian Religious Freedom Act of 1978 established federal policy to
protect traditional Indigenous religious belief and practice, including sacred
objects, ceremonies, and site access.

Therefore:

MODERN_LAKOTA_RELIGIOUS_PRACTICE
cannot be interpreted without
COLONIAL_SUPPRESSION_AND_LEGAL_CONSTRAINT_HISTORY.

But:

SUPPRESSION != TOTAL_EXTINCTION.

And:

MODERN_PRACTICE != UNCHANGED_PRECONTACT_FORM.

## 10. Christian/Lakota interaction requires its own lane

Black Elk's life alone demonstrates why "traditional versus Christian" cannot be
modeled as a simple binary.

He participated deeply in Lakota sacred life and also became Catholic.

This packet records the contact problem but does not yet resolve:
- conversion;
- dual participation;
- syncretism;
- strategic adaptation;
- theological reinterpretation.

Required future axis:

CONTACT_AND_REINTERPRETATION_MODEL
with Lakota-Christian evidence controls.

## 11. What V1 does not admit

Not admitted:
- one fixed Lakota creator theology;
- one universal spirit taxonomy;
- one complete creation narrative;
- one universal human-afterlife map;
- one timeless Sun Dance form;
- one timeless seven-rites system;
- all Black Hills traditions as identical across bands;
- "Sioux mythology" as an adequate evidence category;
- restricted ceremonial reconstruction;
- New Age use of Lakota terminology as Lakota evidence.

## 12. V1 internal result

Strongest current internal proposition:

LAKOTA_PUBLIC_SOURCES_SUPPORT_A_RELATIONAL_LIVING_TRADITION_IN_WHICH_KINSHIP_SACRED_POWER_PRAYER_CEREMONY_AND_LAND_ARE_INTERDEPENDENT_BUT_NOT_REDUCIBLE_TO_ONE_STATIC_THEOLOGICAL_SYSTEM.

This is not yet a cross-tradition comparison claim.
