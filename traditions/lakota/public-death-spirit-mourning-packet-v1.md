# Lakota Public Death, Spirit, and Mourning Packet V1

Status: PUBLIC-CATEGORY / SOURCE-CRITICAL MORTUARY CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

This packet asks what current public Lakota/community sources permit ON_THEO to
say about:
- spirit language;
- death and mourning;
- continuing relation to the deceased;
- the publicly named ceremony Nagi Gluhapi.

It deliberately does NOT reconstruct a complete Lakota afterlife map.

Core rule:

PUBLICLY_NAMED_SPIRIT_CEREMONY != COMPLETE_POSTMORTEM_ONTOLOGY.

A second core rule:

NAGI_LANGUAGE != ONE_FIXED_SOUL_ANATOMY_BY_DEFAULT.

## 1. Standing Rock current traditional-law evidence

Standing Rock Sioux Tribe's current Children's Code publicly names:

Nagi Gluhapi — "keeping of the spirit"

among seven sacred ceremonies.

The same traditional-law section also includes the current ethical statement,
in its English approximation, that "we have a spirit."

Evidence class:
DOCUMENTARY_PRACTICE / MODERN_TRADITION.

Secure propositions:
- a spirit-related mortuary ceremonial category remains publicly recognized in
  current tribal law;
- "spirit" remains a live English approximation in current Lakota/Dakota legal
  self-description.

The Code itself warns that English translations are approximate and the Lakota
term governs.

Therefore:

SPIRIT = EXHAUSTIVE_TRANSLATION_OF_NAGI

is NOT admitted.

Source:
Standing Rock Sioux Tribe, Title VI Children's Code.

## 2. Oglala Lakota College contemporary nagi usage

Oglala Lakota College's Wounspe Oaye Tokahe Head Start program states as its
vision:

children will have "strong nagi."

This is a current Oglala institutional use of the term in a living educational
context, not a funerary text.

Evidence class:
COMMUNITY_EDUCATIONAL_SELF_DESCRIPTION / MODERN_TRADITION.

This independently demonstrates that nagi is not only an antiquarian term
preserved in older ethnography.

But the Head Start page does not define one metaphysical anatomy of nagi.

Therefore:

CURRENT_NAGI_USAGE = SECURE.

ONE_FIXED_SOUL_DEFINITION_FROM_THIS_USAGE = NOT_SUPPORTED.

Source:
https://www.olc.edu/head-start/

## 3. Current community-grounded public description of Nagi Gluhapi

The Afraid of Bear–American Horse Tiospaye Foundation publicly identifies
Nagi Gluhapi / Keeping of the Soul among the seven sacred ceremonies and
describes it as a mortuary relation concerning a deceased person's spirit and
its later release/journey.

The foundation explicitly presents restoration of the seven ceremonies as part
of living Oceti Sakowin/Lakota spiritual practice after historical suppression.

Evidence class:
COMMUNITY_GROUNDED_MODERN_TRADITION.

ON_THEO uses only the high-level propositions:
- deceased-person spirit relation;
- a period of keeping/mourning;
- later release;
- living ceremonial continuity/recovery.

Procedural details are not reproduced.

Source:
https://www.tiospaye1847.org/sun-dance

Guard:

COMMUNITY_PUBLIC_DESCRIPTION != UNIVERSAL_LAKOTA_FUNERARY_TEMPLATE.

## 5. Historical scholarship shows plurality rather than one codified map

Posthumus's source-critical work on historical Oglala/Lakota religious evidence
reviews multiple older descriptions of human spiritual components and
postmortem states.

The important V1 conclusion is not the details of any one old scheme.

It is that historical descriptions are not fully uniform and that later
scholarly systematization can make the evidence appear more coherent than it
is.

Evidence class:
SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION + HISTORICAL_ETHNOGRAPHIC_RECORD.

Current method consequence:

HISTORICAL_VARIATION_STRENGTHENS_THE_HOLD_ON_ONE_UNIVERSAL_SOUL_OR_AFTERLIFE_MAP.

Older detailed spirit taxonomies remain below the current public-community
ceiling unless rebound source by source.

## 6. Mortuary relationship versus postmortem path

The public evidence now supports one distinction strongly.

### Mortuary relationship

Living people maintain a recognized ritual/social relation to a deceased
person's spirit through a publicly named ceremonial category.

Status:

MORTUARY_RELATIONSHIP_MODEL = BOUNDED_PUBLIC_CATEGORY.

### Postmortem path

Some public community-grounded and mediated historical sources describe a
journey/release after the keeping period.

But the current source base does not yet justify one Lakota-wide destination,
judgment system, or route.

Status:

POSTMORTEM_PATH_MODEL = HOLD / PARTIAL_SOURCE_LOCATORS_ONLY.

Therefore:

MORTUARY_RELATIONSHIP != COMPLETE_AFTERLIFE_MAP.

## 7. Mourning and relational continuity

The strongest secure proposition is relational.

The public category "keeping of the spirit" presupposes that death does not
immediately terminate all ritual relation between the living community and the
deceased person's spirit.

Current bounded result:

DEATH_CAN_BE_FOLLOWED_BY_A_PUBLICLY_RECOGNIZED_PERIOD_OF_RITUAL_RELATION_TO_THE_DECEASED_SPIRIT.

This does not establish:
- bodily resurrection;
- reincarnation;
- one permanent ghost state;
- one universal destination;
- empirical proof of postmortem consciousness.

## 8. Spirit terminology requires future lexical work

Future lexical packet should distinguish, where publicly and responsibly
sourceable:

- niya;
- sicun / related spellings;
- wanagi;
- other historical terms.

Current V1 does not create a composite "Lakota soul" out of multiple historical
terms.

Required rule:

MULTIPLE_SPIRIT_TERMS_NE_ONE_INTERCHANGEABLE_SOUL_CATEGORY.

## 9. Christian-contact control

Modern funerary and spirit language exists after long Catholic and Protestant
contact.

Therefore English terms such as:
- soul;
- spirit;
- heaven;
- Creator

must be contact-flagged in historical reconstruction.

Current tribal/community use is authentic current usage.

It is not automatically proof of identical precontact semantics.
