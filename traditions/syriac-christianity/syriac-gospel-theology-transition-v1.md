## Four evidence layers

### 1. Gospel textual forms

Current admitted field includes:
- Tatianic/harmonized Gospel tradition;
- Old Syriac separated-Gospel forms;
- Peshitta separated-Gospel standardization;
- Greek Gospel forms known or used in the Syriac environment.

These are text/translation/transmission categories.

They are not identical to churches, denominations, or theological systems.

Guard:

`GOSPEL_TEXT_FORM != ECCLESIAL_IDENTITY`

### 2. Physical manuscript witnesses

Current witness examples include:
- Chester Beatty Syriac MS 709 for Ephrem's commentary;
- Sinaitic Old Syriac Gospel palimpsest;
- Curetonian Old Syriac witness;
- additional palimpsest fragments;
- early Peshitta manuscripts.

These are physical objects.

Guard:

`MANUSCRIPT_OBJECT != VERSION_ORIGIN_EVENT`

### 3. Exegetical / theological reception

Ephrem:
- comments within a Tatianic Gospel environment;
- knows separated Gospel material as well;
- represents a major Syriac exegetical/theological voice.

Aphrahat:
- argues from scripture concerning Christ, resurrection, faith, ascetic
  practice, and communal identity;
- represents a Sasanian Syriac-Christian setting distinct from but connected
  with other late-antique Christian worlds.

Guard:

`SHARED_LANGUAGE != IDENTICAL_THEOLOGY`

### 4. Polemical boundary-making

Aphrahat's anti-Jewish demonstrations:
- directly evidence Christian polemical construction and boundary work;
- may reflect real contact/argument;
- do not neutrally define Jewish belief;
- do not establish collective Jewish guilt for Jesus's death.

Guard:

`CHRISTIAN_POLEMIC_ABOUT_JEWS != JEWISH_SELF_DESCRIPTION`

## Chronology model

The current evidence should not be narrated as:

`TATIAN -> OLD_SYRIAC -> PESHITTA -> DONE`

Instead, the admitted model is:

`OVERLAPPING_TEXTUAL_FORMS + CHANGING_PREFERENCES + LOCAL_STANDARDIZATION`

while authors such as Aphrahat and Ephrem supply theological/exegetical
reception during the same broad late-antique transformation.

## Translation and interpretation

Translation necessarily requires lexical, syntactic, and semantic choices.

But:

`TRANSLATION_DIFFERENCE != CORRUPTION`

and:

`TRANSLATION_DIFFERENCE != THEOLOGICAL_INTENT_BY_DEFAULT`

A variant can arise through:
- source-text difference;
- translation choice;
- harmonization;
- paraphrase;
- scribal change;
- later revision;
- linguistic constraints;
- interpretive intention.

The mechanism must be established locally.

## Geography and intellectual contact

Aphrahat's Sasanian setting and Ephrem's Nisibis/Edessa trajectory demonstrate
that Syriac Christian intellectual production crossed or sat alongside Roman-
Sasanian political frontiers.

Political geography matters.

It does not create impermeable intellectual walls.

## Non-harmonizing profile

Ephrem contributes:
- Tatianic Gospel reception;
- commentary;
- separated-Gospel awareness;
- poetic/exegetical Syriac theology.

Aphrahat contributes:
- scriptural theology;
- ascetic/communal practice;
- resurrection;
- Christological argument;
- Jewish-Christian polemic in Sasanian setting.

Old Syriac witnesses contribute:
- early Syriac separated-Gospel textual forms.

Peshitta contributes:
- later standard/dominant textual form.

No one of these stands for the whole.

## Testament-ready synthesis

Book VI may now portray "the Syriac Jesus" as plural reception:

Jesus/Christ was translated, harmonized, separated into Gospel books,
commented, preached, argued over, ascetically inhabited, and used in boundary
formation across Syriac-speaking Christian worlds.

That is stronger than:
"Christianity was translated into Syriac."

It is also safer than:
"Syriac Christianity had its own single Jesus."
