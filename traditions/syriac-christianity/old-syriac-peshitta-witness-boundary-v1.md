## Old Syriac — witness category

"Old Syriac" is a modern text-critical category for early Syriac Gospel forms
distinct from the later Peshitta standard.

Its best-known witnesses include:
- the Sinaitic Syriac palimpsest;
- the Curetonian Gospels;
- additional fragmentary/palimpsest material identified more recently.

These witnesses do not all constitute one identical text.

### SYR-OS-V1-001

Proposition:
Old Syriac should be treated as a versional/witness field rather than one
single codex or one perfectly uniform recension.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG`

## Sinaitic Syriac

Evidence class:
`MANUSCRIPT WITNESS`

The Sinaitic Syriac Gospel palimpsest preserves a nearly complete early Syriac
translation of the four Gospels beneath a later overwritten text.

Standard palaeographic descriptions place the Gospel undertext around the late
fourth or early fifth century.

### SYR-OS-V1-002

Proposition:
The Sinaitic manuscript is a physical witness to an early Syriac Gospel form.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG`

### SYR-OS-V1-003

Proposition:
Its late-fourth/early-fifth-century physical date is the date when the Syriac
translation represented by it was first produced.

Status:
`NOT_ADMITTED`

Guard:
`MANUSCRIPT_DATE != VERSION_ORIGIN_DATE`

## Curetonian Gospels

Evidence class:
`MANUSCRIPT WITNESS`

The Curetonian Gospel manuscript/fragments preserve another Old Syriac form and
are generally assigned palaeographically to roughly the fifth century.

The Curetonian and Sinaitic witnesses overlap as early Syriac Gospel evidence
but are not textually identical.

### SYR-OS-V1-004

Proposition:
The Curetonian witness supplies an additional distinct early Syriac Gospel
textual form.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG`

Guard:

`CURETONIAN != SINAITIC_CLONE`

## Additional Old Syriac evidence

Recent work has expanded the known Old Syriac witness field.

A 2023 publication by Grigory Kessel identified a Gospel fragment in a double
palimpsest, dated palaeographically to the first half of the sixth century,
that preserves Old Syriac Gospel text.

This is important methodologically:
new physical witnesses can be later than other surviving witnesses while
preserving a versional form classified as older than the Peshitta standard.

### SYR-OS-V1-005

Proposition:
A later physical manuscript can preserve an older versional/textual tradition.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_AS_METHOD_CONTROL`

Guard:

`LATER_PHYSICAL_WITNESS != LATER_TEXTUAL_FORM_BY_DEFINITION`

## Peshitta

Evidence class:
- `TEXTUAL VERSION / TRANSLATION TRADITION`;
- `MANUSCRIPT WITNESS` only when a specific Peshitta manuscript is named.

The Peshitta became the dominant standard Syriac biblical version across major
Syriac church traditions.

For the New Testament/Gospels, its exact formation date and process should not
be reduced to one editor, one manuscript, or one year.

Early Peshitta Gospel manuscripts are attested in the fifth/sixth-century
manuscript horizon.

### SYR-PESH-V1-001

Proposition:
The Peshitta represents a later standardized Syriac Gospel textual form relative
to the Old Syriac witnesses.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG`

### SYR-PESH-V1-002

Proposition:
The exact production process of the Peshitta Gospels is fully known as one
single editorial event.

Status:
`NOT_ADMITTED`

### SYR-PESH-V1-003

Proposition:
Once Peshitta forms appear, earlier Syriac Gospel forms instantly disappear
from use.

Status:
`NOT_ADMITTED`

## Versional comparison ceiling

Old Syriac and Peshitta readings are important for:
- history of Syriac translation;
- Gospel textual history;
- reception and interpretation;
- comparison with Greek and other versional witnesses.

They do not automatically establish:
- the Greek autograph;
- first-century Aramaic speech;
- independent historical attestation of a Gospel event simply because a
  translation differs;
- a direct line from one specific Syriac witness to another.

### SYR-OS-PESH-V1-001

Proposition:
Syriac versional variation is evidence for textual/translational transmission
history before it is evidence for historical events narrated by the Gospels.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_AS_METHOD_CONTROL`

## Relationship to Tatian / Ephrem

The current project model therefore has at least three analytically distinct
Syriac Gospel lanes:

1. Tatianic/harmonized Gospel tradition reconstructed indirectly;
2. Old Syriac separated-Gospel witnesses;
3. Peshitta standardized separated-Gospel tradition.

Ephrem complicates any clean sequence because:
- Tatianic Gospel material remained central to him;
- he also knew separated Gospel traditions;
- later witness and standardization history continued after him.

### SYR-OS-PESH-V1-002

Proposition:
The current Syriac evidence is better represented as overlapping transmission
lanes than as a simple three-step succession.

Status:
`BOUNDED / MODERATE_TO_STRONG`

## Hostile self-check

Potential overclaim:
"The Sinaitic Syriac is the original Syriac Gospel translation."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

It is an early physical witness.

Potential overclaim:
"The Curetonian and Sinaitic texts are one identical Old Syriac edition."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

Potential overclaim:
"The Peshitta was created in one known revision event and immediately replaced
everything earlier."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

Potential overclaim:
"An Old Syriac variant proves the original Greek reading."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

Versional evidence enters textual criticism as evidence, not automatic verdict.

Potential overclaim:
"Early Syriac means closer to historical Yeshua in every reading."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

Chronological proximity of a witness/version does not remove translation,
copying, dependence, or textual-development problems.

## Testament constraint

Book VI may safely say:
- early separated Syriac Gospel witnesses survive in more than one manuscript
  form;
- Sinaitic and Curetonian preserve important pre-Peshitta Syriac readings;
- Peshitta Gospel traditions later became standard/dominant;
- new palimpsest discoveries continue to expand the Old Syriac witness set;
- manuscript date, version origin, and narrated event date remain separate;
- Syriac Gospel transmission involved overlapping textual forms.

Book VI should not say:
- Old Syriac is one book;
- Sinaitic or Curetonian is the original Syriac translation;
- Peshitta originated at one securely known date;
- versional difference is corruption by default;
- early Syriac reading automatically equals original Gospel wording.
