## Source identity and dating

Aphrahat's *Demonstrations* are a Syriac collection of 23 expositions.

The work itself supplies chronological anchors. In Demonstration 22 Aphrahat
states that:
- the first ten Demonstrations were written in an earlier year;
- the next twelve in a later year during the reign of the Persian king.

Modern reference works conventionally map these groups to approximately:
- Demonstrations 1–10: 336/337;
- Demonstrations 11–22: 343/344;
- Demonstration 23: shortly thereafter, often c. 344/345.

Evidence classes:
- `PRIMARY TEXT` for Aphrahat's own dating statements and theological
  arguments;
- `HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION` for calendar conversion and Sasanian setting;
- later manuscript history remains a separate clock.

Guard:

`APHRAHAT_COMPOSITION_DATE != LATER_MANUSCRIPT_WITNESS_DATE`

## Demonstration 1 — Syriac Christian faith and practice

Demonstration 1 builds Christian faith through:
- one creator God;
- biblical patriarchs/prophets;
- Christ;
- faith;
- love;
- hope;
- prayer;
- fasting;
- almsgiving;
- hospitality;
- chastity/virginity;
- patience and purity.

It describes believers as becoming a temple/dwelling of Christ.

practice, and indwelling/temple imagery rather than separating theology from
lived discipline.

`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_APHRAHAT`

## Demonstration 17 — Christ, Son of God

Aphrahat explicitly frames Demonstration 17 as an answer to Jewish objections
to Christians calling the crucified Jesus Son of God and God.

His argument:
- grants Jesus's humanity;
- defends divine/Son language from scriptural usage;
- reasons from Israel's scriptures and biblical figures/titles;
- argues for Christian worship/Christological naming without relying only on
  later conciliar formulae.

### APH-V1-002

Proposition:
Aphrahat supplies direct fourth-century Syriac evidence for scripturally argued
Christology in which Jesus is both human and honored with divine/Son language.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_APHRAHAT`

Guard:
This does not prove Aphrahat was unaware of Nicaea, nor does absence of Greek
technical vocabulary prove an isolated "pure Semitic Christianity."

## Polemical frame

Demonstration 17's interlocutor is constructed as "the Jews" who object to
Christian Christology.

Demonstrations 11–22 include multiple arguments over:
- circumcision;
- Passover;
- Sabbath;
- food;
- Gentile inclusion;
- Christ/Son language;
- Israel/Jewish identity.

This material is historically important, but its first evidentiary address is:

`FOURTH_CENTURY_SYRIAC_CHRISTIAN_POLEMIC`

not:

`FIRST_CENTURY_JEWISH_BELIEF`

## Sasanian / non-Roman location

Aphrahat is an important witness precisely because his Christian world belongs
to the Sasanian sphere rather than being reducible to Roman imperial
Christianity.

This should not be exaggerated into isolation:
- Syriac Christians participated in wider scriptural and intellectual networks;
- Roman-Sasanian political geography did not create impermeable religious or
  textual borders.

### APH-V1-010

Aphrahat demonstrates that fourth-century Christian theological development was

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG`

## Testament constraint

Book VI may safely use Aphrahat to show that the Syriac reception of Jesus was:
- theological, not merely translational;
- ascetic and practical;
- strongly scriptural;
- Christologically argumentative;
- embedded in Jewish-Christian polemic;
- situated beyond direct Roman imperial control;
- committed to bodily resurrection.

Book VI must keep visible that:
- Aphrahat's anti-Jewish rhetoric is Christian polemic;
- represented Jewish dialogue is not a neutral transcript;
- collective blame is reception history, not historical causation;
- fourth-century Christology is later reception, not first-century self-
  description of Yeshua.
