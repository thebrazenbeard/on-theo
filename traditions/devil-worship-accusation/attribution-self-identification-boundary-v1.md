# Devil-Worship Accusation / Self-Identification Boundary V1

Status: ACTIVE ACCUSATION-HISTORY CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Reconstruct "devil worship" primarily as a historical attribution category
without treating accusation as evidence that the accused actually practiced a
religion centered on Satan or the Christian Devil.

Core rule:

ACCUSATION != SELF_IDENTIFICATION.

Additional rules:

DEMONOLOGICAL_THEORY != OBSERVED_PRACTICE.

LEGAL_ALLEGATION != RELIGIOUS_FACT.

POLEMIC != ETHNOGRAPHY.

## 1. Required provenance fields

Every "devil worship" claim in this lane must identify:

- ACCUSER;
- ACCUSED_PERSON_OR_GROUP;
- DATE_OR_PERIOD;
- INSTITUTIONAL_CONTEXT;
- SOURCE_TYPE;
- EXACT_BEHAVIOR_ALLEGED;
- WHETHER_SATAN_OR_DEVIL_LANGUAGE_IS_SOURCE_INTERNAL;
- WHETHER_SELF_IDENTIFICATION_EXISTS;
- WHETHER_INDEPENDENT_BEHAVIORAL_EVIDENCE_EXISTS;
- CLAIM_CEILING.

No unattributed phrase such as:

"Group X worshipped the Devil"

is admissible merely because a hostile source says so.

## 2. Attribution classes

### POLEMICAL_ATTRIBUTION

A religious, political, literary or confessional opponent labels another group
as satanic, demonic, devil-serving or equivalent.

Evidence for:
- accuser worldview;
- boundary making;
- rhetorical strategy.

Not automatically evidence for:
- accused group's beliefs/practice.

### DEMONOLOGICAL_CONSTRUCTION

A learned theology constructs a model in which alleged magic, heresy,
witchcraft or non-Christian religion is caused by or directed toward demons or
the Devil.

Evidence for:
- demonological theory;
- elite conceptual categories;
- prosecution logic.

Not automatically:
observed ritual description.

### JUDICIAL_OR_INQUISITORIAL_ALLEGATION

A court, inquisitor, missionary tribunal or legal source records accusations,
confessions or testimony involving the Devil/demons.

Evidence value depends on:
- interrogation conditions;
- procedural context;
- coercion;
- leading questions;
- formulaic language;
- independent corroboration.

### COLONIAL_DEMONIZATION

Missionaries/colonial officials reinterpret Indigenous sacred beings, shrines or
rites as manifestations of the Christian Devil or demons.

Evidence for:
- colonial Christian interpretation;
- religious suppression/contact;
- translation and semantic recoding.

Not automatically:
Indigenous devil theology.

### FOLKLORE_LEGEND_CONSPIRACY

Narratives describe hidden devil-worship cults, Black Masses, conspiracies,
sacrificial networks or secret elites without adequate independent evidence.

Evidence for:
- folklore;
- rumor;
- moral panic;
- conspiracy history.

### SELF_IDENTIFIED_DEVOTIONAL_OR_THEISTIC

A person/group explicitly self-identifies with:
- Satan;
- the Devil;
- Satanism;
- literal/devotional veneration.

This is the only class that can directly support:

SELF_IDENTIFIED_DEVIL_OR_SATAN_VENERATION

and still requires source-specific meaning.

## 3. Accused groups are not one category

Historical accusations have been applied to different targets, including:
- heretics;
- Jews;
- alleged witches;
- political/religious enemies;
- Indigenous religions;
- new religious movements;
- daycare workers and other accused persons during the Satanic Panic;
- modern self-identified Satanists;
- criminal or extremist individuals.

These targets must not be treated as one historical movement.

## 4. Medieval / early-modern witch construction

Current scholarship shows learned witch categories were historically
constructed through combinations of:
- heresy;
- harmful magic;
- superstition;
- demonology;
- diabolical pact;
- imagined sabbath/assembly.

The resulting category could define the witch as a willing agent of demonic
power.

Current classification:

DIABOLICAL_WITCH =
HISTORICALLY_CONSTRUCTED_PROSECUTORIAL_AND_DEMONOLOGICAL_CATEGORY.

This does not mean:
all accused witches belonged to a Devil-worship religion.

## 5. Colonial missionization

In colonial Mexico and Peru, missionary sources often interpreted Indigenous
religious difference through Christian demonological categories.

Examples include:
- Indigenous rites described as idolatry inspired by the Devil;
- huacas represented as demonic or demon-inhabited;
- Indigenous ritual specialists classified as sorcerers/agents of demons.

Current classification:

COLONIAL_DEVIL_ATTRIBUTION =
CONTACT_REINTERPRETATION_AND_SUPPRESSION_EVIDENCE.

Not:
primary evidence that Indigenous communities worshipped the Christian Devil.

## 6. Satanic Ritual Abuse / Satanic Panic

Late twentieth-century allegations described:
- hidden Satanic cult networks;
- ritual child abuse;
- sacrifice;
- conspiratorial coordination.

The 1992 FBI/NCJRS investigators' guide stated that widespread existence of the
alleged satanic ritual-abuse phenomenon had not been documented and instructed
investigators to focus on independently verifiable facts and alternative
explanations.

Subsequent scholarly literature commonly analyzes the phenomenon as a moral
panic.

Current classification:

WIDESPREAD_SATANIC_RITUAL_ABUSE_NETWORK =
NOT_ESTABLISHED_BY_CURRENT_ADMITTED_EVIDENCE.

This does NOT mean:
child abuse allegations should be ignored.

It means:
abuse evidence and satanic-cult interpretation must be independently evaluated.

## 7. Relation to modern Satanism

The modern Satanism lane already establishes:
- symbolic/non-theistic Satanisms;
- literary/metaphorical Satan;
- theistic/esoteric self-identification;
- organizational diversity.

Therefore:

MODERN_SELF_IDENTIFIED_SATANISM
cannot be inferred backward from:
MEDIEVAL_OR_EARLY_MODERN_DEVIL_WORSHIP_ACCUSATIONS.

And historical accusation cannot be used to define modern groups.

## 8. Comparative use

This lane introduces a methodological dimension:

ATTRIBUTION_STATUS

with values:
- SELF_IDENTIFIED;
- OUTSIDER_ATTRIBUTION;
- LEGAL_ALLEGATION;
- DEMONOLOGICAL_THEORY;
- COLONIAL_TRANSLATION;
- FOLKLORE_OR_CONSPIRACY;
- MIXED_OR_DISPUTED.

Potential expanded neutral question:

HOW_DOES_A_HOSTILE_OR_EXTERNAL_LABEL_BECOME_ATTACHED_TO_A_RELIGIOUS_OTHER_AND_WHAT_EVIDENCE_SURVIVES_FOR_THE_TARGET_GROUP'S_OWN_PRACTICE?

This is a source-criticism method question, not a theology claim.
