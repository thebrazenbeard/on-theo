# Witch / Demonology Accusation Packet V1

Status: LATE-MEDIEVAL / EARLY-MODERN DEMONOLOGICAL CONSTRUCTION CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Separate learned diabolical witch theory from the religious identities and
actual practices of people accused of witchcraft.

## 1. Constructed category

Current historical scholarship shows that late-medieval witch theory combined
previously distinct categories including:
- harmful magic;
- superstition;
- heresy;
- demonic pact;
- demon worship;
- imagined nocturnal assemblies/sabbaths.

Current classification:

DIABOLICAL_WITCH_MODEL =
LEARNED_COMPOSITE_CATEGORY.

The category was historically real as:
- theology;
- demonology;
- prosecution framework;
- cultural imagination.

It is not automatically evidence for:
an organized religion of Devil-worshipping witches.

## 2. Malleus Maleficarum control

The Malleus Maleficarum:
- is a late fifteenth-century demonological/prosecutorial text;
- argues for the existence and danger of witches;
- embeds witchcraft in a theological model involving Devil, witch and divine
  permission;
- provides legal/spiritual detection and prosecution guidance.

Evidence class:
PRIMARY DEMONOLOGICAL TEXT for its authors' theory.

Relative to accused persons:
HOSTILE/PROSECUTORIAL INTERPRETATION.

Therefore:

MALLEUS_DESCRIBES_WITCHES
does not equal
MALLEUS_PROVES_WITCH_RELIGION.

## 3. Contested learned category

Historical work demonstrates that even within late-medieval ecclesiastical
settings, learned authorities could disagree over:
- what a witch was;
- how witchcraft operated;
- whether particular accusations fit the category;
- appropriate prosecution.

Current result:

DIABOLICAL_WITCH_CATEGORY_WAS_NOT_SEMANTICALLY_FIXED.

This is important negative evidence against treating "witch" or "devil
worshipper" as a transparent historical identity.

## 4. Sabbath model

The witches' sabbath developed as a theory of:
- assembly;
- conspiracy;
- transgression;
- demonic allegiance;
- collective anti-Christian practice.

Current classification:

WITCHES_SABBATH =
HISTORICALLY_DEVELOPED_ACCUSATION_TEMPLATE.

Individual trial claims require separate assessment.

## 5. Pact / worship distinction

Sources may allege:
- pact with Devil;
- renunciation of Christianity;
- service to demons;
- worship;
- sexual relation;
- magical assistance.

These are not one proposition.

Project fields must separate:
- PACT_ALLEGED;
- WORSHIP_ALLEGED;
- MAGIC_ALLEGED;
- ASSEMBLY_ALLEGED;
- HARM_ALLEGED;
- SELF_IDENTIFICATION_PRESENT.

## 6. Confession control

A confession under witch prosecution may have:
- coercive context;
- expectation effects;
- formulaic motifs;
- leading questions;
- plea incentives;
- torture in some jurisdictions/cases.

Therefore:

CONFESSION =
PRIMARY LEGAL TESTIMONY
but
NOT AUTOMATIC UNCOERCED RELIGIOUS SELF-DESCRIPTION.

## 7. Witch-cult survival hypothesis

Older arguments that European witch prosecutions preserved evidence of a
continuous pre-Christian witch religion are not adopted as default.

Current project classification:

ONE_SURVIVING_WITCH_CULT_RELIGION =
NOT_ESTABLISHED.

Specific local survivals or folk practices must be separately evidenced.

## 8. Comparison use

The witch-demonology lane supports:

ATTRIBUTION_STATUS = DEMONOLOGICAL_THEORY / LEGAL_ALLEGATION.

It also supplies a positive control for:

ACCUSATION_MODEL_CAN_GENERATE_EXPECTED_TESTIMONY.

This does not mean every testimony is fabricated.

It means model dependence must be tested.

## 9. Strongest result

LATE_MEDIEVAL_AND_EARLY_MODERN_DEVIL_WORSHIPPING_WITCHCRAFT_IS_BEST_TREATED_AS_A_HISTORICALLY_CONSTRUCTED_DEMONOLOGICAL_AND_PROSECUTORIAL_CATEGORY_WHOSE_APPLICATION_TO_REAL_PEOPLE_REQUIRES_CASE_LEVEL_EVIDENCE_RATHER_THAN_AS_PROOF_OF_ONE_HIDDEN_SATANIC_RELIGION.
