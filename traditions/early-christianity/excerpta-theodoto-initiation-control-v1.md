Purpose:
Use the baptism/initiation material preserved in Clement of Alexandria's
*Excerpta ex Theodoto* without pretending the whole compilation is a direct
work of Theodotus or one internally uniform Valentinian source.

## Source identity problem

The traditional title can mislead.

The *Excerpta* are:
- preserved among Clement of Alexandria's writings;
- a collection of extracts, summaries, notes, and comments concerning
  Theodotus and other Valentinian material;
- not safely attributable line by line to Theodotus.

Modern scholarship explicitly notes that the collection includes Clement's own
comments and may summarize/discuss more than one Valentinian teaching.

Therefore:

`EXCERPTA_EX_THEODOTO != ONE_DIRECT_THEODOTUS_DOCUMENT`

Evidence classes:
- `PRIMARY TEXT` for Clement's compilation as an ancient text;
- `PRIMARY TEXT / REPORTED_TEACHING` for passages explicitly framed as
  Valentinian/"they say" material;
- `SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION` for assigning individual passages to Theodotus,
  another Valentinian source, or Clement.

## Initiation cluster — Excerpta 76–85

The surviving cluster gives unusually concrete baptismal/initiation language.

### EXC-V1-001 — baptism and liberation

Excerpta 76–77 links:
- Christ's baptism;
- release from fate/fire/passion;
- rebirth in Father, Son, and Holy Spirit;
- baptism as death to the old life and life in Christ;
- transformation of the soul rather than visible bodily transformation.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_COMPOSITE_TEXT`

Attribution ceiling:
`VALENTINIAN_REPORTED_TEACHING / EXACT_THEODOTUS_ATTRIBUTION_NOT_ASSUMED`

## EXC-V1-002 — washing plus knowledge

Excerpta 78 says liberation is not accomplished by washing alone but also by
knowledge concerning:
- who "we" were;
- what "we" have become;
- where "we" were;
- where "we" were placed;
- where "we" are going;
- from what "we" are redeemed;
- birth and rebirth.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_COMPOSITE_TEXT`

Historical significance:
This directly resists a false opposition between ritual washing and gnosis.
The text coordinates them.

Guard:

`GNOSIS + BAPTISM != DISEMBODIED_ANTI_RITUALISM`

## EXC-V1-003 — fate and baptism

The cluster represents baptism as changing the person's relation to Fate and
hostile cosmic powers.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_REPORTED_THEOLOGY`

Practice ceiling:
This is theological interpretation of baptism, not empirical evidence that
astrological fate ceased to affect initiates.

## EXC-V1-004 — threefold sealing

Excerpta 80 speaks of being sealed by Father, Son, and Holy Spirit and released
from hostile powers.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_TEXT`

Practice inference:
`PARTIAL`

Reason:
the language may reflect baptismal formula/practice, but V1 does not infer an
exact liturgical script from the passage alone.

## EXC-V1-005 — water and Spirit

Excerpta 81 presents baptism as having:
- a sensible/material water dimension;
- an intellectual/spiritual dimension through Spirit.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_TEXT`

This reinforces:
`MATERIAL_RITE_AND_SPIRITUAL_MEANING_CAN_COEXIST`

## EXC-V1-006 — bread, oil, water, Name

Excerpta 82 says:
- bread and oil are sanctified by the power of the Name;
- they become spiritually efficacious rather than remaining merely as they
  appeared when received;
- water in exorcism/baptism both wards off evil and sanctifies.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_TEXT`

Practice inference:
`PARTIAL / MODERATE`

Guard:

`TEXTUAL_RITUAL_OBJECT_DESCRIPTION != COMPLETE_OBSERVED_LITURGY`

## EXC-V1-007 — preparation for baptism

Excerpta 83–84 links baptismal preparation with:
- purity;
- joy joined with fear;
- fasting;
- supplication;
- prayer;
- raised hands;
- kneeling.

Status:
`BOUNDED_FOR_TEXT / PARTIAL_FOR_PRACTICE`

This is stronger practice texture than abstract sacramental vocabulary, but the
composite transmission still prevents reconstructing one universal Valentinian
initiation rite.

## Intra-Valentinian diversity control — Irenaeus AH 1.21

Irenaeus reports multiple different "redemption" practices among opponents he
groups in this field:
- nuptial-couch / spiritual-marriage rites;
- water baptism with invocations;
- oil/water rites;
- balsam/anointing;
- some groups rejecting visible rites in favor of knowledge alone;
- rites associated with death in some cases.

Evidence class:
`PRIMARY TEXT` for Irenaeus's hostile report.

Status:
`BOUNDED_FOR_DIVERSITY / MEDIATED_FOR_EXACT_PRACTICE`

The polemical tone matters, but the very diversity Irenaeus complains about
undercuts any model of one uniform Valentinian liturgy.

### EXC-V1-008

Class:
`HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION`

Status:
`BOUNDED / MODERATE_TO_STRONG`

Guard:

`IRENAEUS_CATEGORY != ONE_SELF_IDENTIFIED_INSTITUTION`

## Composite conclusion

The *Excerpta* cluster plus *Gospel of Philip* and Irenaeus supports:

`VALENTINIAN_ASSOCIATED_CHRISTIANITY_INCLUDED_RITUAL_THEOLOGY`

but not:

`ONE_VALENTINIAN_RITUAL_SYSTEM`

The evidence instead suggests:
- embodied rites;
- theological interpretation of rites;
- gnosis as part of initiation;
- material elements;
- competing redemption practices;
- internal diversity.

## Hostile self-check

Potential overclaim:
"Excerpta 76–85 is a verbatim Theodotus baptismal manual."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

Potential overclaim:
"The washing-plus-knowledge formula proves Valentinians rejected baptism."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

It says the opposite: washing alone is insufficient, not absent.

Potential overclaim:
"Bread, oil, and water prove a complete reconstructed liturgy."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

Potential overclaim:
"Irenaeus reports a nuptial couch, therefore Gospel of Philip's bridal chamber
was literal sexual ritual."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

The texts, groups, and ritual referents cannot be collapsed that way.

Potential overclaim:
"All Valentinians practiced baptism."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

Irenaeus himself reports some who rejected visible rites.

## Testament constraint

Book V may now state that the Valentinian field included:
- baptism interpreted as liberation/rebirth;
- washing coordinated with knowledge;
- triadic sealing language;
- water and Spirit;
- bread, oil, exorcism/sanctification;
- fasting, prayer, raised hands, kneeling as initiation preparation in the
  composite witness;
- competing forms of "redemption" according to hostile external testimony.

Book V must not attribute all of this:
- specifically to Theodotus;
- to every Valentinian;
- to one standardized church;
- to one exact sequence of ceremonies.
