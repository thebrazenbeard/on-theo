# Jesus Inner-Circle Memory / Roman-Cultivation Hypothesis V1

Status: `SPECULATIVE MODEL / SOURCE-CRITICAL TEST PLAN / REVIEW REQUIRED / NON-CANONICAL`

## Purpose

This packet records and decomposes a family of hypotheses concerning the arrest, betrayal memory, apostolic authority, Mary Magdalene, Judas Iscariot, Thomas, Peter, possible social stigma around Jesus' parentage, and possible Roman observation/cultivation of the post-execution movement.

It does **not** assume that any conspiracy occurred.

The project question is whether later Christian memory may have concentrated, rehabilitated, displaced, or re-authored roles in ways that can be tested against source chronology.

Core guards:

`LATER COUNTER-MEMORY != FIRST-CENTURY EYEWITNESS`

`LITERARY VILLAINIZATION != PROOF OF HISTORICAL FRAME-UP`

`ROMAN CAPABILITY != ROMAN ACTION`

`INSTITUTIONAL BENEFIT != ORIGINAL DESIGN`

`LATER AUTHORITY CONFLICT != DIRECT ACCESS TO JESUS`

`METAPHYSICAL INTERPRETATION != HISTORICAL EVIDENCE`

## Hypothesis family

### H0 — Minimal historical crisis model

A real Jewish teacher, Jesus/Yeshua, was executed under Roman authority. His followers experienced an arrest/execution crisis. Early surviving Christian tradition preserves failure among the inner circle: Judas hands Jesus over in the Synoptic narrative, the disciples flee in Mark, and Peter denies association with Jesus. Later traditions rehabilitate surviving apostolic authorities while Judas becomes the paradigmatic betrayer.

Classification: `HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION`

Current status: `BOUNDED / MODERATE-STRONG` for the broad crisis and early betrayal/flight/denial traditions; exact private events remain less secure.

### H1 — Betrayal-memory concentration

The historical crisis may have involved broader failure, compromise, disclosure, collaboration, or self-preservation among more than one follower. Later memory may have concentrated intentional betrayal into Judas while preserving recoverable failures for apostles whose authority remained useful to surviving communities.

This does **not** require Judas to be wholly invented. A historical Judas could have participated while later tradition simplified a messier crisis.

Classification: `SPECULATIVE MODEL`

Current status: `PARTIAL / LOW-MODERATE`

Evidence that makes the model worth testing:
- Mark already preserves collective flight and Peter's denial rather than idealizing all non-Judas disciples.
- later apostolic authority creates a plausible memory incentive to distinguish forgivable failure from irredeemable betrayal.
- Judas traditions become increasingly elaborated across canonical and later literature.

Evidence currently missing:
- an independently early source saying that multiple apostles intentionally cooperated in Jesus' arrest;
- an early rival accusation that Peter, Thomas, Mary, or others were Roman or priestly collaborators;
- administrative evidence of payments or recruitment.

### H2 — Collective bribery / agent-insertion model

Roman or local authorities bribed, recruited, coerced, or inserted agents among Jesus' followers, after which Judas became the public scapegoat.

Classification: `SPECULATIVE MODEL`

Current status: `UNKNOWN / CURRENTLY WEAK`

Reasons for restraint:
- one insider able to reveal a low-risk arrest location would be operationally simpler than collective recruitment;
- the surviving early evidence contains no direct administrative or rival-Christian accusation of a collectively purchased apostolic circle;
- the model becomes unfalsifiable if every disagreement among early Christians is treated as staged evidence.

Positive evidence that would materially strengthen it:
1. early independent accusations of collaboration by named non-Judas disciples;
2. documentary evidence of Roman/local payments, protection, patronage, or intelligence relationships;
3. anomalous protection of one apostolic faction that cannot be explained by ordinary patronage/citizenship;
4. a demonstrable early textual trajectory systematically erasing named collaborators.

### H3 — Mary Magdalene authority-displacement model

Mary Magdalene was a historically significant follower of Jesus whose later authority was contested. Some later Christian traditions elevate her as a recipient of revelation or a disciple with superior understanding; other traditions subordinate or challenge her. The later Western image of Mary Magdalene as a prostitute is not a canonical Gospel claim.

Classification:
- Mary's historical prominence: `HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION`
- later Mary/Peter authority conflict: `PRIMARY TEXT / LATER TRADITION`
- deliberate coordinated first-century slander campaign: `SPECULATIVE MODEL`

Current status:
- Mary as follower, crucifixion/resurrection witness, and important disciple: `BOUNDED / STRONG`.
- canonical texts calling Mary a prostitute: `NOT_ADMITTED`.
- later Western prostitute conflation: `BOUNDED / STRONG`.
- deliberate coordinated suppression from the first generation: `UNKNOWN`.

The *Gospel of Mary* places Mary in a revelatory/teaching role and preserves conflict with Peter and Andrew, with Levi defending her. This is direct evidence for a later Christian authority discourse, not direct evidence that the exact dispute occurred during Jesus' lifetime.

### H4 — Judas as alternative-understanding figure

The *Gospel of Judas* portrays Judas and the Twelve very differently from the canonical Gospels. In a major scholarly reading, Judas uniquely recognizes Jesus' true origin/identity while the Twelve misunderstand; other scholarship emphasizes that Judas' final role may still be deeply negative.

Classification: `PRIMARY TEXT` for what the *Gospel of Judas* says; `SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION` for whether Judas is positive, negative, or paradoxical.

Current status:
- later Christian tradition could reverse or radically reframe Judas: `BOUNDED / STRONG`.
- *Gospel of Judas* preserves first-century eyewitness memory of the historical Judas: `NOT_ADMITTED` without further evidence.

The text is therefore evidence that Judas' meaning remained contested, not proof that canonical Judas was framed.

### H5 — Mary and Judas as "those who understood"

A literary pattern exists in some extracanonical texts in which a figure marginalized or attacked by other disciples is given privileged understanding. Mary and Judas can each occupy that role in different writings.

Classification: `COMPARATIVE TEXTUAL PATTERN`

Current status: `BOUNDED / MODERATE`.

Guard: shared literary function does not establish a common historical faction, alliance, or conspiracy.

### H6 — Peter as compromised but rehabilitated authority

The Peter-denial tradition is present across all four canonical Gospels and appears embedded in early passion tradition. Peter nevertheless becomes a major resurrection recipient and authority figure.

Classification: `PRIMARY TEXT + HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION`

Current status: `BOUNDED / MODERATE-STRONG`.

This asymmetry is important:
- Peter's failure is narratively recoverable.
- Judas' failure becomes identity-defining and terminal.

That pattern is compatible with memory rehabilitation, but does not prove concealment of a worse Petrine act.

### H7 — Thomas as reluctant / resistant disciple

John portrays Thomas as hesitant and demanding evidence before accepting the resurrection report. Later Thomasine traditions give him a very different revelatory authority.

Classification: `PRIMARY TEXT / LATER TRADITION`

Current status: `BOUNDED` for the literary portrayals.

Guard: "reluctant to participate in a Roman/apostolic plot" is `UNKNOWN`; the texts do not presently establish it.

### H8 — "Son of Mary" and possible social stigma

Mark 6:3 preserves the unusual hometown designation "the son of Mary" without naming a father in the standard reading. The scene is hostile: the hometown audience takes offense at Jesus.

Scholarly explanations include:
- Mary was widowed;
- informal/local naming;
- literary characterization;
- theological development;
- an insinuation of illegitimacy or uncertain paternity.

A 2025 *New Testament Studies* article reviews the long-standing illegitimacy/slur interpretation but proposes a different gendered literary explanation; the question remains contested.

Classification: `PRIMARY TEXT + SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION`

Current status:
- Mark's matronymic in a hostile hometown scene: `BOUNDED / STRONG`.
- it proves Jesus was considered illegitimate: `UNKNOWN / CONTESTED`.
- Jesus was bullied in childhood because Mary was called sexually immoral: `SPECULATIVE MODEL`.

The later Celsus/Panthera accusation is evidence for later hostile paternity polemic, not proof of a Nazareth childhood rumor.

### H9 — psychologically/spiritually unusual Yeshua

The project may explore a non-divine Yeshua whose unusual religious insight emerged from temperament, social marginalization, trauma, contemplative experience, altered-state phenomena, or unusually intense moral/religious cognition.

Classification: `SPECULATIVE MODEL`

Current status: `UNKNOWN`.

Historical method may investigate social context, shame, family conflict, charismatic behavior, apocalyptic cognition, healing reputation, and religious experience. It cannot independently establish that a person was "more tapped into a soul" or saw through a metaphysical veil.

### H10 — Roman cultivation rather than Roman invention

Roman authorities or elites may have regarded a growing posthumous Jesus movement as potentially useful for order, social integration, patronage, or imperial management and may have monitored, protected, steered, or cultivated some forms of it.

This differs from:
- Rome inventing Jesus;
- Tiberius personally ordering Jesus' execution;
- Tiberius believing Jesus was divine;
- Christianity being centrally engineered from its beginning.

Classification: `SPECULATIVE MODEL`

Current status: `UNKNOWN / CURRENTLY LOW CONFIDENCE`.

Relevant background:
- Roman religion was accumulative and capable of incorporating foreign cults.
- Roman elites also regulated cult organization where they perceived political/social risk.
- Tiberius strongly institutionalized the posthumous cult/charisma of Augustus while refusing comparable divine honors for himself.
- this establishes that Roman rulers understood the political usefulness of posthumous charisma in general; it does **not** connect Tiberius to deliberate cultivation of the Jesus movement.

### H11 — Tertullian's Tiberius tradition

*Tertullian, Apology* 5 (c. 197 CE) claims that Tiberius received intelligence from Palestine concerning Christ, referred the matter to the Senate with a favorable judgment, retained his judgment after senatorial rejection, and threatened accusers of Christians.

Classification: `PRIMARY TEXT` for Tertullian's late-second-century claim; `LATER TRADITION` relative to Jesus/Tiberius.

Current status:
- Tertullian made the claim: `BOUNDED / STRONG`.
- Tiberius actually did this: `UNKNOWN`.
- the episode demonstrates a Tiberian attempt to cultivate Christianity: `NOT_ADMITTED` on current evidence.

The chronological gap and apologetic utility of the story require independent corroboration before promotion.

### H12 — institutional capture without original conspiracy

A movement can be genuine at origin and later be appropriated by institutions that did not create it. Roman imperial use of Christianity in late antiquity does not require Tiberian authorship.

Classification: `HISTORICAL MODEL / PROJECT INFERENCE`

Current status: `BOUNDED / STRONG` that the relationship between Roman power and Christianity changed drastically over the first four centuries; causal details require period-specific analysis.

This is currently a stronger explanatory model than an original Tiberian engineering hypothesis because it requires fewer unsupported steps.

## Discriminators

### D1 — Was Judas singular this early?

Seek:
- pre-Markan or independent Judas traditions;
- whether Paul's `the Twelve` language creates any meaningful tension with a dead/defected Judas;
- independent passion strata;
- earliest naming and motive variants.

Potential result:
- multiple independent early Judas traditions strengthen a historical Judas core while still allowing later intensification.

### D2 — Did rival Christians accuse other apostles of collaboration?

Search:
- Jewish-Christian fragments;
- anti-Pauline traditions;
- Petrine/Pauline polemic;
- Gospel of Mary / Thomas / Judas;
- patristic quotations of lost rivals;
- Jewish and pagan polemic.

A specific early accusation of Roman/client collaboration would materially strengthen H1/H2.

### D3 — Is Mary demoted over time in a way correlated with authority competition?

Compare:
- canonical resurrection traditions;
- *Gospel of Mary*;
- *Gospel of Philip*;
- *Pistis Sophia*;
- patristic treatments;
- translation/manuscript substitutions where Mary and Peter compete.

Need distinguish:
`TEXTUAL AUTHORITY POLITICS` from `PROOF OF FIRST-CENTURY EVENTS`.

### D4 — Does the Judas villain profile intensify diachronically?

Track:
- Mark;
- Matthew;
- Luke-Acts;
- John;
- Papias;
- later patristic tradition;
- *Gospel of Judas* as counter-tradition.

Test specific additions: money, Satan, theft, remorse, death, prophetic fulfillment, demonization.

### D5 — Is Roman responsibility progressively displaced?

Track the passion tradition and later Pilate literature proposition-by-proposition:
- Pilate's agency;
- priestly/Judean elite agency;
- crowd agency;
- disciple agency;
- theological necessity.

Guard: theological redaction does not automatically imply Roman direction.

### D6 — Is there early Roman patronage/control anomalous enough to require explanation?

Look for:
- state-connected patrons;
- imperial household Christians;
- protection patterns;
- citizenship/legal privileges;
- informant networks;
- correspondence;
- accusations of political clientage;
- discrepancies between official policy and local treatment.

### D7 — Negative control: anti-imperial Christianity

Any Roman-engineering model must explain rather than ignore:
- execution of Jesus by Roman authority;
- later Roman punishment of Christians;
- apocalyptic anti-imperial imagery;
- Christian refusal of some civic cult practices;
- decentralized and conflict-heavy early communities.

A model that calls every anti-Roman datum "cover" fails falsifiability.

## Current provisional assessment

The strongest evidence-supported observations are not yet a conspiracy:

1. Jesus/Yeshua was a real first-century Jewish figure executed under Roman authority.
2. early tradition preserves serious failure within the disciple circle.
3. Mary Magdalene was an important disciple/witness and is not called a prostitute in the canonical Gospels.
4. later Christian texts preserve genuine disputes over apostolic authority, including Mary-versus-Peter configurations.
5. later texts can radically reinterpret Judas.
6. Mark's "son of Mary" wording is unusual and occurs in a hostile hometown scene, but its social meaning remains contested.
7. Roman rulers understood religion and posthumous charisma as politically consequential.
8. Tertullian preserves a late claim of Tiberian interest in Christ, but the event itself is uncorroborated.

The strongest live speculative question is therefore not:

`DID ROME INVENT CHRISTIANITY?`

but:

`DID A MESSIER ARREST / AUTHORITY CRISIS BECOME SIMPLIFIED INTO CANONICAL MEMORY, AND DID LATER POLITICAL OR ECCLESIAL INSTITUTIONS SELECTIVELY AMPLIFY THE FORMS OF THAT MEMORY MOST USEFUL TO THEM?`

That question is historically testable in parts.
