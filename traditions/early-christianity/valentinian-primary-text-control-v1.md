## Source layer A — Ptolemy, Letter to Flora

Evidence class:
`PRIMARY TEXT` for a second-century Valentinian teacher, preserved inside the
later heresiological transmission of Epiphanius.

The Cambridge Edition identifies Ptolemy as a prominent follower of Valentinus
and treats the *Letter to Flora* as his surviving text. The letter addresses
the status and authorship of Mosaic law.

Bounded propositions:
- Ptolemy rejects both the claim that the whole Mosaic law comes directly from
  the perfect Father and the opposite claim that it comes wholly from the
  devil;
- he distinguishes multiple sources/layers within the law;
- he gives continuing but differentiated value to parts of the law under the
  Savior's interpretation;
- this position is therefore not reducible to "Valentinians rejected the Old
  Testament as evil."

Claim ceiling:
`STRONG_FOR_PTOLEMY / NOT_ALL_VALENTINIANS`

Transmission guard:
the text survives through Epiphanius, so its survival is mediated even though
the embedded letter is treated as Ptolemy's work.

## Source layer B — Tripartite Tractate, NHC I,5

Evidence classes:
- `MANUSCRIPT WITNESS` for the surviving Coptic codex;
- `PRIMARY TEXT` for the theological work transmitted in that witness.

The *Tripartite Tractate* is anonymous and untitled in the manuscript. Einar
Thomassen's study identifies it as a Valentinian treatise, probably translated
from Greek, and places its composition most likely in the later third century.
It is therefore direct evidence for a developed Valentinian theological system,
not direct evidence for the exact teaching of Valentinus in Rome a century
earlier.

Bounded propositions:
- developed Valentinian theology could articulate a hierarchy of Father, Son,
  totality/aeons, rupture, ignorance/error, Savior, and reintegration;
- Valentinian theology was capable of extended systematic Christian argument,
  not merely disconnected mythic names reported by opponents;
- the text belongs to a later development layer and must not be retrojected
  wholesale onto Valentinus or every second-century Valentinian teacher.

Claim ceiling:
`STRONG_FOR_THIS_VALENTINIAN_TEXT / TEMPORAL_GENERALIZATION_RESTRICTED`

Sources:
- Thomassen dissertation:
  https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/2825
- Coptic Gnostic Library Online catalogue:
  https://scholarlyeditions.brill.com/cglo/
- public translation access:
  https://www.gnosis.org/naghamm/tripart.htm

## Source layer C — Gospel of Truth, NHC I,3 / XII,2

Evidence classes:
- `MANUSCRIPT WITNESS` for the surviving Coptic witnesses;
- `PRIMARY TEXT` for the work itself;
- `SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION` for its precise relationship to Valentinus and
  Valentinian origins.

The work was used in Valentinian contexts and has long been associated with
Valentinus. That association must not be collapsed into certain authorship.

A recent Cambridge treatment argues that the Nag Hammadi work can be connected
with the "Gospel of Truth" known to Irenaeus and with Valentinian reception,
while also emphasizing that Valentinian use does not prove Valentinian origin.

Bounded propositions:
- the text is strong evidence for an early Christian discourse centered on
  ignorance/error, knowledge of the Father, revelation, and the Son/Name;
- it is relevant to Valentinian reception history;
- `VALENTINUS_WROTE_GOSPEL_OF_TRUTH` remains unestablished.

Claim ceiling:
`STRONG_FOR_TEXT / BOUNDED_FOR_VALENTINIAN_RECEPTION / UNKNOWN_AUTHORSHIP`

Sources:
- Coptic Gnostic Library Online catalogue:
  https://scholarlyeditions.brill.com/cglo/
- Cambridge 2025 reception chapter:
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/gospel-of-truth/contexts-of-reception/E42F6B007768F70B6436380DE684DF6E

## Source layer D — Irenaeus, Against Heresies I

Evidence class:
`PRIMARY TEXT` for Irenaeus's late-second-century heresiological testimony.

Irenaeus explicitly says he had read writings of disciples of Valentinus and
had personal contact with some adherents, and he focuses especially on the
school of Ptolemy.

This increases the evidentiary value of his report relative to a purely remote
rumor while not removing its polemical purpose.

Bounded proposition:
Irenaeus is an important near-contemporary witness to Ptolemaean Valentinian
teaching, but his taxonomy and evaluative language remain his own.

Claim ceiling:
`STRONG_FOR_IRENAEAN_REPORT / HOSTILE_MEDIATION_VISIBLE`

Sources:
- preface:
  https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103100.htm
- Ptolemaean system opening:
  https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103101.htm

## Testament constraint

Book V may safely portray Valentinian Christianity as a family of Christian
interpretive and theological experiments rather than one monolithic
"Gnosticism."

It may now add direct primary texture:
- Ptolemy's layered reading of Mosaic law;
- the *Tripartite Tractate* as an internal developed Valentinian theological
  witness;
- the *Gospel of Truth* as a text with strong Valentinian reception but
  uncertain authorship.

It should not state:
- every Valentinian rejected Jewish scripture;
- every Valentinian taught one identical aeon genealogy;
- the *Tripartite Tractate* is Valentinus's own system;
- Valentinus certainly wrote the *Gospel of Truth*.
