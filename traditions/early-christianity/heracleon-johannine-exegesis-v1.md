## Ancient witness — Origen, Commentary on John

Evidence class:
`PRIMARY TEXT` for Origen's preservation and criticism of Heracleon.

Access limitation:
V1 read public English translation material from the Ante-Nicene Fathers
edition as hosted by New Advent. It did not collate a modern critical Greek
edition.

### John 1 material

Origen's Book II explicitly attributes interpretations of John 1 to Heracleon.

Examples include:
- Heracleon's restriction of "all things were made through him";
- distinctions involving Logos, creation, and spiritual persons.

This establishes detailed engagement with Johannine wording rather than only a
second-hand doctrinal slogan.

Source:
https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/101502.htm

### John the Baptist material

Origen's Book VI repeatedly names Heracleon while discussing Heracleon's
interpretation of John 1 material concerning John the Baptist.

This confirms sustained verse-level exegesis.

Source:
https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/101506.htm

### Capernaum / Passover / Temple material

Origen's Book X preserves Heracleon's symbolic readings of:
- descent to Capernaum;
- Passover;
- the Temple episode.

These fragments show Heracleon reading narrative and place language
allegorically within a broader theological framework.

Source:
https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/101510.htm

## Modern reconstruction — fragmentary survival

Evidence class:
`SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION`

Timothy Pettipiece describes Heracleon as a second-century Christian writer
whose Johannine commentary survives only through fragmentary citations,
especially in Origen, and treats it as early Valentinian exegesis.

This supports:
- fragmentary survival;
- commentary genre;
- second-century setting;
- sustained allegorical/exegetical engagement with John.

It does **not** convert Origen's surviving selections into the complete shape,
order, or emphasis of Heracleon's lost commentary.

Source:
https://scholars.wlu.ca/etd/130/

## Affiliation control

Evidence class:
`SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION`

Heracleon has long been associated with Valentinian Christianity, and recent
scholarship continues to place him in dialogue with the school of Valentinus.

However, modern work has also directly reopened the question "Was Heracleon a
Valentinian?" The safest project formulation is therefore:

`HERACLEON = VALENTINIAN-ASSOCIATED / PRECISE_AFFILIATION_CONTESTED`

This avoids two opposite errors:
- treating every Heracleon fragment as a transparent sample of one codified
  Valentinian system;
- discarding the substantial historical evidence for his connection with
  Valentinian circles.

Sources:
- Oxford Handbook chapter:
  https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/40575/chapter-abstract/348058188
- Harvard Theological Review:
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/harvard-theological-review/article/abs/was-heracleon-a-valentinian-a-new-look-at-old-sources/F06FAC9E116662868597CA35699787FC

## Earliest-commentary claim

Multiple modern scholarly treatments describe Heracleon's work as the earliest
known commentary on the Gospel of John, and some as the earliest known
commentary on a New Testament text.

Project ceiling:

`EARLIEST_KNOWN_SURVIVINGLY_ATTESTED_COMMENTARY`

not:

`FIRST_CHRISTIAN_COMMENTARY_EVER_WRITTEN`

The surviving evidence establishes present historical visibility, not absence
of all earlier lost commentary.

## Hostile self-check

Potential overclaim:
"Origen quotes Heracleon, therefore we possess Heracleon's commentary."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

We possess fragments mediated by Origen's selection and argumentative frame.

Potential overclaim:
"Heracleon was the first person to comment on John."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

He is the earliest known commentator at current evidence, not proof that no
earlier commentary existed.

Potential overclaim:
"Heracleon's use of John proves one uniform second-century Johannine
community."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

His commentary proves reception and interpretation by this interpreter; it
does not collapse Johannine reception into one social network.

Potential overclaim:
"Heracleon is Valentinian, therefore his reading represents all Valentinians."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

## Testament constraint

Book V can now say, with provenance, that:
- John was being interpreted in detail by a second-century
  Valentinian-associated rival interpreter;
- Origen later argued with that reading rather than merely listing it as a
  foreign mythology;
- Johannine reception itself was part of the contested Christian field.

Book V should not say:
- Heracleon's fragments reconstruct his whole commentary;
- Heracleon represents every Valentinian;
- he was certainly the first commentator ever;
- Johannine theology belonged exclusively to the later catholic trajectory.
