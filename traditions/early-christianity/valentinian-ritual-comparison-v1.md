## Comparison axis 1 — water

Ptolemy:
- does not supply a baptismal system in the surviving letter.

*Gospel of Philip*:
- water is linked with baptism and paired with light/chrism.

*Excerpta*:
- water baptism is explicit;
- water has sensible/material function while Spirit has invisible function;
- water is also linked with exorcism/sanctification.

Irenaeus:
- reports some water-based redemption formulas;
- reports other groups who avoid or reject water.

Result:
`WATER_RITUAL_PRESENT_IN_MULTIPLE_SOURCES / NOT_UNIVERSAL`

## Comparison axis 2 — anointing / oil

Ptolemy:
- no developed initiation-anointing sequence in *Flora*.

*Gospel of Philip*:
- chrism is central and ranked above baptism;
- oil belongs to the material ritual vocabulary.

*Excerpta*:
- oil is sanctified by the Name.

Irenaeus:
- reports balsam and oil/water forms among some opponents.

Result:
`ANOINTING_IMPORTANT_IN_MULTIPLE_LANES / FORM_AND_SEQUENCE_VARIABLE`

## Comparison axis 3 — knowledge

Ptolemy:
- frames Flora as recipient of differentiated teaching and reserves advanced
  cosmology for later instruction.

*Gospel of Philip*:
- ritual images mediate spiritual truth and transformation.

*Excerpta*:
- explicitly says washing alone does not liberate; knowledge is also required.

Irenaeus:
- reports some groups for whom knowledge itself is perfect redemption.

Result:
`KNOWLEDGE_IS_INITIATORY_SOTERIOLOGICAL / RELATION_TO_VISIBLE_RITE_VARIES`

This is a major anti-flattening result.

## Comparison axis 4 — fasting / preparation

Ptolemy:
- says external fasting is still observed "among us" when done reasonably.

*Excerpta*:
- associates fasting, supplication, prayer, raised hands, and kneeling with
  baptismal preparation.

*Gospel of Philip*:
- V1 does not use it to establish an exact fasting rule.

Result:
`FASTING_ATTESTED_IN_MORE_THAN_ONE_INTERNAL_LANE / STANDARDIZATION_UNKNOWN`

## Comparison axis 5 — marriage / bridal language

*Gospel of Philip*:
- bridal chamber is central symbolic/initiation language.

Irenaeus:
- reports some people performing a nuptial-couch/spiritual-marriage rite.

But:

`GOSPEL_PHILIP_BRIDAL_CHAMBER != IRENAEUS_NUPTIAL_COUCH_BY_DEFAULT`

No identity of rite is admitted without carrier/group/textual evidence.

Result:
`MARRIAGE_INITIATION_IMAGERY_RECURS / RITUAL_IDENTITY_UNRESOLVED`

## Comparison axis 6 — eucharistic/material elements

*Gospel of Philip*:
- bread and cup occur in mystery/ritual discourse.

*Excerpta*:
- bread and oil are described as transformed/sanctified by the Name.

Ptolemy:
- Passover/unleavened-bread language is exegetically spiritualized using Paul,
  but this is not enough to reconstruct a eucharistic rite from *Flora*.

Result:
`MATERIAL_ELEMENT_THEOLOGY_ATTESTED / COMPLETE_LITURGY_UNKNOWN`

## Comparison axis 7 — standardization

The sources do not support:
- one standardized initiation formula;
- one ritual sequence;
- one theory of redemption;
- one relation between visible rite and knowledge.

Irenaeus's polemical complaint about variation independently reinforces this
lack of uniformity.

## What this changes for historical reconstruction

The category "Valentinian" should be represented as a field of teachers,
texts, communities, and related interpretive/ritual programs.

Current evidence supports overlap in:
- baptism;
- anointing;
- knowledge;
- redemption;
- union;
- scriptural interpretation;
- material signs.

It also preserves disagreement/variation in:
- whether visible rites are necessary;
- how baptism relates to knowledge;
- how redemption is enacted;
- exact formulas;
- sequence;
- marriage/bridal symbolism;
- community identity.

## Testament constraint

Book V may now move from:

"Valentinians had sacraments"

to the more defensible literary picture:

Valentinian-associated Christians could baptize, anoint, fast, pray, use bread,
cup, oil and water, speak of redemption and bridal union, and argue about what
those acts actually accomplished. Some related teachers could push in the
opposite direction and say knowledge itself was the real redemption.

That plurality is itself historically meaningful.

Do not:
- stage one composite ceremony built from all sources;
- put Ptolemy's, *Philip*'s, and the *Excerpta*'s vocabulary into one speaker's
  mouth as historical reconstruction;
- identify Irenaeus's nuptial couch with *Philip*'s bridal chamber;
- treat repeated vocabulary as proof of one institution.
