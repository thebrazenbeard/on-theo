Purpose:
Use the *Gospel of Philip* to deepen Valentinian ritual texture without
confusing textual ritual language with direct observation of performed rites.

## Witness and composition control

Surviving witness:
- Nag Hammadi Codex II, tractate 3;
- Coptic text at NHC II 51.29–86.19.

Evidence classes:
- `MANUSCRIPT WITNESS` for the Coptic codex;
- `PRIMARY TEXT` for the work transmitted in the codex;
- `SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION` for composition date, literary coherence,
  Valentinian classification, and ritual reconstruction.

Current scholarly orientation:
- associated strongly with Valentinian Christianity;
- composition often placed perhaps in the late second or early third century;
- the text may contain layers or collected material rather than one simple
  single-purpose composition;
- its use as baptismal catechesis / initiatory instruction is a serious
  hypothesis, not uncontested fact.

Core guard:

`COPTIC_MANUSCRIPT_WITNESS != ORIGINAL_COMPOSITION_DATE`

## Primary-text ritual vocabulary

The text explicitly places the following within a Christian "mystery" complex:

- baptism;
- chrism/anointing;
- eucharist;
- redemption;
- bridal chamber.

### PHIL-V1-001

Proposition:
The *Gospel of Philip* explicitly links salvation/theology with baptism,
anointing, eucharistic, redemption, and bridal-chamber language.

Evidence class:
`PRIMARY TEXT`

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_TEXT`

Guard:
The enumeration does not by itself prove five discrete ceremonies were enacted
as separate rites in every community using the text.

## Chrism and baptism

The text:
- associates baptism with water and light;
- identifies light with chrism;
- says chrism is superior to baptism;
- connects anointing with Christian/Christ identity.

### PHIL-V1-002

Proposition:
Chrism/anointing receives unusually high theological importance in the text and
is not merely decorative language attached to baptism.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_TEXT`

Practice ceiling:
`PARTIAL`

Reason:
The text strongly encodes ritualized anointing imagery, but V1 does not possess
independent documentary or material evidence establishing its exact performed
sequence, formula, minister, or frequency.

## Temple hierarchy and ritual hierarchy

The text maps Jerusalem sacred-space imagery onto:
- baptism = holy;
- redemption = holy of holy;
- bridal chamber = holy of holies.

It also associates baptism with resurrection/redemption and gives the bridal
chamber the highest spatial-symbolic position.

### PHIL-V1-003

Proposition:
The text arranges initiation/salvation language hierarchically through temple
imagery.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_TEXT`

Guard:

`TEMPLE_ANALOGY != EVIDENCE_OF_JERUSALEM_TEMPLE_RITUAL_CONTINUITY`

This is Christian symbolic appropriation in the text, not evidence that the
Jerusalem cult performed Valentinian rites.

## Bridal chamber

The bridal chamber is central to the text's symbolism of:
- union;
- restoration;
- light;
- rebirth;
- entry into transcendent reality.

Scholarship has proposed several ways of relating this language to ritual
practice, including initiatory/baptismal interpretations.

### PHIL-V1-005

Proposition:
The *Gospel of Philip* uses bridal-chamber imagery as a high-order symbol of
salvific union and initiation.

Status:
`BOUNDED / STRONG_FOR_TEXT`

### PHIL-V1-006

Proposition:
The bridal chamber certainly denotes a literal sexual ritual performed by
Valentinians.

Status:
`NOT_ADMITTED`

Reason:
The text's marriage/sexual imagery is deeply symbolic, and the exact ritual
referent remains disputed. A symbolic or initiatory rite cannot be converted
into evidence of literal sexual intercourse without additional evidence.

## Ritual and interpretation

Recent scholarship emphasizes that the text situates material ritual signs
inside reflection on invisible/spiritual realities. Baptismal-catechesis and
initiatory-discourse readings are viable, while other scholarship argues that
the text's purpose cannot be reduced to a simple ritual manual.

### PHIL-V1-009

Proposition:
The text is useful evidence that Valentinian-associated Christians could
connect ritual materiality and theological knowledge rather than opposing
"ritual" to "gnosis."

Class:
`HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION`

Status:
`BOUNDED / MODERATE_TO_STRONG`

Guard:

`GNOSIS != RITUAL_REJECTION`

## Relationship to wider Christian ritual

Baptism, eucharistic meal, anointing, scriptural interpretation, and initiation
were not exclusively Valentinian phenomena.

The important difference is therefore not:

`RITUAL_CHURCH vs GNOSTIC_NONRITUAL_OUTSIDERS`

but rather:
competing Christian groups could share ritual forms or vocabulary while
assigning materially different theological meanings to them.

### PHIL-V1-010

Proposition:
The *Gospel of Philip* undermines a simple historical binary in which later
"orthodox" Christians practiced embodied sacraments while Valentinians pursued
only disembodied knowledge.

Class:
`HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION`

Status:
`BOUNDED / MODERATE_TO_STRONG`

## Dating and affiliation limits

The physical Coptic witness is later than the likely composition of the work.

The composition is commonly placed around the late second/early third century,
but layering/coherence remain scholarly questions.

The work is strongly associated with Valentinianism, but:

`GOSPEL_OF_PHILIP != EVERY_VALENTINIAN_COMMUNITY`

and:

`TEXTUAL_RITUAL_SYSTEM != VERIFIED_UNIVERSAL_COMMUNITY_PRACTICE`

## Testament constraint

Book V may now portray Valentinian-associated Christianity as ritually embodied
as well as metaphysically speculative.

Safe literary texture includes:
- water;
- oil/chrism;
- bread and cup;
- baptism;
- anointing;
- eucharistic language;
- redemption;
- bridal-chamber imagery;
- ranked/linked initiatory symbolism.

But narration must preserve:
- text != directly observed practice;
- bridal chamber != proven sexual rite;
- five named mysteries != proven five standardized ceremonies;
- Gospel of Philip != universal Valentinian liturgy;
- likely late-second/early-third-century composition != direct testimony from
  Valentinus himself.
