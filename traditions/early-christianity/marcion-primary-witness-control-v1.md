## Source layer A — Irenaeus, Against Heresies 1.27

Evidence class:
`PRIMARY TEXT` for Irenaeus's late-second-century heresiological testimony.

It is **not** a Marcionite manuscript witness.

Irenaeus reports that Marcion:
- distinguished the creator/law-prophets God from the previously unknown Father
  revealed by Christ;
- used a Gospel associated by Irenaeus with Luke;
- altered that Gospel according to Irenaeus's polemical account;
- altered Pauline letters according to the same account.

Admitted proposition:
Irenaeus is early external evidence that a Marcionite scriptural program
involving a Luke-related Gospel and Pauline letters was known by the later
second century.

Claim ceiling:
`STRONG_FOR_IRENAEAN_REPORT / NOT_DIRECT_MARCIONITE_TEXT`

Guard:
Irenaeus's verbs of mutilation/dismemberment describe his explanation of the
textual relationship. They do not by themselves settle the modern
text-critical direction between the Marcionite Gospel and canonical Luke.

Access:
https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103127.htm

## Source layer B — Tertullian, Adversus Marcionem IV

Evidence class:
`PRIMARY TEXT` for Tertullian's early-third-century polemical testimony and
extended comparison with a Marcionite Gospel.

Tertullian explicitly:
- frames the Marcionite Gospel as a rival Gospel;
- says Marcion did not assign it an author;
- identifies it with a text closely overlapping Luke;
- argues passage by passage from material he says Marcion's Gospel contains or
  lacks;
- makes editorial priority itself part of his theological argument.

This is substantially richer evidence than a bare heresiological notice,
because Tertullian repeatedly argues from textual content attributed to the
Marcionite Gospel.

Admitted propositions:
1. Marcionite communities used a Gospel with extensive Lukan overlap.
2. Tertullian had access to, or at minimum sustained polemical knowledge of, a
   Marcionite Gospel text sufficiently detailed for verse-level comparison.
3. Tertullian understood the relationship as Marcionite alteration of Luke.

Claim ceilings:
- proposition 1: `STRONG`
- proposition 2: `STRONG_BUT_MEDIATED`
- proposition 3: `PRIMARY_EVIDENCE_FOR_TERTULLIAN'S_DIRECTION_CLAIM`,
  not an independently established direction-of-dependence result.

Access:
https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/03124.htm

## Source layer C — Tertullian, Adversus Marcionem V

Evidence class:
`PRIMARY TEXT` for Tertullian's testimony to Marcionite use of Pauline
letters.

Tertullian organizes Book V around Pauline writings accepted by his Marcionite
opponents. His treatment includes Galatians, 1-2 Corinthians, Romans,
1-2 Thessalonians, a letter he discusses under the Ephesians/Laodiceans
problem, Colossians, Philippians, and Philemon. In the Philemon discussion he
explicitly says Marcion rejected the two letters to Timothy and Titus.

Admitted propositions:
1. A Pauline collection was central to the Marcionite scriptural corpus.
2. The collection known to Tertullian excluded the Pastorals.
3. Textual differences between Marcionite Pauline readings and later catholic
   forms require passage-level reconstruction rather than a blanket
   "shortened Paul" assumption.

Claim ceilings:
- 1: `STRONG`
- 2: `STRONG_FOR_TERTULLIAN'S_WITNESS`
- 3: `METHODOLOGICAL_CONTROL`

Access:
https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/03125.htm

## Reconstruction layer — Dieter T. Roth

Evidence class:
`SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION`

Roth's reconstruction program is important because no complete copy of
Marcion's Gospel is extant. The Gospel must be reconstructed from witnesses
such as Tertullian, Epiphanius, the Adamantius Dialogue, and additional
sources, with witness-specific judgments and levels of certainty.

Admitted methodological propositions:
- reconstruction is necessary;
- patristic witnesses differ in value and behavior;
- absence from a polemical citation is not automatically proof of absence from
  Marcion's text;
- wording reconstructed from a father's paraphrase is not equivalent to an
  extant manuscript reading;
- confidence must be local, passage by passage.

Roth's work therefore blocks a false conversion:

`PATRISTIC_TESTIMONY -> ONE_CERTAIN_COMPLETE_MARCION_GOSPEL`

Sources:
- https://era.ed.ac.uk/items/e135efd3-267d-4610-9f96-fc7e9015d169
- https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0014524609357511
- Roth, *The Text of Marcion's Gospel* (Brill, 2015).

## Direction-of-dependence dispute

Evidence class:
`SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION`

Ancient anti-Marcionite writers explain the Marcionite Gospel as a mutilated
Luke. That remains important historical evidence for how they understood the
relationship.

Modern scholarship does not make the direction question disappear.

Matthias Klinghardt has argued the reverse direction: that the Marcionite
Gospel precedes canonical Luke and that Luke expanded an earlier form.

Dieter Roth has emphasized the methodological fragility of large historical
conclusions built on reconstructed details and the need to establish the
reconstructed text before using it to rewrite Gospel history.

Project status:

`MARCION_GOSPEL <-> LUKE EDITORIAL_DIRECTION = CONTESTED`

Not admitted as settled:
- `CANONICAL_LUKE -> MARCION_GOSPEL`
- `MARCION_GOSPEL -> CANONICAL_LUKE`

Sources:
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/markion-vs-lukas-pladoyer-fur-die-wiederaufnahme-eines-alten-falles/5AEB03E63A09382F9A3DB99192D3F58F
- https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0014524609357511

## Explicit non-admissions

V1 does not admit:
- a complete verbatim text of Marcion's Gospel;
- a complete verbatim Apostolikon;
- any reconstructed wording as a manuscript witness unless independently
  attested;
- "Marcion mutilated Luke" as a project-level text-critical fact;
- "Luke expanded Marcion" as a project-level text-critical fact;
- "Marcion invented Paul";
- "Marcion created the New Testament" as a monocausal historical fact;
- later catholic heresiological terminology as Marcion's own self-description;
- every doctrine attributed to Marcion by every later opponent without
  source-specific review.

## Testament constraint

For downstream literary use in *The Testament of the Spark*, Book V may
present Marcionite Christianity as a genuine rival second-century Christian
configuration with a Gospel/Pauline corpus and a sharply different
creator/Father framework.

Book V should not state as narrator-level historical fact that:
- Marcion simply cut canonical Luke;
- his Gospel was certainly earlier than Luke;
- his corpus alone caused the later New Testament canon.

If dramatizing those claims, attribution must remain visible:
"Irenaeus/Tertullian accused...", "one reconstruction argues...", or equivalent.

## Hostile self-check

Potential overclaim tested:
"Tertullian says Marcion mutilated Luke, therefore the editorial direction is
settled."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

Reason:
the statement is admissible as Tertullian's ancient explanation of the
relationship. It is not independent of his anti-Marcionite argument and cannot
replace textual reconstruction.

Potential overclaim tested:
"No Marcionite manuscript survives, therefore nothing strong can be said."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

Reason:
the convergence and density of hostile witness evidence can still strongly
support bounded propositions such as use of a Luke-related Gospel and Pauline
collection. The absence of a direct manuscript lowers exact-text certainty; it
does not erase all historical inference.

Potential overclaim tested:
"Marcion had no scripture because he rejected Jewish scripture."

Result:
`REJECTED`.

Reason:
the evidence instead supports a positive alternative Christian scriptural
corpus centered on Gospel and Paul.

Potential overclaim tested:
"Marcion created the New Testament."

Result:
`HELD / NOT_ADMITTED_AT_THIS_STRENGTH`.

Reason:
influence on later canon formation is historically plausible and important,
but the present packet does not establish one-person monocausal creation.
