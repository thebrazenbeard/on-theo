# Mary Magdalene — Authority vs Marriage Control V1

Status: CROSS-SOURCE ROLE CONTROL / NON-CANONICAL

## Question

Does Mary Magdalene's prominence and later privileged-revelation tradition
support:
- wife status;
- sexual relationship;
- deliberate slander to discredit her;
or does it more securely support a leadership/authority memory?

## 1. Earliest recoverable floor

Canonical traditions place Mary Magdalene among:
- women associated with Yeshua's Galilean movement;
- witnesses to crucifixion/burial traditions;
- primary resurrection witnesses, with variation among Gospels.

Current 2026 scholarship continues to treat her as a real first-century disciple
and one of the most important witnesses in the early Jesus movement.

Current admission:

MARY_MAGDALENE_AS_SIGNIFICANT_DISCIPLE/WITNESS =
STRONG.

## 2. Prostitute claim

No canonical Gospel calls Mary Magdalene a prostitute.

The later Western composite identification of:
- Mary Magdalene;
- Mary of Bethany;
- the unnamed sinful/penitent woman
is later reception history.

Therefore:

MARY_MAGDALENE_PROSTITUTE =
NOT_EARLY_SOURCE_BIOGRAPHY.

## 3. Deliberate slander hypothesis

Later interpretive traditions sometimes sexualize, moralize, subordinate, or
merge Mary with other women.

This can have the effect of diminishing her apostolic/leadership role.

But:

MARGINALIZING_EFFECT
does not automatically establish
COORDINATED_INTENT_TO_SLANDER.

Current state:

MARY_WAS_LATER_MISREPRESENTED =
SUPPORTED_IN_RECEPTION_HISTORY.

MARY_WAS_DELIBERATELY_INVENTED_AS_PROSTITUTE_TO_DESTROY_HER_AUTHORITY =
MOTIVE_CLAIM_UNRESOLVED.

## 4. Gospel of Mary

Mary appears as:
- recipient of private revelation;
- interpreter;
- teacher;
- target of resistance from Peter/Andrew;
- defended by Levi.

Strongest use:

MARY_AUTHORITY_AND_GENDERED_DISCIPLESHIP_CONFLICT_IN_LATER_EARLY_CHRISTIAN_MEMORY.

It does not call her Yeshua's wife.

## 5. Gospel of Philip

Philip associates Mary with:
- privileged companionship;
- intimacy/kissing language;
- bridal chamber symbolism.

Modern scholarly interpretations differ.

One reading sees the text's literary Jesus as married to Mary.
Another sees the relation as privileged revelation/discipleship rather than
marital status.

Even the pro-marriage literary reading explicitly does not establish the
historical Jesus' marital status.

Therefore:

PHILIP_MAY_SUPPORT_LATER_MARRIAGE_IMAGINATION,
not
FIRST_CENTURY_MARRIAGE_FACT.

## 6. Peter / Mary conflict

A recurring later noncanonical pattern places Mary and Peter in tension over:
- revelation;
- authority;
- speech;
- gender;
- legitimacy of teaching.

This is important evidence for early-Christian disputes over authority and
gendered leadership.

It cannot be projected backward as:
PETER_HISTORICALLY_BETRAYED_OR_SLANDERED_MARY
without independent first-century evidence.
