This is **Baseline A: EARLY_SOURCE_CONSTRAINED_BASELINE**. It is a control reconstruction, not a claim that the earliest surviving texts are always the historically best texts and not a final historical Yeshua.

## What was admitted

The baseline privileges:
- the undisputed Pauline layer where directly relevant;
- embedded earlier Pauline traditions where the earlier layer is explicitly marked as reconstructed rather than physically witnessed;
- Mark as the earliest surviving narrative Gospel, with its literary/theological character preserved;
- first-century Jewish and Roman context;
- Josephus and Tacitus only with later-date, authenticity, and information-dependence controls.

Matthew, Luke, John, Thomas, Mary, Judas, Philip, Valentinian texts, later rabbinic counter-memory, Panthera traditions, later mysticism, psychological reconstruction, and Roman-cultivation models are not allowed to fill gaps in this control pass.

That is an experimental constraint, not a declaration that those sources are worthless.

## The central asymmetry

Paul is earliest but not biographical.

Mark is biographical but later.

Josephus and Tacitus are external but later still, and neither receives automatic independence credit.

That means the baseline must resist two opposite errors:

1. using Paul's silence to erase features that a letter writer had no reason to mention;
2. using Mark's narrative richness to pretend that every episode is independently historical.

## Source floor

### Paul

The seven-letter critical core gives the project its earliest surviving documentary access to Jesus-following communities. For this V1 baseline, Galatians and 1 Corinthians do most of the work.

The important constraints are simple:

- Paul reports personal contact with Cephas and James, whom he calls the Lord's brother;
- he knows other apostles and the Lord's brothers;
- he transmits a tradition of "the Twelve";
- crucifixion is already central;
- he says he received traditions concerning the final meal and a death/burial/raised/appearance sequence;
- he offers almost no ordinary biography of Jesus' childhood, Galilean itinerary, miracle stories, arrest logistics, or Temple action.

Where 1 Corinthians 15:3-5 is called pre-Pauline, that is a **source-critical reconstruction**, not a physically separate manuscript layer. The historical datum is strongest at the level "Paul says he received this tradition"; exact formula boundaries and age remain scholarly questions.

### Mark

Mark supplies the first surviving continuous narrative in the admitted source set.

It places Jesus:
- from Nazareth/Galilee;
- in relation to John the Baptist;
- preaching the Kingdom;
- teaching and gathering followers;
- performing healings/exorcisms;
- in conflict around authority and purity;
- finally in Jerusalem and the Temple;
- arrested and crucified under Pilate;
- buried;
- followed by women at the passion/tomb.

Mark cannot be converted wholesale into an event transcript. It is evidence simultaneously for early Christian memory and for Mark's literary/theological construction.

### Josephus

Josephus provides valuable external control but requires passage-specific caution.

Antiquities 20.200's James/Jesus wording is strongly defended as authentic in much scholarship, while recent scholarship continues to test dissenting arguments.

Antiquities 18's John the Baptist notice independently confirms John as a historical movement leader, but it does **not** independently say that John baptized Jesus.

The Testimonium Flavianum cannot serve as a clean R1 control without reconstructing its textual history because its received wording is disputed.

### Tacitus

Annals 15.44 is useful as later Roman evidence linking Christus' execution to Pilate under Tiberius, but R1 refuses to assume that Tacitus obtained this information from independent Roman archives. Recent scholarship has specifically argued for Plinian/Christian informational pathways.

Tacitus therefore corroborates the persistence of the Pilate-execution tradition; his informational independence remains contested.

## Revised Baseline A after first hostile review

The strongest provisional floor is:

1. A real first-century Yeshua/Jesus stands behind the movement.
2. He belongs inside first-century Jewish religious history.
3. A Galilean/Nazareth origin is probable, though in this constrained set Mark carries most of the geographic burden.
4. Association with John the Baptist, probably including baptism, is historically plausible and widely accepted, but Josephus independently proves John—not Jesus' baptism.
5. A Kingdom-centered public message remains probable but explicitly provisional at moderate confidence pending R2; its precise present/future/inward/social content is deliberately deferred.
6. An early healer/exorcist reputation is historically plausible; specific miracle mechanisms are not historically established.
7. A real follower/family network is unusually well anchored by Paul: Cephas, James, other apostles, the Lord's brothers, and an early group-label/tradition of "the Twelve". R1 does not recover a roster or prove that Yeshua historically constituted exactly the later canonical Twelve.
8. A Temple-linked final crisis remains UNRESOLVED in this baseline. Mark supplies the admitted narrative, but R1 does not independently secure the Temple-action-to-execution causal chain.
9. Crucifixion is among the hardest historical facts.
10. Crucifixion under Pontius Pilate is strong, while the Gospel trial script and exact charge remain reconstructive.
11. Burial was already part of the early proclamation; tomb logistics are a different claim.
12. Followers very early believed Yeshua had been raised/vindicated and reported appearances to named people/groups. R1 establishes the conviction/reporting layer, not that the appearances occurred in any particular ontological or perceptual mode.

## What Baseline A does NOT establish

It does not establish:
- virgin conception;
- a biological father other than Joseph or any Panthera identification;
- childhood shame, bullying, trauma, diagnosis, dissociation, altered states, or unusual "soul access";
- that Judas was framed;
- that Judas acted exactly as later narratives describe;
- that Mary Magdalene or Thomas historically understood Jesus better than other followers;
- an inward mystical Kingdom as the central historical teaching;
- a Roman infiltration/cultivation program;
- a precise messianic/divine self-conception;
- an exact Temple incident, trial transcript, crowd scene, legal charge, or burial tomb;
- empty-tomb historicity;
- supernatural resurrection;
- hallucination, fraud, or any other exclusive naturalistic explanation of resurrection belief.

UNKNOWN is not evidence for the preferred alternative.

## Disposition semantics after hostile review

The first hostile review requires an explicit distinction between:

- a proposition being rejected as a conclusion supported by **Baseline A**; and
- the logical negation of that proposition being historically established.

Accordingly, exact self-conception, detailed Judas-betrayal mechanics, and an inward/mystical core are `REJECTED` **as Baseline-A-supported conclusions**. They are not thereby declared historically false. R2, R3/R4, R7 and R8 may alter their status only with additional source-specific evidence.

## Negative result with project significance

Baseline A is strikingly conventional.

Its strongest Yeshua is not yet the project's distinctive inward/mystical, psychologically wounded, institutionally obscured Yeshua. The constrained sources instead yield a Jewish figure associated with a Kingdom movement, followers/family, Roman crucifixion, and rapid post-death vindication belief.

That does not falsify the project's distinctive hypotheses.

It means they have not earned promotion from the early-source control.

The burden now belongs to later or more deeply stratified evidence to **change** this baseline rather than to assume it. The same rule applies in both directions: R1's failure to promote a distinctive project hypothesis is not evidence for its negation.

## Current claim ceiling

Baseline A can support a historically real Jewish Yeshua, probably Galilean, attached to a real follower network, probably associated with John, remembered as Kingdom preacher and charismatic healer/exorcist, crucified under Pilate, with an early burial tradition and an extremely early follower conviction of resurrection/vindication.
