# R6 Mark 6:3 — "Son of Mary" Test V1

Status: LEXICAL / SOCIAL-HISTORICAL TEST / NON-CANONICAL

## 1. Textual datum

The dominant critical reading of Mark 6:3 identifies Yeshua as:
- the carpenter;
- the son of Mary;
- brother of James, Joses, Judas and Simon;
- with sisters in the hometown.

An early alternative reading normalizes toward:
"son of the carpenter and Mary."

Current result:

SON_OF_MARY_READING =
EARLY_AND_TEXTUALLY_SIGNIFICANT.

The variant demonstrates that the wording attracted scribal pressure or
alternative transmission.

It does not explain why Mark's dominant reading arose.

## 2. Why it is unusual

Patronymics are common in Mark.

A maternal identification without named father is unusual.

This creates real historical/literary pressure.

It does not uniquely select one explanation.

## 3. Illegitimacy reading

In a hostile hometown scene, "son of Mary" can plausibly carry a derogatory or
status-lowering implication.

This is strengthened by:
- paternal absence;
- offense context;
- ancient naming expectations.

But the inference remains indirect.

Current disposition:

ILLEGITIMACY_STIGMA_READING =
PLAUSIBLE.

SPECIFIC_BASTARD_SLUR =
UNRESOLVED.

## 4. Widowhood / absent father reading

If Joseph or another socially recognized father had died or was absent, maternal
identification is possible.

But:
- Mark never names Joseph;
- no early source dates Joseph's death.

Current disposition:

DECEASED_OR_ABSENT_FATHER_EXPLANATION =
PLAUSIBLE_NOT_PROVED.

## 5. Virgin-conception retrospective reading

A Christian reader aware of extraordinary-conception tradition could interpret
"son of Mary" as compatible with no ordinary human father.

But Mark itself does not narrate virgin conception.

Current disposition:

MARK_6_3_AS_VIRGIN_BIRTH_SIGNAL =
NOT_ESTABLISHED.

## 6. Literary matronymic reading

Recent scholarship argues Mark may use matronymics as literary/gendered
characterization, linking "son of Mary" and "daughter of Herodias" within female
lines of naming/authority.

Current classification:

MARKAN_LITERARY_MATRONYMIC =
SERIOUS_SCHOLARLY_ALTERNATIVE.

If correct, paternal absence need not be the primary point of the phrase.

## 7. Informal/local identification

Another possibility is that maternal naming functioned informally in a local
community context.

Current state:

INFORMAL_LOCAL_MATRONYMIC =
PLAUSIBLE_ALTERNATIVE.

## 8. Relation to Panthera

A tempting historical chain is:

SON_OF_MARY
->

->
ILLEGITIMACY
->
PANTHERA.

No source demonstrates that transmission chain.

The equally plausible polemical chain is:

VIRGIN_CONCEPTION_CLAIM
->
COUNTERCLAIM_OF_ADULTERY
->
PANTHERA_NAME/TRADITION.

Current disposition:

MARK_TO_PANTHERA_CONTINUITY =
UNRESOLVED.

## 9. Historical ceiling

R6 can say:

- Mark preserves an unusual maternal identification.
- the hometown context is hostile.
- stigma/illegitimacy is a legitimate historical interpretation.
- other explanations remain live.
- the wording does not establish a specific biological father.

Current claim:

DISPUTED_OR_UNUSUAL_PATERNAL_STATUS =
TRACKED_POSSIBILITY.

HISTORICAL_ILLEGITIMACY =
UNRESOLVED.

## 10. Psychological firewall

Even if illegitimacy were established, R6 could not infer:
- childhood bullying;
- trauma;
- dissociation;
- mystical sensitivity;
- social mission
without separate evidence.

Current guard:

PARENTAGE_STATUS_NE_PSYCHOLOGICAL_BIOGRAPHY.

## 11. Strongest result

MARK_6_3_PRESERVES_REAL_AND_UNUSUAL_MATRONYMIC_DATA_THAT_PERMITS_AN_ILLEGITIMACY_OR_STIGMA_READING_BUT_CURRENT_TEXTUAL_SOCIAL_AND_LITERARY_EVIDENCE_DOES_NOT_DISCRIMINATE_THAT_READING_FROM_ABSENT_FATHER_INFORMAL_MATRONYMIC_OR_MARKAN_LITERARY_EXPLANATIONS.
