## 3. Sedition is Roman perception, not necessarily self-description

A Roman prefect could interpret:
- messianic/regal language;
- volatile Temple action;
- mass following;
- festival preaching;
- public disturbance

as politically dangerous without Yeshua planning military rebellion.

Therefore:

PILATE_PERCEIVED_SEDITION_RISK
can coexist with:
YESHUA_NONMILITARY_PROGRAM.

The project must distinguish:
- Yeshua's intent;
- local elite accusation;
- Pilate's legal/political classification.

## 4. Pilate had agency

Recent Roman-legal historical reconstruction emphasizes that Pilate did not
merely execute an acquitted man automatically at the demand of a crowd.

As prefect/judge, he possessed substantial discretion and responsibility.

Current result:

PILATE_AS_ROMAN_DECISION_MAKER =
STRONG.

Chief-priestly accusation or referral can be historically important without
removing Pilate's responsibility.

## 5. Gospel Pilate-reluctance problem

The canonical Gospels increasingly preserve or develop scenes in which:
- Pilate questions guilt;
- other actors press for death;
- responsibility becomes distributed away from Rome.

This must be read against:
- Roman punishment;
- Pilate's documented coercive record;
- later Christian life under Roman power;
- later traditions of Pilate exculpation.

Current disposition:

PILATE_CONVINCED_OF_YESHUA_INNOCENCE =
NOT_SECURE_HISTORICAL_CONCLUSION.
