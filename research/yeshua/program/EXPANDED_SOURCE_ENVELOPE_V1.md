# Expanded Yeshua Research Source Envelope V1

Status: PROGRAM-LEVEL SOURCE ROUTING / NON-CANONICAL

Purpose: define the full evidence environment for **The Real Testament of Yeshua**
without allowing later material to leak backward into early historical claims.

Core rule:

SOURCE_RELEVANCE != SOURCE_EQUIVALENCE.

A text may be relevant to:
- first-century context;
- Yeshua's historical teaching;
- early memory;
- reception;
- later theological development;
- mystical comparison;
- polemical counter-memory

without being equally useful for all of them.

## 1. Hebrew Bible / Tanakh / textual traditions

Required source families include:
- Torah/Pentateuch;
- Prophets;
- Psalms;
- Wisdom/poetic materials;
- Daniel;
- relevant Hebrew/Aramaic textual witnesses;
- Septuagint/Old Greek where early Christian wording depends on Greek scripture;
- Dead Sea biblical manuscripts where textual plurality matters.

Use:
- scriptural language available to first-century Jews;
- Kingdom, covenant, Temple, purity, prophetic, wisdom, messianic, resurrection,
  heavenly-court, suffering-righteous and apocalyptic antecedents.

Guard:

LATER_CHRISTIAN_READING != EARLIER_JEWISH_MEANING_BY_DEFAULT.

## 2. Second Temple Jewish literature

The project must include the period's actual plurality rather than treating
later rabbinic Judaism as the sole Jewish comparator.

Priority corpora include:
- 1 Enoch, stratified by component;
- Jubilees;
- Wisdom of Solomon;
- Ben Sira / Sirach;
- Tobit;
- Judith;
- 1–4 Maccabees with work-specific date/provenance controls;
- Psalms of Solomon;
- Testamentary literature where Jewish strata and Christian interpolation are
  independently tested;
- 4 Ezra and 2 Baruch as post-70 Jewish apocalypse relevant to trajectory and
  reception, not direct pre-30 evidence;
- other Pseudepigrapha only work-by-work.

Do not use "Apocrypha" or "Pseudepigrapha" as one theological corpus.

## 5. Early rabbinic material

Mishnah, Tosefta, Tannaitic midrash and later Talmudic traditions may illuminate:
- legal debates;
- social memory;
- terminology;
- later Jewish counter-tradition;
- trajectories from earlier practices.

But:
- redaction is later;
- retrojection is dangerous;
- named first-century authorities may be preserved through later editorial
  systems.

Guard:

RABBINIC_LATER_SOURCE != DIRECT_FIRST_CENTURY_TRANSCRIPT.

## 8. "Gnosticism" / Nag Hammadi

The category **Gnosticism** is contested in modern scholarship and must not be
treated as one ancient religion.

Source-specific labels are preferred:
- Valentinian;
- Sethian where defensible;
- Thomasine as a heuristic where source-specific;
- individual Nag Hammadi tractate;
- heresiological attribution when only hostile witnesses survive.

Nag Hammadi codices are fourth-century physical manuscripts preserving works
whose compositions can be earlier.

Required distinction:

MANUSCRIPT_DATE != COMPOSITION_DATE != TRADITION_DATE.

Use:
- later interpretations of Jesus;
- anthropology/self-knowledge;
- cosmology;
- salvific knowledge;
- Mary/Judas/Thomas authority traditions;
- possible preservation of earlier sayings only after independence tests.

Do not use a generalized "Gnostic Jesus" as a historical source.

Modern scholarship itself disputes whether "Gnosticism" is a coherent ancient
category, so ON_THEO records the category dispute as evidence rather than
choosing one reified system.

Later comparators:

Use:

Guard:

SECOND_TEMPLE_HEAVENLY_VISION != DEVELOPED_HEKHALOT_MYSTICISM.

The developed Hekhalot corpus is late antique/early medieval and has a long,
uncertain redaction history. Earlier parallels may be antecedents without proving
direct continuity.

Guard:

MYSTICAL_RESEMBLANCE != SHARED_MYSTICAL_DOCTRINE.

## 11. Kabbalah

Kabbalah proper is principally medieval and later Jewish esoteric theology,
especially emerging in twelfth–thirteenth-century Provence/Spain and developing
through works/circles including:
- Sefer ha-Bahir;
- early Provençal/Gerona kabbalists;
- the Zohar;
- later Safedian/Lurianic Kabbalah.

Earlier materials such as:
- Sefer Yetzirah;
- Merkavah/Hekhalot traditions;
- late-antique Jewish esotericism

must be treated as antecedent/source environments, not simply called
first-century Kabbalah.

Use for Yeshua research:
- **not** as direct evidence of his teaching;
- as later Jewish reception/esoteric development;
- as a comparative test of whether themes claimed to be "Yeshua mysticism" are
  actually much later constructions;
- as a possible trajectory only where intermediate links are independently
  demonstrated.

Core guard:

KABBALAH != SECOND_TEMPLE_JUDAISM.

## 14. Historical-Yeshua relevance tiers

TIER_A_DIRECT_OR_NEAR_DIRECT:
earliest sources capable of constraining Yeshua historically.

TIER_B_CONTEXTUAL:
Jewish/Roman sources sufficiently early to constrain plausible concepts and
institutions without being Jesus witnesses.

TIER_C_POSSIBLE_INDEPENDENT_MEMORY:
later sources that may preserve earlier tradition but require a demonstrated
independence/stratification argument.

TIER_D_RECEPTION_AND_DEVELOPMENT:
later texts valuable for how Yeshua was interpreted, not for direct access to
him.

TIER_E_COMPARATIVE_ANALOGY:
useful only for testing models/terminology; no historical transmission claim.

Kabbalah defaults to D/E for historical Yeshua.
Developed Hekhalot defaults to D/E.
Nag Hammadi works vary C/D work-by-work.
Second Temple Pseudepigrapha may be B, while post-70 works are generally
trajectory/reception controls depending proposition.
