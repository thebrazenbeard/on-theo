## 1. Mark

Mark names Mary Magdalene in:
- crucifixion observation;
- burial observation;
- tomb visit.

In the earliest recoverable ending:
- she receives no individual narrated appearance;
- the women encounter the messenger;
- they flee in fear;
- no successful proclamation scene is narrated before the abrupt ending.

Current result:

MARY_PASSION_BURIAL_TOMB_CONTINUITY =
EARLY_GOSPEL_MEMORY.

## 2. Matthew

Matthew develops:
- women leaving the tomb;
- encounter with risen Yeshua;
- commission to tell the disciples.

Mary is part of a group.

Current use:

MARY_RESURRECTION_WITNESS_TRAJECTORY =
PRESENT_IN_MATTHEAN_REDACTION/TRADITION.

Not independently:
MARY_FIRST_APPEARANCE_RECIPIENT_PROVED.

## 3. Luke

Luke names Mary Magdalene among women reporting the empty-tomb message to the
apostles.

Luke does not narrate a unique resurrection appearance to Mary Magdalene.

Current result:

MARY_AS_RESURRECTION_PROCLAMATION_FIGURE =
CANONICALLY_PERSISTENT.

## 4. John

John gives Mary the most individualized canonical resurrection role:
- she comes to the tomb;
- speaks to Peter/beloved disciple;
- remains weeping;
- encounters Yeshua;
- initially does not recognize him;
- recognizes him;
- is commissioned to tell the disciples.

Current classification:

JOHANNINE_MARY =
PROMINENT_INDIVIDUAL_RESURRECTION_REVELATION/COMMISSION_FIGURE.

This is strong evidence for Johannine memory/theology.

Historical priority over Cephas cannot be inferred simply from John's later
narrative order.

## 5. Paul silence

1 Corinthians 15 does not name Mary Magdalene.

Possible explanations include:
- Paul's received list had a different function;
- women were absent from that particular formula/list;
- different resurrection-memory streams;
- later Gospel development;
- omission for reasons now unrecoverable.

Current rule:

PAUL_DOES_NOT_NAME_MARY
does not prove
MARY_HAD_NO_EARLY_RESURRECTION_ROLE.

But it prevents claiming:
MARY_FIRST_WITNESS
as securely established by the earliest surviving source.

## 6. Mary versus Peter/Cephas

Earliest surviving appearance list:
Cephas is first.

Later John:
Mary receives the first individualized narrated appearance.

These are different source traditions/layers.

Current result:

CEPHAS_PRIORITY_IN_PAUL
and
MARY_PRIORITY_IN_JOHN
must coexist as source data.

Do not harmonize them into one sequence without argument.

Do not use one to erase the other.

## 7. Gospel of Mary

The Gospel of Mary is later and centers:
- revelation;
- authority;
- dispute with male disciples.

It does not function as an independent first-century resurrection eyewitness
record.

Current use:

LATER_MARY_AUTHORITY_TRAJECTORY.

## 8. Historical assessment

Most defensible:
- Mary was probably a historical follower.
- she belongs to early passion/burial/tomb memory.
- later canonical tradition increasingly gives her resurrection-proclamation
  importance.
- John gives her exceptional individual prominence.

Unresolved:
- whether she historically discovered an empty tomb;
- whether she was historically the first person to experience/see the risen
  Yeshua;
- whether Cephas and Mary traditions reflect different communities, sequences
  or memory streams;
- whether later male authority structures suppressed an earlier Mary priority.

Not established:
- Mary was Yeshua's spouse;
- Mary alone understood him;
- Peter deliberately removed her first-witness status.
