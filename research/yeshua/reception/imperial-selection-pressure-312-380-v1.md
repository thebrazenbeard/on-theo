# Imperial Selection Pressure on Christianity, 312–380 V1

## Research question

What changed in Christianity between Constantine's rise and the Theodosian settlement that can reasonably be attributed to imperial selection pressure rather than to developments already underway inside Christianity?

The model under test is **not**:

`ROME INVENTED CHRISTIANITY`.

It is:

`AN EXISTING, INTERNALLY DIVERSE RELIGIOUS MOVEMENT ENTERED AN IMPERIAL ENVIRONMENT THAT COULD REWARD, FUND, ORGANIZE, PUNISH, MONUMENTALIZE, AND DIFFERENTIALLY AMPLIFY SOME CHRISTIAN INSTITUTIONS AND MEMORIES.`

Core guards:

`PATRONAGE != TRUTH OR FALSEHOOD`

`STATE AMPLIFICATION != ORIGINAL INVENTION`

`ORTHODOX VICTORY != PROOF OF HISTORICAL ACCURACY`

`DISSIDENT LOSS != PROOF OF HISTORICAL AUTHENTICITY`

`IMPERIAL PRESSURE != COMPLETE IMPERIAL CONTROL`

## 1. Pre-312 control: what Constantine did not create

Before Constantine, Christians already possessed:
- bishops, presbyters, deacons, and other clergy;
- property and organized charity;
- transregional correspondence networks;
- multiple Gospel traditions and a broadly established fourfold Gospel in many communities;
- Pauline collections;
- theological disputes over Christology, resurrection, scripture, Torah, church authority, ritual, and asceticism;
- competing movements later labeled Marcionite, Valentinian, Montanist, Novatian, and others.

High Christology also long predates Constantine:
- Paul's letters already place Jesus in extraordinarily elevated theological roles;
- John, Ignatius, and other pre-Constantinian texts make strong divine/Logos claims.

Therefore:

`CONSTANTINE_CREATED_DIVINE_JESUS = REJECTED`.

`NICAEA_CREATED_THE_IDEA_THAT_JESUS_WAS_DIVINE = REJECTED`.

The fourth-century controversy concerns the **relation of the Son to the Father and the legitimate language for that relation**, not the first appearance of exalted/divine Christology.

## 2. 312–313: from vulnerability to protected corporate existence

After Constantine's victory over Maxentius and the 313 settlement with Licinius, Christian communities receive:
- legal freedom of worship;
- restoration of confiscated property;
- recognition as corporate property holders.

This changes selection pressure immediately.

A community that can legally own buildings, receive restored assets, and operate publicly has advantages that a persecuted network does not.

This alone does not privilege one Christian faction uniformly.

But the Donatist controversy shows that the emperor quickly begins distinguishing among claimants to the label "catholic."

## 3. Clerical privilege links religious service to state welfare

Eusebius preserves Constantine's letter to Anulinus exempting clergy attached to Caecilian's catholic church from burdensome public duties.

The reasoning is explicit:
when proper religion flourishes, benefits accrue to the Roman state.

This is one of the strongest primary-source indicators that Constantine did not regard Christianity as merely private belief.

The state has an interest in the functioning of the church.

Selection mechanism:

`CLERICAL_STATUS -> MATERIAL/CIVIC ADVANTAGE`.

Caution:
similar exemptions existed for other privileged professions and priesthoods.
The significance is not that Constantine invented clerical privilege, but that he incorporated Christian clergy into Roman systems of privileged status.

## 4. Donatism: imperial recognition becomes a scarce resource

The Donatist conflict is a crucial early test.

The Donatists themselves initially appealed to Constantine.

Constantine:
- referred the dispute to bishops;
- supported the Roman synod of 313;
- summoned/supports the Council of Arles in 314;
- eventually heard the appeal himself;
- backed the recognized Caecilianist/catholic side;
- at points authorized confiscation/exile before temporarily returning to toleration.

The important mechanism is not:
`CONSTANTINE INVENTED A CATHOLIC FACTION`.

It is:
`IMPERIAL RECOGNITION COULD NOW DETERMINE WHO RECEIVED PROPERTY, LEGAL STANDING, AND COERCIVE SUPPORT`.

That is a new kind of competitive pressure inside Christianity.

## 5. 321 and Christian-compatible public time

Constantine's Sunday legislation made the "venerable day of the Sun" a day of rest for judges and urban populations, while allowing agricultural work where necessary.

The wording remains compatible with solar religious language and should not be flattened into a modern Christian Sabbath law.

Nevertheless, a weekly day already central to Christian assembly now receives public legal reinforcement.

Selection mechanism:

`RELIGIOUS RHYTHM -> CIVIC RHYTHM ALIGNMENT`.

Disposition:
`PARTIAL / MULTIVALENT`.

## 6. 324–325: imperial unity becomes ecclesial policy

After defeating Licinius and becoming sole emperor, Constantine becomes much more directly involved in eastern Christian disputes.

In his letter to Alexander and Arius preserved by Eusebius, Constantine describes two goals:
- religious judgments brought toward settled uniformity;
- the "system of the world" restored to health.

This does not prove cynical mass control.

It does prove that **religious uniformity and imperial order had become linked in the emperor's own political-religious reasoning**.

Nicaea in 325 therefore changes the scale of Christian dispute:
- bishops gather under imperial summons and logistical support;
- a theological formula can acquire empire-wide prestige;
- refusal can have political consequences including exile.

The emperor does not invent the theology under discussion.
He changes the **stakes and enforcement environment**.

## 7. Nicaea and anti-Jewish calendar separation

Nicaea's Easter settlement has special importance for a historical-Yeshua project.

Before 325, diversity in Easter calculation existed and was not automatically treated as heresy.

Sacha Stern argues that after Constantine/Nicaea, calendar cohesion becomes a social imperative and by the late fourth century calendrical difference can become heresiological.

Constantine's post-Nicene letter, as preserved by Eusebius, uses explicitly hostile anti-Jewish rhetoric and urges Christians not to follow Jewish calendrical practice.

Selection effect:

`YESHUA'S JEWISH FESTIVAL CONTEXT`
becomes increasingly mediated through
`A CHRISTIAN IMPERIAL CALENDAR DELIBERATELY DISTANCED FROM JEWISH PRACTICE`.

This does not erase Jewish origins from every Christian source.

It does materially reinforce a Christian identity that defines itself over against living Judaism.

## 8. Nicaea did not settle Nicaea

Imperial power does not produce a clean doctrinal line.

After 325:
- Arius is exiled;
- later recalled;
- Constantine pressures church leaders toward his restoration;
- Athanasius and other Nicene partisans are themselves sidelined/exiled;
- under Constantius II, imperial policy often favors non-Nicene formulations;
- Nicaea's creed is challenged, reinterpreted, and temporarily displaced in imperial politics.

This is a critical control.

If Constantine had simply "created orthodox Christianity," the decades after Nicaea make little sense.

Instead:

`IMPERIAL POWER AMPLIFIES THE FACTION IT CURRENTLY RECOGNIZES`.

That can produce unstable swings rather than one master plan.

## 9. Book suppression: direct pressure on textual survival

A later-preserved Constantinian edict against Arius orders his writings burned and threatens severe punishment for concealing them.

The precise history, dating, transmission, and enforcement of the edict require source criticism.

But scholarship accepts an anti-Arius edict as part of the post-Nicene coercive environment.

Eusebius also preserves legislation against several groups labeled heretical:
- Novatians;
- Valentinians;
- Marcionites;
- Paulianists;
- Cataphrygians/Montanists;

with prohibitions on assemblies and confiscation/transfer of meeting places.

Selection mechanism:

`STATE-BACKED COPYING FOR FAVORED INSTITUTIONS + TARGETED SUPPRESSION OF SOME RIVAL TEXTS/MEETING PLACES = ASYMMETRIC SURVIVAL CONDITIONS`.

This is historically real in principle.

Guard:
there is **no evidence that Constantine ordered every noncanonical Gospel destroyed**, nor that the New Testament canon was selected by state book-burning.

## 10. Fifty Scripture copies: amplification, not canon invention

Eusebius preserves Constantine's commission for fifty copies of sacred Scripture for churches in Constantinople.

What this means materially:
- imperial money;
- professional scribes;
- parchment;
- logistics;
- coordinated production.

This is state-supported textual amplification.

But the surviving commission does not securely identify:
- the exact set of books;
- a modern 27-book New Testament;
- Codex Sinaiticus or Vaticanus as one of the fifty.

The fifty copies therefore support:

`IMPERIAL AMPLIFICATION OF SCRIPTURE`.

They do not support:

`CONSTANTINE CHOSE THE BIBLE`.

## 11. Eusebius and the fourfold Gospel as information architecture

This is a subtler selection mechanism.

Eusebius' Canon Tables, created in the early fourth century, turn Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John into a navigable four-text information system.

Modern scholarship argues that the apparatus:
- binds four originally separate Gospel texts into one corpus;
- encourages cross-reading among them;
- implicitly excludes other Gospel texts from this particular fourfold structure;
- becomes extraordinarily widespread in later Gospel manuscripts.

This development is not demonstrably ordered by Constantine.

But Eusebius is simultaneously:
- a major Constantinian-era bishop;
- a recipient of Constantine's fifty-copy commission;
- an architect of one of the most successful textual technologies in Christian history.

Therefore the fourth century contains a material convergence:

`IMPERIAL SCRIPTURE PRODUCTION + FOUR-GOSPEL INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY + GROWING CANONICAL CONSOLIDATION`.

Causation between all three must not be assumed.

The convergence is nevertheless historically consequential.

## 12. Sacred geography: imperial power fixes Jesus-memory into stone

This is one of the strongest and most underappreciated mechanisms.

After Constantine became sole emperor:
- monumental basilicas were constructed at sites in Palestine associated with Jesus and biblical history;
- the Holy Sepulchre complex monumentalized a specific Golgotha/tomb tradition;
- Bethlehem, the Mount of Olives, and Mamre received imperial development;
- pilgrimage infrastructure expanded.

Joan Taylor argues that the Constantinian program is foundational for the emergence of Palestine as a Christian "Holy Land," and that several later-important sites lack strong evidence of pre-Constantinian Christian cult.

This means Constantine's government did not merely sponsor a theology.

It helped decide which **places** would become physically unavoidable embodiments of Christian memory.

Selection mechanism:

`TEXTUAL/TRADITIONAL CLAIM -> IMPERIAL MONUMENT -> PILGRIMAGE -> LITURGY -> REPEATED EMBODIED MEMORY`.

A basilica does not prove the site is historically authentic.

But once built, it can make a location culturally authoritative for centuries.

## 13. Destruction and replacement of sacred space

Eusebius narrates Constantine ordering removal of a preexisting pagan shrine/structure at the site identified with Jesus' tomb and constructing the Holy Sepulchre complex.

Eusebius also celebrates targeted destruction or stripping of some pagan temples.

Modern scholarship warns against imagining a simple empire-wide instant abolition of pagan religion; Constantine's pagan policy remains complex and contested.

The historically safer conclusion is:

`CONSTANTINE COULD USE IMPERIAL BUILDING/DEMOLITION POWER TO RECODE SPECIFIC SACRED LANDSCAPES`.

That is a powerful memory technology.

## 14. Christian symbols enter imperial victory language

Constantine's reign gradually integrates Christian signs, especially the Chi-Rho, into imperial military and numismatic imagery.

British Museum numismatic evidence places explicit Christogram-centered imperial coin imagery by the late 320s, while earlier appearances are subtler and sometimes disputed.

This creates another transformation:

`CROSS/CHRISTOGRAM -> IMPERIAL VICTORY SYMBOL`.

Christianity had already used cross symbolism.

What changes is that the victorious emperor now helps attach that symbolism to Roman public power.

## 15. Eusebian imperial theology

Eusebius becomes a major interpreter of what Christian empire means.

His panegyric and later *Life of Constantine* construct an emperor:
- favored by God;
- victorious because of piety;
- governing in imitation of heavenly order;
- promoting religious unity.

Recent scholarship cautions against saying Eusebius simply identifies Constantine's empire with the kingdom of God.

But Eusebius undeniably develops a Christian theory of monarchy in which:
- one divine monarch rules heaven;
- an emperor can model earthly order on divine monarchy.

Selection effect:

`IMPERIAL MONARCHY GAINS CHRISTIAN THEOLOGICAL LEGITIMATION`.

At the same time, later bishops also develop theories limiting emperors and insisting they remain subordinate to God.

So Christian imperial theology is contested from birth.

## 16. 326: the moral control case — Crispus and Fausta

Around 326 Constantine ordered the death of his son Crispus and, soon afterward, his wife Fausta died under circumstances ancient sources associate with Constantine.

The motives remain obscure and later sources disagree.

This matters historiographically because Eusebius' *Life of Constantine* largely constructs a saintly/pious imperial portrait and does not provide a transparent account of these events.

The lesson is not:
`CONSTANTINE WAS SECRETLY A MONSTER, THEREFORE CHRISTIANITY IS FALSE`.

It is:
`IMPERIAL CHRISTIAN MEMORY COULD SANITIZE OR SELECT A RULER'S BIOGRAPHY`.

The same source-critical skepticism applied to Gospel reception must also be applied to Constantinian hagiography.

## 17. "The Great" is not evidence of goodness

Constantine was already receiving maximal honorific language during his lifetime.

A 324 inscription calls him:
`Constantini Maximi Victoris semper Augusti`
— Constantine, greatest/maximus victor, perpetual Augustus.

He also used/received `Maximus Augustus` after his victory over Maxentius.

Therefore "the Great" should not be read as a later moral certification, nor assumed to be ironic.

Ancient and later honorifics reward:
- victory;
- supremacy;
- founding;
- expansion;
- transformation;
- dynastic success;
- sacred/political memory.

Constantine objectively qualifies as "great" in the older sense of **enormously consequential**:
- reunified sole rule;
- founded Constantinople;
- transformed imperial religious policy;
- stabilized the gold solidus;
- reshaped church/state relations;
- became foundational to later Byzantine and Christian political memory.

Moral admiration is a separate question.

## 18. Constantine did not complete the transformation

The fourth-century control becomes stronger after his death.

### Constantius II
Uses imperial councils, exiles, and doctrinal policy extensively, often against Nicene positions.

This demonstrates that imperial selection pressure is **portable**:
the machine can favor a different theological faction.

### Julian
Attempts a reversal and reduction of Christian privilege.

Again:
the emperor's religious policy materially changes the competitive environment.

### Valentinian/Valens
Continue different mixes of Christian patronage, toleration, and doctrinal intervention.

### Theodosius I
In 380, CTh 16.1.2 privileges the Nicene Christianity associated with Damasus of Rome and Peter of Alexandria and labels adherents "Catholic Christians."

This marks a stronger stage:
`IMPERIAL RECOGNITION OF A SPECIFIC CHRISTIAN DOCTRINAL IDENTITY`.

The subsequent 380s–390s intensify coercion against rival Christians and traditional cult.

Therefore:

`CONSTANTINE BUILDS THE IMPERIAL-CHRISTIAN CONTROL SURFACE`.

`LATER EMPERORS USE AND ESCALATE IT`.

## 20. What this does to the Roman-cultivation hypothesis

The research now strongly favors a two-stage distinction.

### H3 — first-century/Tiberian cultivation
Still:
`UNPROVEN / LOW CONFIDENCE`.

Constantine does not retroactively establish it.

### H4 — later imperial capture / amplification
Now:
`STRONGLY SUPPORTED AS A GENERAL HISTORICAL MECHANISM`.

By the fourth century, we can directly observe:
- state money;
- legal privileges;
- faction recognition;
- councils;
- exile;
- meeting-place confiscation;
- book suppression;
- scripture production;
- sacred-site construction;
- calendar standardization;
- theological legitimation of monarchy.

This is not a hypothetical toolkit.

It is a documented one.

## 21. The hardest historical conclusion

The evidence does **not** justify:

> "Constantine rewrote Jesus."

It does justify:

> "Constantine and the imperial order changed the ecology in which memories of Jesus competed."

That distinction is crucial.

A historical memory can be:
- true and amplified;
- false and amplified;
- true and suppressed;
- false and suppressed.

Power affects **survival and reach**, not automatically truth value.

## 22. Direct relevance to the historical Yeshua project

The Constantine lane should force every later Jesus tradition to answer:

1. Is this belief/text/practice attested before imperial patronage?
2. Did its institutional carriers gain imperial support?
3. Did competitors lose property, copying capacity, meeting space, or legal status?
4. Was the tradition monumentalized in architecture or pilgrimage?
5. Did imperial ritual/calendar policy reinforce it?
6. Is its apparent later universality partly a survival/amplification effect?

Particularly important:
- Yeshua's Jewishness;
- Easter/Passover separation;
- Roman execution responsibility;
- sacred geography;
- Christological formulas;
- four-Gospel dominance;
- memory of rival Christian groups.
