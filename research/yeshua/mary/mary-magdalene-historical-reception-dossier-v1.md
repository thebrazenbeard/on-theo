## Purpose

Separate claims that are often fused:

1. a historical Mary Magdalene existed;
2. she followed Yeshua during the Galilean ministry;
3. she was present at crucifixion/burial/tomb memory;
4. she received a resurrection appearance;
5. she held leadership or apostolic authority;
6. she received private/revelatory teaching;
7. she was in conflict with Peter;
8. she was a prostitute or sexually stigmatized woman;
9. later church tradition deliberately discredited her;
10. she understood Yeshua better than Peter/James/Thomas.

These are not one claim.

## 1. Paul: silence, not erasure proof

The undisputed Pauline letters do not name Mary Magdalene.

1 Corinthians 15 preserves a received appearance list naming Cephas, the Twelve,
James, all apostles, and Paul.

Mary is absent from that list.

Current control:

PAUL_DOES_NOT_NAME_MARY =
TEXTUALLY_TRUE.

But:

PAULINE_SILENCE
!=
MARY_DID_NOT_EXIST
!=
MARY_WAS_NOT_A_FOLLOWER
!=
MARY_RECEIVED_NO_APPEARANCE.

Paul's list is not a complete biographical roster of everyone associated with
Yeshua or every later appearance tradition.

The absence therefore remains weak negative evidence only.

## 2. Mark: earliest explicit Mary Magdalene layer

Mark 15:40–41 names Mary Magdalene among women watching the crucifixion from a
distance and says the named women had followed and served Yeshua in Galilee.

Mark 15:47 names Mary Magdalene as observing where Yeshua was laid.

Mark 16:1 names her among the women who go to the tomb.

In the earliest recoverable Markan ending, the women receive the tomb
announcement but no appearance of the risen Yeshua to Mary is narrated.

Current dispositions:

HISTORICAL_MARY_MAGDALENE =
PROBABLE.

Mark is the earliest surviving explicit named source and therefore carries most
of the historical burden. The repeated name/identifier and stable placement
make a historical referent the lower-assumption reconstruction, but R3 does not
pretend Mary has Pauline or external attestation comparable to James/Cephas.

MARY_AS_GALILEAN_FOLLOWER =
PROBABLE.

MARY_IN_PASSION_BURIAL_TOMB_MEMORY =
STRONG_AT_MARKAN_TRADITION_LEVEL.

MARKAN_INDIVIDUAL_RESURRECTION_APPEARANCE_TO_MARY =
NOT_PRESENT_IN_EARLIEST_RECOVERABLE_ENDING.

Mark remains the most important historical source for Mary in the current
source clock.

## 4. Matthew

Matthew inherits/develops the Markan women-at-tomb tradition and narrates an
encounter with the risen Jesus in which the women are commissioned to tell the
disciples.

This gives Mary Magdalene a resurrection-message role within Matthew's
narrative.

But Matthew is later than Mark and literary dependence must be preserved.

Current use:

MATTHEAN_MARY =
LATER_CANONICAL_DEVELOPMENT_OF_WOMEN_WITNESS_AND_MESSENGER_ROLE.

This is not an independent first-generation witness merely because the event is
narrated separately.

## 5. Luke

Current controls:

## 7. The historical prostitute claim

The canonical Gospels do not identify Mary Magdalene as:
- prostitute;
- adulteress;
- the unnamed sinful woman of Luke 7;
- Mary of Bethany.

Modern scholarship widely distinguishes these figures.

The Western composite prostitute/penitent Mary is later reception history,
crystallizing through patristic/medieval interpretation rather than arising from
an explicit Gospel statement.

Current disposition:

EARLIEST_SOURCES_IDENTIFY_MARY_MAGDALENE_AS_PROSTITUTE =
NOT_SUPPORTED.

The positive prostitute identification lacks support in the earliest explicit
Mary sources.

This is a source-identification result, not a recovered private biography.

Therefore:

MARY_NEVER_ENGAGED_IN_SEX_WORK =
UNKNOWN / NOT_HISTORICALLY_DEMONSTRABLE_FROM_CURRENT_EVIDENCE.

But a second proposition must remain separate:

LATER_CONFLATION_WAS_DELIBERATELY_CREATED_TO_DISCREDIT_A_HISTORICAL_FEMALE_LEADER =
NOT_ESTABLISHED.

A later interpretive development can diminish or reshape Mary without the project
inventing a coordinated suppression motive.

## 8. Gospel of Mary: manuscript and composition clocks

The Gospel of Mary survives in:
- Papyrus Berolinensis 8502, a fifth-century Coptic manuscript;
- Greek fragments including P.Oxy. 3525 and P.Ryl. 463, generally dated around
  the early third century.

The work itself is commonly dated to the second century, with the precise range
debated.

Therefore:

GOSPEL_OF_MARY_MANUSCRIPT_DATE
!=
GOSPEL_OF_MARY_COMPOSITION_DATE
!=
HISTORICAL_MARY_TRADITION_DATE.

The text's protagonist is "Mary."

Mary Magdalene is the dominant scholarly identification, but the surviving text
does not make the identification so explicit that alternative Mary
identifications have never been proposed.

Current rule:

GOSPEL_OF_MARY_PROTAGONIST_EQUALS_MAGDALENE =
PROBABLE_SCHOLARLY_IDENTIFICATION_NOT_TEXTUALLY_AUTOMATIC.

## 15. Current strongest reconstruction

The strongest bounded Mary model is:

- Mary Magdalene was probably a real woman in the Yeshua movement;
- Mark preserves strong memory of her as a Galilean follower and as a named
  crucifixion/burial/tomb figure;
- later surviving canonical sources preserve increasingly individualized
  resurrection-message/appearance roles, but extant-source order alone does not
  prove a smooth historical development sequence;
- the earliest sources do not call her a prostitute;
- the prostitute/sinner composite is later Western reception;
- later noncanonical texts give Mary major revelatory authority and sometimes
  stage conflict with Peter/other male disciples;
- those later texts prove authority-memory and reception conflict more securely
  than they prove first-generation biography.

What is not established:
- chief-disciple rank;
- historical spouse/sexual partner role;
- historical private revelation;
- historical superiority of understanding;
- deliberate coordinated prostitute-slander campaign;
- a direct line from historical Mary to every later Mary authority text.
