# Lane 1 — Names, Titles, and Identity Language V1

Status: `SOURCE-CRITICAL LINGUISTIC LANE / NON-CANONICAL`

Date: 2026-10-04

## Question

How does a first-century Jewish man named Yeshua become linguistically identified as
`Iesous Christos`, `Kyrios`, `Son of God`, and `Son of Man`?

## Yeshua -> Iesous

Greek `Iesous` is an established Jewish Greek rendering of Joshua/Yeshua.
It predates Christianity.

Disposition:
`ROMAN_REBRANDING_BY_NAME_CHANGE = REJECTED`.

## Christos

Greek `christos` means "anointed" / "anointed one" and renders messiah language.

Matthew Novenson's onomastic analysis argues that in Paul `Christ` functions
best as an **honorific/adopted epithet**, neither a modern surname nor simply a
predicate title.

Useful comparison:
`Antiochus Epiphanes`
`Caesar Augustus`
`Jesus Christos`.

Therefore English "Jesus Christ" hides an ancient claim inside what sounds like
a personal name.

Disposition:
`CHRIST_AS_SURNAME = REJECTED`.
`CHRIST_AS_HONORIFIC_EPITHET_WITH_MESSIANIC_CONTENT = SURVIVES`.

## Kyrios / Lord

`Kyrios` is multivalent:
- polite/master language;
- lordship;
- political superiority;
- Greek-scriptural surrogate for the divine name in many Jewish textual/
  liturgical contexts.

This means calling Jesus `Kyrios` does not have one automatic metaphysical
value.

Paul's use is especially significant because some passages place Jesus inside
scriptural language otherwise associated with Israel's God.

1 Corinthians 8:6 is the sharpest case:
- one God, the Father;
- one Lord, Jesus Christ;
- "all things" from/through them.

Scholars disagree on whether this constitutes full divine-identity language,
creation mediation, liturgical/soteriological language, or some combination.

Disposition:
`JESUS_AS_LORD_BY_PAUL = SURVIVES`.
`EVERY_KYRIOS_INSTANCE_MEANS_YHWH_IDENTITY = REJECTED`.

## Son of God

"Son of God" is not a uniquely Christian phrase and does not automatically mean
"God the Son" in later Nicene ontology.

Ancient Jewish/Roman semantic possibilities include:
- Israel;
- king/Messiah;
- righteous person;
- divinely favored/adopted ruler;
- heavenly/divine figure.

Michael Peppard emphasizes the Roman social/political context of divine sonship
and adoption and argues that Mark's baptismal scene can be heard as an accession/
adoption event without implying a low-status son.

Disposition:
`SON_OF_GOD_NE_NICENE_DEFINITION_AUTOMATICALLY`.

## Son of Man

This remains one of the most unstable identity expressions in Jesus research.

Facts:
- overwhelmingly appears on Jesus' lips in the Gospels;
- almost disappears as a title in later early-Christian usage;
- may reflect Aramaic idiom;
- may connect to Daniel 7;
- may be self-reference;
- may refer to another eschatological figure;
- may reflect later church language.

Modern scholarship does not possess consensus sufficient to use every Son-of-Man
saying as direct access to Yeshua's self-consciousness.

Disposition:
`SON_OF_MAN_AS_HISTORICAL_YESHUA_SELF_TITLE = UNRESOLVED`.

## Linguistic transformation rule

Do not translate:

`YESHUA -> JESUS CHRIST`

as:

`PERSONAL_NAME -> PERSONAL_NAME`.

Translate analytically:

`YESHUA`
->
`YESHUA IDENTIFIED AS THE ANOINTED`
->
`IESOUS CHRISTOS AS FIXED HONORIFIC IDENTITY`.

The linguistic fusion itself is historical evidence that interpretation became
attached tightly enough to the person that title and name could operate together.
