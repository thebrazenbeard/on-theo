Purpose: reconstruct what can actually be said about people around Yeshua while

polemical, visionary and literary developments.

Core rule:

LATER_PRIVILEGED_DISCIPLE_PORTRAIT != FIRST_CENTURY_HISTORICAL_PROOF.

## 1. Person-level source clocks

Every claim must distinguish:

A. EARLIEST NAMED ATTESTATION
B. FIRST NARRATIVE ROLE
C. LATER CANONICAL DEVELOPMENT
D. NONCANONICAL AUTHORITY / REVELATION DEVELOPMENT
E. LATER RECEPTION / POLEMIC / HAGIOGRAPHY
F. PHYSICAL WITNESS DATE

No later source may silently become the earliest historical witness.

## 2. Cephas / Peter

Earliest strong evidence:
- Galatians: Paul reports personal contact and conflict with Cephas.
- 1 Corinthians: Cephas appears in factional memory and resurrection tradition.

Historical floor:
PETER/CEPHAS_WAS_A_REAL_EARLY_LEADER_WITH_DIRECT_RELATION_TO_THE_EARLY_YESHUA_MOVEMENT.

Not established by Paul alone:
- later Petrine supremacy;
- every canonical Peter scene;
- Peter's exact relation to Mary, Thomas or Judas;
- one uninterrupted institutional succession.

## 3. James

Earliest strong evidence:
- Galatians: James is called "the brother of the Lord" and later a pillar.
- Josephus Antiquities 20.200 is an external later witness to James/Jesus linkage if authentic.

Historical floor:
JAMES_WAS_A_REAL_EARLY_LEADER_AND_WAS_IDENTIFIED_BY_PAUL_AS_THE_LORDS_BROTHER.

Later claims about:
- perpetual virginity;
- cousin/stepbrother explanations;
- exact succession office
remain later theological or historical questions.

## 4. Mary Magdalene

Earliest surviving narrative:
Mark.

Mark places Mary Magdalene:
- among women who followed/supported Yeshua in Galilee;
- at the crucifixion;
- at burial observation;
- at the tomb.

Later canonical developments:
- Matthew/Luke reshape the tomb/resurrection role;
- John gives Mary an especially prominent individual encounter/commissioning role.

Critical negative result:

EARLIEST_CANONICAL_SOURCES_DO_NOT_IDENTIFY_MARY_MAGDALENE_AS_A_PROSTITUTE.

The later Western conflation of:
- Mary Magdalene;
- Mary of Bethany;
- the unnamed "sinful woman"
is reception history, not first-century biographical evidence.

This permits:

MARY_MAGDALENE_PROSTITUTE =
REJECTED_AS_EARLY_HISTORICAL_CLAIM.

It does not by itself prove:
A_DELIBERATE_EARLY_CONSPIRACY_TO_SLANDER_MARY.

Motive requires separate evidence.

## 5. Gospel of Mary

Witnesses include:
- Coptic Papyrus Berolinensis 8502, fifth-century physical witness;
- Greek fragments POxy 3525 and PRyl 463.

The work is commonly placed in the late-second/early-third-century Christian
literary environment, though dating and compositional history remain debated.

Its Mary is usually identified by scholars with Mary Magdalene, though that
identification is not absolutely uncontested.

The text portrays:
- Mary as recipient/interpreter of revelation;
- Mary strengthening or instructing other disciples;
- opposition/challenge from male disciples, especially Peter/Andrew in the
  surviving text;
- Levi defending her.

Admissible historical use:

GOSPEL_OF_MARY =
EVIDENCE_FOR_LATER_EARLY_CHRISTIAN_AUTHORITY_CONFLICT_AND_MARY_TRADITION.

Not automatically:

MARY_HISTORICALLY_RECEIVED_SECRET_TEACHING_FROM_YESHUA.

## 6. Judas Iscariot

Earliest explicit named betrayal narrative:
Mark.

Paul's `paradidomi` / "handed over" language in 1 Corinthians 11:23 does not
independently identify Judas.

Therefore R3 begins from:

JUDAS_BETRAYAL_CORE =
EARLY_NARRATIVE_TRADITION_IN_MARK,
HISTORICAL_MECHANICS_UNRESOLVED.

The project must separately test:
- whether an insider handover is historical;
- motive;
- payment;
- information supplied;
- arrest logistics;
- later villainization.

## 7. Gospel of Judas

The Gospel of Judas is attested by Irenaeus around 180 CE and survives in the
Coptic Codex Tchacos, copied centuries after Yeshua.

Current scholarship places the work in the second-century Christian literary
world.

It portrays Judas through an alternative cosmological/authority framework and
associates him with privileged knowledge relative to other disciples.

Admissible use:

GOSPEL_OF_JUDAS =
EVIDENCE_FOR_SECOND_CENTURY_REINTERPRETATION_OF_JUDAS_AND_DISCIPLE_AUTHORITY.

Not:

INDEPENDENT_FIRST_CENTURY_PROOF_THAT_JUDAS_WAS_INNOCENT_OR_FRAMED.

The very existence of an alternative Judas tradition is historically important,
but chronology prevents it from overturning Mark without an independently
demonstrated earlier tradition.

## 8. Thomas

Earliest canonical role:
- named among disciples in Synoptic lists;
- individualized more heavily in John.

Gospel of Thomas:
- survives complete in Coptic in Nag Hammadi Codex II;
- also has Greek Oxyrhynchus fragments;
- composition/date/dependence remain debated;
- some individual sayings may preserve older material even if the extant work is
  later.

Admissible use:

THOMAS =
POSSIBLE_SAYING_LEVEL_EARLY_MEMORY + STRONG_LATER_AUTHORITY_TRAJECTORY.

Not:

WHOLE_GOSPEL_OF_THOMAS =
INDEPENDENT_FIRST_CENTURY_TRANSCRIPT.

## 9. Authority-conflict versus historical relationship

Later texts repeatedly stage conflicts involving:
- Peter;
- Mary;
- Thomas;
- Judas;
- other disciples.

These can preserve:
- second-century authority competition;
- gendered leadership conflict;
- competing revelation claims;
- alternative apostolic lineages.

They do not automatically preserve:
an actual conversation among the historical disciples.

Core distinction:

LITERARY_DISCIPLE_CONFLICT
!=
HISTORICAL_DISCIPLE_CONFLICT_BY_DEFAULT.
