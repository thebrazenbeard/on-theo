## Earliest floor

Mark is the earliest admitted continuous narrative source naming Mary Magdalene.

At the Markan level she is associated with:
- following Yeshua in Galilee;
- presence at crucifixion;
- observation of burial;
- discovery of the tomb.

The most secure R3 historical claim is not:
MARY_WAS_YESHUA'S_SECRET_PRIMARY_DISCIPLE.

It is:

MARY_MAGDALENE_WAS_REMEMBERED_EARLY_AS_A_FOLLOWER_PRESENT_IN_PASSION_AND_TOMB_TRADITION.

Historical reconstruction status:
MARY_AS_ACTUAL_FOLLOWER = PROBABLE.

## The prostitute question

No canonical Gospel identifies Mary Magdalene as:
- a prostitute;
- the unnamed sinful woman of Luke 7;
- Mary of Bethany.

The Western conflation developed later. Modern scholarship explicitly traces
the composite Magdalene image through later Christian reception, including
Gregory the Great's conflation of different Gospel women.

Current disposition:

MARY_MAGDALENE_PROSTITUTE =
REJECTED_AS_EARLY_HISTORICAL_CLAIM.

This is one of the user's hypotheses that currently survives strongly.

But the project does **not** infer from the later conflation alone:

THE_PROSTITUTE_IMAGE_WAS_INVENTED_SPECIFICALLY_TO_DESTROY_MARYS_APOSTOLIC_AUTHORITY.

That motive remains:
UNRESOLVED.

## Canonical authority trajectory

Mark:
Mary is embedded in a group of women and passion/tomb memory.

Matthew/Luke:
the tradition is reshaped in different ways.

John:
Mary becomes individually prominent in the resurrection scene and is
commissioned to tell the disciples.

This demonstrates:
MARYS_AUTHORITY_TRAJECTORY_EXISTS_WITHIN_CANONICAL_DIVERSITY.

It does not prove:
one stable first-century office.

## Gospel of Mary

Physical witnesses:
- BG 8502 in Coptic, fifth century;
- POxy 3525 in Greek;
- PRyl 463 in Greek.

The work belongs to a later early-Christian literary environment, commonly
placed in the late second or early third century, with textual history and exact
dating debated.

The surviving narrative portrays:
- Mary receiving/understanding teaching;
- Mary speaking when other disciples are distressed;
- male disciples challenging the legitimacy or credibility of her revelation;
- Levi defending her.

This is strong evidence that some later Christians could imagine Mary as:
- visionary;
- authoritative interpreter;
- rival/alternative apostolic authority.

## Peter–Mary conflict

The literary opposition between Peter and Mary appears in more than one later
Christian trajectory.

That recurrence is historically important for:
- authority competition;
- gendered memory;
- revelation legitimacy.

But:

RECURRING_PETER_MARY_LITERARY_CONFLICT
!=
PROOF_OF_AN_ACTUAL_PETER_MARY_FIRST_CENTURY_FEUD.

## What R3 can now say

Strong:
- Mary was not called a prostitute in the earliest sources.
- Mary belongs to early passion/tomb memory.
- later canonical and noncanonical traditions elevate her authority.
- some later texts place her in conflict with male disciples.

Not established:
- she was Yeshua's spouse;
- she alone understood his teaching;
- Peter historically suppressed her;
- the prostitute identity originated as an intentional anti-Mary conspiracy.
