This packet does **not** decide who understood Yeshua best, who betrayed him, who succeeded him, or which later authority tradition preserves the truest memory.

## Governing rule

PERSONAL_PROMINENCE_IN_A_LATER_TEXT != HISTORICAL_ACCESS_TO_YESHUA

Likewise:

EARLIER_SOURCE != AUTOMATICALLY_COMPLETE_SOURCE

The task is to preserve what each layer can actually establish.

## 1. John the Baptist

### Earliest admitted Jesus-linked narrative

Mark 1 places Yeshua in relation to John and narrates his baptism.

### External control

Josephus, *Antiquities* 18.116-119, independently attests John as a significant baptizing/preaching figure whose following concerned Herod Antipas.

Recent Josephus scholarship continues to debate interpolation questions around New Testament figures, so the Josephus notice must remain passage-controlled. The important R3 distinction is:

JOHN_HISTORICAL_MOVEMENT =
EXTERNALLY_SUPPORTED

while

JOHN_BAPTIZED_YESHUA =
EARLY_GOSPEL_TRADITION / PROBABLE,
not independently established by Josephus.

### Current ceiling

John is a historically strong contextual figure.
Yeshua's association with him is probable.
The precise teacher-disciple relationship, duration, theological dependence, and cause of separation remain open.

## 2. James

### Earliest source

Paul is decisive.

Galatians 1:18-19 reports Paul's meeting with Cephas and James, called "the brother of the Lord."

Galatians 2:9 presents James, Cephas, and John as recognized pillars.

1 Corinthians 15:7 includes an appearance tradition to James.

### External control

Josephus, *Antiquities* 20.200, identifies James in relation to Jesus "who was called Christ."

T. C. Schmidt's 2025 treatment argues strongly for authenticity and notes broad scholarly support; Rivka Nir's 2026 Oxford Handbook chapter keeps authenticity questions open enough that ON_THEO must retain the dispute rather than call it literally unanimous.

### Current ceiling

James was an unusually early and important Jerusalem figure associated with Yeshua and identified as his brother in the strongest reading of the evidence.

This does not yet establish:
- exact biological kinship model;
- dynastic succession;
- that James understood Yeshua better than Peter or Paul;
- the historical authorship of the canonical Letter of James;
- that later Jewish-Christian traditions preserve James without development.

## 3. Cephas / Peter

### Earliest source

Paul again precedes the Gospels.

Galatians 1-2 depicts Cephas as a major early leader whom Paul personally met and later opposed at Antioch.

1 Corinthians 15:5 places Cephas first in Paul's received appearance list.

1 Corinthians 9:5 treats Cephas as a recognizable apostolic figure.

### Markan layer

Mark gives Peter a much fuller narrative identity:
- call and inner-circle role;
- confession;
- misunderstanding/rebuke;
- Gethsemane failure;
- denial;
- post-resurrection Galilee message addressed to the disciples "and Peter."

### Current ceiling

Cephas/Peter is one of the best-attested early movement leaders.

But the source clock matters:
PAULINE_CEPHAS =
EARLY SOCIAL/AUTHORITY EVIDENCE

MARKAN_PETER =
LATER NARRATIVE MEMORY.

Peter's denial is early Gospel tradition but is not independently attested by Paul.

The combination:
FAILURE + LATER AUTHORITY
is historically interesting.

It does **not** prove:
- a hidden worse betrayal;
- deliberate rehabilitation of treachery;
- Roman collaboration.

## 4. Mary Magdalene

### Earliest surviving narrative layer

Paul does not name Mary Magdalene.

Mark gives the earliest surviving explicit narrative attestation:
- Mary Magdalene among women who followed/served from Galilee;
- witness to crucifixion;
- witness to burial location;
- participant at the tomb in Mark 16:1-8.

The earliest recoverable Markan ending does not narrate an appearance of the risen Jesus to Mary.

Matthew later gives the women an encounter with the risen Jesus.
John later individualizes Mary as the recipient of an appearance and messenger.

### Later authority trajectory

The Gospel of Mary preserves a literary authority conflict involving Mary, Peter, and Andrew.

This is strong evidence for later Christian debates about revelation/authority.

It is not automatically a first-century transcript of a historical Peter-Mary conflict.

Recent Oxford historical synthesis (2026) treats Magdalene as a key witness in Gospel tradition and emphasizes that the canonical texts do not identify her as a prostitute.

### Current ceiling

Mary Magdalene was an important remembered follower associated especially with the final Jerusalem events and tomb/resurrection tradition.

R3 does not yet establish:
- that she was Yeshua's chief disciple;
- that she was his spouse/sexual partner;
- that she received a historical secret teaching;
- that Peter deliberately suppressed her;
- that her later authority literature is independent first-generation memory.

## 5. Judas Iscariot

### Earliest explicit named betrayal source

Mark is the first admitted source in this R3 clock that explicitly names Judas Iscariot as one of the Twelve who hands Yeshua over.

Mark 14 supplies:
- approach to chief priests;
- promised money;
- opportunity-seeking;
- arrival with arrest party;
- identifying greeting/kiss.

### Pauline control

Paul does not name Judas in the surviving undisputed letters.

1 Corinthians 11:23 uses `paradidomi` language in the meal tradition, but that verb cannot simply be converted into an independent named-Judas witness.

1 Corinthians 15:5 preserves a "Twelve" appearance formula after the death sequence.

This creates a source-critical question because later narrative arithmetic treats Judas as lost from the Twelve.

But:

PAUL_SAYS_TWELVE
!=
JUDAS_SURVIVED
!=
JUDAS_WAS_INNOCENT
!=
JUDAS_WAS_NOT_HISTORICAL.

"The Twelve" can function as a group label.

### Later development

Matthew develops payment/repentance/death traditions.
Luke-Acts and John intensify moral/demonic characterization in distinct ways.
Later Papias material and other traditions expand Judas grotesquely.
The Gospel of Judas radically reconfigures Judas and the Twelve in a second-century Christian polemical/cosmological setting.

### Current ceiling

A Judas-handover tradition is early at the Markan narrative level.

R3 has not yet established:
- why Judas acted;
- what unique information he supplied;
- whether payment was primary or developed;
- whether the arrest actually required identification;
- whether Judas's role grew over time from a smaller historical core;
- whether the entire betrayal role was invented;
- whether broader disciple failure was concentrated into Judas.

These belong to the dedicated Judas/R4 dossier.

## 6. Thomas

### Earliest source

Thomas is named in the Markan Twelve roster.

That establishes an early narrative/group memory of a disciple named Thomas, subject to the normal Markan-history caveat.

### Johannine layer

John develops Thomas into a distinctive literary character:
- willingness to die with Jesus;
- questioning;
- absence from first appearance;
- demand for embodied verification;
- confession.

The familiar "doubting Thomas" portrait is therefore principally Johannine, not Pauline or Markan biography.

### Thomasine trajectory

The Gospel of Thomas attributes a sayings collection to Didymos Judas Thomas and gives Thomasine tradition major revelatory prestige.

The Acts of Thomas develops the figure much later.

R2 already established:
THOMAS_WITNESS_DATE != THOMAS_COMPOSITION_DATE != SAYING_INDEPENDENCE.

### Current ceiling

A disciple called Thomas belongs to early Gospel group memory.

R3 does not establish:
- that John preserves literal Thomas dialogue;
- that historical Thomas opposed betrayal;
- that Thomasine Christianity directly descends from the disciple;
- that the Gospel of Thomas as a whole preserves his private archive;
- that Thomas historically understood Yeshua better than Peter.

## 7. The Twelve collectively

### Earliest source

1 Corinthians 15:5 gives the earliest surviving "Twelve" group label in the current source clock, within a received appearance tradition.

### Markan layer

Mark supplies the earliest surviving roster and narrative commissioning of twelve.

### Historical caution

The early existence of a symbolic/recognized Twelve tradition is stronger than our ability to reconstruct:
- exact original membership;
- continuity of roster through Yeshua's ministry;
- which later named disciples were historically in the group at every stage;
- whether "Twelve" could remain a corporate title despite temporary numerical mismatch.

### Current ceiling

An early Twelve tradition is strong.

The later canonical roster is not automatically a verbatim first-generation membership list.

## 8. Cross-person asymmetries

The source clock makes several asymmetries unavoidable.

### James and Peter

They have first-generation Pauline social evidence.

### Mary

Her earliest explicit evidence is Mark, not Paul, but her passion/tomb role is multiply developed later and cannot be dismissed because Paul is silent.

### Judas

His explicit betrayal narrative begins with Mark.

That makes the tradition early enough to matter but later than the Pauline social network evidence.

### Thomas

Named early as part of the Twelve narrative, but most of the distinctive personality/revelation material is later.

### John the Baptist

Uniquely among this set, he has substantial non-Christian external attestation independent of the Jesus narratives, but that external source does not independently establish his relation to Yeshua.

The earliest evidence does show a real and internally differentiated follower network.

It does **not** yet show that the later winners misunderstood Yeshua while Mary, Judas, or Thomas preserved the authentic inner teaching.

That proposition remains a hypothesis to be earned person by person.
