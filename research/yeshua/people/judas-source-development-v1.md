## Research question

What is the smallest historically defensible Judas core, and which familiar Judas features belong only to later extant portrayals?

This dossier deliberately refuses to treat "Judas betrayed Jesus" as one indivisible claim.

The source field is decomposed into:

1. existence / membership in the Twelve;
2. some insider handover role;
3. operational function;
4. motive;
5. money/payment;
6. identification/sign;
7. timing/location/access;
8. demonic/theological characterization;
9. scriptural-fulfillment framing;
10. remorse;
11. death;
12. later moral/revelatory reception.

A conclusion about one component does not automatically establish another.

## 1. Pauline control: the earliest surviving layer does not name Judas

### 1 Corinthians 11:23

Paul transmits the meal tradition using the imperfect passive form of `paradidomi`:

`en te nykti he paredideto`

The phrase can be rendered along the lines of:
"on the night when he was handed over."

The verb itself does not lexically identify Judas.

Paul elsewhere can use `paradidomi` with:
- God as the one who hands over;
- Jesus as one who gives/hands himself over;
- human authorities or agents in other contexts.

Therefore:

`1_COR_11_23_PARADIDOMI = NAMED_JUDAS_BETRAYAL`

is NOT ADMITTED.

The saying may preserve passion tradition compatible with betrayal, but agent identification must come from elsewhere.

### 1 Corinthians 15:5

Paul's received appearance sequence says Christ appeared to Cephas and then "the Twelve."

That is a real early datum.

But it does not establish:
- Judas was alive after the death;
- Judas remained a member;
- Judas was innocent;
- Judas never existed.

Corporate group labels can persist despite numerical mismatch.

The datum is valuable as a discriminator because the later narrative loss of Judas creates a tension that requires explanation. It is not an exoneration.

### Pauline silence

Paul does not explicitly name Judas Iscariot in the undisputed letters.

This is weak negative evidence.

Paul's letters are occasional, non-biographical, and normally uninterested in narrating arrest mechanics.

Therefore:

`PAUL_DOES_NOT_NAME_JUDAS`
does not imply
`NO_PRE_MARKAN_JUDAS_TRADITION`.

## 2. Mark: earliest surviving explicit named handover narrative

Mark is the first admitted source in the current source clock that explicitly identifies Judas Iscariot as:
- one of the Twelve;
- the one who handed Yeshua over.

Relevant loci:
- Mark 3:19;
- Mark 14:10–11;
- Mark 14:18–21;
- Mark 14:43–46.

### What Mark actually gives us

Mark 14:10–11 says Judas goes to the chief priests in order to hand Yeshua over.

The priests are pleased and promise to give Judas money.

Judas then seeks an opportune way/time to hand him over.

Important distinction:

MARK_HAS_MONEY
but
MARK_DOES_NOT_EXPLICITLY_SAY_GREED_WAS_JUDAS'S_MOTIVE.

Money belongs to the earliest surviving explicit Judas narrative.
A psychological greed explanation does not.

### Operational function

Mark 14:1–2 portrays chief-priestly leaders wanting to seize Yeshua by stealth rather than provoke crowd disturbance.

Mark 14:43–46 gives Judas an arrest-party role and an identifying signal/kiss.

This makes an insider function narratively coherent:
- opportunity;
- low-publicity timing;
- access to Yeshua away from crowds;
- identification/sign.

But narrative coherence is not independent historical proof.

The historical function must remain decomposed:

`JUDAS_PROVIDED_SECLUDED_LOCATION_OR_TIMING` = plausible/probable candidate.

`AUTHORITIES_COULD_NOT_RECOGNIZE_YESHUA_WITHOUT_JUDAS` = unresolved and probably too strong.

## 3. Matthew: payment motive and death/fulfillment expansion

Matthew closely follows Mark for much of the passion narrative but adds materially distinctive Judas features.

### Thirty silver

Matthew 26:14–16 has Judas ask what the priests will give him, and they weigh out thirty pieces of silver.

This is a stronger money-motive presentation than Mark.

Because Matthew is literarily related to Mark and because the amount resonates with scriptural fulfillment patterns, the exact amount cannot be treated as an independent early historical datum.

Current classification:

MONEY_IN_JUDAS_TRADITION = PRESENT_ALREADY_IN_MARK.

THIRTY_SILVER_EXACT_AMOUNT = MATTHEAN-SPECIFIC / HISTORICITY UNRESOLVED.

GREED_AS_HISTORICAL_MOTIVE = NOT ESTABLISHED.

### Remorse and death

Matthew 27:3–10 gives:
- remorse;
- return/throwing back of silver;
- hanging;
- priestly purchase of the field;
- fulfillment framing.

These details are not independently confirmed by Mark.

They also differ substantially from Acts.

Therefore Matthew is primary evidence for a Matthean Judas-death/remorse tradition, not a transparent report that resolves the historical death.

## 4. Luke–Acts: Satan, opportunity, money, alternate death/field tradition

Luke 22 adds an explicit theological psychology:

"Satan entered Judas."

Judas then confers with leaders, agrees to money, and seeks an opportunity to hand Yeshua over away from the crowd.

Luke therefore strengthens:
- demonic causation;
- secret/opportune arrest logic.

Acts 1:15–20 develops:
- Judas's place among the Twelve;
- scriptural necessity/fulfillment;
- field tradition;
- gruesome death.

The Acts account and Matthew 27 cannot simply be harmonized as though they were one report.

The contradiction itself is historically useful:

JUDAS_DEATH_MEMORY =
PLURAL / DEVELOPED / UNRESOLVED AT EVENT LEVEL.

It does not prove Judas was invented.

## 5. John: moralization and insider-location function

John contains some of the strongest negative Judas characterization in the canonical field.

It portrays Judas as:
- "a devil" within the Twelve (John 6:70–71);
- a thief who took from the common purse (John 12:4–6);
- the object of diabolic prompting/entry (John 13:2, 27).

These are later extant theological/moral characterizations.

They cannot be projected automatically into historical psychology.

### John 18 and the practical role

John 18:2 says Judas knew the place because Yeshua often met there with his disciples.

Judas arrives with an arresting force.

But unlike the Synoptic kiss scene, John has Yeshua identify himself openly.

This creates an important discriminator.

Across the traditions, Judas's most plausible practical value may not have been:
"authorities had no idea what Jesus looked like."

It may instead have been:
- knowledge of where Yeshua would be;
- knowledge of when he would be relatively isolated;
- access to a private/semi-private meeting location;
- facilitation of a low-risk arrest.

Current state:

INSIDER_LOCATION_TIMING_ACCESS_FUNCTION =
PLAUSIBLE / HISTORICALLY ECONOMICAL.

IDENTIFICATION_NECESSITY =
UNRESOLVED.

John is later and its literary relation to the Synoptics remains debated, so it does not independently prove the historical function.

## 6. The word `paradidomi`: "hand over" is safer than lexical "betray"

The project should avoid making English moral interpretation do Greek historical work.

`Paradidomi` can describe:
- delivery;
- handing over;
- transfer into custody/control;
- betrayal where context makes treachery explicit.

In the passion traditions, Jesus is handed over through a chain:
- Judas -> authorities;
- authorities -> Pilate;
- Pilate -> crucifixion/execution.

The verb does not make all actors morally identical and does not erase Judas's hostile narrative context.

Therefore:

`PARADIDOMI_DOES_NOT_INTRINSICALLY_MEAN_BETRAY`
can survive as a lexical guard.

But:

`PARADIDOMI_PROVES_JUDAS_DID_NOT_BETRAY_YESHUA`
is rejected.

Context, not dictionary substitution, decides whether a given handover is represented as treacherous.

## 7. Papias: grotesque death as later moral pedagogy

A later Judas-death tradition associated with Papias portrays Judas with grotesque bodily corruption.

Candida Moss's 2019 study argues that the imagery fits ancient associations among:
- greed;
- diseased/swollen bodies;
- worms/parasites;
- divine punishment.

This gives the tradition a strong pedagogical/moralizing function.

Its immediate evidence class is:

LATER TRADITION + SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION.

It does not independently establish how Judas historically died.

It does show that Judas became an exceptionally productive moral symbol whose body itself could be made to enact punishment.

## 8. Gospel of Judas: radical reinterpretation is not historical rescue

The Gospel of Judas is invaluable for a different question:

How could later Christians rethink the theological paradox that the salvific passion requires the act condemned as betrayal?

The text gives Judas distinctive revelatory access relative to the other disciples, but whether Judas is finally positive, negative, tragic, or paradoxical is disputed.

Modern scholarship after the initial publication substantially complicated the simplistic headline:
"Judas was the good disciple."

The source therefore establishes:

JUDAS_MEANING_REMAINED_THEOLOGICALLY_CONTESTABLE_IN_LATER_CHRISTIANITY.

It does not establish:

HISTORICAL_JUDAS_ACTED_ON_YESHUA'S_SECRET_INSTRUCTIONS.

Nor does reversal itself prove that the canonical tradition suppressed a favorable first-century memory.

## 9. Diachronic pattern: what actually changes

The surviving source sequence supports a real observation:

### Earliest explicit named layer: Mark
- one of Twelve;
- initiates contact with chief priests;
- money promised;
- seeks opportunity;
- gives arrest signal.

### Matthew
- Judas asks what payment he will receive;
- exact thirty-silver amount;
- remorse;
- hanging;
- returned money / field;
- explicit fulfillment structure.

### Luke–Acts
- Satan enters;
- money/opportunity;
- scriptural-necessity frame;
- distinct field/death tradition.

### John
- devil/thief characterization;
- Satan enters;
- insider knows meeting place;
- arrest force;
- no kiss needed because Yeshua identifies himself.

### Papias/later tradition
- grotesque punished body;
- greed/pedagogical moralization.

### Gospel of Judas
- theological/cosmological reconfiguration of Judas and the Twelve.

This sequence demonstrates expanding **extant portrayals**.

It does not by itself prove a linear historical evolution from innocent Judas to villain Judas.

Lost traditions and literary dependence prevent that simplification.

## 10. Historical hypotheses

### H0 — Historical insider handover core

A real member/associate named Judas played some role in enabling Yeshua's arrest.

Current state:
PROBABLE, not independently proven.

Why it survives:
- Mark's named insider tradition is early enough to require explanation;
- Matthew/Luke/John all preserve the core while reshaping it;
- the act solves a plausible operational problem of low-publicity access/timing.

Limitations:
- later Gospels are not four independent witnesses;
- no surviving Pauline named-Judas control;
- Mark can already contain literary/theological construction.

### H1 — Smaller historical core, larger later villain profile

Judas historically supplied opportunity/location/access, while motive, exact payment, greed, demonic agency, fulfillment and death imagery accumulated or diversified in later extant traditions.

Current state:
PLAUSIBLE / MODERATE.

This is currently the most economical narrowing hypothesis.

It does not exonerate Judas.
It limits what can be responsibly attributed to him.

### H2 — Judas invented as scapegoat

Judas as the singular betrayer was created to concentrate a broader follower failure into one disposable villain.

Current state:
UNRESOLVED / CURRENTLY WEAK-TO-MODERATE AS A FULL INVENTION CLAIM.

Evidence favoring investigation:
- Paul does not name Judas;
- broad disciple failure exists independently in Mark;
- later portrayals become highly moralized.

Evidence against promotion:
- Mark already contains a specific Judas-insider tradition;
- no independently early source accuses another disciple/group of the intentional handover instead;
- Mark itself preserves Peter's denial and collective flight, so it does not erase non-Judas failure.

### H3 — Broader failure concentrated into Judas without full invention

A historical Judas may have been involved, but the arrest crisis contained broader compromise/failure later narratively concentrated into him as intentional betrayer.

Current state:
POSSIBLE / LOW-MODERATE.

This is more economical than total invention because it permits both:
- historical Judas core;
- real collective flight/denial/failure.

Positive evidence still missing:
- early rival accusation of intentional collaboration by named non-Judas disciples;
- administrative/payment evidence;
- early alternative arrest narrative assigning intentional handover elsewhere.

### H4 — Judas acted on Yeshua's authorization

Judas carried out an agreed role in accordance with Yeshua's instructions.

Current state:
NOT SUPPORTED BY EARLY EVIDENCE.

The Gospel of Judas cannot promote this historical claim by itself.

This hypothesis remains logically possible, not evidentially established.

### H5 — Collective bribery / Roman-agent conspiracy

Multiple disciples were bribed, recruited, coerced, or inserted by Roman/local authorities, with Judas later serving as scapegoat.

Current state:
UNKNOWN / CURRENTLY WEAK.

No direct early evidence currently establishes:
- recruitment;
- payment to multiple disciples;
- Roman intelligence penetration;
- named non-Judas collaborators.

The hypothesis must not become self-sealing by treating all missing evidence as successful concealment.

## 11. Current claim ceiling

The strongest responsible Judas reconstruction at this stage is:

A historical insider-handover core is probable, but the surviving evidence is much stronger for the fact that later communities **interpreted and elaborated Judas's role** than for the historical truth of his familiar motive/profile.

The most plausible historical function is opportunity/location/timing/access for an arrest away from public crowds.

Money appears already in Mark's narrative, but greed as motive, the exact thirty-silver amount, demonic control, theft, scriptural inevitability, remorse, and the manner of death are source-specific or later features and should not be fused into one historical biography.

This dossier does not establish Judas's innocence.

It also does not establish the full canonical composite as historical fact.
