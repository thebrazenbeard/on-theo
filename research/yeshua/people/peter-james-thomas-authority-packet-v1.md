# R3 Peter, James, Thomas — Authority Trajectories V1

Status: SOURCE-CRITICAL PERSON PACKET / NON-CANONICAL

## Peter / Cephas

Paul's letters provide unusually early evidence that Cephas:
- was a real person;
- held substantial standing;
- interacted personally with Paul;
- was associated with resurrection tradition;
- could disagree with Paul over practice.

This is much stronger historically than later literary scenes.

Current floor:

PETER_REAL_EARLY_LEADER =
SURVIVES_STRONGLY.

But later claims of:
- universal Petrine supremacy;
- precise succession structures;
- historical hostility to Mary
require separate evidence.

## James

Paul names James as:
- "the brother of the Lord";
- a recognized leader/pillar.

Josephus later supplies an external James/Jesus linkage if the passage is
authentic.

Current floor:

JAMES_REAL_EARLY_LEADER =
SURVIVES_STRONGLY.

JAMES_KINSHIP_WITH_YESHUA =
PROBABLE_TO_STRONG.

This makes James especially important for reconstructing:
- Yeshua's family;
- Jerusalem leadership;
- post-death authority competition.

R3 does not yet decide why James, apparently not foregrounded in Markan ministry
narrative, becomes so important early.

## Thomas

In the Synoptic tradition Thomas is mostly a named disciple.

John develops him into an individualized character associated with:
- willingness to accompany Yeshua into danger;
- questioning;
- doubt about resurrection testimony;
- climactic confession.

These are Johannine narrative data, not automatically historical personality
traits.

## Gospel of Thomas

The Gospel of Thomas survives:
- complete in Coptic in Nag Hammadi Codex II;
- partially in Greek Oxyrhynchus papyri.

Its date and relationship to Synoptic traditions remain debated.

Current scholarship therefore requires:

WORK_LEVEL_DATE
!=
SAYING_LEVEL_TRADITION_DATE.

Some sayings may contain older material even if the extant collection is later.

Current disposition:

THOMAS_AS_LATER_PRIVILEGED_SAYINGS_AUTHORITY =
SURVIVES.

THOMAS_HISTORICALLY_UNDERSTOOD_YESHUA_BETTER =
UNRESOLVED.

## Cross-person authority pattern

Later Christian texts repeatedly redistribute privileged authority among
different remembered disciples.

Examples include trajectories centered on:
- Peter;
- James;
- Thomas;
- Mary;
- Judas.

This suggests:

EARLY_CHRISTIANITY_CONTAINED_COMPETING_APOSTOLIC_AUTHORITY_MEMORIES.

That proposition is stronger than:

YESHUA_HISTORICALLY_RANKED_THESE_DISCIPLES_IN_THE_SAME_WAY.

## User-hypothesis pressure

The user's intuition that Mary, Judas and Thomas appear disproportionately in
later alternative-authority texts is correct as a reception observation.

The historical inference that they therefore were the three who "really
understood" Yeshua best does not yet follow.

A later marginalized group has incentives to:
- elevate an alternative apostolic guarantor;
- invert canonical reputations;
- claim secret teaching;
- dramatize conflict with dominant authority figures.

But dominant groups also have incentives to:
- centralize authority;
- standardize memory;
- marginalize rivals.

Therefore the correct method is symmetrical:

CANONICAL_AUTHORITY_MEMORY
and
NONCANONICAL_COUNTERAUTHORITY_MEMORY

both require source criticism.

THE_PERSONS_AROUND_YESHUA_ARE_HISTORICALLY_RECOVERABLE_AT_VERY_DIFFERENT_DEPTHS:
