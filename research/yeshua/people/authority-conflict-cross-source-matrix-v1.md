# R3 Authority-Conflict Cross-Source Matrix V1

Status: CROSS-SOURCE MEMORY / AUTHORITY TEST / NON-CANONICAL

## Question

When early Christian texts elevate, criticize, rehabilitate, or oppose named
disciples, what can be recovered about:
1. first-generation historical standing;
2. later community authority claims;
3. literary/theological role?

Core rule:

APOSTOLIC_CHARACTERIZATION =
HISTORICAL_MEMORY + LITERARY_SHAPING + COMMUNITY_AUTHORITY_PRESSURE,
in proportions that must be tested source by source.

## Source/person matrix

### Paul — 40s/50s CE

Peter/Cephas:
- real personal contact with Paul;
- recognized leader;
- resurrection tradition prominence;
- capable of public disagreement with Paul.

James:
- real personal contact network;
- "brother of the Lord";
- pillar/leader.

Mary Magdalene:
- not named.

Thomas:
- not named.

Judas Iscariot:
- not named in betrayal context.

Historical value:
VERY_HIGH_FOR_PETER_AND_JAMES_SOCIAL_EXISTENCE.
LOW_OR_NONE_FOR_MARY_THOMAS_JUDAS_BY_NAME.

Important:
Paul's silence about Mary/Thomas/Judas is not evidence they were unimportant or
fictional.

### Mark — earliest surviving continuous Gospel narrative

Peter:
- prominent disciple;
- insight and misunderstanding coexist;
- denial/failure becomes major passion memory.

James:
- Yeshua's brother named in hometown family list;
- not foregrounded as ministry leader.

Mary Magdalene:
- follower memory;
- crucifixion;
- burial;
- tomb.

Thomas:
- disciple-list presence only.

Judas:
- one of Twelve;
- explicit handover/betrayal narrative.

Historical value:
EARLY_NARRATIVE_MEMORY_WITH_STRONG_LITERARY_SHAPING.

### Matthew / Luke

These reuse Mark plus other source/tradition material.

They cannot be counted as independent witnesses merely when repeating Mark.

Their changes are still evidence for:
- evolving characterization;
- authority;
- apologetic pressure;
- community theology.

### John

Peter:
- major disciple;
- failure/restoration and leadership themes.

Mary Magdalene:
- individually prominent resurrection witness/messenger.

Thomas:
- individually prominent;
- questioning/doubt/confession complex.

Judas:
- strongly negative betrayer characterization.

Historical use:
LATER_FIRST-CENTURY_OR_EARLY_SECOND-CENTURY_CANONICAL_MEMORY,
with independence tested per unit.

John demonstrates that canonical tradition itself contains major character
development.

### Gospel of Mary

Likely late-second/early-third-century literary environment.

Mary:
- privileged visionary/interpreter;
- stabilizes distressed disciples;
- challenged by Andrew/Peter;
- defended by Levi.

Peter:
- authority rival/challenger.

Historical use:
STRONG_FOR_LATER_MARY_AUTHORITY_AND_CONFLICT_MEMORY.
WEAK_FOR_DIRECT_FIRST-CENTURY_CONVERSATION.

### Gospel of Thomas

Later work with debated saying-level independence.

Thomas:
- named as recipient/guarantor of secret sayings.

Historical use:
STRONG_FOR_THOMAS_AUTHORITY_TRAJECTORY.
VARIABLE_FOR_INDIVIDUAL_SAYINGS.
WEAK_FOR_BIOGRAPHICAL_THOMAS.

### Gospel of Judas

Second-century work, known to Irenaeus by c. 180 CE.

Judas:
- receives distinctive/exceptional knowledge;
- relationship to betrayal is cosmologically reinterpreted;
- other disciples are criticized within the work's theological framework;
- Judas's final valuation remains disputed in scholarship and is not flattened into a simple hero reading.

Historical use:
STRONG_FOR_ALTERNATIVE_JUDAS_RECEPTION.
WEAK_FOR_FIRST-CENTURY_EXONERATION.

### Apocryphon of James / James trajectories

Later revelation traditions elevate James as recipient/guarantor.

Historical use:
STRONG_FOR_JAMES_AUTHORITY_TRAJECTORY.
EARLIEST_JAMES_HISTORICAL_STANDING_COMES_FROM_PAUL_NOT_FROM_LATER_REVELATION_TEXT.

## Pattern 1 — privileged disciple is a reusable literary technology

Multiple early-Christian communities can authorize teaching by presenting:
- Peter;
- James;
- Thomas;
- Mary;
- Judas

as specially authorized, insightful, commissioned or revealed-to.

Therefore:

PRIVILEGED_DISCIPLE_TROPE =
PROJECT_INFERENCE / RECEPTION_ANALYSIS:
COMMUNITY_AUTHORITY_TECHNOLOGY_CANDIDATE.

This reduces the evidentiary value of any **single later privileged-disciple
portrait** for historical hierarchy.

## Pattern 2 — marginalization can be real without one conspiracy

A figure can lose prestige through:
- changing theology;
- gender hierarchy;
- canon formation;
- institutional consolidation;
- literary characterization;
- polemic;
- later conflation;
- simple displacement by more useful authority figures.

Therefore:

LATER_MARGINALIZATION
does not require
CENTRAL_COORDINATED_SUPPRESSION.

A conspiracy hypothesis requires its own evidence.

## Pattern 3 — canonical sources are not neutral either

A critical method cannot say:

NONCANONICAL = IDEOLOGICAL
CANONICAL = HISTORY.

Mark, Matthew, Luke and John also:
- select;
- arrange;
- theologize;
- characterize;
- polemicize.

The difference is source date, dependence, genre, access to earlier traditions
and corroboration—not canonical status alone.

## Pattern 4 — alternative traditions matter even when historically late

The Gospel of Mary and Gospel of Judas cannot simply be discarded because they
are later.

They prove that:
- Mary could function as a major authority figure;
- Peter's authority could be challenged literarily;
- Judas could be radically reinterpreted;
- disciple hierarchy was not remembered uniformly.

That matters for:
HISTORY_OF_EARLY_CHRISTIAN_MEMORY.

It does not automatically revise:
HISTORY_OF_YESHUA.

## Direct tests of project hypotheses

### "Mary understood Yeshua better than Peter"

Later-source support:
YES, as a literary/authority trajectory.

First-century historical support:
INSUFFICIENT.

Disposition:
UNRESOLVED_HISTORICALLY;
SURVIVES_AS_LATER_AUTHORITY_MEMORY.

### "Judas understood Yeshua better than the others"

Later-source support:
YES for distinctive Judas knowledge/authority contrast in the Gospel of Judas;
NO for a universally agreed positive/exonerating valuation of Judas.

First-century historical support:
INSUFFICIENT.

Disposition:
UNRESOLVED_HISTORICALLY;
SURVIVES_AS_SECOND_CENTURY_ALTERNATIVE_MEMORY.

### "Thomas understood Yeshua better"

Later-source support:
THOMAS_AUTHORITY_TRAJECTORY_EXISTS.

First-century historical support:
INSUFFICIENT.

Disposition:
UNRESOLVED.

### "Peter / dominant apostles suppressed the others"

Evidence:
later texts dramatize authority conflict.

But direct historical mechanism:
NOT_ESTABLISHED.

Disposition:
UNRESOLVED.

### "Mary was slandered as a prostitute to discredit her"

The prostitute identity is demonstrably later than the earliest sources.

Intentional anti-authority motive:
NOT_ESTABLISHED.

Disposition:
LATER_FALSE_CONFLATION_SURVIVES;
