## Earliest evidentiary problem

Paul is earlier than Mark but does not clearly name Judas in the relevant
"handing over" language.

Mark is the earliest surviving explicit narrative source that:
- names Judas Iscariot;
- places him among the Twelve;
- gives him a role in handing Yeshua over;
- connects him to the arrest.

## Gospel of Judas

Irenaeus witnesses knowledge of a Gospel of Judas around 180 CE.

The surviving Codex Tchacos version is later and Coptic, preserving a work
generally placed in the second century.

It associates Judas with distinctive knowledge and a sharply alternative
cosmological interpretation of his role. Scholarship disputes whether the text
ultimately evaluates Judas positively, negatively, tragically, or ambivalently.
The manuscript therefore cannot be reduced to a simple 'Judas was the hero'
reading.

That means:

ALTERNATIVE_JUDAS_RECEPTION_EXISTED_BY_THE_LATE_SECOND_CENTURY.

JUDAS_DISTINCTIVE_KNOWLEDGE_IN_GOSPEL_OF_JUDAS = SURVIVES.

GOSPEL_OF_JUDAS_POSITIVE_EXONERATION = DISPUTED_NOT_ADMITTED.

It does not mean:

THE_GOSPEL_OF_JUDAS_PRESERVES_AN_INDEPENDENT_EYEWITNESS_DEFENSE_OF_JUDAS.

## Exoneration test

For the user's hypothesis "Judas was not the betrayer" to rise above
speculation, the project would need one or more of:

- pre-Markan evidence contradicting the Judas handover;
- an independently dated early tradition absolving Judas;
- evidence that Mark's Judas role was demonstrably created from scripture or
  literary necessity;
- historical arrest logistics showing no insider role was needed combined with
  evidence of later blame concentration;
- transmission evidence linking an alternative Judas memory to first-generation
  witnesses.

Current evidence does not yet meet that threshold.
