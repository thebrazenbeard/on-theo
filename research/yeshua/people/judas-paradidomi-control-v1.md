# `paradidomi` and Judas — Lexical / Historical Control V1

Status: LOAD-BEARING PHILOLOGICAL GUARD / INTERNAL REVIEW REQUIRED

## Question

Does the Greek verb `παραδίδωμι` itself establish that 1 Corinthians 11:23 independently knows Judas as a treacherous betrayer?

## Result

No.

The verb's semantic range includes handing over, delivering, transferring, and—in a hostile context—betraying.

The historical inference must therefore distinguish:

LEXEME
from
AGENT
from
CONTEXT
from
MORAL EVALUATION.

## Pauline use

1 Corinthians 11:23 says Yeshua was `paredideto` on the night of the meal tradition.

The grammatical subject/agent of the passive is not Judas.

Other Pauline uses can make God or Jesus the active source of a "handing over."

That makes it unsafe to claim:

PAUL_INDEPENDENTLY_NAMES_JUDAS_BETRAYAL.

It also makes it unsafe to claim the opposite:

PAUL_DENIES_A_HUMAN_BETRAYAL.

Paul's wording is compatible with theological passive/handing-over tradition and does not settle the arrest agent.

## Passion-chain control

The Gospel traditions use handing-over language for multiple transfers in the passion sequence.

A single English word "betray" can obscure that broader transfer/custody semantic field.

This matters because the project is specifically testing whether Judas's historical function was:
- treacherous initiation;
- logistical facilitation;
- disclosure;
- identification;
- delivery into custody;
- some combination.

The lexeme alone does not decide among them.

## Cahana control

Jonathan Cahana's 2016 study deliberately translates Judas-related `paradidonai` as "handed over" in comparative discussion, emphasizing the theological paradox produced by the fact that the condemned act is also necessary to the passion story.

This is scholarly interpretation, not a lexical monopoly.

## Historical ceiling

The safest project language before source-specific context is:

"HAND OVER."

Where a narrative explicitly casts Judas's action as disloyal/hostile, "betray" may be an interpretive English rendering of that context.

No historical exoneration follows from the lexical guard.
