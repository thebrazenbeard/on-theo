## 2. Thomas 3

Thomas 3 rejects locating the Kingdom simply:
- in the sky;
- in the sea.

It then places the Kingdom:
- inside;
- outside;

and connects this with:
- self-knowledge;
- recognition;
- relation to the living Father.

Current textual classification:

THOMAS_3 =
EXPLICIT_PRESENT_INTERIOR_EXTERIOR_SELF_KNOWLEDGE_KINGDOM.

This is stronger inward language than can safely be extracted from Luke 17:21
alone.

## 3. Thomas 113

Thomas 113 rejects waiting for the Kingdom in a way that can be located by
"look here / look there" language and says the Father's Kingdom is spread out
upon the earth while people fail to see it.

Current textual classification:

THOMAS_113 =
PRESENT_HIDDEN_UNPERCEIVED_KINGDOM.

Its emphasis is perceptual/recognitional at least as much as interior.

## 4. The relationship to Luke 17 is real

Luke 17:20–21 and Thomas 3/113 share a recognizable tradition complex:

- question/expectation of Kingdom location or coming;
- rejection of ordinary spatial pointing;
- a present/immediate counterclaim.

Therefore:

LUKE_THOMAS_KINGDOM_COMPLEX =
REAL_TRADITIONAL_PARALLEL.

## 5. Independence cannot be assigned to Thomas as a whole

Current scholarship remains divided over:
- Thomas's date;
- use of Synoptic Gospels;
- oral versus literary mediation;
- independence of specific sayings.

Simon Gathercole has argued for Lukan influence in Thomas using Lukan
redactional material elsewhere.

Other scholars have argued that some Thomas sayings preserve independent
tradition and that archaic/Semitic features can occasionally support
saying-specific independence.

Stephen Patterson's Oxford synthesis emphasizes that no consensus exists and
that Thomas can contribute to historical-Jesus research only under critical
analysis.

Current project rule:

THOMAS_INDEPENDENCE =
LOGION_BY_LOGION,
FEATURE_BY_FEATURE.

## 6. Thomas 3 independence state

Evidence favoring possible independence:
- substantially expanded and differently organized saying;
- sky/sea contrast absent from Luke 17;
- inside/outside + self-knowledge complex absent from Luke;
- Greek witness establishes that this form is not merely late Coptic
  translation expansion.

Evidence favoring dependence/redaction:
- recognizable relation to Luke's here/there / Kingdom-location complex;
- broader arguments exist for Thomas's knowledge of Luke/Synoptic redaction;
- Thomas 3's self-knowledge language fits Thomas's own literary-theological
  program.

Thomas 113 stands verbally/conceptually closer to Luke's:

Older scholarship has argued for a shared pre-Lukan/Aramaic tradition.

Other dependence models remain available.

## 8. Doublet significance

Thomas contains both:
- logion 3;
- logion 113.

The two sayings express related but non-identical Kingdom conceptions.

That can indicate:
- multiple inherited forms;
- internal redactional development;
- deliberate preservation of variants;
- compositional reuse.

It does not automatically prove:
two independent first-century sayings.

## 9. Thomas is evidence for trajectory even if dependent

This distinction matters.

Even if Thomas knew Luke, Thomas still provides primary evidence that an early
Christian community/textual tradition developed Kingdom language toward:
- hidden presence;
- interior/exterior reality;
- self-knowledge;
- perceptual failure.

Therefore:

THOMAS_HISTORICAL_YESHUA_VALUE
may remain unresolved

while

THOMAS_RECEPTION_TRAJECTORY_VALUE
is high.

## 10. "Gnostic" classification is not allowed to decide the source question

Thomas is frequently called:
- Gnostic;
- proto-Gnostic;
- Thomasine;
- wisdom gospel;
- sayings gospel.

The category "Gnosticism" is disputed and Thomas does not simply reproduce one
Valentinian or Sethian system.

Therefore:

GNOSTIC_LABEL
cannot be used either to:
- dismiss Thomas as late corruption;
or
- privilege Thomas as suppressed secret truth.

The source question remains textual.

## 11. Historical-Yeshua inference

Thomas 3/113 materially increase the plausibility that:
PRESENT/HIDDEN/INTERIOR_KINGDOM_LANGUAGE
circulated in early Christianity.

They do not yet establish that:
this was Yeshua's central original teaching.

The historical inference would strengthen substantially if R2 can demonstrate:
- independence from Luke;
- archaic wording;
- coherence with independently early sayings;
- Jewish first-century conceptual plausibility without relying on later
  Gnostic systems.
