# Enoch Astronomical Numerical Anomaly Audit V1

Status: `ADVERSARIAL NUMERICAL AUDIT / NON-CANONICAL`

Date: 2026-10-03

## Question

Does the earliest recoverable Enochic astronomical material contain quantitative
knowledge that exceeds what is plausibly available from ancient Near Eastern,
Babylonian, Greek, and Second Temple Jewish astronomy?

This is a stronger discriminator than vocabulary.

A positive anomaly should be:
- numerically specific;
- physically accurate beyond ordinary period knowledge;
- difficult to explain through known ancient mathematical schemes;
- independently preserved early enough to exclude late correction;
- predictive rather than merely retrospectively mappable.

## Reference values for modern comparison

Approximate modern values used only as external controls:
- tropical year: 365.2422 days;
- mean synodic month: 29.53059 days;
- twelve synodic months: 354.36708 days;
- solar-lunar annual difference: about 10.87512 days.

These values are not expected of ancient authors.
They are used only to test claims of extraordinary accuracy.

## TEST 1 — 364-day year

### Enochic claim

The Astronomical Book treats 364 days as the exact/correct year:
- 12 months;
- eight 30-day months;
- four 31-day seasonal months;
- four quarters of 91 days;
- exactly 52 weeks.

### Mathematical strengths

364 is exceptionally elegant for a priestly/liturgical calendar:
- divisible by 7;
- 52 exact weeks;
- 91-day quarters = 13 exact weeks;
- festivals remain on fixed weekdays;
- seasonal boundaries fit a symmetric arithmetic scheme.

### Physical test

Compared with a tropical year of 365.2422 days:

`364 - 365.2422 = -1.2422 days/year`.

Without correction:
- after 8 years the scheme is about 9.94 days short of the seasonal year;
- after roughly 25 years the seasonal displacement exceeds a month.

### Historical comparison

The 364-day year is not uniquely Enochic.
Scholarship traces it within Mesopotamian and ancient Jewish calendrical development.

A review of Ben-Dov notes that MUL.APIN II ii 11–12 implies a 364-day scheme from:
- lunar year = 354 days;
- 10-day annual difference;
- 30-day intercalation after three years.

Later Jewish literature makes 364 the cornerstone of fixed liturgical calendars.

### Disposition

`364_AS_EXTRAORDINARY_SOLAR_ACCURACY = FAIL`.

`364_AS_ELEGANT_RELIGIOUS_MATHEMATICAL_CALENDAR = STRONG`.

This is a case where mathematical order is more important than physical-year accuracy.

## TEST 2 — 354-day lunar year

### Enochic claim

The lunar year is treated as 354 days:
- six 30-day months;
- six 29-day months.

### Physical test

Modern mean:
`12 × 29.53059 = 354.36708 days`.

Enochic approximation error:
`354 - 354.36708 = -0.36708 day/year`.

This is a perfectly respectable ancient integer-day approximation.

It is not anomalous.

Babylonian calendars already used lunar months of 29 or 30 days.

### Disposition

`354_DAY_LUNAR_YEAR = GOOD_ANCIENT_APPROXIMATION / NOT_ANOMALOUS`.

## TEST 3 — 10-day solar/lunar difference

### Enochic claim

364 - 354 = 10 days.

1 Enoch 74 extends this:
- 3 years: 1092 solar vs 1062 lunar = 30 days;
- 5 years: 1820 vs 1770 = 50 days;
- 8 years: 2912 vs 2832 = 80 days.

The arithmetic is internally exact.

### Physical test

Modern annual difference:
`365.2422 - 354.36708 = 10.87512 days`.

Over 8 years:
`10.87512 × 8 ≈ 87.00096 days`.

Enoch gives 80 days.

The difference is not a computational error inside the Enochic model.
It follows exactly from its schematic 364/354 assumptions.

### Historical comparison

MUL.APIN itself contains a 10-day difference between a 354-day lunar year and an implied 364-day calendrical year.

Therefore the exact feature that can look striking in Enoch has a Babylonian precedent.

### Disposition

`10_DAY_DIFFERENCE_AS_ANOMALOUS_REVELATION = FAIL`.

`10_DAY_DIFFERENCE_AS_TRANSMITTED/REWORKED_ANCIENT_SCHEMATIC_ASTRONOMY = STRONG`.

## TEST 4 — Eight-year calculation

### Enochic structure

The 3-, 5-, and 8-year totals reproduce the same 10-day yearly offset.

Older scholarship compared the sequence to the Greek octaeteris.

A classical eight-year luni-solar cycle used 99 lunar months and approximately
2922 days in later descriptions.

Enoch's eight-year solar total is 2912 days.

### Comparison

The Enochic 8-year total is:
- arithmetically consistent with 8 × 364;
- about 9.94 days short of 8 modern tropical years;
- not a superior hidden luni-solar synchronization.

The later 19-year Babylonian/Metonic scheme is substantially more accurate for
reconciling lunar months and the seasonal year.

### Disposition

`ENOCHIC_8_YEAR_SCHEME_AS_ADVANCED_HIDDEN_CYCLE = FAIL`.

`ENOCHIC_8_YEAR_ARITHMETIC_AS_SCHEMATIC_CALENDAR_REASONING = SURVIVES`.

## TEST 5 — 2:1 solstitial daylight ratio

### Enochic claim

1 Enoch 72 divides day + night into 18 parts.

At the summer extreme:
- day = 12 parts;
- night = 6 parts.

At winter:
- day = 6;
- night = 12.

Thus:
`LONGEST_DAY : SHORTEST_NIGHT = 2 : 1`.

If read as literal 24-hour day length:
- 16 hours daylight;
- 8 hours night.

Ignoring refraction and using modern obliquity near 23.44 degrees, that ratio
corresponds to approximately 49 degrees north latitude.

This is far north of Judea.

### Tempting anomaly

A naive anomaly reading could claim:
- the Enochic astronomer possessed observational data from a northern latitude;
- the "ends of the earth" traditions preserve memory of a northern observation site.

### Babylonian control

This inference fails badly.

The 2:1 solstitial ratio is already a known feature of Babylonian schematic
astronomy.

The Cambridge History of Science notes that Enuma Anu Enlil Tablet 14 uses:
- a schematic 360-day year;
- linear daylight variation;
- a 2:1 longest-to-shortest daylight ratio.

A 2025 Dead Sea Discoveries study directly analyzes 1 Enoch 72:8–32 alongside:
- BM 17175 + 17284;
- Enuma Anu Enlil XIV;
- MUL.APIN II i 17;

and concludes that 1 Enoch 72 shows appropriation, reinterpretation, and
modification of Babylonian schematic astronomy.

### Disposition

`49N_SECRET_OBSERVATORY_FROM_2_TO_1_RATIO = NOT_ADMITTED`.

`BABYLONIAN_SCHEMATIC_SOURCE/COMPARATOR = STRONG`.

This is one of the strongest examples of why apparent modern anomalies require
history-of-science controls.

## TEST 6 — Linear monthly day-length changes

### Enochic claim

Daylight changes in neat integer steps month by month:
- 9:9;
- 10:8;
- 11:7;
- 12:6;
then symmetrically reverses.

### Physical test

Real daylight duration varies continuously and nonlinearly with solar
declination and latitude.

The Enochic scheme is an idealized linear zigzag.

### Historical comparison

Linear zigzag functions are characteristic of early Babylonian schematic
astronomy.

The 2025 analysis of 1 Enoch 72 explicitly places its monthly day/night clauses
in this Babylonian schematic tradition.

### Disposition

`LINEAR_DAYLIGHT_SCHEME_AS_HIDDEN_PHYSICAL_MODEL = FAIL`.

`LINEAR_DAYLIGHT_SCHEME_AS_ANCIENT_MATHEMATICAL_APPROXIMATION = STRONG`.

## TEST 7 — Lunar illumination fractions

### Enochic claim

1 Enoch 73 and the Aramaic Astronomical Book use fractions involving:
- sevenths;
- fourteenths;
- possibly twenty-eighths in some reconstructions;

to model lunar illumination.

### Scholarly state

Interpretation is technically difficult.

Neugebauer and earlier commentators reconstructed linear illumination schemes.
Later work by VanderKam, Drawnel, Duke/Goff, Ratzon, and others has continued to
reconstruct the Aramaic lunar computations.

Ratzon's mathematical reconstruction argues that the earlier Aramaic evidence
fits a coherent ancient model and also demonstrates development within the
Astronomical Book's composition/transmission.

### Physical assessment

A linear increase in illuminated fraction by fixed daily parts is an
approximation.

Actual apparent lunar illumination follows orbital geometry and is not a
simple linear function of days since conjunction.

### Disposition

`LUNAR_FRACTION_SCHEME = SOPHISTICATED_ANCIENT_MODEL / NOT_MODERN_ANOMALY`.

Potential value remains for history of mathematics, not external information.

## TEST 8 — 29/30-day lunar months

### Enochic feature

### Physical/historical assessment

The mean synodic month is about 29.53059 days, making 29/30-day months an
obvious and ancient practical solution.

Babylonian lunar calendrics were already built around 29/30-day months.

### Disposition

`29_30_MONTHS = ACCURATE_ANCIENT_PRACTICE / NON_ANOMALOUS`.

The basic observation that sunrise/sunset positions migrate seasonally is
available to naked-eye observers.

### Disposition

`SIX_GATES_AS_PRECISE_UNKNOWN_ASTRONOMICAL_COORDINATE_SYSTEM = NOT_ADMITTED`.

`SIX_GATES_AS_SCHEMATIC_HORIZON_MODEL = STRONG`.

## TEST 12 — Was Enoch behind contemporary knowledge?

No simple conclusion.

The Enochic material should not be dismissed as stupid astronomy.

It:
- performs nontrivial arithmetic;
- models solar/lunar cycles;
- coordinates lunar phases with a fixed calendar;
- uses schematic mathematical astronomy;
- participates in international knowledge networks;
- turns scientific knowledge into theological revelation.

The important historical result is not that the authors were ignorant.

It is that they used the **best or inherited mathematical structures available
to their scholarly environment for a theological calendrical project**.
