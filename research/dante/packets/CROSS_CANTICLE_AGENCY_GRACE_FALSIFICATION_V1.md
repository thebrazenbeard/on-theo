# Cross-canticle agency and grace — focused falsification packet V1

Scope: directly checked line ranges in Inferno V, Purgatorio XVI, Paradiso III and XXXIII; comparison against preceding Purgatorio XVII–XVIII and XXVII packet.

## Hypothesis under test

The poem contrasts fixation in disordered choice (Inferno), revisable moral responsibility (Purgatorio), and harmonized will (Paradiso). This is a *cross-canticle literary-theological pattern* to be challenged, not a claim that all characters satisfy a strict psychological three-state algorithm.

## 1. Inferno V.28–39 — the lustful

Source: https://dante.princeton.edu/cgi-bin/dante/campuscgi/mpb/GetCantoSection.pl?INP_LEN=15&INP_POEM=Inf&INP_SECT=5&INP_START=29&LANG=2

**Poem's narrative explanation:** The infernal storm persistently carries the lustful, whom the narrator characterizes as subordinating reason to desire (verse 39). That supplies a concrete, physicalized moral pattern, but it does not establish that each damned person is incapable of change because of a modern psychological condition. Francesca's later narrative may cause sympathy and interpretive uncertainty; pilgrim compassion is not necessarily the poet's ultimate judgment.

**Testable claim:** This passage associates an infernal punishment with an ordering of faculties. **Do not infer:** All Inferno circles are reducible to a single psychological principle.

## 2. Purgatorio XVI.64–81 — Marco Lombardo and freedom

Source: https://dante.princeton.edu/cgi-bin/dante/campuscgi/mpb/GetCantoSection.pl?INP_LEN=15&INP_POEM=Purg&INP_SECT=16&INP_START=66&LANG=2

**Primary speaker:** Marco Lombardo challenges the assumption that the stars/sky necessitate every human action. If so, free will and justice would be destroyed. The heavens may initiate inclinations; an agent still has reason and free will, capable of overcoming influences when nourished correctly. This supplies an important precondition for Virgil's later account of free choice in Purgatorio XVII–XVIII.

**Limitation:** The stars in this passage participate in Dante's medieval moral cosmology; neither astronomical determinism nor the modern scientific status of astrology is established by its appearance.

## 3. Purgatorio XVII–XVIII and XXVII — the intermediate arc

See: research/dante/packets/PURGATORIO_XVII_XVIII_LOVE_WILL_PACKET_V1.md

Marco's insistence on freedom is followed by Virgil's account of natural/elective love, assent, and eventually a restored will. The sequence warrants testing a literary pedagogy of moral freedom. The poem still requires encounters with Matelda and Beatrice, and privileges grace beyond merely human rational guidance.

## 4. Paradiso III.79–90 — Piccarda, hierarchical peace

Source: https://dante.princeton.edu/cgi-bin/dante/campuscgi/mpb/GetCantoSection.pl?INP_LEN=200&INP_POEM=Par&INP_SECT=3&INP_START=1&LANG=2

**Primary speaker:** Piccarda describes blessedness as having one's will within divine will. Levels of beatitude do not entail resentment among the blessed; the narrator concludes that heaven is everywhere Paradise though grace is not distributed identically. These are claims voiced within Dante's theological poetic construction, not directly verifiable observations about postmortem reality.

**Counterexample to simplistic equality:** Paradiso contains degrees of glory without interpersonal competitive suffering; harmony is not identical sameness.

## 5. Paradiso XXXIII.133–145 — the final limit

Source: https://dante.princeton.edu/cgi-bin/dante/campuscgi/mpb/GetCantoSection.pl?INP_LEN=30&INP_POEM=Par&INP_SECT=33&INP_START=116&LANG=2

**Narrator-poet:** The geometer simile (133–135) marks the inadequacy of intellectual calculation; after sight cannot attain its goal through its own powers, the mind is struck by an enabling flash (139–141). Imagination falters (142), while desire and will move in harmony with cosmic divine love (143–145).

**Interpretive correction:** Transformation cannot be represented accurately as autonomous, linear moral conditioning alone. The final vision depends on an act presented as grace. Even the most sympathetic modern, non-theological analogy must specify that it removes or changes a load-bearing theological mechanism.

## Rival models that survive

H1 — Three conditions of agency: supported as a **PARTIAL** literary-theological pattern.
H2 — Fixed/unfixed/harmonized will is a complete deterministic schema for all characters: **UNRESOLVED**, and counterexamples expected; full 100-canto audit absent.
H3 — The poem is centrally about inward psychological self-transformation independently of Christian grace: **NOT_ADMITTED**; Paradiso XXXIII resists.
H4 — The entire Earth is literally Dante's postmortem Purgatory: **NOT_ADMITTED as textual literal geography**, interpretive metaphor remains possible.
H5 — Dante encoded physical extra dimensions: **NOT_ADMITTED**; numbers are not coordinates, and no predictive mapping exists.

> HOSTILE COUNTERCLAIM: A selective five-locus arc can manufacture a triadic theory from a poem already divided into three canticles. One cannot call the pattern universal before testing incompatible characters, supernatural aid, theology, political judgments, and the poet's narrative irony.

Response: Accepted. Outcome is a line-scoped provisional hypothesis, not a universal theory. Next pass must include Inferno V.70–142 (Francesca's self-presentation), Inferno XI and XXXIV, Purgatorio XXVIII–XXXIII (Beatrice's correction), and Paradiso's hierarchy/intercession and doctrinal disputes. Test a poem-wide model against counterexamples before promoting.
