**Date researched:** 2026-10-19  
**Status:** RESEARCH DRAFT / SOURCE-CONTROLLED COMPARISON / REVIEW REQUIRED  
**Scope:** Mesopotamian narrative, attested ritual continuity, contemporary experiential practices, and limitations of the label "ego death".

## Question

Does the seven-gate descent in *Inana's Descent to the Netherworld* document an initiatory human ritual that can be authentically performed today? If not, which living practices offer documented methods for confronting attachment to the self, without laundering modern invention into ancient religious authority?

## A. Seven gates: what the text supports

**PRIMARY TEXT / VERIFIED_WITH_SCOPE.** The Oxford ETCSL composite translation of Sumerian composition c.1.4.1 describes Inana approaching palace Ganzer, confronting gatekeeper Neti, and passing through seven gates of the underworld (`kur`). Ereshkigal orders the gates bolted and opened in succession (lines 114–128).

At the gates Inana relinquishes the following according to ETCSL lines 129–163 (textual variations explicitly exist):
1. Turban/headgear (a manuscript variant additionally mentions measuring rod and line at the first gate).
2. Small lapis-lazuli neck beads.
3. Twin egg-shaped breast beads.
4. A named pectoral.
5. Golden ring.
6. Lapis-lazuli measuring rod and measuring line.
7. The `pala` garment of ladyship.

At each gate she questions the removal and receives a formula about the powers and rites of the underworld. The subsequent narrative includes her death-like incapacitation, aid mediated by Ninshubur/Enki and emissaries, restoration, and the condition that a substitute be provided (lines 164–289). The story does not end at a triumphant ego purge; the substitution of Dumuzid makes the return ethically and narratively complicated.

**HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION / BOUNDED.** These are features of a Sumerian literary composition transmitted by ancient manuscript witnesses. They are *not* automatically instructions for human participants. The underworld is not safely equated with the later Christian idea of Hell.

**UNKNOWN.** No primary ritual instruction or archaeological demonstration of a continuous, seven-stage human "death and rebirth" initiation based on these exact gates has been substantiated in this research pass. This is an evidence limit, **not a categorical proof that no such rite was ever performed**. A positive claim requires a dated ritual text, described cultic performance, or comparably probative evidence—not a modern ceremony invoking the myth.

## B. A living practice with direct concern for self-clinging: Chöd

**HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION / SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION.** Tibetan Buddhist Chöd (`gcod`, "cutting" or "severance") is associated with the 11th–12th-century teacher Machig Labdrön. Sources describe established ritual and meditation lineages, a philosophical concern with cutting fixation on self, and practices of imaginative self-offering and compassion. Chöd combines vocal liturgy, visualization and instruments. It is **a living religious practice**, not a Sumerian survival or an Inana-derived seven-gate rite.

The contemplative "body offering" is a **visualization**, not a physical sacrifice or instruction to inflict harm. Its primary doctrinal purpose concerns self-clinging and compassion, not reliably producing the modern phenomenological event called "ego dissolution". Different lineages, qualifications and transmissions matter; do not claim a self-paced course is equivalent to teacher-guided initiation.

## D. "Ego death" is an ambiguous goal, not an objectively verifiable rite outcome

**SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION.** Empirical research on ego dissolution, nondual awareness, mystical experiences, and altered self-boundaries finds overlapping and variable constructs. A transient change in self-experience is neither proof of permanent transformation nor necessary for ethical or relational change. Expecting one extraordinary episode to permanently remove habitual defenses is an unsupported inference.

- "Clarifying and measuring the characteristics of experiences that involve a loss of self or a dissolution of its boundaries," *Consciousness and Cognition* (2024): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38346364/

**SAFETY / SCOPE.** Intensive meditation, extreme austerity and drug-induced altered states are not interchangeable with a historically grounded ritual. They carry distinct risks, and spiritual providers are not automatically trained for psychiatric screening or trauma-sensitive practice. A 2020 systematic review identifies documented meditation-related adverse events, including severe cases, while noting wide variation in studies. A safer approach to any immersive retreat is to consult a qualified clinician if there is a relevant psychiatric/neurological history, ask providers about screening and the ability to stop or leave, and avoid fasting, dehydration, sleep deprivation, substances, hazardous exposure or simulated physical death. These precautions are general; no personal medical facts are recorded here.

- Farias et al., "Adverse events in meditation practices and meditation-based therapies: a systematic review," *Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica* (2020): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32820538/
- American Psychological Association, Roland Griffiths interview, "Psychedelic therapy": https://www.apa.org/research/action/speaking-of-psychology/psychedelic-therapy

## E. Comparison and current conclusion

| Criterion | Inana myth / seven gates | Contemporary Inana-themed workshop | Tibetan Chöd |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ancient text directly attests seven gates | Yes: Sumerian composition | Derived from it | No |
| Verifiable continuous historical ritual lineage for this specific practice | **UNKNOWN / not established in this packet** | No: modern adaptation | Yes: documented Tibetan Buddhist ritual lineage, *not* Inana's |
| Practice available now | Historical narrative; no verified original rite | Advertised modern online group course | Advertised study/practice through living institution |
| Main mode | Narrative text | Guided modern reflection and ritual | Liturgical/visualization practice aimed at self-clinging and compassion |
| Physical ordeal inherent | No evidence of human rite | No; online workshop | Not inherently a heroic endurance challenge |
| Guarantees ego dissolution/psychological healing | No | No | No |

**Disposition:** The strongest evidence-backed proposal is to choose *what type of authenticity matters*: (1) seven-gate iconography via a clearly labeled modern Inana-inspired workshop; or (2) a living, documented ritual lineage whose stated purpose includes cutting through self-clinging, such as Chöd, practiced with informed guidance. An Inana–Chöd hybrid cannot honestly be marketed as an ancient continuous rite, and combining the two would need explicit framing as new creative construction, not as a lineage claim.

### Hostile review

> **Objection:** The comparison may illicitly suggest that an intense spiritual rite offers clinical treatment or that Buddhist selflessness corresponds to Western "ego death". It may also privilege exoticized practices over ordinary supported change.

**Disposition: ACCEPTED.** Neither tradition substantiates clinical effectiveness for affect regulation, nor guarantees an altered state. Retain the comparison as *historical and phenomenological discovery*, not treatment advice, and avoid encouraging intensity for its own sake.

## Research gaps

1. Find any primary **ritual practice** (rather than myth narration) associated with descent to `kur`, and assess manuscript date/context before claiming initiation.
2. Distinguish Sumerian Inana composition c.1.4.1 from the later Akkadian *Descent of Ishtar*; avoid conflating gates, dates or ritual contexts.
3. Investigate modern providers' teacher qualifications, screening, participation criteria, trauma-awareness, ability to leave and current offerings; do not assume an advertised course has been independently evaluated.
4. Test whether other living rites of passage are historically grounded and suitable for the intended transformation without equating religious initiation with ego-dissolution outcomes.
