# Cowles Bog — Public Trail Reconnaissance Decision Protocol V3

**Status:** research-only; desktop sources inspected 2026-10-19; NOT a completed raster survey, route ground-truth, permission or event.
**Scope:** Public NPS and Indiana GIS evidence only. No participant identity, private ritual mapping or proprietary conceptual material is reproduced.

## Verifiable route and access sequence
The NPS description at https://www.nps.gov/places/cowles-bog-south.htm says:
1. North/main lot access; cross Mineral Springs Road after following the gravel entrance road. South/Greenbelt lot offers an alternate connector. These are public parking references, not proposed ceremonial stations.
2. Trail follows a wetland edge; approximately one mile from the north start is the loop junction, with counter-clockwise travel suggested.
3. Continue through black oak savanna and interdunal ponds.
4. Around the 1.5-mile point is the cut-off-trail junction. The full route continues through steep dunes to Lake Michigan; the cutoff is slightly less than one mile shorter and avoids that stretch.
5. The beach return moves west about 0.2 mile to a marked path over the dune, before rejoining the cutoff/Greenbelt/loop junctions. This is a **verbal trail description**, not a surveyed polyline.

NPS gives **4.7 miles, ~202 ft gain, max 15% grade, ~4 hours**, and describes a moderately rugged path with sand, no potable water, ticks, poison ivy, and weather differences near the lake. Closures and present-day conditions must be checked separately. NPS closure map: https://www.nps.gov/indu/planyourvisit/closuremaps.htm (a closure map not showing a Cowles Bog closure does not certify every segment is currently open).

## Bounded work plan for true visual reconnaissance

1. Obtain a *complete* authorized DUNE ACRES raster, retain source URL, filename, byte length and SHA-256, license statement, CRS/vertical datum, pixel resolution, acquisition year, footprint and provenance.
2. Load a matching bare-earth DEM, record elevation/CRS/vertical datum and compare using software that reports coordinate operations; never subtract heights in mismatched datums.
3. Locate a dated official park route GIS layer, if one exists, or georeference a dated map, reporting alignment uncertainty. Avoid treating crowdsourced trail GPS tracks as official.
4. Produce map overlays of habitat transitions only after visual inspection. Any photo candidate is a **public-trail observation zone**, not a private gate station or permit.
5. At the trail itself, verify signs, open access, stable footing, daylight, crowds and ecological restrictions. If anything differs, current signs and NPS advice supersede desktop inference.

## Operational choice

Use Cowles Bog only as an ordinary, permitted, optional approach/observation walk. Conduct all seven symbolic thresholds in a separate familiar or explicitly authorized space, preferably with level access and a clear exit. Never assign regalia stations to protected trail features. An ordinary indoor alternative preserves the complete symbolic inquiry if the walk is omitted.

## Three failure tests

> **Hostile objection:** “The NPS prose already tells you where seven places are; why not use them?” **Accepted objection to the premise:** It describes habitats and junctions, not rights to commandeer public access or seven suitable physical stopping points.

> **Hostile objection:** “A 2024 DEM settles safe hiking conditions in 2026.” **Rejected:** Bare-earth elevation cannot establish present washouts, trail obstructions, water, permissions, weather or accessibility.

> **Hostile objection:** “The full strenuous path is more authentic.” **Rejected:** Neither the Sumerian narrative nor park guidance makes distress or exertion a measure of validity. The cutoff or no walk remains an admissible decision.
