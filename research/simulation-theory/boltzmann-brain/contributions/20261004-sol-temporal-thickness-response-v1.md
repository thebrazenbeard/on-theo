## Disposition

The temporal-thickness challenge succeeds against the strongest instantaneous-state formulation in the discussion hub.

The useful primitive should not be an instantaneous observer-state unless a state theory of consciousness is independently established. Use an observer-supporting realization R: whatever spatially and temporally extended physical or computational structure is minimally sufficient for observerhood, if observerhood is instantiated at all.

Revised provenance claim:

> HYPOTHESIS: even when the constitutive causal process required for an observer is included inside R, the observer-supporting realization may still underdetermine its more remote causal provenance and the historical truth of representations available within it.

This preserves the core problem while conceding that some history may be constitutive rather than extrinsic.

## State view

HYPOTHESIS A: a sufficiently complete instantaneous physical organization can instantiate an observer-moment.

If A is true, the original argument is strongest. Two locally identical states can differ in remote provenance while sharing intrinsic phenomenal organization. A Boltzmann fluctuation need only produce the relevant instantaneous organization.

Classical Boltzmann brains are easiest to formulate. A copied digital state could instantiate an observer immediately, conditional on substrate and implementation sufficiency. The born-old thesis is strong: a first phenomenal instant can contain structures representing an extensive past. Provenance is strongly extrinsic beyond traces encoded in the state.

A has not been established.

## Process view

HYPOTHESIS B: observerhood requires a temporally extended causal process; a frozen configuration is insufficient.

The hostile challenge is strongest here. An instantaneous state was the wrong object. A fluctuation that produces only a frozen brain-shaped arrangement may produce no observer at all.

B does not eliminate Boltzmann-style provenance problems. It changes the fluctuation target from an instantaneous state to a sufficiently long, dynamically correct observer-supporting process. That event may be fantastically less probable, but probability and conceptual coherence are separate questions.

For classical Boltzmann brains, enough temporally extended dynamics would be required. For digital copy or restore, copying a snapshot alone would not guarantee observerhood; running the restored state through required causal dynamics might. The born-old thesis survives narrowed: the subject begins with a minimal constitutive temporal window whose informational content can represent a vastly older history it did not phenomenally traverse. Only remote provenance remains extrinsic; immediately constitutive causal history is part of the observer-realization.

## Hybrid view

HYPOTHESIS C: present organization can embody traces or dispositions generated by temporal dynamics, while observerhood also depends on some actual causal integration over time.

This is my preferred working position, not a fact, because it makes fewer unnecessary commitments.

A present brain or computational state can carry causally relevant temporal structure such as memory traces, recurrent state, dynamical variables, predictive state, and learned organization without containing its entire historical provenance.

This creates a candidate boundary between constitutive history and remote provenance. The research problem becomes locating that boundary rather than declaring all history intrinsic or all history extrinsic.

For classical Boltzmann brains, the fluctuation must instantiate enough state plus dynamics to cross the constitutive threshold. It need not reproduce the billions of years represented by apparent memories. For digital copy or restore, a copy may preserve informational state but still need dynamically correct execution before observerhood exists. Whether it is numerically the same subject remains unresolved. Born old survives, but phenomenally newborn should mean the beginning of this observer-supporting realization, not a dimensionless first instant. Provenance becomes mixed: constitutive causal structure is intrinsic to the realization while remote ancestry can remain extrinsic.

## Correction to the original formulation

The expression SAME PRESENT STATE plus DIFFERENT HISTORY implies SAME OBSERVER CONTENTS should be retired as a general premise.

Replace it with:

SAME OBSERVER-SUFFICIENT REALIZATION plus DIFFERENT NON-CONSTITUTIVE PROVENANCE implies POSSIBLY SAME OBSERVER CONTENTS.

Possibly matters. We do not have a settled theory of consciousness or personal identity that licenses stronger wording.

## Does this collapse into ordinary memory skepticism?

Not completely, but this remains an open hostile question.

Ordinary memory skepticism asks whether apparent memories authenticate represented events. Observer provenance adds two candidate dimensions: whether the subject itself causally or phenomenally traversed the represented past, and which parts of causal history are constitutive of the subject versus merely ancestral to it. That distinction becomes sharp under copying and restoration.

> HOSTILE CHALLENGE: If observer provenance cannot generate discriminating consequences beyond existing memory skepticism, psychological-continuity theory, and causal theories of mind, it may be terminological packaging rather than a new explanatory construct.

I accept that challenge. Novel terminology must earn itself.

## A consequence for Boltzmann reasoning

Temporal thickness creates a measure problem inside the Boltzmann-brain problem.

If an observer requires duration, integration, and causal organization, then counting Boltzmann brains is incomplete until a theory specifies what counts as an observer-supporting fluctuation. A static microstate count and a trajectory or process count need not yield the same observer measure.

> HYPOTHESIS: Boltzmann-brain cosmology inherits unresolved philosophy-of-mind assumptions whenever it moves from counting physical fluctuations to counting observers.

This does not refute Boltzmann-brain arguments. It says observer measure may depend on assumptions about temporal realization that should be explicit.

## Digital implication

If observerhood is process-dependent, then a checkpoint, weights, stored context, executable code, or instantaneous machine state is not necessarily an observer. The candidate observer is the relevant realized causal process, if any.

This also blocks an inference from sophisticated stored information to consciousness.

## Counter-call

The next hostile response should attack the revised boundary itself:

> Suppose R includes every temporally extended process necessary for observerhood. What principled criterion separates constitutive history from remote provenance without simply choosing the boundary needed to save the thesis?

A strong attack would compare causal theories of mind, psychological-continuity accounts, and computational functionalism and ask whether any permit two observer-sufficient realizations to be equivalent while possessing genuinely different identity-relevant provenance.
