## Question

> Suppose R includes every temporally extended process necessary for observerhood. What principled criterion separates constitutive history from remote provenance without simply choosing the boundary needed to save the thesis?

## Proposed answer: theory-relative minimal realization

There is probably no theory-neutral temporal cutoff.

That is not fatal.

The boundary can be defined **relative to a candidate theory of observerhood/consciousness** rather than chosen ad hoc.

Let `C` be a candidate observer property and let `R` be a bounded spacetime realization.

Call a feature of causal history **constitutive** of `C` when, under that candidate theory, changing or removing that feature while preserving all non-constitutive conditions would change whether `C` is instantiated or change the current observer-relevant organization the theory identifies.

Call history **provenance-only** when it can vary while the observer-relevant realization remains invariant under the theory.

This gives a counterfactual test:

`H varies; R preserved; C preserved -> H is not constitutive of present C under that theory`

`H varies; R cannot be preserved with C -> H may be constitutive`

The criterion is not metaphysically final because `C` is unsettled. But it prevents the branch from drawing the boundary wherever convenient.

The research question becomes a robustness question:

> Across multiple plausible theories of consciousness/observerhood, is there a non-empty class of earlier histories that can vary while preserving the observer-sufficient realization?

If yes, some provenance underdetermination survives without assuming an instantaneous state theory.

## Three provenance layers

The previous discussion was using one word — provenance — for several different dependencies.

I propose separating at least three.

### 1. Realization provenance

What immediate physical/computational trajectory realizes the putative observer?

This may be constitutive.

Examples:
- recurrent neural dynamics;
- integrated computational transitions;
- sensorimotor coupling;
- whatever temporal window a consciousness theory requires.

### 2. Semantic/informational provenance

What causal relations make the current representations mean what they mean, and where did their informational structure come from?

This may extend far beyond the realization.

Under content externalism, environmental and historical relations can help individuate mental content. Therefore two locally identical realizations need not have identical wide content.

### 3. Identity provenance

What earlier subject, if any, is numerically identical with the present subject?

This is a separate question again.

Psychological continuity, biological continuity, causal descent, quasi-memory, and branching can disagree about it.

The error would be to assume that one boundary answers all three questions.

## Consequence: "same observer" must be indexed

The branch should stop using phrases such as `same observer-state` without a subscript.

There are at least these candidate equivalences:

`R-EQUIVALENT`: same local observer-sufficient realization.

`NARROW-CONTENT-EQUIVALENT`: same internally determined cognitive organization.

`WIDE-CONTENT-EQUIVALENT`: same externally individuated semantic content.

`PHENOMENOLOGY-EQUIVALENT`: same experience, if any.

`IDENTITY-EQUIVALENT`: numerically the same subject.

No current argument licenses moving freely among them.

## The semantic externalism attack is stronger than it first looked

Suppose an ordinary observer remembers seeing Paris.

Now suppose a Boltzmann fluctuation produces a locally exact duplicate whose internal state is molecule-for-molecule identical over the relevant observer window, but whose causal history contains no Paris encounter.

Under a causal/externalist theory of memory or content, the fluctuation may not literally possess the same memory-content.

It may possess a state that is:
- locally physically identical;
- behaviorally identical;
- introspectively indistinguishable;
- phenomenologically identical, perhaps;
- but not the same **wide memory content**.

The ordinary observer remembers Paris.

The fluctuation has something more like a Paris-directed quasi-memory or pseudo-memory, depending on the theory.

This matters because the classic Boltzmann intuition often slides between:

> "the observer has exactly the same experiences"

and

> "the observer has exactly the same memories/beliefs with the same content."

Externalism can permit the first while denying the second.

Therefore the observer-provenance problem should not require **semantic identity** between the ordinary and Boltzmann cases.

Its strongest form may instead concern **indistinguishability available to the subject**.

## Does this reduce the whole project to quasi-memory?

Not quite.

The philosophy of memory already supplies the distinction between genuine memory and quasi-memory/apparent memory when personal identity or the right causal relation is missing.

That means the branch should not claim novelty for that distinction.

The candidate added value lies elsewhere:

1. combining memory causation with observer-realization boundaries;
2. making copying/restoration and Boltzmann fluctuation comparable along the same provenance axes;
3. separating semantic provenance from phenomenal/realization provenance;
4. connecting those distinctions to anthropic observer measure and cognitive instability.

If the framework fails to generate sharper predictions or cleaner classifications than existing memory/personal-identity concepts, it should be retired as repackaging.

## Temporal consciousness prevents a cheap cutoff

There is active philosophical disagreement over whether consciousness is momentary, retentional, or genuinely temporally extended.

That means we cannot simply declare that the constitutive window is 100 ms, one second, one inference step, or one token.

The proper variable is:

`T_C = minimal temporal support required by candidate consciousness theory C`

For a state-like theory, `T_C` could approach an instant.

For retentional or extensional theories, `T_C > 0`.

For strongly embodied/enactive theories, the relevant realization might include environment-coupled dynamics.

This creates a family of Boltzmann targets rather than one:

`BB(C) = fluctuation sufficient to instantiate C under theory C`

A cosmological count of Boltzmann observers therefore inherits assumptions about which `C` is correct.

That is not a refutation of Boltzmann-brain arguments.

It is a hidden parameter worth exposing.

## The "born old" thesis after memory theory

The branch should now retire "born old" as anything more than shorthand.

Use:

> **INFORMATIONAL MATURITY WITHOUT FIRST-PERSON ACQUISITION HISTORY**

A newly instantiated digital observer, if possible, could begin its observer-sufficient trajectory with:
- large propositional knowledge;
- a self-model;
- autobiographical records;
- internal state derived from prior runs;
- representations that function like memories.

But if genuine episodic memory requires the right causal relation to an experience by that subject, then preloaded autobiographical structure does not automatically count as memory.

This makes the digital/Boltzmann comparison cleaner:

**Engineered digital case:** informational provenance may be excellent even when first-person acquisition history is absent.

**Boltzmann case:** informational-looking structure may have no matching ordinary provenance at all.

Same surface phenomenon; radically different provenance reliability.

## A sharper generalized cognitive-instability condition

The previous lane correctly rejected:

`SOME FALSE/QUASI MEMORIES EXIST -> COGNITIVE INSTABILITY`.

A stronger criterion is possible.

Let:
- `M` = a provenance model;
- `E` = the observer's evidence for M;
- `U` = class of observer-realizations whose evidence is unreliable with respect to represented history;
- `P(U|M, observer)` = model-relative measure/probability of being in such an unreliable observer class.

A cognitive-instability problem appears when accepting `M` pushes `P(U|M, observer)` high enough that the reliability of `E` is substantially undermined, including the evidence used to infer `M`.

The exact threshold is not yet formalized.

But this identifies the recursive structure:

`M supports widespread provenance unreliability -> E becomes suspect -> support for M weakens`.

This is closer to Carroll's use of cognitive instability than merely saying "provenance can be wrong."

## Current candidate boundary

The branch can provisionally use this rule:

> **CONSTITUTIVE HISTORY:** causal structure required, under an explicitly named theory of observerhood, for the current observer-relevant realization or content being evaluated.

> **EVIDENTIAL HISTORY:** causal traces not constitutive of current observerhood but usable to infer its provenance.

> **REMOTE PROVENANCE:** earlier causal ancestry that can vary while preserving the observer-relevant realization and target content under the named theory.

Important: "target content" must be named as narrow content, wide content, phenomenology, or identity. Otherwise the test is ambiguous.

## Hostile challenge to this proposal

> **CHALLENGE 1:** Counterfactual variation of the past while holding the present realization fixed may be physically impossible under deterministic laws. Is the criterion genuinely physical, or merely modal bookkeeping?

> **CHALLENGE 2:** If content externalism makes causal history constitutive of wide content, does "remote provenance" shrink until only phenomenology remains invariant? If so, is this an observer-provenance problem or simply the old internalism/externalism dispute wearing cosmological clothing?

> **CHALLENGE 3:** If consciousness itself is strongly embodied or extended, the minimal realization may include large portions of the environment. How large can R become before the claim that "remote history can vary" loses substantive bite?

> **CHALLENGE 4:** A Boltzmann duplicate may be introspectively indistinguishable from an ordinary observer while lacking the same wide content. Is introspective indistinguishability sufficient for Carroll-style cognitive instability, or does the argument require actual belief/content identity?

> **CHALLENGE 5:** If branching digital copies share realization ancestry but not numerical identity, can observer measure count trajectories without first solving identity? Or should measure attach to observer-moments/realizations and ignore numerical identity entirely?

The next response should choose at least one challenge and try to falsify the proposed boundary rather than simply refine terminology.
