# Flood Tradition Transmission Model V1

Status: HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION / CARRIER MODEL / REVIEW REQUIRED

## Question

How could Mesopotamian flood traditions reach the Hebrew Genesis tradition?

## Established contact capacity

ON_THEO already records direct material evidence of Mesopotamian literary circulation in the Levant:

- Middle Babylonian Gilgamesh tablets at Ugarit;
- Akkadian school/lexical texts at Ugarit;
- multilingual cuneiform scribal culture.

Therefore:

`MESOPOTAMIAN_LITERARY_CIRCULATION_IN_LEVANT = SUPPORTED`.

This prevents the false premise that Genesis and Mesopotamian literature existed in isolated cultural bubbles.

## Possible transmission windows

### Late Bronze Age Levant

Akkadian functioned as a major scribal/diplomatic language.
Mesopotamian literary and school texts circulated in western scribal centers.

This supplies early contact capacity.

It does not prove direct continuity into Genesis.

### Assyrian imperial period

Judah and Israel existed within an Assyrian imperial cultural world where Mesopotamian political, scribal, theological, and literary forms could circulate.

Specific flood-text carrier remains unknown.

### Babylonian exile

Judean elites living in Babylonia provide an obvious high-capacity contact environment.

This is historically important if major Genesis shaping occurred in exilic/post-exilic periods.

But:

`BABYLONIAN_EXILE = DIRECT_COPYING_OF_GILGAMESH`

is not established.

### Persian / early Second Temple scribal world

Continued Aramaic/Akkadian intellectual circulation offers additional channels for literary inheritance and redaction.

## Model ranking

### C0 — independent invention

No meaningful Mesopotamian tradition input.

Status:
WEAK relative to density/order of parallels.

### C1 — diffuse common ancient Near Eastern tradition

Genesis inherited a broad flood tradition through long oral/literary circulation without a recoverable immediate source.

Status:
PLAUSIBLE.

### C2 — Mesopotamian literary/tradition mediation

Biblical flood tradition was shaped by known Mesopotamian flood literature or closely related West-Semitic recensions through one or more scribal channels.

Status:
CURRENTLY STRONGEST.

### C3 — direct Gilgamesh manuscript copying

A specific Genesis author copied a Gilgamesh text.

Status:
POSSIBLE / NOT REQUIRED / NOT ESTABLISHED.

### C4 — direct Ziusudra manuscript copying

Genesis directly copied the surviving Sumerian Ziusudra text.

Status:
NOT ESTABLISHED / UNNECESSARY.

## Why direct copying is unnecessary

Transmission can occur through:
- oral retelling;
- bilingual scribal explanation;
- school tradition;
- summaries;
- intermediary recensions;
- adaptation from an already transformed West-Semitic version;
- multiple sources.

Therefore a strong dependence conclusion does not require identifying one physical source tablet.

## Evidence ceiling

The current evidence can support:

`GENESIS_ULTIMATELY_PARTICIPATES_IN_AND_REWORKS_A_MESOPOTAMIAN_FLOOD_TRADITION`.

It cannot yet support:

`WE_CAN_NAME_THE_EXACT_TABLET_OR_SCRIBE_THAT_TRANSMITTED_IT`.
