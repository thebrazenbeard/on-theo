## Scope

This packet inventories:
1. direct named Mastema occurrences in the current VanderKam verse numbering;
2. one immediate lexical bridge where the same narrative calls the retained spirits subject to "the satan";
3. physical/versional witnesses that actually cover those loci;
4. ancient Hebrew related-tradition evidence that preserves `שר המשטמה` but is **not** a manuscript copy of Jubilees.

It does not infer that every use of "satan" in Jubilees names Mastema.

## Immediate lexical bridge: Jubilees 10:11

Jub. 10:11 does not repeat the name Mastema in VanderKam's English rendering. It says that the retained tenth of the evil spirits exercises power on earth "before the satan."

Because this is the immediate continuation of Mastema's request in 10:8-9, it is a strong contextual bridge between the Mastema narrative and `satan` language.

But the versional problem stays explicit:
- the Ethiopic expression can be rendered "Satan/the satan";
- Ethiopic article behavior complicates deciding whether this is a proper name;
- this single bridge does not license replacing every Mastema occurrence with Satan or every Jubilees `satan` occurrence with Mastema.

Classification:
`CONTEXTUAL_LEXICAL_BRIDGE / IDENTITY_NOT_GENERALIZED`.

## Other Jubilees satan/adversary vocabulary

Jubilees also contains uses of satan/adversary language outside direct named-Mastema narration.

Those occurrences are not admitted into the named-Mastema inventory merely because English translations may use "satan".

Rule:
`SATAN_LEXEME_OR_FUNCTION != AUTOMATIC_MASTEMA_PROPER_NAME`.

## Direct physical/versional witness inventory

### Ethiopic / Geʿez tradition

The complete text of Jubilees survives in Geʿez manuscripts. James VanderKam's 1989 critical edition collated fifteen Ethiopic manuscripts and is the textual basis for the current passage numbering/readback used here.

A key exact physical witness is:

- British Library `Or 485`
- language: Geʿez
- material: parchment codex
- date: 16th century
- Jubilees begins at fol. 1r; Enoch begins at fol. 102r
- role: complete direct textual witness; VanderKam's edition treats manuscript 25/B, identified with Or 485, as the best available Ethiopic copy and uses it heavily in establishing the critical text.

This witness can in principle cover all twelve named-Mastema loci because its Jubilees text is complete across the relevant chapters.

A second exact catalogued Geʿez witness is:
- British Library Endangered Archives `EAP286/1/1/414`
- title: Book of Jubilees
- date: 1505-1506
- parchment codex
- role in V1: independent catalogued Geʿez manuscript witness; passage-by-passage collation against Mastema loci has not been performed here.

V1 does not treat either late physical manuscript date as the composition date of Jubilees.

### Latin palimpsest

- Biblioteca Ambrosiana `C 73 inf` / `S.P. 9/9-10`
- language: Latin
- date: fifth century CE
- format: palimpsested codex
- preserved Jubilees: fragmentary, portions from chapters 13-49
- critical coverage reported for the Latin includes `46:12-48:5`.

Therefore the surviving Latin witness directly covers Jub. 48:2, one of the named-Mastema loci.

This matters because it supplies a substantially earlier external witness than the extant Geʿez manuscripts for at least that part of the Mastema narrative.

V1 does **not** claim Latin coverage for the other named loci unless the preserved range explicitly includes them.

### Direct Hebrew Jubilees manuscripts at Qumran

The published Hebrew Jubilees fragments are ancient direct witnesses to the work, but their surviving passage ranges do **not** include the twelve named Mastema loci inventoried above.

Important negative result:

`NO_SURVIVING_DIRECT_QUMRAN_JUBILEES_FRAGMENT_IN_THIS_COVERAGE_SET_PRESERVES_A_NAMED_MASTEMA_LOCUS`.

That is a manuscript-survival statement, **not** evidence that the Hebrew original lacked Mastema.

2Q20 preserves Jub. 46:1-3, including nearby adversary language; 4Q222 preserves Jub. 48:5, close to the Exodus Mastema sequence. Neither surviving fragment covers the named Mastema verses themselves.

## Ancient Hebrew related witness: 4Q225 / Pseudo-Jubilees

4Q225 is not catalogued here as a manuscript of the Book of Jubilees.

It is a related Hebrew retelling/Pseudo-Jubilees text.

Its Akedah material is nevertheless high-value transmission evidence because the surviving Hebrew explicitly preserves Mastema language:

- 4Q225 frg. 2 i,9: reconstructed `שר המשטמה` in the Akedah setup;
- 4Q225 frg. 2 i,10: Mastema opposes/challenges Abraham in relation to Isaac;
- 4Q225 frg. 2 ii,6-7: Mastema/angels-of-Mastema language;
- 4Q225 frg. 2 ii,13: prince Mastema is bound.

The relationship of the 4Q225 Akedah to Jubilees is close enough that scholarship analyzes it as drawing on the Jubilees Akedah while also abbreviating and innovating.

Classification:
`ANCIENT_HEBREW_RELATED_TRADITION / DEPENDENCE_OR_SHARED_TEXTUAL_TRADITION_STRONGLY_SUPPORTED / NOT_DIRECT_JUBILEES_MANUSCRIPT`.

## Indirect Greek reception witness

A Greek indirect quotation preserved by Syncellus for the Jubilees Akedah tradition uses a form of Mastema and describes him as ruler/prince of demons.

Because this is an indirect later quotation, V1 records it as:
`INDIRECT_GREEK_RECEPTION_WITNESS`.

It is useful for version history but is not treated as a surviving ancient physical Greek manuscript of Jubilees.

## Witness conclusion

The witness topology is asymmetric:

1. the **complete named-Mastema narrative** is recoverable principally through the later complete Geʿez tradition;
2. the **fifth-century Latin palimpsest** independently covers at least Jub. 48:2;
3. the surviving **direct Hebrew Qumran Jubilees fragments** do not preserve any of the twelve named-Mastema loci;
4. **4Q225**, a related Hebrew Pseudo-Jubilees retelling, independently proves that the title/name `שר המשטמה` and Mastema's Akedah role existed in ancient Hebrew Jubilees-related tradition.

That combination supports ancient Hebrew antiquity for the Mastema tradition without pretending 4Q225 is a direct copy of Jubilees.

## Claim ceiling

This packet does not establish:
- that every Mastema verse has an extant Hebrew witness;
- that 4Q225 is a Jubilees manuscript;
- that every Jubilees use of satan/adversary language denotes Mastema;
- that Mastema is identical with Belial;
- that Mastema is identical with Job's `ha-satan`;
- that the late Geʿez physical manuscript dates reflect the work's composition date;
- one exact direction of dependence for every Mastema tradition outside the close Akedah relationship.
