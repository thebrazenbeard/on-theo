# Lailoken / Llallogan Witness Control V1

Status: PROVISIONAL / SOURCE-BOUND
Date: 2026-10-03
Parent lane: Merlin historical-core research
Repository role: ON_THEO

## Research question

Does the Lailoken tradition independently preserve a pre-Geoffrey northern wild-man biography that can be identified with Myrddin/Merlin, or is that identity itself a later medieval synthesis?

## Evidence firewall

- LAILOKEN != MYRDDIN unless a witness makes the identification.
- A LATE MANUSCRIPT may preserve older material, but manuscript age and composition age must remain separate.
- GEOFFREY PARALLEL != INDEPENDENCE.
- JOCELIN'S LALOECEN != FULL VITA MERLINI SILVESTRIS BY DEFAULT.
- THREEFOLD DEATH != HISTORICAL DEATH.
- REAL RHYDDERCH != REAL LAILOKEN.

## Witness A — Jocelin of Furness, Life of St Kentigern

Evidence class: LATER HAGIOGRAPHY

Jocelin of Furness composed his *Life of St Kentigern* in the later twelfth century. In the Rhydderch material he includes a `homo fatuus` at court named Laloecen/Laloicen who possesses prophetic ability and foretells royal and ecclesiastical deaths.

This is important because:
- the Laloecen name occurs independently of the fully developed Merlin romance;
- he belongs to Rhydderch's northern British setting;
- he functions as a prophet/fool.

But Jocelin does **not** supply the entire later wild-man biography as found in the *Vita Merlini Silvestris*.

## Witness B — Vita Merlini Silvestris / Lailoken and Kentigern

Evidence class: LATER HAGIOGRAPHIC / LEGENDARY TEXT

Winifred and John MacQueen's edition shows that the *Vita Merlini Silvestris* survives in only one manuscript: BL Cotton Titus A XIX, fols 74–75, a fifteenth-century witness.

The text introduces a naked, hairy, mad wilderness figure named Lailoken. Crucially, it does not simply identify him as Merlin without qualification. It says that **some people say** Lailoken was Merlin, distinguished among the Britons for prophecy, while the narrator marks the identification as uncertain.

Source:
Winifred and John MacQueen, "Vita Merlini Silvestris," *Scottish Studies* 29 (1985):
https://open.journals.ed.ac.uk/ScottishStudies/article/view/596
PDF:
https://open.journals.ed.ac.uk/ScottishStudies/article/download/596/622/651

Implication:
The equation LAILOKEN = MERLIN is itself visible as a debated transmission claim inside the medieval source.

State:
LAILOKEN/MERLIN IDENTITY = UNRESOLVED IN THE WITNESS ITSELF.

## Witness C — Possible relationship to earlier Kentigern material

The same Cotton manuscript places the Lailoken narratives alongside an anonymous fragmentary Life of Kentigern composed for Herbert, bishop of Glasgow (1147–1164). MacQueen and MacQueen explored whether some Lailoken material may depend on or preserve matter associated with an earlier Kentigern tradition.

This is a plausible transmission route, not a demonstrated date for the surviving Lailoken text.

Source:
MacQueen & MacQueen 1985, introduction:
https://open.journals.ed.ac.uk/ScottishStudies/article/download/596/622/651

State:
EARLIER SOURCE BEHIND VMS = POSSIBLE / NOT DEMONSTRATED.

## Witness D — Lailoken's battle confession

In the *Vita Merlini Silvestris*, Lailoken says he caused the deaths in a famous battle fought on a plain between Liddel and Carwannok/Carwinley.

This northern topography lies close to the traditional Arfderydd/Arthuret zone, which makes the parallel important.

However:
- the text does not provide contemporary battle testimony;
- the surviving witness is fifteenth century;
- identifying the battle explicitly with historical Arfderydd is reconstruction;
- the causal confession itself belongs to legend.

State:
NORTHERN BATTLE ASSOCIATION = SURVIVES AS LATER TRADITION.
ARFDERYDD IDENTIFICATION = PLAUSIBLE RECONSTRUCTION / NOT PRIMARY FACT.

## Witness E — visionary rupture and wilderness

The *Vita Merlini Silvestris* portrays Lailoken as a wilderness madman whose condition is associated with a terrifying visionary experience: the sky opens and armed hosts appear, after which he lives among beasts.

This differs from a simple "battle trauma -> forest" modern reconstruction. The source's own causal imagination is visionary/religious.

Implication:
A literary reconstruction should not retroactively diagnose the legendary Lailoken. Trauma/pattern-recognition is one modern explanatory option, not the source's own settled explanation.

State:
VISIONARY MADNESS MOTIF = LATER TRADITION.
MEDICAL/PSYCHOLOGICAL CAUSE = UNKNOWN.

## Witness F — threefold death

Lailoken predicts and undergoes the famous triple death motif.

Comparative folklore shows the threefold-death pattern has a broader distribution, so it cannot function as a uniquely biographical fingerprint for a historical northern seer.

State:
FOLKLORE MOTIF / NOT HISTORICAL DEATH EVIDENCE.

## Relationship to Welsh Llallogan

Welsh Myrddin material contains forms such as Llallawg/Llallwgan associated with Myrddin. Scholarship has long compared these with Lailoken/Laloecen.

The linguistic and textual relationship remains relevant, but resemblance of names does not by itself establish:
- one historical man;
- direction of borrowing;
- chronological priority.

State:
RELATIONSHIP = SCHOLARLY PLAUSIBILITY / HISTORICAL IDENTITY UNRESOLVED.

## Revised model

The Lailoken lane is **not** strong enough to anchor a historical Merlin biography.

What it does establish is more subtle and more valuable:

1. by the later twelfth century, a northern prophetic/fool figure named Laloecen existed in Kentigern tradition;
2. a later Lailoken wild-man narrative explicitly knew that some people equated him with Merlin;
3. the source itself preserves uncertainty about that equation;
4. the northern battle/wilderness/prophecy complex therefore existed as a partly independent-looking tradition, but its exact pre-Geoffrey depth remains unresolved.

## Hostile challenge

> Calling Lailoken "the real Merlin" simply shifts the evidentiary problem from one name to another. Jocelin is late, the full wild-man narrative survives later still, the Merlin identification is explicitly qualified, and parallels with Geoffrey can reflect shared sources, later cross-contamination, or deliberate harmonization. The northern setting may be genuinely old while the individual biography remains legendary.

Status:
- LAILOKEN TRADITION: SURVIVES.
- LAILOKEN AS HISTORICAL SIXTH-CENTURY MAN: UNRESOLVED.
- LAILOKEN = MYRDDIN/MERLIN: UNRESOLVED.
- NORTHERN WILD-MAN COMPLEX AS ONE INPUT TO MEDIEVAL MERLIN: SURVIVES.

## Downstream consequence for Testament

Book IV should not reveal that the unnamed survivor "really was Lailoken."

Instead, the Lailoken layer should be narrated as a later northern witness tradition that may or may not preserve the same person. The uncertainty belongs inside the story.
