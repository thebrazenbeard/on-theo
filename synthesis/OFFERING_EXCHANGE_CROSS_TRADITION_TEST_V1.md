# Offering Exchange Cross-Tradition Test V1

Status: THREE-LANE PARTIAL/BOUNDED COMPARISON / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

The neutral question is:

What is offered, to whom or what, through what mediator, for what outcome,
and how reciprocal is the relationship represented?

Three current source lanes are admitted:
- Vedic / Hindu;
- Norse;
- Sumerian.

Buddhist, Daoist, and Shinto lanes are not filled merely for symmetry.

A key methodological correction is required:

OFFERING_ACT != RECIPROCAL_EXCHANGE.

The comparison therefore decomposes offering action from recipient, effect, and
reciprocity.

Method schema:

synthesis/OFFERING_EXCHANGE_COMPARISON_SCHEMA_V1.yaml

## Cross-lane comparison

The three lanes occupy different evidentiary positions.

Vedic:
- strongest evidence: prescribed ritual action, ordered offerings, and
  source-asserted or implied ritual effect;
- weakest current field: one generalized reciprocity theory.

Norse:
- strongest evidence: ritual-context deposition and feasting materially
  attested;
- weakest current fields: named recipient, requested outcome, reciprocity.

Sumerian:
- strongest evidence: selected texts explicitly link funerary provisioning to
  represented welfare of the dead, and some death narratives specify recipients
  and offerings;
- material archaeology confirms grave provisioning and differentiation but does
  not independently prove the literary mechanism.

## Reciprocity test

The axis name OFFERING_EXCHANGE_MODEL must not pre-decide that exchange is
reciprocal.

At current evidence depth:
- Vedic reciprocity: rite-specific and unresolved as one general mechanism.
- Norse reciprocity: unknown from the current material lane.
- Sumerian funerary provisioning: maintenance/obligation is better supported
  than quid-pro-quo reciprocity.

Therefore:

OFFERING_PRESENT != RECIPROCAL_TRANSACTION_ESTABLISHED.

## Recipient test

The recipient field is also asymmetrical:
- Vedic: varies by rite and is not yet reduced to one global recipient model.
- Norse: often unidentified from the archaeological evidence alone.
- Sumerian: deceased persons/ghosts or named underworld/divine authorities are
  explicit in selected textual cases.

Therefore:

OFFERING_SIMILARITY cannot be measured responsibly without recipient control.

## Efficacy test

The three lanes also differ on what can be said about efficacy:
- Vedic ritual texts may assert or imply effects from correct ritual
  configuration.
- Norse material evidence demonstrates action but often not its intended or
  believed effect.
- Sumerian literary evidence can explicitly represent provisioning as affecting
  postmortem welfare.

Therefore:

RITUAL_ACTION != CLAIMED_EFFECT != EMPIRICALLY_VERIFIED_EFFECT.

## Core result

Supported:

OFFERING_AND_DEPOSITION_PRACTICES_CAN_MEDIATE_RELIGIOUS_OR_POSTMORTEM_RELATIONSHIPS_THROUGH_DISTINCT_SOURCE_SPECIFIC_MECHANISMS.

Not supported:

ALL_THREE_SHARE_A_DO_UT_DES_OR_QUID_PRO_QUO_SYSTEM.

Not supported:

ALL_OFFERINGS_HAVE_AN_IDENTIFIABLE_DIVINE_RECIPIENT.

Not supported:

MATERIAL_DEPOSITION_ALONE_REVEALS_REQUESTED_OUTCOME.

Not supported:

SIMILAR_OFFERING_FORMS_IMPLY_TRANSMISSION_OR_COMMON_ORIGIN.

## Comparative-problem-space consequence

The shared problem-space is stronger than any shared exchange theory:
- what is transferred;
- who or what receives it;
- who mediates the transfer;
- what makes it ritually valid;
- whether an effect is represented;
- whether reciprocity is expected;
- how kinship, status, feast, sacrifice, landscape, and funerary obligation
  change the mechanism.

Therefore:

SHARED_OFFERING_PROBLEM_SPACE != ONE_SHARED_SACRIFICIAL_ECONOMY.
