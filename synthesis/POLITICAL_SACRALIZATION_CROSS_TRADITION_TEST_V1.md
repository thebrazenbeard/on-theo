# Political Sacralization Cross-Tradition Test V1

Status: FOUR-LANE BOUNDED/PARTIAL COMPARISON / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

The neutral axis is:

> How is political authority legitimized, granted, inherited, ritually
> maintained, cosmically ordered, divinely authorized, or contested?

Four current source lanes now contain sufficient exact evidence to compare
different relationships between sacred order and political authority:

- Sumerian;
- Vedic / Hindu;
- early religious Daoism;
- classical / state-ritual Shinto.

Buddhism and Norse religion remain unadmitted for this axis.

The comparison does not assume that all four possess a single institution or
doctrine called "sacred kingship."

## Cross-lane comparison

The four lanes answer the same neutral question through materially different
mechanisms.

### Authorization source

- Sumerian: divine/cosmic kingship authorization in controlled literary
  traditions.
- Vedic: ritual performance and ritual institutions structure sovereignty.
- Daoist: religious office becomes an instrument of territorial governance.
- Shinto: celestial genealogy legitimates rule in court myth, while state
  institutions administer cult.

### Institutional mediator

- Sumerian: king / deity / temple-political order.
- Vedic: sacrificer-king and specialist ritual system.
- Daoist: Celestial Masters hierarchy and libationers.
- Shinto: court lineage, ritual offices, shrine/state administration.

### Relation between religion and office

- Sumerian: political office is represented as divinely authorized.
- Vedic: political status is ritually constituted or consolidated in bounded
  royal rites.
- Daoist: religious office performs political-administrative functions.
- Shinto: court myth supplies genealogical authorization while state ritual
  supplies institutional maintenance.

These are not interchangeable.

## Core result

The evidence supports:

`SACRED_ORDER_CAN_STRUCTURE_POLITICAL_AUTHORITY_IN_MULTIPLE_DISTINCT_WAYS`.

It does not support:

`ONE_SACRED_KINGSHIP_MODEL`.

Nor does it support:

`COMMON_MECHANISM -> COMMON_ORIGIN`.

The neutral axis is useful precisely because it preserves difference.

## Evidence-layer result

The four cells are not evidentially symmetrical:

- Sumerian: bounded literary/source-verification result;
- Vedic: partial textual-ritual plus scholarly historical reconstruction;
- Daoist: partial primary historiographic plus scholarly reconstruction;
- Shinto: bounded court primary text plus institutional historical
  reconstruction.

Comparison therefore occurs at the proposition/mechanism level, not by treating
all evidence classes as equivalent.

## Non-admitted lanes

### Norse

Current source-bound lane does not admit a political-sacralization cell.

## Method consequence

Future political-sacralization comparison should record at least:

1. source/historical layer;
2. evidence mode;
3. political office or authority at issue;
4. authorization or legitimation source;
5. ritual/institutional mediator;
6. succession/continuity mechanism if present;
7. contestation or refusal where present;
8. claim ceiling.

The label `POLITICAL_SACRALIZATION_MODEL` is an axis, not a mechanism.
