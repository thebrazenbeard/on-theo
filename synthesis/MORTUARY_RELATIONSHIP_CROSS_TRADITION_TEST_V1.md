# Mortuary Relationship Cross-Tradition Test V1

Status: THREE-LANE BOUNDED/PARTIAL COMPARISON / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

The neutral axis is:

> How are the dead related to the living through kinship, memory, offerings,
> ritual maintenance, social identity, protection, danger, or obligation?

Three current source lanes are admitted:

- Sumerian;
- Norse;
- Shinto, in later and diachronically controlled layers.

Buddhist, Hindu, and Daoist lanes are not filled merely to make the matrix
symmetrical.

The test distinguishes two questions that are often collapsed:

1. How do living communities treat bodies, graves, possessions, memories, or
   posthumous identities?
2. What does a source explicitly claim about the continuing person/spirit and
   the effect of those actions on that postmortem subject?

The first can be supported materially without proving the second.

Method schema:

`synthesis/MORTUARY_RELATIONSHIP_COMPARISON_SCHEMA_V1.yaml`.

## Cross-lane comparison

The three admitted lanes share one broad feature:

`DEATH_DOES_NOT_END_SOCIAL_OR_RITUAL_RELATION_BY_DEFAULT_IN_THE_CONTROLLED_EVIDENCE`.

But this proposition has different evidence and mechanisms.

### Sumerian

Strongest controlled relationship:
- descendants/provisioning/offerings and represented welfare of the dead.

### Norse

Strongest controlled relationship:
- bodies, graves, possessions, memory, reopening, and protection as continuing
  social/ritual concerns.

### Shinto

Strongest controlled relationship:
- later appeasement, memorialization, enshrinement, and posthumous status
  transformation of human spirits.

These are not equivalent.

## Postmortem agency test

The strongest cross-lane statement is not:

`ALL_THREE_PROVE_CONSCIOUS_DEAD`.

Instead:

- Sumerian literary evidence explicitly represents postmortem dead-person
  conditions affected by living relationships.
- Shinto later documentary/historical evidence explicitly treats some human
  spirits as continuing social/causal agents.
- Norse literary evidence supports bounded postmortem agency, while archaeology
  independently supports continuing social treatment without proving that
  agency.

Therefore:

`CONTINUING_SOCIAL_RELATIONSHIP`
is more securely cross-lane than
`ONE_SHARED_POSTMORTEM_PERSON_ONTOLOGY`.

## Material mediation test

All three lanes include material mediation, but with different evidentiary force:

- Sumerian: burial/provisioning and elite/ordinary differentiation constrain
  literary reconstruction.
- Norse: grave architecture, objects, bodies, reopening, and material sequences
  are the strongest evidence mode.
- Shinto: later shrines, mausolea, spirit vessels, household memorials, and
  ritual institutions are historically relevant, but should not be projected
  into ancient funerary practice.

Therefore:

`MATERIAL_MEDIATION_OF_RELATION_TO_DEAD`
is comparable.

But:

`SAME_MATERIAL_FUNCTION_OR_METAPHYSICS`
is not established.

## Social differentiation result

Mortuary relation is strongly conditioned by social differentiation:

- Sumerian: royal/elite and ordinary mortuary evidence must remain separate;
  descendant support can matter in literary representations.
- Norse: burial forms and richness vary by region, period, and social status.
- Shinto: court, dangerous-dead cult, named posthumous cult, household ancestor,
  and modern institutional layers are not interchangeable.

This makes:

`WHO_THE_DEAD_PERSON_WAS_AND_WHICH_COMMUNITY_IS_ACTING`

a required comparison field.

## Comparative-problem-space consequence

The common problem-space is strong:

- how communities maintain or terminate ties to the dead;
- whether the dead can benefit, threaten, protect, or remain socially present;
- how bodies, graves, objects, offerings, memory, and institutions mediate that
  relationship;
- how social status changes treatment before and after death.

The answers remain historically and evidentially heterogeneous.

Therefore:

`SHARED_MORTUARY_PROBLEM_SPACE`
!=
`ONE_SHARED_AFTERLIFE_SYSTEM`.

## Systems-model boundary

Possible downstream analogies to persistent identity, external state,
maintenance, or transformed status are `PROJECT ANALOGY` or
`SPECULATIVE MODEL`.

They are not historical evidence and do not establish a technological account
of death or consciousness.
