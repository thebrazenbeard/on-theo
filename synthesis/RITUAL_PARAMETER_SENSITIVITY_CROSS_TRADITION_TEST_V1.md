# Ritual Parameter Sensitivity Cross-Tradition Test V1

Status: NON-CANONICAL / EXACT-HEAD-BOUND / HOSTILE COMPARATIVE TEST

## Question

Do source-secure religious practices preserve procedures whose validity or
represented efficacy depends on structured combinations of operator, state,
sound/speech, sequence, time, place, material, symbol or authorization?

Yes.

Does that establish one hidden ritual technology, anomalous information channel,
architectural API, simulator interface or exploit?

No.

The strongest result at the current evidence cut is:

`SOURCE_SECURE_PARAMETER_SENSITIVITY_EXISTS`

while simultaneously:

`ONE_SHARED_HIDDEN_MECHANISM = NOT_SUPPORTED`

and:

`EMPIRICALLY_VERIFIED_EXTERNAL_EFFECT_FROM_PARAMETER_EXACTNESS = NONE_ESTABLISHED`.

Exact source heads are frozen in
`research/ritual-interface-exploit/SOURCE_BINDINGS_V1.yaml`.

## Why “brittleness” must be decomposed

A procedure can be brittle for radically different reasons.

A Buddhist ordination can fail because the community act is constituted by its
quorum, motion, announcements and objection rules. No hidden external causal
mechanism is required.

A Vedic ritual text can connect exact fire, timing, offering, role and sequence
with a source-asserted effect, while also preserving disruption/expiation rules.
That is a stronger candidate for the present research question because the
represented efficacy is not exhausted by legal status. It is still not empirical
verification of the asserted effect.

A Maya or Mexica calendrical/divinatory system can be highly formal in its input
structure while leaving the accuracy of its future-oriented interpretation
unverified.

A Norse or Olmec archaeological deposit can be demonstrably structured while the
recipient, requested effect and exact operating procedure remain unrecoverable.

Therefore the comparison separates at least:

1. constitutive procedural validity;
2. ritual-state eligibility;
3. source-asserted causal or cosmological efficacy;
4. formal divinatory information practice;
5. material ritual order without recoverable semantics;
6. empirically demonstrated external effect.

The first five are historically real source categories. Only the sixth would
begin to carry direct weight for an anomalous-interface hypothesis.

## Strong negative control — Buddhist Vinaya

The selected Vinaya packet is one of the cleanest controls against overreading
ritual exactness.

Uposatha / Pātimokkha and selected ordination procedure include combinations of:

- calendrical observance;
- properly constituted assembly;
- competent reciter or officiant;
- candidate/preceptor relation;
- formal motion;
- repeated announcements;
- silence as procedural approval;
- objection as blocking action;
- disclosure/confession;
- qualifications and disqualifications;
- quorum or jurisdiction conditions.

This is genuine procedural brittleness.

Yet the effect is largely legible as a religious-legal/institutional status:
the communal act is valid or invalid, a person becomes ordained, an observance
is properly performed.

This demonstrates:

`EXACT_RELIGIOUS_PROCEDURE != EVIDENCE_OF_EXTERNAL_INTERFACE`.

Any cross-tradition model that treats exactness itself as exotic fails this
control.

## Strongest current claimed-efficacy case — selected Vedic ritual

The current Vedic packet binds:

- sacred-fire configuration;
- sunrise/sunset or other ritual timing;
- offerings;
- specialist roles;
- ordered action;
- ritual speech;
- disruption and expiation.

The project already records the safe abstraction:

`RITUAL_CONFIGURATION -> SOURCE_ASSERTED_OR_IMPLIED_EFFECT`.

The current external scholarship pass strengthens the importance of the error
dimension without upgrading efficacy.

Ute Hüsken's edited volume *When Rituals go Wrong: Mistakes, Failure, and the
Dynamics of Ritual* (Brill, 2007; DOI 10.1163/EJ.9789004158115.I-377) includes
Axel Michaels on “Perfection and Mishaps in Vedic Rituals.” Kathryn
McClymond's *Ritual Gone Wrong* (Oxford University Press, 2016;
DOI 10.1093/ACPROF:OSO/9780199790913.001.0001) likewise treats disruption and
repair as revealing ritual structure rather than as proof of supernatural
mechanics.

Frits Staal's formal study of Vedic ritual and later work on ritual syntax are
relevant because ordered forms can be analyzed independently of doctrinal
interpretation. Richard K. Payne's “Ritual Syntax Revisited” (*Numen*, 2022;
DOI 10.1163/15685276-12341664) explicitly develops syntactic analysis of ritual
while distinguishing production from analysis.

Current result:

`VEDIC_PARAMETER_SENSITIVITY = BOUNDED`.

`VEDIC_EXTERNAL_EFFECT_VERIFIED = NO`.

This is the strongest current “Luke in Lakeview” candidate only in the limited
sense that apparently exact configurations plus error repair and represented
efficacy coexist. It is not evidence that the procedure exploits hidden
architecture.

## Shinto — operational ritual state, not proven sonic key

The Engishiki/norito packet supports:

- ritual speech;
- named purification figures;
- calendrical purification;
- pollution/transgression distinctions;
- purification actions;
- authorized ritual contexts;
- offerings and place as relevant in the broader ritual system.

The strongest neutral abstraction remains:

`RITUAL_STATE -> POLLUTION_OR_TRANSGRESSION -> PURIFICATION -> RESTORED_ELIGIBILITY_ORDER`.

The current packet does not establish that a particular acoustic frequency,
pitch or pronunciation independently causes an external effect.

Therefore:

`RITUAL_SPEECH_PRESENT != ACOUSTIC_KEY_ESTABLISHED`.

## Qur'anic layer — direction and time without retrojected later liturgy

The Qur'anic packet directly supports:

- qibla / directional prayer instruction;
- fasting windows and allowances;
- prayer commands and communal religious order.

It does not provide the entire later Islamic prayer, tajwid, dhikr or legal
ritual corpus as one self-contained procedure.

That later procedural system requires its own diachronic source packet.

This case is useful because it shows how easily a modern comparison could
retroject later precision into an earlier textual layer if the source boundary
were ignored.

## Early Christian / Valentinian-associated material — multimodal but not one sequence

The Excerpta packet contains an unusually rich cluster:

- water;
- Spirit;
- Name/sealing language;
- bread and oil;
- fasting;
- supplication;
- prayer;
- raised hands;
- kneeling.

But the source is composite, exact attribution is locally uncertain, and
Irenaeus independently reports diverse rites among groups he treats as related.

The project therefore rejects reconstruction of one standardized Valentinian
“protocol.”

This is a useful hostile control:

`MULTIPLE_MODALITIES != ONE_EXECUTABLE_SEQUENCE`.

## Calendrical divination — Maya and Mexica/Nahua

The Dresden Codex and Codex Borbonicus provide strong source-level evidence for
formal temporal/symbolic systems.

Dresden:

- 260-day Tzolk'in structure;
- astronomical/calendrical tables;
- divinatory classification;
- ritual response;
- offerings/deity relations.

Borbonicus:

- tonalpohualli;
- trecenas;
- day signs and numbers;
- deity/patron imagery;
- festival cycle.

These are genuinely parameterized information practices.

But:

`FORMAL_DIVINATORY_INPUT != PROSPECTIVE_PREDICTIVE_VALIDITY`.

A modern experiment would have to freeze the output rule before knowing the
target and forbid post-hoc interpretive matching.

## Ifá — formal signs plus expert interpretation

Public-source reconstruction supports:

- generated signs;
- a fixed 256-odù index;
- an open/distributed oral verse corpus;
- trained specialist interpretation;
- situational application.

This is particularly useful because it separates:

`FORMAL_INDEX`

from

`OPEN_CORPUS`

from

`INTERPRETIVE_EXPERTISE`.

The interpretive step creates a major ordinary/null pathway. Any anomalous
information test must predeclare what counts as the output and prevent a
post-result choice among a large repertoire from manufacturing a “hit.”

Restricted initiatory material is not required and will not be collected merely
to make the test look complete.

## Material negative controls — Norse and Olmec

Tissø and La Venta show why archaeology cannot be forced into an interface model.

The material can securely establish:

- structured deposition;
- ritual landscapes;
- labor-intensive configurations;
- objects/animals/feasting in ritual contexts;
- staged placement.

It often cannot securely establish:

- exact spoken formula;
- recipient;
- requested outcome;
- semantic rule;
- failure condition.

These are not weak cases to repair with imagination.

They are negative controls on what material evidence can tell us.

## Ordinary explanations are not afterthoughts

The external scholarship pass makes several ordinary mechanisms mandatory
rivals.

Hobson, Schroeder, Risen, Xygalatas and Inzlicht, “The Psychology of Rituals”
(*Personality and Social Psychology Review*, 2018;
DOI 10.1177/1088868317734944) models ritual in terms of predefined, rigid
sequences and reviews effects on emotion, performance and social connection.

Work on causal opacity and overimitation shows that humans can preserve actions
with unclear instrumental purpose at high fidelity. Ritualized actions can be
copied precisely precisely because learners cannot safely infer which components
are dispensable.

Research on synchrony shows that moving, singing, chanting or marching together
can affect affiliation/cooperation without requiring an external sacred agent.
See “The cultural evolutionary trade-off of ritualistic synchrony,”
*Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* 375 (2020), article
20190432, DOI 10.1098/rstb.2019.0432.

Costly-signaling accounts likewise provide ordinary mechanisms for expensive,
difficult or painful rites to stabilize commitment and cooperation.

These models make an important prediction:

`APPARENTLY_ARBITRARY_PRESERVATION`
can arise even when the preserved detail has no external causal role.

Therefore “why else would they preserve that weird detail?” is not an adequate
argument.

## What would count as a low-base-rate residue?

A stronger case would require several features together:

- source-secure claim that a low-semantic parameter materially changes outcome;
- explicit correct-versus-incorrect contrast;
- semantically equivalent substitution reported as non-equivalent;
- order sensitivity that is not simply constitutive of a legal/social act;
- an outcome external to the ritual's own institutional definition;
- prospective output narrow enough to score;
- a plausible way to test authentic versus matched-sham conditions;
- survival after acoustic, physiological, expectancy, authority and
  transmission controls.

No current ON_THEO case satisfies all of these with an empirically verified
external effect.

That is not a failure of the hypothesis test.

It is the current evidence ceiling.
