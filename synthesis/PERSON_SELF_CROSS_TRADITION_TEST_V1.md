# Person / Self Cross-Tradition Test V1

Status: SIX-LANE BOUNDED COMPARISON / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

All six current non-Abrahamic lanes now have admitted evidence for
`PERSON_OR_SELF_MODEL`.

This test asks:

> Do the six source lanes converge on one model of what a person or self is?

At current evidence depth, they do not.

The stronger result is that a simple binary such as:

`SOUL`
versus
`NO_SOUL`

is too crude to represent the controlled record.

Exact source heads are locked in:
- `synthesis/BOUNDED_NEUTRAL_COMPARISON_MATRIX_V4.yaml`.

## 1. Buddhism

The bounded Buddhist lane uses:
- SN 22.59;
- SN 44.10;
- cross-transmission controls.

The five aggregates are subjected to not-self analysis.

The source treatment does not support turning this into the simplistic claim:

> "Buddhism says persons do not exist."

Nor does the continuity lane support inserting a hidden permanent self merely
because renewed existence is represented.

Project abstraction:

`AGGREGATE_ANALYSIS_WITH_NOT_SELF_CRITIQUE`.

The important methodological feature is that:
- analysis of components;
- continuity;
- identity language;
- permanent-self assertion

must remain separate questions.

## 2. Hindu / early Upaniṣadic layer

The selected Bṛhadāraṇyaka and Chāndogya material contains:
- `ātman` / self language;
- death-transition;
- desire/action/becoming;
- continuity;
- essence/identity formulations.

But the current project explicitly blocks collapsing this material into one
later Vedānta system.

Project abstraction:

`SELF_LANGUAGE_WITH_CONTINUITY_AND_INTERPRETIVE_OPENNESS`.

The lane is therefore partial, not because "self" is absent, but because the
semantic and school-historical interpretation is not exhausted by the selected
passages.

## 3. Daoism

Selected Zhuangzi Inner-Chapter material emphasizes:
- perspective;
- instability of fixed distinctions;
- transformations of form;
- life/death transformation.

The current bounded packet is stronger on transformation than on an enduring
self-substance.

Project abstraction:

`TRANSFORMATIONAL_PERSPECTIVAL_PERSONHOOD`.

This does not mean:
- no continuity of any kind;
- annihilation;
- a Buddhist not-self doctrine.

Different source grammar must remain different.

## 4. Shinto

The new Shinto personhood packet adds a different structure.

`tama/mitama` language and the court `chinkon-sai` tradition support a
ritual model in which a human spirit can be represented as distinguishable
enough from the living body to:
- depart;
- be recalled;
- be pacified;
- be strengthened.

Later historical layers then represent:
- ancestral spirits;
- dangerous dead;
- named humans becoming enshrined as kami;
- modern ancestor and funeral systems.

Project abstraction:

`EMBODIED_PERSON_WITH_RITUALLY_DISTINGUISHABLE_SPIRIT_COMPONENT`.

This is deliberately not called:
- immortal soul;
- Cartesian dualism;
- universal Shinto anthropology.

The evidence changes by period.

## 5. Norse

The Ask and Embla sequence represents human beings as initially lacking
important capacities and receiving them through divine agency.

Later human-afterlife material preserves recognizable dead persons who retain:
- names;
- relationships;
- social identity;
- narrative agency.

Project abstraction:

`CAPACITY_ENDOWED_AND_NARRATIVELY_PERSISTENT_PERSON`.

The current record still lacks a single explicit philosophical account of the
self.

## 6. Sumerian

The deep Sumerian death lane distinguishes:
- `gidim` as a deceased-person spirit/ghost category;
- place terms such as `kur`, Arali, and Ganzer;
- differentiated mortuary status;
- descendants and offerings;
- named/social identity of the dead.

Project abstraction:

`SOCIALLY_IDENTIFIABLE_POSTMORTEM_PERSON`.

The evidence is strong enough to preserve deceased-person identity, but not to
construct one complete Sumerian philosophical anthropology.

## 7. Six-lane result

The six source lanes currently yield at least six different analytical forms:

1. aggregate analysis with not-self critique;
2. self-language with continuity and interpretive openness;
3. transformational/perspectival personhood;
4. embodied person plus ritually distinguishable spirit component;
5. capacity-endowed person with persistent narrative identity;
6. socially identifiable deceased-person/ghost continuity.

These are not interchangeable.

Therefore:

`ALL_TRADITIONS_HAVE_A_MODEL_OF_PERSONHOOD`
does not imply
`ALL_TRADITIONS_HAVE_A_SOUL_DOCTRINE`.

And:

`POSTMORTEM_CONTINUITY`
does not imply
`UNCHANGED_SUBSTANTIAL_SELF`.

## 8. A useful negative result

The modern philosophical category:

`ONE_AUTONOMOUS_UNITARY_SELF`

is not a safe neutral default for this comparison.

That conclusion does **not** mean all six traditions reject such a self in the
same way.

It means the comparison schema should not assume the answer in advance.

Likewise:

`SOUL`

is too overloaded to serve as a neutral axis value without source-specific
definition.

## 9. Relation to the continuity test

This person/self test sharpens the earlier continuity result.

A tradition can model continuity through:
- causal conditioning;
- self-linked rebirth;
- transformation;
- spirit continuity;
- narrative/social identity;
- ghost/ancestor relation.

So ON_THEO should keep:

`PERSON_OR_SELF_MODEL`

separate from:

`CONTINUITY_MODEL`.

The two interact but are not equivalent.

## 10. Systems-model consequence

A computational model might analogize personhood to:
- persistent identity;
- process;
- state bundle;
- object plus metadata;
- runtime instance;
- distributed relational state.

None of those analogies is neutral enough to replace the source models.

For example:
- Buddhist aggregate/not-self analysis may map poorly to a persistent-object model;
- Shinto `tama` language may superficially resemble detachable state but should
  not be rewritten as data storage;
- Sumerian `gidim` social continuity may be more relational than object-like;
- Zhuangzi transformation may resist identity-preserving serialization.

Therefore:

`SOURCE_PERSONHOOD`
->
`NEUTRAL_PERSON_SELF_AXIS`
->
`OPTIONAL_SYSTEMS_MAPPING`.

Not the reverse.

## 11. Project-level conclusion

The person/self comparison reinforces the broader ON_THEO direction:

The repeated cross-cultural feature is a **problem space**:
- embodiment;
- agency;
- identity;
- continuity;
- transformation;
- death;
- relational recognition.

The source-supported solutions remain heterogeneous.

That is analytically stronger than claiming a universal soul doctrine.

## Next frontier

The strongest next discriminator is now
`CONTACT_AND_REINTERPRETATION_MODEL`.

Four lanes already have admitted evidence.

The largest high-value gap is Daoist-Buddhist interaction because:
- the Daoist lane already has a classical -> Celestial Masters transition;
- Buddhist contact later materially changes Daoist texts, cosmology, ritual,
  institutions, and salvation structures;
- the interaction can test whether ON_THEO can model mutual transformation
  without reducing it to simple borrowing.

A dedicated Daoist-Buddhist contact packet should therefore be the next source
pass.
