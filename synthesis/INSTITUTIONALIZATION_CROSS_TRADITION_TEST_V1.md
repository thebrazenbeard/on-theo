# Institutionalization Cross-Tradition Test V1

Status: FIVE-LANE BOUNDED/PARTIAL COMPARISON / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

The neutral question is:

> How are religious teachings, specialists, communities, authorities, sites,
> texts, rituals, and material resources organized and stabilized?

Five current source lanes are admitted:
- Buddhism;
- Daoism;
- Shinto;
- Norse;
- Sumerian.

The Hindu lane is not filled merely for symmetry because its current exact
source state does not explicitly admit `INSTITUTIONALIZATION_MODEL`.

Method schema:

`synthesis/INSTITUTIONALIZATION_COMPARISON_SCHEMA_V1.yaml`.

The test does not assume that all five traditions possess:
- churches;
- priesthoods;
- centralized bureaucracies;
- formal membership systems;
- one institution type.

## Cross-lane comparison

The shared axis survives, but there is no shared institutional form.

### Community constitution

- Buddhist: formal communal/legal acts and ordination boundaries.
- Daoist: registers, ranks, offices, and revealed/ritual authorization.
- Shinto: court registration and administration of ritual sites/obligations.
- Norse: materially organized elite ritual landscape; formal membership not
  demonstrated.
- Sumerian: city/temple/administrative and domestic cult infrastructure.

### Political relationship

Political power occupies different positions:
- external patron/regulator in selected Buddhist inscriptional evidence;
- partially fused with territorial religious administration in the Hanzhong
  Daoist case;
- direct administrative organizer in the ritsuryō Shinto layer;
- elite/magnate-linked but incompletely specified at Tissø;
- embedded with city/kingship administration in selected Sumerian contexts.

Therefore:

`RELIGION_STATE_RELATION`
is not one binary variable.

### Centralization

The evidence does not support treating "institutionalized" as "centralized."

- Buddhist saṅgha procedure can be highly formal while local/plural.
- Daoist registers can formalize membership without proving one uniform empire-
  wide organization.
- Shinto court administration can coordinate many shrines without erasing local
  cult plurality.
- Norse ritual sites can be organized without evidence of formal centralized
  governance.
- Sumerian monumental institutions vary city by city and coexist with domestic
  cult.

Therefore:

`FORMALIZATION_NE_CENTRALIZATION`.

## Comparative-problem-space consequence

The strongest common problem-space concerns:
- who may perform valid acts;
- how membership/status is recognized;
- how ritual obligations are stabilized;
- how sacred sites and resources are maintained;
- how political power relates to religious organization;
- how organization scales without necessarily centralizing.

The answers remain heterogeneous.

Therefore:

`SHARED_INSTITUTIONAL_PROBLEM_SPACE`
!=
`ONE_SHARED_RELIGIOUS_ORGANIZATION`.
