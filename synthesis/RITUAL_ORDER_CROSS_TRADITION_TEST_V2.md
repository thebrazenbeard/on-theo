# Ritual Order Cross-Tradition Test V2

Status: SIX-LANE BOUNDED COMPARISON / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

All six current non-Abrahamic lanes now have exact source-bound evidence for
`RITUAL_ORDER_MODEL`.

The question is not whether all six "have rituals."

The question is whether their admitted ritual systems instantiate the same
mechanism.

They do not at current evidence depth.

## Buddhism

Selected Vinaya procedures support a communal legal/ritual system.

Uposatha and Pātimokkha recitation require combinations of:
- calendar;
- constituted assembly;
- competent reciter;
- disclosure of offences;
- communal purity/status;
- quorum-sensitive procedure.

Selected full ordination requires:
- candidate;
- preceptor;
- qualified saṅgha;
- formal motion;
- repeated announcements;
- silence as approval;
- objection as a blocking condition.

Project abstraction:

`COMMUNAL_LEGAL_RITUAL_ORDER`.

This is not a claim that Buddhist ritual is merely secular administration.

## Six-lane result

The strongest neutral common structure is not:

`ONE_RITUAL_TECHNOLOGY`.

It is:

`RITUAL_VALIDITY_DEPENDS_ON_STRUCTURED_RELATIONS`.

Across the six lanes those relations can include:
- authorized persons or offices;
- communal status;
- time;
- place;
- material;
- speech;
- sequence;
- offerings;
- purity or disciplinary status;
- institutional authorization.

But the source-specific validity conditions differ substantially.

Therefore:

`ALL_SIX_HAVE_ORDERED_RITUAL`
!=
`ALL_SIX_SHARE_ONE_RITUAL_MECHANISM`.

## Systems-model consequence

The GOD model can use protocol-like language downstream:
- permissions;
- state transitions;
- valid calls;
- role authorization;
- boundary conditions.

But the cross-tradition ritual evidence does not independently establish that
rituals are computational APIs, hidden system calls, or controller interfaces.

A safe mapping is:

`SOURCE_RITUAL_STRUCTURE`
->
`NEUTRAL_VALIDITY_RELATIONS`
->
`OPTIONAL_SYSTEMS_ANALOGY`.

The analogy must be allowed to fail.

## Project consequence

This comparison strengthens the existing project-level conclusion:

ON_THEO is finding repeated **formal problem structures** more reliably than one
repeated hidden doctrine.

Ritual is a particularly strong example.

All six traditions can be compared on:
- what makes an act valid;
- who may perform it;
- what states participants must occupy;
- what material/time/place constraints matter;
- what institutional relationships are required.

The answers remain different.
